# The Bessel functions

[The implementation](Bessel.lean) exports the Bessel functions of the first and second kinds `j0`, `j1`, `y0`, `y1`, `jn`, and `yn`, ports of fdlibm's `e_j0.c`, `e_j1.c`, and `e_jn.c` as FreeBSD has them [1, 2, 3], with the earlier ports of `sin`, `cos`, and `log` in place of the C library's.  The decided bound is in units of the modulus `√(J² + Y²)` of the order, `c · 2^-53 · √(J² + Y²)` with `c` set by the proof, since no permissively licensed implementation reaches a step bound next to the zeros of the functions.  `transcendentals` proves in ProofKit the real functions `J0`, `J1`, `Y0`, and `Y1`, their large-argument forms, fdlibm's rational approximations of those forms, and lower bounds on the moduli, with only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.  It also proves the exceptional results of `j0`, `j1`, `y0`, and `y1` and their accuracy theorems: `j0` with `c = 10` and `j1` with `c = 12` for every finite argument, `y0` with `c = 11` for every finite `x > 0`, and `y1` with `c = 12` for every finite `x ≥ 2^-1022`, together with one representable step from the nearest double to `Y₁(x)` for `0 < x` below about `2^-54`, subnormal `x` included.  The accuracy theorems of `jn` and `yn` are open.  The module compiles with `verified_compile`, which checks the encoding one function at a time in about 1.0 GB, `BesselBytes` proves the byte theorems of `j0`, `j1`, `y0`, and `y1`, and `BesselFile` proves the file theorem.

## Specification

`j0`, `j1`, `y0`, and `y1` compute `J₀`, `J₁`, `Y₀`, and `Y₁` of the value of the argument, and `jn n x` and `yn n x` compute `J_n` and `Y_n` with the order `n` read as C's `int` in the low 32 bits of the word, in two's complement.  `J_(-n) = (-1)^n J_n` and `Y_(-n) = (-1)^n Y_n` define the negative orders, and the port takes `|n| = 2^31` for `n = -2^31`, where C's `-n` overflows, a choice that the decision of 2026-10-11 accepts ([the journal](../../devnotes.md#2026-10-11-a-software-fused-multiply-add-core-maths-gamma-functions-and-fdlibms-bessel-functions)).  The first-kind functions are defined for every finite `x`, and the second-kind functions for `x > 0`.  [ProofKit's definitions](../../LeanExe/ProofKit/Bessel.lean) give `J₀`, `J₁`, `Y₀`, and `Y₁` by their power series in `z = x²/4`, DLMF 10.2.2, 10.8.1, and 10.8.2 [4], with Euler's constant `Real.eulerMascheroniConstant` and the harmonic numbers, and no definition of `J_n` or `Y_n` for `|n| ≥ 2` exists yet.

The decided statement, from the integration of 2026-10-12 and [the plan](../../transcendentals.md#functions), is `|w - F (a)| ≤ c · 2^-53 · √(J_n (a)² + Y_n (a)²)` for the value `w` of the result, the real function `F`, and a constant `c` that the proof sets.  For `jn` and `yn`, the decision of 2026-10-11 allows a bound `a + b |n|` that grows with the order ([the journal](../../devnotes.md#2026-10-11-decisions-on-tanh-the-reflector-and-the-bessel-bounds)).  The modulus never vanishes, so the allowed error stays positive at the zeros of `J` and `Y`, where a bound in steps would need the distance of every zero from its nearest doubles, which has not been computed for these functions ([the review](../../devnotes.md#2026-10-10-review-of-the-options-for-stages-7-and-8)).  For a negative `x`, `J_n` is defined, `Y_n` is not, and the native check measures with the modulus at `|x|`.  `j0_accuracy`, `j1_accuracy`, and `jn_accuracy` state the bound for every finite `x` with the modulus at `|x|`.  Below about `2^-54`, `y1` returns `-tpi/x`, whose exact value exceeds the largest double for `x` below about `0.64 · 2^-1024`, so the result is `-∞` there, and a bound in units of the modulus cannot describe it.  The decision of 2026-10-12 states this range as `F64SignedRounding.WithinSteps n (Y₁ (a)) w`, the distance in representable steps from the nearest-even double to `Y₁(a)`, which counts `-∞` as the step beyond the largest finite double, and that the two statements of `y1` together cover every `x > 0`.

The native check measures in another unit: `2^(⌊log₂ M⌋ - 52)` for the modulus `M`, which lies between `2^-53 M` and `2^-52 M`.  An error of `e` of the check's units is therefore between `e` and `2e` units of `2^-53 M`, and the check's bounds of 4 and `4 + |n|/3` do not fix `c`.  A result within 4 of the check's units lies within `8 · 2^-53 M`, and the largest measured error of the four functions, 3.243 units for `y1`, is at most `6.49 · 2^-53 M`.

The C standard's Annex F and the recommended operations of IEEE 754-2019 include no Bessel function, and POSIX specifies them in its XSI option [6, 7].  The port follows fdlibm's special values, which agree with POSIX.1-2017 and with POSIX.1-2024 except where the table says.  The table lists the port's results and the rule of POSIX.1-2024, and `BesselSpecial` proves the rows for NaN and the infinities of `j0`, `j1`, `y0`, and `y1` and the rows for the zeros and the negative arguments of `y0` and `y1`.

| Input | Result of the port | POSIX.1-2024 [6] |
|---|---|---|
| NaN, every function | the canonical NaN | NaN |
| `j0 (±0)` | `1` | not addressed |
| `j1 (±0)` | `±0` | not addressed |
| `jn n (±0)` with `\|n\| ≥ 2` | `±0`, negative for odd `n` and a negative signed argument after the change of sign for a negative order | not addressed |
| `j0 (±∞)` | `+0` | `+0` for `+∞` |
| `j1 (±∞)` | `±0` | `+0` for `+∞` |

| `y0`, `y1`, `yn` at `x < 0`, `-∞` included | the canonical NaN | NaN, or the result at 0 |

POSIX.1-2017 gives `-HUGE_VAL` for `yn` at 0 with every order [7], and the review of 2026-10-10 recommended that rule as POSIX's, so the port's `-∞` for a negative odd order follows the 2017 edition and fdlibm and departs from the 2024 edition.  `yn (-1) (+∞) = -0` comes from fdlibm's `sign*y1(x)`, while POSIX.1-2024 gives `+0` for `+∞`.  fdlibm raises the divide-by-zero and invalid exceptions on these paths, and the port does not.

## Algorithm

The methods are those that fdlibm's comments describe [1, 2, 3].  Below 2, `j0` is `1 - z/4 + z² R₀(z)/S₀(z)` with `z = x²`, and `j1` is `x/2 + x z R(z)/S(z)`, rational approximations whose stated errors are `2^-63.67` and `2^-61.51`.  `Y₀ - (2/π) J₀ ln x` is even and `Y₁ - (2/π)(J₁ ln x - 1/x)` is odd, as the series of DLMF 10.8.2 and 10.8.1 show, so `y0` is `U(z)/V(z) + (2/π) j0(x) ln x` and `y1` is `x U(z)/V(z) + (2/π)(j1(x) ln x - 1/x)`, with stated errors `2^-72` and `2^-65.89`.  Tiny arguments take the leading terms: `j0 = 1` and `j1 = x/2` below `2^-27`, `y0 = u₀₀ + (2/π) ln x` below `2^-27`, and `y1 = -2/(π x)` below `2^-54`.

From 2 up, the functions are `√(2/(π x)) (P cos θ ∓ Q sin θ)` and `√(2/(π x)) (P sin θ ± Q cos θ)` with `θ = x - π/4` for order 0 and `θ = x - 3π/4` for order 1, the large-argument forms of DLMF 10.17.3 and 10.17.4 [4].  fdlibm's `pzero`, `qzero`, `pone`, and `qone` approximate `P` by `1 + R(t)/(1 + t S(t))` and `Q` by `(q₀ + R(t)/(1 + t S(t)))/x` with `t = 1/x²`, `q₀ = -1/8` or `3/8`, on the four ranges that start at 2, at about 2.8571, at about 4.5454, and at 8, chosen by the high word of `x`.  `cos θ` and `sin θ` are `(sin x ± cos x)/√2`, and the one of `sin x ± cos x` that cancels comes from `-cos (2x)` divided by the other, which avoids the cancellation.  Above `2^129` the code drops `P - 1` and `Q`, and from `2^1023` on it does not form `cos (2x)`.

`jn` uses the forward recurrence `J_(k+1) = (2k/x) J_k - J_(k-1)` from `j0` and `j1` when `n ≤ x`, where it is stable, and the leading term of the asymptotic expansion above `2^302`.  Below `2^-30` it takes the leading term `(x/2)^n/n!` of the series, and 0 for `n > 33`.  The test `ix < 0x3e100000` of `e_jn.c` compares the high word with that of `2^-30`, while its comment and the docstring of `jnBackward` say `2^-29`.  Otherwise it evaluates a continued fraction for `J_n/J_(n-1)`, whose depth a recurrence for its convergents sets, runs the recurrence backward to orders 0 and 1, rescaling by `1/b` when `b` exceeds `10^100` if `n ln (2n/x)` reaches `ln` of the largest double, 709.78, and normalizes by the larger of `j0 x` and `j1 x`.  `yn` uses the forward recurrence from `y0` and `y1`, which is stable for `Y`, and stops when the value reaches `-∞`.

## Implementation

The sources are FreeBSD's `lib/msun/src/e_j0.c`, `e_j1.c`, and `e_jn.c` at commit `20381bce4b63975494a2f4bc84257f6932e6d379` [1, 2, 3], each with the 1993 copyright notice of Sun Microsystems, which states that permission "to use, copy, modify, and distribute this software is freely granted, provided that this notice is preserved".  The module docstring of [the implementation](Bessel.lean) gives the copyright line and paraphrases the permission as "freely distributable with this notice", the same defect that the plan records for the docstrings of `Erf.lean`, `Tanh.lean`, `Sinh.lean`, and `Asinh.lean`.  The port follows the C code operation for operation in the Verified dialect, and the `verified_compile` call lists 40 functions: 13 of [the trigonometric functions](Trig.lean), 3 of [the logarithm](Log.lean), and 24 of this module.

| C code | Port | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `sincos (x, &s, &c)` | `Trig.sinWith` and `Trig.cosWith` with `Trig.invPi` and `Trig.halfPi` | FreeBSD's `sincos` evaluates the kernel formulas of its `sin` and `cos`, of which `Trig` is a port, so the journal concludes that the values are FreeBSD's. |
| `cos (x + x)` | `Trig.cosWith` | The same. |
| `log` | `Log.logWith` with `Log.logTable` and `Log.logTable2` | Arm's `log`, whose results may differ from fdlibm's `log` in the last bit. |
| `sqrt` | `Float.sqrt` | Correctly rounded in both. |
| `int n` | the low 32 bits of a `UInt64`, two's complement, with `\|n\| = 2^31` for `n = -2^31` | The dialect's words are unsigned, and C's `-n` overflows there. |
| the `while` loop that sizes the continued fraction | `LeanExe.repeatWhile` with `2^32 - 1` steps of fuel | The dialect's loops are bounded, and the module's docstring states that the fuel exceeds the steps the loop can take. |
| `huge + x > one`, `one/vzero`, `vzero/vzero` | the same expressions with `1e300`, `1.0/0.0`, and `0.0/0.0` | The values are kept, and the dialect has no flags. |
| `verified_compile` | present | The command checks the encoding one function at a time, and the module's build needs about 1.0 GB, as [the gamma function's Proof section](Tgamma.md#proof) describes. |

The tables `j0Table` (92 words) and `j1Table` (102 words) hold the coefficients of `pzero` and `qzero`, or `pone` and `qone`, on the four ranges, and `j1Table` also those of `y1` below 2.  The other coefficients below 2 are written inline, and each word carries fdlibm's decimal literal in a comment.  The journal records that a script checked fdlibm's bit patterns against the rounding of each decimal literal.  A comparison of the port with the three C files at the pinned commit, run outside the repository, gives the results in the table.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|

| `double` arrays | All 34 occur in order as contiguous runs of `j0Table` or `j1Table`. |

## Theorems

`BesselJnTheorem.jn_accuracy` states that `Bessel.jn` lies within `(23 + 12 |N|) · 2^-53 · M_|N|(|x|)` of `J_N(x)` for every finite `x` and every order `N`, C's `int` in the low 32 bits of the word, outside the range `2 ≤ |x| < 2^302` with `|x| < 2 |N|`, which the decision of 2026-10-12 leaves open.  `BesselYnTheorem.yn_accuracy` states that for every finite `x > 0` and every order `N` outside the same range, `Bessel.yn` is finite and within `(250 + 12 |N|) · 2^-53 · M_|N|(x)` of `Y_N(x)`, or it is the infinity of the sign of `Y_N(x)` and `|Y_N(x)| > 2^1023`, and `yn_overflow` states that it is that infinity from `|Y_N(x)| ≥ 2^1025` on, which is the statement of the decision of 2026-10-12.  [The proof of `jn` and `yn`](#proof-of-jn-and-yn) lists the modules of both theorems, and `BesselNSpecial` proves the exceptional results of both functions in the table of the specification.  [The byte theorems](BesselBytes.lean) join the compiled module `compiled` to the accuracy theorems of the six functions.  Each of `j0_bytes_accuracy`, `j1_bytes_accuracy`, `y0_bytes_accuracy`, `y1_bytes_accuracy`, `jn_bytes_accuracy`, and `yn_bytes_accuracy` states that the module's bytes decode to the module, that functions 36 to 41 are the exports `j0`, `j1`, `y0`, `y1`, `jn`, and `yn`, and that a call of the function from a ready store returns without a trap the bits of the Lean function's result, which satisfies its accuracy theorem under that theorem's hypotheses, and leaves a ready store.  The entries `jn` and `yn` take the order and then the argument.  A store is ready when the allocator invariant holds, the six tables `Trig.invPi`, `Trig.halfPi`, `j0Table`, `j1Table`, `Log.logTable`, and `Log.logTable2` are borrowed arrays at 4096, 4264, 4288, 5032, 5856, and 7912, and the memory cap is at most 65,535 pages, and `bessel_module_accuracy` states the six theorems together.  [The file theorem](BesselFile.lean) states that the `bessel.wasm` that `tests/verified/run.sh` writes holds the module of `bessel_module_accuracy`, and its kernel check needs about 0.9 GB.  The run also checks [the accuracy](BesselAccuracy.lean) natively. ### Method

The bounds on `P` and `Q` avoid an integral representation of the Bessel functions and the connection formulas.  With `U = √(π x/2) J` and `V = √(π x/2) Y`, Bessel's equation becomes `U'' = -(1 + κ/x²) U`, and the rotated pair `P`, `Q` satisfies a second-order system.  For an approximation with residuals `r₁` and `r₂`, the square root of the energy `E = (p' - q)² + (q' + p)² + (1 + κ/x²)(p² + q²)` of the errors `p` and `q` grows by at most `|r₁| + |r₂|` when `κ ≥ 0`, and a weighted energy gives `(10/9)(|r₁| + |r₂|)` when `κ < 0` and `1 + κ/x² ≥ 81/100`.  The errors therefore stay within their value at `x = 2`, from the series, plus the integral of the residuals, which the kernel bounds from polynomials it computes.  The chain starts at `2^-83` at `x = 2` and ends below `2^-80`, and Hankel's expansion takes over at 30, below which its smallest term, near `e^(-2x)`, cannot reach `2^-80`.

The comparison with fdlibm splits each piece into dyadic segments of width `c/8` to `c/11` with polynomials of degree 16 within `2^-71` of the piece's polynomial, truncates Hankel's expansion to degree 26 within `2^-72`, and writes `A - pFd` as `E/W` with polynomials of degree 28.  `ratioCheckI` bisects a segment until the coefficients of `E` and `W` at each center give `|E| ≤ ε W`, in integers, so the kernel does no rational normalization.  The first check in rational arithmetic on the degree-40 pieces filled the runner's memory limit and was stopped, and integer bisection on degree 30 ran to the 60-minute timeout, which is why the segments exist ([the journal](../../devnotes.md#2026-10-12-bessel-functions-fdlibms-p-and-q-and-the-moduli-track-log)).

### Analysis of `jn` and `yn`

The decided bound for the orders `n` with `|n| ≥ 2` is `|w - J_n(x)| ≤ (a + b |n|) · 2^-53 · M_n(x)` for `jn` and the same with `Y_n` for `yn` on `x > 0`, with `M_n = √(J_n² + Y_n²)` (DLMF 10.18.6) [4].  `J_n` and `Y_n` for `n ≥ 0` are the functions of the series 10.2.2 and 10.8.1, and the negative orders follow `J_(-n) = (-1)^n J_n` and `Y_(-n) = (-1)^n Y_n` (10.4.1), which the code applies before it computes.  Mathlib defines no Bessel function, so a proof would define `J_n` by its series with ProofKit's `psum`, as `Bessel.J0` and `J1` are, prove the recurrence `C_(n-1) + C_(n+1) = (2n/x) C_n` (10.6.1) for that series, and define `Y_n` for `x > 0` from `Y0` and `Y1` by the same recurrence.  The cross product `J_(n+1) Y_n - J_n Y_(n+1) = 2/(π x)` (10.5.2) then holds for every `n` by induction from `Bessel.wronskian`, since each step of 10.6.1 keeps it.

After the reflection of the order the code takes one of five paths, which the table lists with the mechanism of the error and the growth in `|n|` that the analysis finds.  In the table, `u = 2^-53` and `λ_k = 2k/x`.  The numbers of the last column come from `uv run tools/bessel/ordern.py`, which computes `J_k(x)` and `Y_k(x)` to 200 digits for `x = 20`, `100`, and `300`.  Its values satisfy the cross product within `2 · 10^-70`.

For the computed `f_(k+1) = λ_k f_k - f_(k-1) + δ_k` with starting errors `e₀` and `e₁`, 10.6.1 and the cross product give the error at `n` exactly: `e_n = (π x/2) [e₁ (Y₀ J_n - J₀ Y_n) + e₀ (J₁ Y_n - Y₁ J_n)] + Σ_(k=1)^(n-1) δ_k (π x/2)(Y_k J_n - J_k Y_n)`.  The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality bounds each kernel by `M_k M_n`, so in units of `M_n` the error is at most `(π x/2)(|e₀| M₁ + |e₁| M₀) + Σ_k |δ_k| (π x/2) M_k`, where `|δ_k| ≤ (2λ_k |f_k| + |f_(k-1)| + |f_(k+1)|) u` up to second order.  For `x ≥ 2` the starting term is at most about `22 · 1.3 u`, from the accepted bounds of `j0` and `j1` (or `y0` and `y1`) and `M₀ M₁ ≤ 1.3 · 2/(π x)` from `BesselModulus.modulus0_large` and `modulus1_large`.  For `k < x`, the weight `(π x/2) M_k²` agrees with `x/√(x² - k²)` at `k = x/2` and `k = 3x/4` within 1.1% for `x = 20` and within 0.06% for `x = 100` and `300`.  At `k = x` the weight is `1.257 x^(1/3)`, and for these three values of `x` the sum `S_n = Σ_(k<n) (π x/2) M_k²` stays below `(π/2) n` for `n ≤ x` (`S_x/x` is 1.32, 1.43, and 1.48).  For `k > x`, `M_k` grows with `|Y_k|` and the Cauchy–Schwarz bound grows without limit, while the exact kernels keep the sum at most `3.72 n`, because there `J_k > 0 > Y_k` (10.21.3 places the first zeros above `k`) and `|Y_k J_n|` and `|J_k Y_n|` stay below `M_n` times quantities of order one.

Miller's method (DLMF 3.6(iii), and 10.74(iv) for the choice of backward recurrence when `n > x`) starts from fdlibm's continued fraction, whose depth `k` is the first with `Q(k) > 10^9`, the threshold that fdlibm's comment calls "good for double" [3].  The truncated fraction equals `g_n/g_(n-1)` for the solution `g` of 10.6.1 that vanishes at index `n + k + 1`, so its relative error is of the order of `J_(n+k+1) Y_(n-1)/(Y_(n+k+1) J_(n-1))`, which needs bounds on `J_m/Y_m` for `m > x`.  Backward, `J` dominates `Y` above `x`, so the rounding of the steps there stays relative to `J`, and below `x` the backward steps propagate as the forward ones do, with the same weights `(π x/2) M_k²`.  The normalization divides by the larger of `|j0 x|` and `|j1 x|`, which is at least `√((J₀² + J₁²)/2)`, and `ordern.py` finds the accepted errors `10 u M₀` of `j0` and `12 u M₁` of `j1` at most `18.4 u` relative to the value chosen, at 2,000 points from 2 to 201.9 in steps of 0.1.  The zeros of `J₀` and `J₁` interlace (10.21.2), so the code never divides by a value near a zero of both.

The analysis supports the form `a + b |n|` on every path: the starting errors give `a` of about 30, and the rounding gives `b` of about 3 to 4 in the worst-case sums above, against measured maxima of at most 10.4 check units for `jn` and 15.4 for `yn` up to order 100.  `yn` overflows to `-∞` once `|Y_n(x)|` passes the largest double, where the decided form `F64SignedRounding.WithinSteps` replaces the modulus bound, as for `y1_tiny`.  Underflow adds at most `2^-1075` per operation, and the cross product gives `M_n M_(n+1) ≥ 2/(π x)`, which with the upper bounds on the moduli keeps `M_n` far above the subnormal range, so no path needs the step form for underflow.  The obstacle is the proof of the weights near the turning point `k = x`, where `(π x/2) M_k(x)²` grows like `x^(1/3)`.  The backward recurrence of `jn` for `2 ≤ x < n` and the forward recurrence of `yn` for `2 ≤ x < n` cross that point, and the forward recurrence of `jn` approaches it as `n → x`.  A proof needs an upper bound on `M_k(x)` that holds through the turning point for every `k` up to `2^31` and every `x` from 2 to `2^302`.  DLMF gives Nicholson's integral `J_ν² + Y_ν² = (8/π²) ∫₀^∞ cosh(2νt) K₀(2x sinh t) dt` (10.9.30) and the asymptotic expansion 10.18.17, from which such a bound would have to be derived.  The recurrence gives a bound away from the turning point: `E_k = f_(k-1)² - λ_k f_(k-1) f_k + f_k²` changes by `-(2/x) f_k f_(k-1)` per step, which bounds `f_k²` by its start times about `(x/(x - k))²`: a factor of 4 for `k ≤ x/2`, and unbounded as `k → x`.

One part remains. ## Tests

[The accuracy check](BesselAccuracy.lean) compares each result natively with [the Bessel references](BesselReference.lean) in units of the modulus: with `J` and `Y` the references of the order at `q` bits, `M = isqrt (J² + Y²) · 2^-q`, the unit is `2^(⌊log₂ M⌋ - 52)` without a clamp at the subnormal range, and the error is computed exactly. ## Limits

`transcendentals` proves the decided theorems of `j0`, `j1`, `y0`, and `y1` for the Lean functions and for the bytes of the compiled module, and the decided theorems of `jn` and `yn` for the Lean functions and for the bytes outside the open range.  The results for `jn` and `yn` in the open range come from tests.  The limits of [the exponential](Exp.md#limits) apply to the byte theorems: Talos and the decoder are trusted, the theorems cover the canonical NaN argument only, and host actions between calls need their own theorem.  The file theorem also trusts `binary_file%`.  The ProofKit results rest on polynomials that a Python model produced, and the proofs use only the residuals and bounds that the kernel computes from them, so the model is not trusted.

The modulus bound is weaker than a relative bound: next to a zero of `J` or `Y` the result may have few or no correct digits, and a theorem of this form permits that.  The constant `c` is 10 for `j0`, 12 for `j1` and `y1`, and 11 for `y0`.  Below about `2^-54`, `y1` has a bound of one step instead.  The check's unit differs from the plan's by a factor between 1 and 2, and the proofs give `a + b |n|` the values `23 + 12 |n|` for `jn` and `250 + 12 |n|` for `yn`.  The port keeps fdlibm's `-∞` for `yn` of a negative odd order at 0 and `-0` for `yn (-1) (+∞)`, where POSIX.1-2024 gives `+HUGE_VAL` and `+0`.

`yn` forms the product `((2i)/x) · b` before the difference, so it overflows on part of the band below the overflow threshold.  When `|Y_N(x)|` lies just below the threshold `2^1024 - 2^970`, `λ_(N-1) |Y_(N-1)| = |Y_N| + |Y_(N-2)|` can exceed it, and the result is `-∞` while `Y_N(x)` rounds to a finite double.  For `N = 171` and `x = 0x1.f324ae2682cfdp+0`, about 1.9498, `Y_171(x) = -0.999985 · 2^1024` and `λ_170 |Y_170| = 1.000018 · 2^1024`; the result `-∞` lies about `1.3 · 10^11` steps and `1.5 · 10^-5 · 2^1024` from `Y_171(x)`.  The band is `|Y_N(x)| ∈ [2^1024 (1 - r), 2^1024)` with `r = |Y_(N-2)/Y_N| ≈ x²/(4 N (N - 1))`, it matters where `r` exceeds a few units of `2^-53`, which needs `N ≥ 44` or so, and for `N = 171` near `x = 1.95` it holds about `1.7 · 10^9` doubles.  fdlibm and glibc compute the same product, so the port inherits this deviation from the decided bound and stays faithful to fdlibm.  The decision of 2026-10-12 states it exactly: for `|Y_N(x)| ≤ 2^1023` the result is finite within the bound, from `2^1025` on it is the infinity of the sign of `Y_N(x)`, and in between it is that infinity or finite within the bound.  `BesselYnSmall.yn_small` and `yn_small_inf` prove this statement for `0 < x < 2`, and on the range `2 |N| ≤ x` the loop's values are finite (`BesselOrderLoops.yn_forward`).  The script `w-log/ynover/probe2.py` of track `log` found the example with 200-digit values of `Y_k` and a binary64 simulation of the loop.
