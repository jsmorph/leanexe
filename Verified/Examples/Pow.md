# The power function

[The implementation](Pow.lean) exports `pow`, a port of Arm's optimized-routines `pow` [1, 2, 3] in its configuration without fused multiply-add, which computes `x^y` as `exp (y log |x|)` with a logarithm carried to about 15 bits beyond double precision and with the exponential's table of 128 values of `2^(j/128)` [4].  The decided bound is one representable step from the nearest-even rounding of `x^y`, as for [the exponential](Exp.md).  No theorem about `pow` exists in the checkout this document describes: the port, its sampled accuracy check, and its WebAssembly module are in place, and the full theorem is in progress on the `exp2` track's branch, which `transcendentals.md` records.  The sections below describe the method, the port, and the check, and they list what a proof can reuse and what remains open.

## Specification

`pow x y` computes `x^y` for doubles `x` and `y`.  For a positive finite `x` and a finite `y` the value is `exp (y log x)`, and for a negative finite `x` it is defined only for an integer `y`, as `|x|^y` with the sign of `x` when `y` is odd.  The results overflow to `±∞` from the threshold `2^1024 - 2^970` of clause 4.3.1 of IEEE 754-2019 [9] and results of magnitude at most `2^-1075` round to `±0`.  The decided bound is one step, `F64SignedRounding.WithinSteps 1`, defined in [the signed rounding reference](../../LeanExe/ProofKit/F64SignedRounding.lean), which places both zeros at position 0 and measures the distance of a word from the nearest-even reference of a signed real number.  The checkout fixes no statement yet, so the real function for a negative base and the form of the exceptional results are not settled here.

| Inputs | Result of the port | IEEE 754-2019 9.2.1 [9] and C99 F.9.4.4 [10] |
|---|---|---|
| `y = ±0` | 1, or `x + y` for a signaling NaN `x` | `pow (x, ±0)` is 1 if `x` is not a signaling NaN.  C99 gives 1 even for a NaN. |
| `x = +1` | 1, or `x + y` for a signaling NaN `y` | `pow (+1, y)` is 1 for any `y`, even a quiet NaN. |
| `x` or `y` NaN otherwise | `x + y`, the canonical NaN | A quiet NaN for a NaN operand (clause 9.2). |
| `x = -1`, `y = ±∞` | 1 | 1. |
| `y = ±∞`, `\|x\| ≠ 1` | `+0` for `\|x\| < 1` and `y = +∞` or `\|x\| > 1` and `y = -∞`, else `+∞` | The same, with `pow (±0, -∞) = +∞` in IEEE 754-2019, C11, and C23, a case that C99 does not list. |
| `x = ±0` or `±∞`, `y` finite and nonzero | `x²` or `1/x²` for negative `y`, negated for negative `x` and odd integer `y` | `±∞` (with divideByZero) or `±0` for zero `x`, and `±0` or `±∞` for infinite `x`, signed for odd integer `y`. |
| `x < 0` finite, `y` finite and not an integer | NaN, as `(x - x)/(x - x)` | NaN with the invalid operation exception. |
| `x < 0` finite, `y` an odd integer | the result for `\|x\|`, negated through the sign bias `0x800 << 7` | `pow (x, y) = -\|x\|^y`. |
| `\|y\| < 2^-65` | 1 for `\|x\| = 1`, else `1 + y` for `\|x\| > 1` and `1 - y` for `\|x\| < 1` | No special rule.  `x^y` rounds to 1 or a neighbor. |
| `\|y\| ≥ 2^63` | 1 for `\|x\| = 1`, else the overflow product when `\|x\| > 1` and `y > 0` or `\|x\| < 1` and `y < 0`, and the underflow product otherwise | No special rule.  `\|y log x\|` exceeds the finite range. |
| `x` subnormal | normalized by `2^52` before the logarithm | No special rule. |

The rows follow [the ports' journal section](../../devnotes.md#2026-10-10-ports-of-exp2-exp10-log2-log10-and-pow) and the code of `powWith`.  C99's clause F.9.4.4 lists the cases of zero, infinite, and negative arguments [10].  The same list appears as F.10.4.4 in C11 [11] and as F.10.4.5 in C23 [12], and C11 adds `pow (±0, -∞) = +∞`, the rule of IEEE 754-2019.  The dialect has no floating-point flags, so the port signals neither divideByZero nor invalid operation, and with `WANT_ERRNO` 0 the division by zero of `__math_divzero` does not occur.  Lean's `Float` holds no signaling NaN, so the `issignaling` tests matter only to a WebAssembly caller that passes one.

## Algorithm

`pow` evaluates `exp (y log |x|)` with the logarithm in two parts.  A double `log |x|` would lose the accuracy of the result for large `|y log x|`, since an absolute error `δ` in the exponent's argument becomes a relative error of about `δ` in the result, and `|y log x|` reaches 745 within the finite range.  Arm's comment states the budget: a worst error of 0.54 units, about the exponential's 0.511 units without fused multiply-add plus `1024 · ln 2 · 2^53` times the logarithm's relative error of `1.5 · 2^-68`, a product of 0.0325 units [1].

The logarithm writes `x = 2^k z` with `z` in `[0x1.69555p-1, 0x1.69555p0)`, an interval around 1 chosen so that `log x` near `x = 1` suffers no cancellation, and splits that interval into 128 subintervals.  For subinterval `i` the table holds `invc = 1/c` for a `c` near its center, `logc`, and `logctail`, so that `log x = k ln 2 + log c + log (z/c)`.  This is the reduction that Harrison, Kubaska, Story, and Tang describe for the logarithm, `ln x = ln (1/c) + ln (c x)` with `ln (1/c)` from a table indexed by the top bits of `x` [7].  Arm chooses `1/c` with few bits, so `r = z/c - 1` is exact, and rounds `logc` to a multiple of `2^-43`, so that `k ln2hi + logc` is exact [3].  Without fused multiply-add the code splits `z` at bit 32 into `zhi + zlo`, so that `rhi = zhi · invc - 1`, `rlo = zlo · invc`, and `rhi²` are exact.  A polynomial of degree 8 with leading coefficients 1 and `-1/2` approximates `log (1 + r)`, with a stated relative error of `0x1.11922ap-70` on `|r| ≤ 0x1.6bp-8`, and the code collects the rounding errors of the leading terms into a tail `lo`, returning `log x` as `hi + lo`.

The product `y (hi + lo)` keeps a tail as well.  The code clears the low 27 bits of `y` and of `hi`, so that `yhi · lhi` is exact, and forms `ehi = yhi · lhi` and `elo = ylo · lhi + y · llo`, with `|elo| < |ehi| · 2^-25` by Arm's comment [1].  The exponential `exp_inline (ehi, elo, sign_bias)` is [the exponential's](Exp.md#algorithm) table-driven reduction and polynomial [7, 8] with `elo` added to the residual, and with the sign bias `0x800 << 7` added to the key so that the scale's sign bit is set for a negative result.  Its `specialcase` differs from `exp.c`'s in two ways: it keeps the sign of the scale and compensates a sum whose magnitude is below 1 with `±1`, and for a positive key it returns `DBL_MAX` for `pow (0x1.fffffffffffffp1023, 1)` when rounding upward, a test that is false in rounding to nearest.

## Implementation

The sources are Arm's `math/pow.c` (`Copyright (c) 2018-2026, Arm Limited`), `math/pow_common.h` (`2026`), `math/pow_log_data.c` (`2018`), and `math/exp_data.c` (`2018-2023`), at commit `503fafe311c177de0e571c458c7c337b1ca5f522`, under `SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT OR Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception` [1, 2, 3, 4, 6].  The module docstring of [the implementation](Pow.lean) repeats the notice.  The configuration is the one without fused multiply-add or rounding intrinsics: `POW_LOG_TABLE_BITS` 7, `POW_LOG_POLY_ORDER` 8, `EXP_TABLE_BITS` 7, `EXP_POLY_ORDER` 5, `WANT_ROUNDING` 1, `WANT_ERRNO` 0, and `IEEE_754_2008_SNAN` 1 [5].

`verified_compile compiled := [checkint, zeroinfnan, issignaling, powLog, powSpecialCase, powExpCore, powExp, powMain, powWith, pow]` generates the module, whose functions are `alloc` (0), `release` (1), and the ten functions of the list (2 to 11), so that `pow` is function 11.  `pow` passes `powLogTable`, borrowed at address 4096, and `Exp.expTable`, borrowed at 8200, to `powWith`, and `wasm-tools print` of the 11,156-byte `pow.wasm` shows the two data segments at those addresses and every function exported.  `powExp` and `powExpCore` port `exp_inline` rather than call `Exp.expWith`, since `exp_inline` takes the tail and the sign bias.

| C code | Port | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `(a) == (b)` on two comparisons, in the `y = ±∞` case and the `\|y\| ≥ 2^63` case | nested tests with the same results | The dialect compares no Booleans. |
| `checkint (iy)` evaluated once for a negative `x` | evaluated in each of the two tests that use it | `checkint` is a pure function, so the results are the same. |
| `k = (int64_t) tmp >> 52`, an arithmetic shift | the logical shift with the sign bits set for a negative `tmp`, and `kd` converted through its magnitude | Words are unsigned. |
| `abstop = 0` marks the special path | the Boolean `special` of `powExpCore` | As in `Exp.lean`. |
| `opt_barrier_double`, `force_eval_double`, `check_oflow`, `check_uflow` | identities or omitted | WebAssembly has no floating-point status flags. |
| `1.0 + 0x1p-60 != 1.0` in `specialcase` | kept | It is false in rounding to nearest, so the `DBL_MAX` branch is never taken. |
| `__math_oflow (s)`, `__math_uflow (s)`, `__math_invalid (x)` | the signed products `±0x1p769 · 0x1p769` and `±0x1p-767 · 0x1p-767`, and `(x - x)/(x - x)` | These are the calls' results [5]. |
| `__pow_log_data`, `__exp_data` | the tables `powLogTable` and `Exp.expTable`, and constants written as bit patterns | The dialect has no global data other than borrowed tables. |

`powLogTable` holds Arm's `__pow_log_data.tab` for `N = 128`: 512 words, `invc`, the unused `pad`, `logc`, and `logctail` for each subinterval.  A script compared its words with the 128 entries of `pow_log_data.c` at the pinned commit, and all 512 agree.  The same script compared 27 constants of the port with Arm's expressions evaluated in binary64: `ln2hi`, `ln2lo`, the seven polynomial coefficients written as Arm's scaled products such as `0x1.555555555556p-2 · -2`, the reduction and polynomial constants that `powExpCore` shares with `Exp.expCore`, and the factors of `specialcase`, `__math_oflow`, and `__math_uflow`, and all agree.  For this document a second script checked the table against Arm's comment with 80-digit decimal arithmetic: every `invc` is a multiple of `1/256`, every `logc` a multiple of `2^-43`, and `|log c - logc - logctail|` is at most `3.05 · 10^-30`, below the `2^-97 ≈ 6.31 · 10^-30` that Arm states.  The kernel checks no entry of `powLogTable`, and `ExpTableBounds.checked_table_bounds` covers `Exp.expTable`.

## Theorems

## Proof

No proof modules exist for `pow` in this checkout, so this section lists what the existing proofs provide and what remains.  The parts that `pow` shares with `exp` word for word, the table, the reduction constants `InvLn2N`, `NegLn2hiN`, and `NegLn2loN`, and the polynomial of degree 5, have kernel-checked bounds in `ExpTableBounds`, `ExpReductionBounds`, and `ExpApproximation`.  `powExpCore` adds `elo` to the residual and the sign bias to the key, so `exp`'s path lemmas, stated for `Exp.expCore`, do not apply to it unchanged, while `ArmReconstruction`, stated for any polynomial word, and `ExpOneStep.one_step_of_error` take arbitrary inputs.

| Part | Proved results that apply | Open |
|---|---|---|
| Table of `2^(j/128)` and the exponential's constants | `ExpTableBounds`, `ExpReductionBounds`, `ExpApproximation` | Nothing for the words. |
| Exponential with tail and sign | `ExpOneStep.one_step_of_error`, and `ArmReconstruction`'s lemmas for a polynomial word within `2^-56` of `T_j + ρ - 1`, a bound that `ExpApproximation` and `ExpEvaluationBounds` give only as `2^-55` for the shared polynomial | The residual `x - k ln 2/128 + elo` with `\|elo\|` bounded by `ehi`, the signed scale, and `powSpecialCase`, whose compensation differs from `exp`'s. |
| Logarithm `hi + lo` | None specific to it.  The proofs of Arm's `log` and `log2` check their own tables and polynomials with the kernel-checked bound of `PolyBound` | The table of `invc`, `logc`, and `logctail` against `log c`, the exactness of `r` and of `k ln2hi + logc`, the polynomial against `log (1 + r)`, and the roundings of the tail. |
| Product `ehi + elo` | None | The error of the split product against `y (hi + lo)`. |
| Special cases | None | `checkint`, `zeroinfnan`, `issignaling`, the subnormal normalization, the thresholds `2^-65` and `2^63`, and the exceptional results. |
| WebAssembly | The generated compiler theorems | `Ready` with two borrowed tables, the byte theorem, a file theorem, and its entry in `tests/verified/run.sh`. |

With `exp`'s proved bounds for the shared polynomial, `exp`'s budget leaves about 7 percent of the one-and-a-half-spacing allowance unused, as [the exponential's error budget](Exp.md#error-budget-of-the-main-path) computes.  Arm's estimate puts the logarithm's contribution near 0.0325 units, so a proof that reuses `exp`'s budget must fit the logarithm's error and the tail's effect on the residual into that margin or tighten `exp`'s bounds.  The checkout contains no analysis that decides which.

## Tests

[The accuracy check](PowAccuracy.lean) compares `pow` natively with the double nearest to `x^y` from [the power references](PowerReference.lean), which compute `y log |x|` at 500 bits after the binary point and its exponential with `Reference.exp`, and with the special cases of C99's Annex F implemented separately in the check's `reference`.  It fails at the first result more than one step away.  Its 110,922 pairs are 60,000 hashed pairs with positive `x` from `2^-60` to `2^60` and `|y|` from `2^-10` to `2^10`, 20,000 pairs with `x` within `2^20` units of 1 and `|y|` from `2^20` to `2^51`, 20,000 pairs of negative `x` with magnitudes from `2^-8` to `2^8` and the integers `y` from -100 to 100, the 6,333 pairs `(2, k)`, `(2, k + 1/2)`, and `(-2, k)` for the integers `k` from -1080 to 1030, 4,002 pairs with `x = 10` and `x = 0.1` near overflow and underflow, the 11 pairs of `WorstCases.pow`, and the 576 pairs of 24 special values.

The worst-case pairs are those that give the largest known errors in Tables 5 to 11 of Gladman, Innocente, Mather, Ozaki, and Zimmermann's report [13].  Its Table 3 lists for `pow` 0.523 units for GNU libc 2.44 and the Arm Performance Library, 0.525 for Musl, and 636 for FreeBSD 15.1.  In `opus-verified-tests-13.log` all 110,922 results lie within one step of the reference, 79 of them unequal to it, so the largest measured distance equals the decided bound.  The C library on the build host also lies within one step with 79 results unequal, which fits glibc's use of Arm's routine, as [the ports' journal section](../../devnotes.md#2026-10-10-ports-of-exp2-exp10-log2-log10-and-pow) notes.  The first passing run, before the worst-case pairs were added, is `ports-PowAccuracy-4.log` of the ports track, with 110,911 results and 78 unequal.  The reference's truncation errors are stated in docstrings and are not proved.

## Limits

Every limit of the exponential's theorem would apply to a theorem for `pow`: Talos's semantics, the trusted decoder and `binary_file%`, the deterministic profile's canonical NaN, the canonical NaN argument of a Lean `Float`, and host actions between calls.  The present limit is larger, since no theorem covers the port.  The accuracy check is a sample of 110,922 pairs, and the evidence for one step consists of that check, the Python models of the ports track, which lay within one unit of 150-digit values at about 23,000 pairs, and Arm's stated error of 0.54 units.  The signaling NaN cases depend on NaN words that a Lean `Float` cannot hold, so a theorem stated for `Float` arguments will not cover them.
