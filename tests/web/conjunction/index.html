Conjunction Probability

This page computes the two-dimensional probability of collision of two space objects from the quantities of the encounter plane: the mean miss vector, its covariance, and the combined hard-body radius. It calls the export assess of the module that the verified compiler produced from Demos/Conjunction/Program.lean, which returns an interval that contains the model's probability and a verdict against the threshold, and it draws the plane with the covariance ellipses at one, two, and three standard deviations and the disk.

Theorems and trusted code

compiled.assess.implements of Demos/Conjunction/Verified.lean states that entry 55 of the module, the export assess, called with the words of its arguments from a store with the allocator's invariant that holds the table of exp, returns without a trap the words of Demos.Conjunction.assess: the status, lo, hi, and the verdict. The generated compiled.initial states that a fresh instance's store is such a store, and the page calls assess on a fresh instance. assess_sound of Demos/Conjunction/Soundness.lean states that status 0 gives the result of pc, whose pc_enclosure places the model's probability for the real values of the arguments between lo and hi, that verdict 1 implies a probability below t, and that verdict 2 implies one at or above it. conjunction_file of Demos/Conjunction/File.lean states that build/verified/conjunction.wasm holds the encoding of the module, and the SHA-256 above identifies the bytes that this page loaded. These theorems depend on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound alone.

The page's JavaScript, assess.js and app.js, lies outside the theorems. It converts each decimal entry to the nearest double, refuses an empty or malformed entry and a panel count outside the 64-bit words, passes the doubles and the count to assess, and shows the returned words. The drawing is unverified: it computes the ellipses' axes and the view in JavaScript's binary64 arithmetic. The browser's WebAssembly engine is trusted to agree with the semantics of the theorems. The theorems concern the integral of the model, which assumes rectilinear relative motion, Gaussian position errors constant during the encounter, independent errors of the two objects, and spherical objects. The realism of the covariance, the choice of the radius, and the choice of the threshold are the user's.
