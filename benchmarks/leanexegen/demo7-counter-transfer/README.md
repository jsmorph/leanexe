# Demo 7 counter-transfer benchmark

Demo 7 is a held-out scalar-loop case generated from a prose request on 2026-08-08.  Its public function is the identity on `Array UInt64`, but a singleton passes through a separate scalar helper that transfers its value by decrementing one counter and incrementing another.  This gives the artifact proof a two-accumulator invariant, wrapping addition and subtraction, and a decreasing measure without adding application-specific mathematics.

The reference package binds a 1,750-byte WASM module with SHA-256 digest `f437ebc16e352391ff05ff79d957eb7ef5652424d6d28c3f279e782980eeb7a5`.  Existing scalar post-test annotations exposed the exact loop, compact transitions, scalar entry, and complete singleton wrapper, but the proof agent derived the fixed-width counter arithmetic itself.  Stage 5 took 577.039 seconds, produced a 171-line proof, and required four edited candidates.

The prior retained LTG adds `State.localU64ToNat`, `CounterTransition.decrement_add_increment`, and `CounterTransition.decrement_toNat_lt`.  The recipe reports the counter theorems only when the checked scalar descriptor contains both unit decrement and unit increment, and it reports `U64Op.apply` when a generated transition contains a descriptor binary operation.  These declarations depend on scalar values and operations rather than the generated function, frame size, or public specification.

Three prior retained runs completed in 520.815, 405.284, and 816.771 seconds.  Their 520.815-second median is 56.224 seconds, or 9.7 percent, below the reference, while their 411.486-second range records substantial agent variance.  The proofs contain 125, 135, and 167 lines, giving a 135-line median, and the journals record three, one, and ten edited candidates.  The host UTC clock moved during several runs, so every comparison uses the monotonic durations stored in proof telemetry rather than differences between wall-clock timestamps.

The slow retained journal identified unstable composition between `postTestProgram_spec` and the generated scalar-entry theorem.  A generated `terminates_with_of_post_test` theorem moved that composition into checked annotation support and passed exact-artifact verification.  Three screens then completed in 387.160, 558.581, and 748.263 seconds, giving a 558.581-second median that is 7.3 percent above the retained configuration, so the active recipe omits the theorem and the `composed-*` packages preserve the rejected experiment.

A deterministic cut-point starter next derived a scalar result function from the formal singleton result, applied the complete wrapper theorem, and left the scalar proof followed by two formal-result equations.  Its only screen took 936.788 seconds, 79.9 percent above the retained median, and produced a 155-line proof after four edits.  That starter was removed, while `cutpoint-rejected-1` preserves the accepted proof and journal from the rejected structure.

That journal exposed a coordinate mismatch between combined-local indices in scalar annotations and indices into `State.locals`.  A candidate `combinedLocalU64ToNat` helper accepted the annotation coordinate directly and all three agents used it, reducing edited candidates to one, two, and one.  The runs took 651.892, 577.172, and 521.718 seconds, whose 577.172-second median is 10.8 percent above the retained median, so the helper and automatic recipe selection were removed and the `combined-measure-*` packages preserve the screen.

The next experiment selected detailed catalog entries and strategy sections only for functions left uncovered by a checked whole-function composition.  It reduced the Demo 7 prompt and supplied guidance from about 13,500 words to about 6,800 words, selecting `Array`, `ScalarTransition`, and `FixedArraySingletonWrapper` while retaining the other permitted modules as named fallbacks.  Both agents followed the selected composition and scalar recipe without a fallback, but their runs took 777.102 and 818.470 seconds, giving a two-run median of 797.786 seconds, 53.2 percent above the prior retained median.  A third run could not lower the three-run median below 777.102 seconds, so the generator restored the full context and the `focused-context-*` packages preserve the rejected proofs and journals.

The promoted annotation summary recognizes the counter-transfer semantics before proof generation.  It checks the initial pair `(input, 0)`, the zero and nonzero body transitions, the exit condition, the returned accumulator, and the store-neutral suffix.  Lean then proves the generated store-preserving identity theorem against the compact transition equations, exact loop entry, decoded suffix, and shared `CounterTransition.postTestProgram_spec` theorem.

Three summary runs completed in 386.828, 371.243, and 354.004 seconds.  Their 371.243-second median is 149.572 seconds, or 28.7 percent, below the prior retained median, and their 32.824-second range is smaller than the prior 411.486-second range.  All three agents used the generated theorem directly; their proofs contain 72, 68, and 67 lines, and the journals record one, one, and three edited candidates.

The complete-composition starter uses the checked wrapper callee identity to apply `FixedArraySingletonWrapper.wrapperProgram_spec` with the generated counter-transfer theorem before Codex begins.  It leaves only the two equations between the wrapper result and `FormalSpec.expected`, unlike the rejected cut-point starter, which still left the scalar loop proof open.  The recipe validator confirms that the scalar theorem belongs to the exact callee recorded by the checked wrapper composition.

Three complete-composition runs finished in 232.164, 201.366, and 204.537 seconds.  Their 204.537-second median is 166.706 seconds, or 44.9 percent, below the checked-summary median, and their 30.798-second range remains narrow.  Each first edited candidate passed, the proofs contain 73, 67, and 72 lines, and separate `leanexegen verify -s` runs accepted all three packages.

A residual normalizer then discharged the two formal-result equations by unfolding the generated specification and reducing the singleton array.  Both Codex sessions accepted the untouched 67-line starter in 242.798 and 211.558 seconds.  This isolated screen could not beat the retained median, so both packages remain worked examples of the proof step.

Three fixed runs completed in 112.152, 125.103, and 156.268 seconds, giving a 125.103-second median that is 38.8 percent below the complete-starter median; every proof has 67 lines, every Codex interval is zero, and separate verification accepted every package.
