# Shortest paths by Dijkstra's algorithm

## Request and specification

[The request](request.txt) asks for the least total length of a walk from a source `s` to each of the `n` vertices of a directed graph in forward-star form, or `2^64 - 1` for a vertex that no walk reaches or whose least length is `2^64 - 1` or more, by Dijkstra's algorithm with the tentative lengths in an array and a binary heap of pairs of a tentative length and a vertex, with no allocation but the tentative lengths, the marks of the done vertices, and a heap of `first[n]` pairs, and with a call stack that does not grow.  [The specification](Spec.lean) states an arc `Arc n first heads lens u v w` as a position `k` of the range `first[u] ≤ k < first[u + 1]` of a vertex `u` below `n` that lies below the sizes of `heads` and `lens`, with `heads[k] = v` below `n` and `lens[k] = w`, and a walk `Walk n first heads lens s v L` as a source below `n` followed by arcs of total length `L`, with lengths as natural numbers.  `Distances` states that the result holds `n` words and that word `v` is the least length of a walk from `s` to `v` when that length is below `2^64 - 1`, and `2^64 - 1` when every walk to `v`, if any, has length `2^64 - 1` or more, which determines the result, as `distances_unique` states.  `WellFormed` states the request's form of the graph: `first` holds at least `n + 1` words, the first `n + 1` in nondecreasing order, and `first[n]` is at most the size of `heads` and of `lens`.  The specification names the result and not the method, and the program text shows the algorithm.  The theorems of the module read the allocation requirement as a call that raises the allocator's `top` by at most the four blocks, which is weaker than the request, since allocations from free blocks below `top` do not raise it and no theorem states that the call releases the marks and the heap's arrays.  [The review](review.md) records the specification's two independent reviews and the changes that they led to.

## The program

[The program](Program.lean) keeps the tentative lengths in `dist`, with `2^64 - 1` for a vertex not yet reached, the heap in two arrays of `first[n]` words, the keys `hk` and the vertices `hv`, of which the first `h` entries are in heap order, and the marks of the done vertices in an array of `n` flags.  `addSat` adds two lengths and returns `2^64 - 1` when the sum is `2^64 - 1` or more, so no sum wraps.  `relax` takes each arc out of a vertex `u` in turn, and when its head `v` is below `n` and the length of `u` plus the arc's length is below the tentative length of `v`, it lowers that length and adds the pair to the heap at position `h`, from which `siftUp` moves it up while its key is below its parent's.  `dijkstra` sets the length of the source to 0, relaxes its arcs, and marks it done, and then each of the `first[n]` steps, when the heap is not empty, removes the root, moves the last entry to the root, and lets `siftDown` move it down, in the branch form of the library's heapsort, and when the removed vertex is not done, the step relaxes its arcs and marks it done.  The program adds a pair for a vertex once for each arc that lowers its length, and a step skips a removed pair whose vertex is done.

Each vertex is done at most once, and each of its arcs adds at most one pair, so the heap never holds more than `first[n]` pairs, and since each step removes one pair when the heap is not empty, the `first[n]` steps of the loop leave the heap empty.  The program takes time proportional to `n + m log m` for `m = first[n]`, and the proofs do not state this bound.  For a source of `n` or more, the program returns `n` words of `2^64 - 1`.  `relax` takes the arrays as separate parameters, and the main loop keeps them in a flat tuple, since the compiler lends a tuple parameter and an update of a lent array copies it.

## The module

`verified_compile compiled := [swap, addSat, siftUp, siftDown, relax, dijkstra]` in [the verified compilation](Compiled.lean) compiles the program into a module whose export `dijkstra` is function 7.  The compiler translates the tail recursions of `siftUp` and `siftDown` and the loops of `relax` and `dijkstra` into loops, so no theorem of the module has a call-depth premise, and the generated `compiled.relax.bound_eq` states a bound of 0, since `relax` updates its arrays in place.  The caller lends `first`, `heads`, and `lens`.

| Theorem | File | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| `addSat_toNat` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | `addSat a b` is `sat (a + b)`: the sum, or `2^64 - 1` when the sum is `2^64 - 1` or more. |
| `push_spec`, `pop_spec` | [The heap](Heap.lean) | An entry added at position `h` and moved up by `siftUp` keeps the heap order of `h + 1` entries and adds the pair to the entries as a multiset, and the removal of the root with `siftDown` keeps the heap order of `h - 1` entries and removes the root's pair. |
| `walk_exit` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | A walk from a done vertex to a vertex that is not done leaves the done vertices by an arc from a done vertex `y` to a vertex `x` that is not done, at a length at most the walk's. |
| `RelaxInv`, `relax_spec`, `relax_arcs` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | `relax` for `u` lowers lengths only to the length of `u` plus an arc, adds to the heap a pair with each lowered length, and leaves every arc out of `u` relaxed. |
| `MainInv`, `exit_entry`, `settle_inv` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | A step of the main loop keeps the invariant: lengths below `2^64 - 1` are walk lengths, done vertices have their least lengths, their arcs are relaxed, every vertex that is not done and has a length below `2^64 - 1` has a pair with that length in the heap, every pair in the heap has a vertex below `n` and a key that is below `2^64 - 1` and at least the length of its vertex, and the heap is empty or its pairs and the steps taken number at most the positions in the ranges of the done vertices.  By `exit_entry`, a walk shorter than `2^64 - 1` to a vertex that is not done gives a pair in the heap whose key is at most the walk's length. |
| `init_inv`, `main_spec` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | The state after the source holds the invariant, and the invariant after `first[n]` steps gives `Distances`. |
| `dijkstra_spec` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | `Distances n s first heads lens (dijkstra n s first heads lens)` for every graph that is `WellFormed`. |
| `distances_unique` | [The proof](Proof.lean) | Two arrays that satisfy `Distances` for the same graph and source are equal. |
| `dijkstra_export` | [The compilation](Compiled.lean) | Export `dijkstra` of `compiled.module` is function 7. |
| `dijkstra_call` | [The compilation](Compiled.lean) | Function 7, called with a count, a source, and three lent arrays from a ready store with room for `compiled.dijkstra.bound`, returns without a trap the owned array `dijkstra n s first heads lens`. |
| `dijkstra_bound` | [The compilation](Compiled.lean) | The bound is two blocks of `n` words and two blocks of `first[n]` words for a source below `n`, and otherwise one block of `n` words. |
| `dijkstra_file`, `dijkstra_file_decode` | [The file theorems](File.lean) | `build/verified/dijkstra.wasm` holds the encoding of `compiled.module` and decodes to the module of `compiled.bytes`. |

The proof treats the heap as the multiset of its pairs, `pairs hk hv h`, which `push_spec` and `pop_spec` change by `List.Perm`, and the root's key is at most every key in the heap.  When a step removes the root and its vertex `u` is not done, the root's key is at least the length of `u`, and every walk to `u` shorter than `2^64 - 1` leaves the done vertices by a relaxed arc to a vertex with a pair in the heap whose key is at most the walk's length, by `walk_exit` and `exit_entry`, so the length of `u` is at most the walk's and is the least.  A relaxation from `u` lowers no length of a done vertex, since each lowered length is a walk length and a done vertex has the least.  The count `degSum` of the positions in the ranges of the done vertices bounds the pairs in the heap plus the steps taken while the heap is not empty, which gives the capacity of the heap for each relaxation and, after `first[n]` steps, an empty heap, so a vertex that is not done has no walk shorter than `2^64 - 1`.  The axiom reports of `dijkstra_call`, `dijkstra_spec`, `dijkstra_bound`, and `distances_unique` name only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## Building, running, and testing

```sh
tools/leanrun --timeout 60m lake build Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.Compiled
tests/verified/run.sh --cases dijkstra
tests/native/verified.sh --module dijkstra
```

The commands build the verified compilation and its theorems and run the module's cases, with the setup of [the repository README](../../../README.md#commands).  The cases come from `dijkstraInputs` in [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Dijkstra.lean): small graphs with sources outside the graph, some with words of `first` that give a range of arcs, paths, unreachable vertices, parallel arcs, loops, arcs of length 0, heads outside the graph, lengths whose sums reach `2^64 - 2`, `2^64 - 1`, and `2^64`, forward stars with words before `first[0]` and after `first[n]`, two graphs that detect wrong parents and children in the heap, random graphs drawn with SplitMix64 of up to 1,000 vertices and 5,000 arcs, two with 64-bit lengths from the source to every vertex, a grid of 1,600 vertices, a complete graph of 40 vertices, and 100 arcs to one vertex that fill the heap.  Each case expects `shortest`, which computes the least lengths by rounds of Bellman and Ford's relaxation of every arc, as natural numbers, apart from the program.  [The verified driver](../../../tests/verified/run.sh) runs the cases in Wasmtime and checks that a call leaves four blocks, the host's three arrays and the result, after at most seven allocations, and [the native driver](../../../tests/native/verified.sh) runs them on the module's translation to C, compiled with CompCert.
