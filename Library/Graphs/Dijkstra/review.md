# Review of the specification

Two fresh agents reviewed [the specification](Spec.lean), the statement of `dijkstra_spec`, and [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Dijkstra.lean) against [the request](request.txt), from the files alone, each with an independent Python model of the program at 64-bit words and an oracle of the request: the first an implementation of Dijkstra's algorithm on exact integers, checked against Bellman and Ford's, and the second a search of every walk of at most `n` arcs.  Both found that `Distances` with `WellFormed` states the request's result: `n` words, the least walk length when it is below `2^64 - 1`, and `2^64 - 1` otherwise, with an arc whose head is `n` or more as no arc and a source of `n` or more reaching no vertex, and both showed that `Distances` determines the result.  Their models agreed with their oracles on 503,042 and 222,300 inputs, and the second checked `RelaxInv` and `MainInv` on 3,839,201 states of its model and found that the heap never needed more than `first[n]` pairs and that the `first[n]` steps always emptied it.  Both noted that `WellFormed` orders only the first `n + 1` words of `first`, a weaker premise than the request's other reading, which orders every word, so the theorem covers both readings.

The reviews found sentences that said more or less than the code.  The docstring of `INF` called `2^64 - 1` the length of no walk, though one arc can have that length, the comment of `dijkstra` left out the source of `n` or more and the steps on an empty heap, `deg` and `degSum` count positions where their docstrings said arcs, the docstrings of `RelaxInv` and `MainInv` left out clauses, the docstring of `main_spec` stated an empty heap that its statement does not, the specification's comment ordered all of `first`, and the docstring of `Distances` left out the size.  In the case file, `shortest` claimed that a least walk visits no vertex twice, which fails with a loop of length 0, and the docstring of the inputs claimed that a 32-bit reading places the source `2^64 - 1` at 0, that parallel arcs in increasing length add a vertex to the heap several times, and lengths below `2^62` that the generator did not reach.  The comments now state these exactly.

Both ran wrong programs on the case inputs.  In the first review, a program without the test `s < n` passed every case, since each source of `n` or more read empty ranges of `first`, and the cases now include a source of `n` with a range of one arc and a source of `2^64 - 1` whose `s + 1` wraps to 0.  In the second, a `siftUp` with the parent `i / 2` and a `siftDown` whose guard is `i < (h - 1) / 2` passed every case, and the cases now include the second review's two graphs that detect them.  The random graphs, drawn at a fixed step of the sequence `rw`, used few tails, two gave the source no arcs, and the graph of 1,000 vertices from source 999 reached 4 vertices, so the random graphs now draw from SplitMix64.  Wrong programs that only one case detected now fail on a second input as well: a 32-bit source of `2^32 + 1`, 32-bit heads in a graph of 4 vertices, a second forward star with `first[0]` above 0, and two graphs whose source has an arc with a 64-bit length to every other vertex, which put keys on both sides of `2^63` in the heap.

The reviews found variants that no input detects, since they compute the same result: no skip of done vertices, relaxation with `≤`, a head test of `v ≤ n` or none, since `dist[v]!` reads 0 for `v ≥ n`, a heap or a loop one smaller than `first[n]`, and a source that is not marked done.  The request asks for the skip of done vertices, which only the program text shows.  Both checked that the program allocates only the tentative lengths, the done marks, and the heap's two arrays of `first[n]` words, as `dijkstra_bound` states, and that the deepest chain of calls is `dijkstra`, `relax`, `siftUp`, and `swap`.
