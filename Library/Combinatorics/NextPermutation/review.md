# Review of the specification

Two fresh agents reviewed [the specification](Spec.lean) and [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/NextPermutation.lean) against [the request](request.txt), from the files alone, each with an independent Python model of the program at 64-bit words and oracles of the request that enumerate the distinct arrangements of a multiset.  Both found that `IsNext` states the request on every input they checked, with repeated words, words at and above `2^63`, arrays of 0 and 1 words, and the greatest arrangement, and that `<` on `List UInt64` is Lean's `List.Lex` over the unsigned `<` of words.  The first compared the model and `expectedOf` with its oracles on 351,811 arrays, among them every array of up to 7 words over six values near 0, `2^63`, and `2^64`, and the second on every array of up to 5 words over eight such values and on 40,000 random arrays.  The second also checked that repeated calls from the least arrangement visit every distinct arrangement once, for 352 multisets and for 8 and 9 distinct words, and neither found a difference from the request.

Both found that the comment's claim that `IsNext xs r` determines `r` had no theorem, and `isNext_unique` now states it, with the totality of the order, `list_lt_trichotomy`.  Both found that the docstrings of `least_above` and `nextPerm_isNext` left out a premise, the first with a counterexample to `least_above` without its premise that the result is an arrangement of the input, and both docstrings now state their premises.  Both found that the specification's comment ascribed the readings of "in place" and of the call stack to `IsNext`, which states neither, and that a call that does not raise `top` is weaker than a call in place.  The comment now names the theorems of the module that state the readings and says that the first is weaker than the request.

The second found that the docstring of `lastAbove` described a test of position `lo` that the code never makes and that the docstring of `ascent` used one name for two values, and the first found that the docstring of `reverseRange` called every reversal step L4 and left out its premise on `hi`.  These docstrings now state what the code does.  Both found that the comment of the compilation could be read as a theorem about the module's result, while `nextPerm_isNext` concerns the Lean function, and the comment now states that the representation of every call meets the size premise and that no theorem joins the two theorems.

Both ran wrong programs on the case inputs.  The cases detected every wrong result except that of a step L3 that scans at most 64 or 1,000 positions, since no case made step L3 scan more than 16 positions.  Both found no case of 6 to 999 words, no word at or above `2^63` in an array of 4 to 999 words, no long greatest arrangement with repeated words, and no array of more than 10,000 words, and the first found no last ascent inside a long array.  The cases now hold arrays of 6 to 999 words drawn from four words near 0, `2^63`, and `2^64`, each with a variant whose second half is nonincreasing, an array of 20,000 words on which step L3 passes over 19,997 words, an array of 20,000 words whose last ascent is at position 0, 1,000 equal words, 2,000 nonincreasing words with repetitions, and 1,000 words on which step L3 passes over 997 words below `2^63` to reach one above it.  No case passes a module's result back to the module, and the cycle of repeated calls follows from `IsNext`.
