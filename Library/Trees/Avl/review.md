# Review of the specification

Two fresh agents reviewed [the specification](Spec.lean) and [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Avl.lean) against [the request](request.txt), from the files alone, each with a Python model that follows Knuth's steps A1 to A10 with a head node, mutable links, and 64-bit factor words.  The first compared the model with `algorithmA` on 4,782,183 insertions into every balanced search tree of heights 1 to 5, absent and present keys alike, and on 74,917 insertions into random trees of up to 400 keys.  The second compared Knuth's steps, `algorithmA`, and the program on every balanced tree of at most 12 nodes with every key and gap, and on about 536,000 insertions into trees that are not balanced, wherever Knuth's steps define a result.  Neither found an input on which `algorithmA`, its growth flag, or `searchA` differs from Knuth's steps, and both confirmed the factors that `growLeft` and `growRight` assign in step A9.

Both found that the comments stated the meaning of the growth flag without the condition that the tree is balanced, while `algorithmA_props` proves it only for balanced trees, and the comments of the specification and the program now state that condition.  Both found two choices that the comment did not record, for trees where Knuth's steps define no result: a factor word other than 0, 1, and `2^64 - 1` counts as a lean toward the side that grew, and a node that leans toward the side that grew keeps its factor and stops the growth when its grown subtree is a single new node or has no inner grandchild.  The comment now records both, together with the absent head node that holds Knuth's count of the height, and the fact that `insert` does not report whether it found the key.  The comment of the program now names steps A6 and A7, the case of a found key, and the rotation of a node with any factor other than 0 and the opposite one.  The comment of the proof now limits the application of `growLeft` and `growRight` to the steps of `ascend` with the flag set, and the docstring of `descend_bound` speaks of the generated bound.

The second review found that the properties of `SearchTree` do not determine the tree, since another balanced insertion with the same keys and height meets them, while `algorithmA` fixes the result and `insert_implements` states it.  It also found that `ordered` follows from `keyList`, and that `grew` is the only property that rules out a taller tree when the key is present.  Both found that no theorem states that the module reuses the tree's nodes, allocates only the new node, or keeps a call stack that does not grow, and that the comments attribute these to the compiler's rules, which the manual states.

Both confirmed the claims of the inputs' docstrings, including the coverage of steps A7 to A9.  They found that the cases detect every wrong program that changes a result on a balanced tree: wrong factors after either rotation, a single rotation in place of a double one and the reverse, growth that continues after a rotation or a balancing step, exchanged children, an equal key inserted as a duplicate, a new node with factor 1, signed or 32-bit comparisons, and a fresh node for each node on the path, which the allocation counts detect.  They named inputs that no case covered: trees of one to four nodes, double rotations around a leaning grandchild below the root, factor words other than 0, 1, and `2^64 - 1`, factors that do not match the heights and reach a leaning node, and trees whose keys do not increase.  The cases now include the trees that Algorithm A builds from every order of one, two, and four keys, four sequences of 10 to 16 keys that give double rotations one and two levels below the root around a grandchild that leans either way, five small trees whose factors do not match their heights, a chain of 3,000 nodes with factor 1, and two trees that are not search trees.  A Python model confirms that the cases now hold every case of steps A7 to A9 at the root and one and two levels below it.
