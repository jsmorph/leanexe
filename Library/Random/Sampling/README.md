# Random sampling and shuffling: TAOCP §3.4.2

The module holds Algorithm P of TAOCP §3.4.2, which shuffles an array, Algorithm S, which chooses `n` of `N` records in their order, and Algorithm R, which keeps a reservoir of `n` candidates.  Each takes its random numbers as an array of words that the caller supplies, each word `u` serving as the fraction `u / 2^64`, and each specification states four properties: the equation with the algorithm written with natural-number arithmetic, the shape of the result, the independence of the positions chosen from the words, and the number of word arrays that give each outcome.  The theorems concern the map from word arrays to results, and whether a generator's words behave as independent uniform words lies outside them: a word array filled from one 64-bit seed reaches at most `2^64` outcomes, fewer than `n!` arrangements once `n ≥ 21`.  [The review](review.md) records the reviews of the designs and of the specifications.

## Shuffling: Algorithm P

The first part of [the request](request.txt) asks for Algorithm P on an array of words, in place, with the random numbers supplied as an array of words, each word `u` serving as the fraction `u / 2^64` of step P3.  The result must be a permutation for every word array, and for independent uniform words every arrangement of distinct words must be equally likely up to the rounding of 64-bit fractions.  [The specification](Spec.lean) states four properties of a function `f`, which the structure `Shuffles` collects.  The result `f us xs` is Knuth's Algorithm P on the words of `us`, `algorithmP`, which exchanges the words at `j` and at `⌊(j + 1)·us[n - 1 - j] / 2^64⌋` for `j` from `n - 1` down to 1, so that the steps take the words in order.  The second and fourth properties state the guarantees that the request asks for, and the third extends the distribution to inputs with repeated words.  For arrays of fewer than `2^32` words the other three follow from the first, and their proofs check the index arithmetic of `algorithmP`.  The result is a permutation of `xs` for every word array.  The positions that `f` moves do not depend on the words, so `f us xs` is the shuffle of the positions `iota n` with each position replaced by the word of `xs` there.  For `n` distinct words and each permutation `ys` of them, the number of word arrays of length `n - 1` with `f us xs = ys` lies between `fewest n = ∏ ⌊2^64 / (j + 1)⌋` and `most n = ∏ ⌈2^64 / (j + 1)⌉`, the products over `j` from 1 to `n - 1`.

For uniform words each permutation therefore has a probability within a relative error of about `n(n - 1) / 2^65` of `1 / n!`, and the third property carries the distribution to inputs with repeated words.  Algorithm P reads only the first `n - 1` words, so further words do not change the distribution.  The method and the count concern arrays of fewer than `2^32` words, which include every array in a 32-bit WebAssembly memory.

[The program](Program.lean) is Knuth's Algorithm P with indices from 0.  For `j` from `n - 1` down to 1, step P2 takes the fraction `U = us[n - 1 - j] / 2^64`, so that the steps read the words in the order in which Knuth's steps generate their numbers, and step P3 exchanges the word at `j` with the word at `k = ⌊(j + 1)U⌋`, which lies from 0 to `j` because `U < 1`.  `scale u m` computes `⌊u·m / 2^64⌋` from the halves of `u = u₁·2^32 + u₀` as `⌊(u₁·m + ⌊u₀·m / 2^32⌋) / 2^32⌋`, whose products and sum stay below `2^64` when `m < 2^32`.  The exchange is heapsort's `swap`, and the loop makes `n - 1` exchanges and reads the `n - 1` words `us[0, n - 1)`.

Knuth's indices are 1-based and his `U` is a real number, while here `U` is a 64-bit fraction, so an index `i < m` comes from `⌊2^64 / m⌋` or `⌈2^64 / m⌉` words where a real `U` would give each index the probability `1 / m` exactly.  That rounding is the only departure in the distribution.  A missing word of `us` reads as 0, which selects position 0, and arrays of 0 and 1 elements return unchanged.

The proof states Algorithm P with given indices as `byIndices k m a`, which exchanges the words at `k j` and `j` for `j` from `m` down to 1.  With indices `k j ≤ j` it permutes `a` and leaves the words after `m` in place, and the word that ends at position `m` is the word that was at `k m`.  For distinct words the last word of a result therefore fixes the last index, and by induction the whole sequence of indices, while every arrangement has a sequence of indices, obtained by moving the word that belongs at `m` there first.  The module computes `byIndices` with the indices `scale us[n - 1 - j] (j + 1)`, so the word arrays that give one arrangement are those whose word `n - 1 - j` lies, for each `j`, in the set of words that `scale` maps to the arrangement's index `j`.  Their number is the product of the sizes of those sets, and each size is the length of the interval of words `u` with `i·2^64 ≤ u·(j + 1) < (i + 1)·2^64`.

## Selection sampling: Algorithm S

The second part of [the request](request.txt) asks for Algorithm S, which chooses `n` of the `N` words of an array and keeps their order, with the word `u` serving as the fraction of step S3.  `Samples` in [the specification](Spec.lean) states that `f us xs n` is `algorithmS`, which, with `m` of `k = min n N` records chosen, chooses record `t` when `(N - t)·us[t] < (k - m)·2^64`, that is, exactly when Knuth's test `(N - t)U ≥ n - m` of step S3 fails.  Knuth assumes `0 < n ≤ N`, and `algorithmS` extends his algorithm to `n = 0`, which chooses nothing, and to `n > N`, which chooses every word, and a missing word is 0.  The result is a sublist of `xs` of `k` words, the positions chosen do not depend on the words of `xs`, and for `n ≤ N` every subset of `n` positions comes from between `n!(N - n)! ∏ ⌊2^64 / M⌋` and `n!(N - n)! ∏ ⌈2^64 / M⌉` word arrays of `N` words, the products over `M` from 1 to `N`.  For uniform words each subset therefore has a probability within a relative error of about `N(N - 1) / 2^65` of `1 / C(N, n)`.

[The program](Program.lean) `sample` keeps `m` and an array of `k` words.  For record `t` it writes the record at position `m` and advances `m` when `scale us[t] (N - t) < k - m`, so a record that is not chosen is overwritten by the next one, and a write at `m = k` is out of range and changes nothing.  Knuth stops at the `n`-th choice, and the program reads all `N` words: after that choice `k - m = 0` and no word chooses, so the extra words change no result.

The proof in [the selection sampling proofs](Select.lean) keeps, at record `t`, the first `m` words of the array equal to the records chosen so far and `k - m ≤ N - t`, which forces `m = k` at the end, since a word `u` gives `⌊(N - t)·u / 2^64⌋ < N - t`, so every remaining record is chosen once as many remain as are needed.  For the count, the word arrays that choose a given list split at the first record: it is chosen by the words `u` with `⌊M·u / 2^64⌋ < c`, where `c` records of the `M` left are still to choose, which number between `c⌊2^64 / M⌋` and `c⌈2^64 / M⌉`, and passed over by the others, which number between `(M - c)⌊2^64 / M⌋` and `(M - c)⌈2^64 / M⌉`.  By induction on the records the counts multiply to `c!(M - c)!` times the products of the floors and ceilings.

## Reservoir sampling: Algorithm R

The third part of [the request](request.txt) asks for Algorithm R with the candidates in an array, as Vitter states it: the first `k = min n N` records are the candidates, and record `t ≥ k` takes the index `M = ⌊(t + 1)U⌋` and replaces candidate `M` when `M < k`.  `Reservoirs` in [the specification](Spec.lean) states that `f us xs n` is `algorithmR`, which reads the word `us[t - k]` for record `t`, that the result holds `k` words of `xs` as a sub-multiset, that the positions kept do not depend on the words of `xs`, and that for `n ≤ N` every set of `n` positions comes from between `(N - n)! ∏ ⌊2^64 / (t + 1)⌋` and `(N - n)! ∏ ⌈2^64 / (t + 1)⌉` word arrays of `N - n` words, the products over `t` from `n` to `N - 1`.  The order of the candidates depends on the words, so the property counts sets.  Vitter's paper gives the version with the candidates on secondary storage, which keeps a table of their indices and sorts it before it reads the sample, and the program, which keeps the candidates in memory, returns them in reservoir order.

[The program](Program.lean) `reservoir` builds the array of the first `k` records and, for record `t`, writes it at position `scale us[t - k] (t + 1)`.  A position of `k` or more is out of range, so the write changes nothing, which is the test of Algorithm R.  The proof in [the reservoir proofs](Reservoir.lean) shows that a replacement removes one candidate and adds the record, so the candidates are a sub-multiset of the records.  The count goes forward in time from the first `k` records: after the records up to `t`, every set of `k` positions below `t + 1` comes from the same number of index sequences.  A set without record `t` comes from the earlier word arrays that gave it and the `t + 1 - k` indices of `k` or more, and a set with record `t` from those that gave one of the `t + 1 - k` sets in which a position outside it takes the record's place, each with the one index at which that position sits.

## The module

`verified_compile compiled := [scale, swap, shuffle, sample, reservoir]` in [the verified compilation](Compiled.lean) compiles the programs into a module whose exports `shuffle`, `sample`, and `reservoir` are functions 4, 5, and 6.  `shuffle` borrows the word array and consumes the array it shuffles, which the caller hands over as `Moved (Array UInt64)`, and by the compiler's [ownership rules](../../../docs/verified-manual.md#ownership) every exchange changes the array in its own block.  `sample` and `reservoir` borrow both arrays and build one array of `min n N` words.  The generated `trapFree` theorems state that the functions return their Lean results without a trap, and the three specification theorems state the properties of those results.  The axiom reports of the generated `trapFree` theorems and of `shuffle_spec`, `sample_spec`, and `reservoir_spec` name only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## Building, running, and testing

```sh
tools/leanrun --timeout 60m lake build Library.Random.Sampling.Compiled
tests/verified/run.sh --cases sampling
tests/native/verified.sh --module sampling
```

The commands build the verified compilation and its theorems and run the module's cases, with the setup of [the repository README](../../../README.md#commands).  The cases come from [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Sampling.lean), whose docstrings list them.  `shuffleInputs` covers arrays of 0, 1, and 2 words, the words 0, `2^64 - 1`, and `2^63`, a missing word, repeated words, and the least word that selects an index and the word below it for 3, 5, 7, 1,000, and 1,024 positions, where at an inexact threshold the carry from the low half in `scale` decides the least word's index, with SplitMix64 words for up to 4,500 positions.  `sampleInputs` and `reservoirInputs` cover sizes of 0, of `N`, and above `N`, including `2^32 + 1` and `2^64 - 1`, records of 0 and `2^64 - 1`, word arrays that are too short and too long, the least word that passes over a record and the word below it, and SplitMix64 words for up to 5,000 records.  [The verified library cases](../../../tests/TestSupport/LibraryCases.lean) print each case with the Lean function.  [The verified driver](../../../tests/verified/run.sh) runs the cases in Wasmtime and checks the blocks that each call leaves, and [the native driver](../../../tests/native/verified.sh) runs them on the module's translation to C, compiled with CompCert.
