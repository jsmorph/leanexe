# Review of the specification

A fresh agent reviewed the design before the proof, and two fresh agents reviewed [the specification](Spec.lean) and [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Sampling.lean) against [the request](request.txt), from the files alone, with Python models at small word sizes.  The design review found the program and the counting argument correct and led to the word order, the size premises, the property of the positions moved, and the error figure that the specification states.

The first specification review found that the bounds `fewest` and `most` hold and are reached in exhaustive models, and that the property of the positions moved is needed: a shuffle whose indices depend on the words to shuffle meets the other properties and fails only that one.  It found that the three properties of the first draft accepted methods that the request excludes, such as an index `u mod (j + 1)` or a loop with rising `j`, so the specification now has a first property, the equation with Knuth's Algorithm P, `algorithmP`, written with natural-number arithmetic.  It found that no case depended on the carry from the low half in `scale`, which the boundary words now test, and it corrected the docstring's count of words and the comment's description of memory.

The second specification review checked `algorithmP` against a model of Knuth's 1-based steps with exact rational `U` on 3,000 cases and the bounds exhaustively, and found no defect.  It noted that the draft gave the first step the last word, where Knuth's steps take the numbers in the order they generate them, and the steps now read the words in order.  It found that the comment overstated which properties the request asks for, that a long case shifted its words so far that every step selected position 0, and that the cases left out a nonempty word array for one word, a missing word with large words to shuffle, and boundary words at large multipliers.  The comment, the long case, and the inputs are corrected.

Both reviews recorded classes that no case can reach: the distribution, which rests on the proof alone, since each case compares the module with `shuffle` at one word array, and arrays of `2^29` words or more, which a 32-bit memory cannot hold, so multipliers near `2^32` and the size premises of the theorems cannot be tested.
