# Review of the specification

A fresh agent reviewed the design before the proof, and two fresh agents reviewed [the specification](Spec.lean) and [the case inputs](../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Sampling.lean) against [the request](request.txt), from the files alone, with Python models at small word sizes.  The design review found the program and the counting argument correct and led to the use of the first `n - 1` words, the size premises, the property of the positions moved, and the error figure that the specification states.  The first specification review found that the bounds `fewest` and `most` hold and are reached in exhaustive models, and that the property of the positions moved is needed: a shuffle whose indices depend on the words to shuffle meets the other properties and fails only that one.  It found that the three properties of the first draft accepted methods that the request excludes, such as an index `u mod (j + 1)` or a loop with increasing `j`, so the specification now has a first property, the equation with Knuth's Algorithm P, `algorithmP`, written with natural-number arithmetic.  It found that no case depended on the carry from the low half in `scale`, which the boundary words now test, and it corrected the docstring's count of words and the comment's description of memory.

The second specification review checked `algorithmP` against a model of Knuth's 1-based steps with exact rational `U` on 3,000 cases and the bounds exhaustively, and found no defect.  It noted that the draft gave the first step the last word, where Knuth's steps take the numbers in the order they generate them, and the steps now read the words in order.  It found that the comment overstated which properties the request asks for, that a long case shifted its words so far that every step selected position 0, and that the cases left out a nonempty word array for one word, a missing word with large words to shuffle, and boundary words at large multipliers.  The comment, the long case, and the inputs are corrected.  Both reviews recorded classes that no case can test: the distribution, which only the proof covers, since each case compares the module with `shuffle` at one word array, and arrays of `2^29` words or more, which a 32-bit memory cannot hold, so multipliers near `2^32` and the size premises of the theorems cannot be tested.

## Selection sampling and reservoir sampling

A fresh agent reviewed the design of Algorithms S and R with models, and two fresh agents reviewed each specification and its case inputs against [the request](request.txt).  The design review found both programs correct on 40,000 cases against Knuth's steps and Vitter's array form in exact rationals, and the counts exhaustively correct at small word sizes.  It recalled that Knuth's Algorithm R keeps a table of indices and sorts it at the end, which Vitter's paper gives as the version for a reservoir on secondary storage, so the request and the README name Vitter's array form, and it led to the test of Algorithm S without a floor and to the reservoir's count over sets of positions.

The first review of Algorithm S checked `algorithmS` against Knuth's steps on 2,931 cases and the bounds exhaustively, and showed that the method property is needed: three wrong methods, a floor in the threshold, the reverse word order, and a missing word read as `2^64 - 1`, meet the other three properties.  It corrected the error figure to `N(N - 1) / 2^65` and the docstrings, and named the classes without cases: short and long word arrays, large records, sizes of `2^32` or more, boundary words at a later record or at an inexact threshold for a large multiplier, and arrays beyond one page, which the inputs now cover.  The second review checked about 1.5 million inputs at small word sizes and 200,000 at 64 bits, found that the comment called the negation of step S3's test the test itself, and named `n = 0` with several records, an empty word array, a boundary word at a later record with another multiplier, and forced choices for more records, which the inputs now cover.

The first review of Algorithm R checked `algorithmR` against Vitter's array form on 20,000 cases and the bounds exhaustively, and showed that the method property is needed: an index `u mod (t + 1)`, the reverse word order, the reversed slot `k - 1 - M`, and the complement of the word meet the other three properties.  It corrected the error figure to `(N(N - 1) - n(n - 1)) / 2^65`, added the missing word and the words read to the comment, and named `n = 0` with several records, an empty word array, thresholds between two candidates, and the carry from the low half in `scale` at a large multiplier, which the inputs now cover.  The second review of Algorithm R checked `algorithmR` against Vitter's statement on every case input and 20,000 random inputs, the count of `(N - n)!` index sequences for each set exhaustively for up to 8 records, and the bounds exhaustively at small word sizes, where both are reached, and found no defect.  It found that a missing word puts its record into candidate 0 only when there is a candidate, that the docstrings misdescribed one array of records and the use of `least`, and that the cases left out exact thresholds at another multiplier, `n = 0` with no words, and no records with a nonempty word array, which the comment, the docstrings, and the inputs now cover.  All four reviews of Algorithms S and R recorded the classes that no case can test: the distribution, which only the proofs cover, and arrays of `2^29` words or more.
