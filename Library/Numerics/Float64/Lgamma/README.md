# The logarithm of the gamma function

[The implementation](Program.lean) ports CORE-MATH's `cr_lgamma` [1, 2], using [the software fused multiply-add](../../Arithmetic/Fma/README.md).  `lgammaR` returns `ln |Γ x|` and a sign as `±1.0`, and `lgamma` returns its first component.  `LgammaWithin.lgamma_within` proves one representable step from nearest-even rounding at every finite input except zero and the negative integers.  `LgammaSign.lgammaR_sign` proves the sign on that domain.  [The byte theorem](LgammaBytes.lean) carries the accuracy bound to the module, and [the file theorems](LgammaFile.lean) identify `build/verified/lgamma.wasm`.

## Specification

`lgamma` computes `ln |Γ a|` for the value `a` of its argument, and `lgammaR` adds the sign of `Γ a`.  In Mathlib's terms the real function is `Real.log |Real.Gamma a|` [5].  `Real.Gamma` is 0 at zero and the negative integers by convention, so the poles are outside the domain of the accuracy statement, and the domain is every finite double other than zero and the negative integers.  The decided statement for such an `x` is `F64SignedRounding.WithinSteps 1 (Real.log |Real.Gamma (value x.toBits)|) (Lgamma.lgamma x).toBits`, with `WithinSteps` from [the signed rounding reference](../../../../LeanExe/ProofKit/F64SignedRounding.lean) as for the other functions, and the second result of `lgammaR x` equal to the sign of `Real.Gamma (value x.toBits)`.

The bound, the API, and CORE-MATH as the source are the decisions of 2026-10-10 recorded in [the journal](../../../../devnotes.md#2026-10-10-decisions-after-the-review-of-gamma-bessel-and-worst-inputs) and in [the plan](../../../../transcendentals.md#decisions).  The decision of 2026-10-11 keeps CORE-MATH's sign of -1 at the negative integers, where the review had proposed +1 and fdlibm gives +1 ([the journal](../../../../devnotes.md#2026-10-11-a-software-fused-multiply-add-core-maths-gamma-functions-and-fdlibms-bessel-functions)).  `lgamma` overflows from `0x1.754d9278b51a8p+1014`, about `2.56 · 10^305`, where `ln Γ` reaches the threshold `2^1024 - 2^970` of IEEE 754-2019 clause 4.3.1 [6].

| Input | `lgamma` | Sign | Source of the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1`, `2` | `+0` | `+1` | C99 F.9.5.3 [7]; POSIX [8] |
| `+0` | `+∞` | `+1` | C99 F.9.5.3; POSIX (a pole error) |
| `-0` | `+∞` | `-1` | C99 F.9.5.3; POSIX |
| a negative integer below `0x1.754d9278b51a6p+1014` in magnitude | `+∞` | `-1` | C99 F.9.5.3; POSIX leaves `signgam` unspecified |
| a negative integer from `0x1.754d9278b51a6p+1014` in magnitude | `+∞` | `+1` | the same |
| `+∞` | `+∞` | `+1` | C99 F.9.5.3; POSIX |
| `-∞` | `+∞` | `+1` | C99 F.9.5.3; POSIX leaves `signgam` unspecified |
| NaN | the canonical NaN | `+1` | C99 F.9 paragraph 11; the deterministic profile of WebAssembly [9]; POSIX leaves `signgam` unspecified |
| `0x1.754d9278b51a6p+1014`, `0x1.754d9278b51a7p+1014` | `0x1.ffffffffffffep+1023`, `0x1.fffffffffffffp+1023` | `+1` | the two largest finite results, special cases of the code |
| positive `x` from `0x1.754d9278b51a8p+1014` | `+∞`, as `0x1.fp1023 · 0x1.fp1023` | `+1` | overflow |

The signs at `±0` follow the sign of `Γ` near zero.  POSIX leaves `signgam` unspecified at NaN, `-∞`, and the negative integers, so the table records CORE-MATH's choices there, which the port keeps.  CORE-MATH sets `errno` when built with `CORE_MATH_SUPPORT_ERRNO` and raises the divide-by-zero, overflow, and invalid exceptions on these paths, and the port does neither.

## Algorithm

CORE-MATH's method evaluates `ln |Γ x|` in double-double arithmetic, a pair `h + l` of doubles, with an explicit error bound `eps`.  The project's paper describes the method: a fast path with an error bound, a rounding test, and an accurate path when the test fails [3].  When `h + (l + eps)` and `h + (l - eps)` round to the same double, that double is the correctly rounded result, and otherwise `as_lgamma_accurate` evaluates the function to about 100 bits, with a table of 19 hard cases for the inputs whose rounding test that path fails.  The structure below comes from the source [1].

| Range of `\|x\|` | Fast path |
|---|---|
| below `2^-75` | `-ln \|x\|`, from `as_logd` in double-double |
| `2^-75` to `2^-5` | `-ln \|x\|` plus `x` times a polynomial in `x` whose constant term is `-γ` |
| `2^-5` to 0.5 | `-ln \|x\|` plus the piecewise polynomial of the next line at `1 + x`, which gives `ln Γ (1 + x) = ln \|Γ x\| + ln \|x\|` |
| 0.5 to 8.29541 | a piecewise polynomial on 19 pieces, each of degree 12 in `z = \|x\| - c_j`, whose borders `ubrd` the code finds from a quadratic estimate and one comparison.  The pieces that contain the zeros 1 and 2 of `ln Γ` are multiplied by `1 - \|x\|` and `\|x\| - 2`, which keeps the result's relative accuracy next to the zeros |
| from 8.29541 | Stirling's series, `(\|x\| - 1/2)(ln \|x\| - 1)` plus the constant `ln (2π)/2 - 1/2` plus a sum of odd powers of `1/\|x\|` (DLMF 5.11.1) [4], with the sum dropped from `2^100` on |

For a negative `x` the code evaluates the function at `|x|` and applies the reflection (DLMF 5.5.3), in the form `ln |Γ x| = -ln Γ (|x|) - ln (|x| |sin (π x)|/π)`, with `sin (π x)/π` from `as_sinpipid` and its logarithm from `as_logd`.  The sign of `Γ x` for a negative non-integer `x` is `(-1)^m` with `m = -⌊x⌋`, -1 on `(-1, 0)` and +1 on `(-2, -1)`.  Next to the negative zeros of `ln |Γ|`, the subtraction of the two logarithms cancels, and `as_lgamma_accurate` handles it with 17 polynomials about the zeros from -10.5 to -2 (`rootRange`), where the fast path's test fails.  fdlibm's `lgamma` has no such polynomials and errs by up to 4.45e15 units next to these zeros, the reason the review gives for choosing CORE-MATH.

## Implementation

The source is `src/binary64/lgamma/lgamma.c` of CORE-MATH [1], `Copyright (c) 2025 Alexei Sibidanov`, under the MIT license [2].  The module docstring of [the implementation](Program.lean) quotes the copyright and permission notice of `lgamma.c` verbatim, as the license requires in all copies or substantial portions.  The port follows the C code operation for operation, with [the gamma function's](../Tgamma/README.md#implementation) changes: pair results for pointer outputs, tables as borrowed arrays, integer-valued doubles and two's complement words for C's integers, `Fma.fma`, rounding by adding and subtracting `2^52`, and a scan of the hard cases in place of the binary search.  It reuses `Tgamma`'s double-double helpers, `roundEven`, `floorF`, `asSinpid`, and `Tgamma.logdTable`, the tables of `as_logd`, which `lgamma.c` repeats.  The `verified_compile` call lists 43 functions: the 14 of `Fma`, 10 of `Tgamma`, and 19 of this module, among them `lgammaFirstWith`, the first component of `lgammaWith` with the tables as parameters, which `lgamma` calls, since the reflector does not take a projection of a wrapper's result.

## Proof

The proof combines the theorem for `Fma.fma`, real bounds on `ln Γ`, a rounding-error analysis of each execution path, and byte and file theorems for the compiled module.  It uses Mathlib's `Real.Gamma`, recurrence, and reflection formula [5], together with the repository's Stirling remainder and approximation bounds.  [The gamma guide](../Tgamma/README.md#proof) describes the shared mathematical results.  The table lists those specific to `lgamma`.

| Theorem | Module | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| `lgamma_add_one`, `lgamma_one`, `lgamma_two` | `GammaBasic` | `lgamma (x + 1) = lgamma x + ln \|x\|` away from the poles, and `lgamma 1 = lgamma 2 = 0`, where `lgamma x = Real.log (Real.Gamma x)`, which is `ln \|Γ x\|` |
| `lgamma_reflect` | `GammaBasic` | `lgamma x + lgamma (1 - x) = ln π - ln \|sin (π x)\|` for non-integer `x` |
| `abs_lgamma_add_log_le` | `GammaBasic` | `\|lgamma x + ln \|x\|\| ≤ 4 \|x\|` for `0 < \|x\| ≤ 1/4`, so `-ln \|x\|` is within `2 ^ -73` of `lgamma x` below `2 ^ -75` |
| `lgamma_x1`, `lgamma_x2` | `GammaThresholds` | at `0x1.754d9278b51a6p+1014` and the next double, `lgamma` lies within `3/5` and `1/5` of the spacing `2 ^ 971` of the two largest doubles that the code returns |
| `lgamma_overflow` | `GammaThresholds` | `lgamma x ≥ 2 ^ 1024 - 2 ^ 970` from `0x1.754d9278b51a8p+1014` on, which confirms the code's threshold over the comment's `0x1.006df1bfac84ep+1015` |
| `lgamma_model` | `GammaModels` | a Taylor model that `TaylorModel.tm` computes for Stirling's series after `n` shifts bounds `lgamma (a + t)` on `[-r, r]` |

[The approximation facts](LgammaApprox.lean) define, without proofs, the bounds on CORE-MATH's polynomials and tables that the analysis takes as hypotheses (`LgammaApprox.AllFacts`): the fast path's pieces, Stirling's series, logarithm, and sine, the accurate path's series, polynomials, logarithm, and sine, the 17 polynomials about the negative zeros with `RootMinFact`, the 19 hard cases, and `CancelFact`, which bounds the cancellation of the reflection from -10.5 down by `2^32`.  [The branch selection](LgammaBranches.lean) reduces `lgammaWith` to the code of the large inputs, the poles, and 1 and 2 by rewriting with `lgammaWith.eq_1` and naming the local definitions with `extract_lets`, since `simp only` and `rfl` on the unfolded body each ran past four minutes and 7 GB.  [The exceptional values](LgammaSpecial.lean) evaluate the last operation of each of those paths on its words with `decide +kernel` after the `F64Bits` bridge lemmas, prove with `floorF_int` that `Tgamma.floorF` returns an integer-valued double unchanged, and compare the results with the reference through `GammaThresholds.lgamma_x1`, `lgamma_x2`, and `lgamma_overflow`, the last of which leaves the result `+∞` exact.

[The sign](LgammaSign.lean) reads the sign from the code of `lgammaWith` without evaluating the fast path and compares it with the sign of `Real.Gamma` from `GammaBasic.gamma_pos_of_even` and `gamma_neg_of_odd`.  Its first build stalled at 4.1 GB in the kernel, which compares a projection `r.2.2.2` with another term by evaluating `r` with `whnf`, and for the fast path's block that evaluation runs `piecePoly`, the loop of `polyDDDFst`, and the other calls.  The module therefore wraps the projection in a regular definition `sgnOf`, which the kernel compares through its argument, and reads it through the block with lemmas for `if`, `have`, and the code's pair and triple matchers, which a small tactic `sgn_step` instantiates with the goal's own subterms.  [The journal's section on the floating-point proof](../../../../devnotes.md#2026-10-12-the-floating-point-proof-of-lgamma-track-expm1) records the bisection that found this cause.

[The rounding test](LgammaRoundTest.lean) proves the step that the fast path's analyses share: `round_test` gives one step for `fh + (fl + eps)` when the code's test passes and `fh + fl` lies within `2 eps` of `ln |Γ x|`, twice the error that CORE-MATH's `eps` allows.  The factor of two lets the approximation facts carry the margins of `LgammaApprox` next to the zeros of `ln |Γ|`, where no relative bound holds.  [The walker](LgammaWalk.lean) states the bounds on the fast path's block that give one step (`FastSpec`), gives one step for the code's choice between the fast result and the accurate path (`select_within`), and reads the block through its branches.  `LgammaApprox.LogFact` bounds the logarithm's kernel by an absolute `2^-70`, which on the branches below `2^-5` in magnitude allows an error of about `3.4 · 10^-22`, more than twice the code's `eps` of `1.5 · 10^-22`.  Those branches use the relative bound `|L - (fh + fl)| + 2 eps ≤ |L|/2^54` in place of the rounding test (`rel_within`), which holds since `|ln |Γ x||` is at least 3 there.

Both ports compute CORE-MATH's `as_logd`: `Tgamma.asLogd` for normal arguments, and `Lgamma.asLogd`, which applies the same operations after `logReduce` and also normalizes subnormal arguments, with an exponent down to -1074.  [The gamma function's Proof section](../Tgamma/README.md#proof) lists the modules of `Tgamma.asLogd`.  [Their last theorem](../Tgamma/TgammaLogd.lean), `asLogd_spec`, states under `FmaSpec.FmaNearest` and `TgammaApprox.LogFact` that for every positive normal `x` both parts of `Tgamma.asLogd Tgamma.logdTable x` are finite, `|lh + ll - ln x| ≤ 2^-69 + |ln x|/2^78`, and `|ll| ≤ 2^-22 + |lh|/2^52`.  [The theorem for `Lgamma.asLogd`](LgammaLogd.lean) states the same bound for every positive finite `x`, under `FmaSpec.FmaNearest` and `LgammaApprox.LogFact` (`LgammaLogd.asLogd_spec`).  A normal argument gives `Tgamma.asLogd` itself (`asLogd_normal`).  A subnormal `x = f 2^-1074`, with the highest set bit of `f` at position `P`, becomes `2^(P - 1074) t` with `t` in `[1, 2)` after `logReduce` shifts `f` left by `52 - P` (`subnormal_parts`), and `TgammaLogd.logd_core`, the part of the proof of `asLogd_spec` after the exponent and the mantissa, gives the bound for exponents down to -1074, since `TgammaLogdTail.logTail_spec` admits every exponent with `|E| ≤ 1074`.

`Lgamma.asSinpipid` calls `Tgamma.asSinpid` with `Lgamma.sinpiTable` for the rows `ky ≤ 62`, at least `3/256` away from the integers.  [The sine theorem](../Tgamma/TgammaSinpid.lean) `asSinpid_gen` holds for any table that passes the word check `TgammaSinpidTab.sinTabOK`, whose kernel words `Lgamma.sinpiTable` repeats, with rows within `εT` of `κ sin (k π/128)` and kernels within `εs |d|` and `εc d²`.  With `κ = 1/π` and the tolerances `2^-100`, `2^-73`, and `2^-76` of `SinpiFact`, it gives a relative error below `2^-63.7` on those rows, where `y ≥ 3/2`, and a low part at most `2^-50` of the high part.  [The theorem for `Lgamma.asSinpipid`](LgammaSinpi.lean), `asSinpipid_spec`, states under `FmaSpec.FmaNearest` and `LgammaApprox.SinpiFact` that for every `x = n/2^53` with `0 < n < 2^53` both parts are finite, the error is at most `2^-63` of `sin (π x)/π`, and the low part is at most `2^-50` of the high part.  The kernel checks `sinTabOK Lgamma.sinpiTable`, `SinpiFact`'s kernels equal `TgammaSinpidTab.Ks` and `Kc` (`sinKernel_eq`, `cosKernel_eq`), and [the branch near the integers](LgammaSinpiNear.lean) evaluates `z + z³ nearPoly (z²)` within `z/2^74` (`near_spec`), so with the fact's `2^-76 z` and `sin (π z)/π ≥ 2z/π` that branch is within `2^-72` relative.

The fast path has a theorem for each of its branches below `2^800` in magnitude.  Below `1/2` in magnitude the code adds `-log |x|` to `ln Γ (1 + x)`, and `|ln |Γ x||` is at least `1/10` there, so these branches use the relative form of `FastSpec`.  From 0.5 to 8.29541 the result can be zero, at 1 and 2, and the pieces use the absolute form, `|ln Γ a - (fh + fl)| ≤ 2 eps`, with a margin of `|fh|/2^65` that the reflection uses.  Stirling's series leaves a margin of `|fh|/2^67` from 8.29541 to `2^800`.  Above `2^800` the low part of the logarithm exceeds the bound `2^800` that `TgammaDD.fastSum_spec` requires, and `ln Γ x` approaches `2^1024`, beyond the range `|fh| + 2^49 eps ≤ 2^1020` of `LgammaRoundTest.round_test`.  `F64TopPack`, `F64TopMul`, and `F64TopTwoSum` prove the roundings of sums, products, and Dekker's two-sum up to the overflow threshold `2^1024 - 2^970`, and `TgammaDDTop` the double-double operations of `TgammaDD` with high parts up to it.  `LgammaStirTop.stir_top_core` bounds Stirling's series from `2^800` to `2^1015` given a bound `H ≤ 2^1024 - 13 · 2^968` on `p1 x` for the pair `(p1, p2)` near `log x - 1`, which a kernel check gives at `0x1.754cp+1014`, where the code starts to normalize the pair with `fastTwoSum`, and at `x1` (`LgammaFastTop.products_ok`).  `LgammaFastTop.top_within` replaces the rounding test there, with `ln Γ x ≤ 2^1024 - 5 · 2^969` up to `x1` (`lgamma_le_top`).

Next to the negative zeros the fast path's absolute error, near `10 ^ -21`, can exceed the result itself, so a relative bound of about `2 ^ -60` cannot cover that path, and its facts must meet CORE-MATH's `eps` of `8.7 · 10 ^ -20 |h| + 10 ^ -24` plus `8 · 10 ^ -22` times the logarithm of the reflection.  The sign of `Γ` is exact and needs only the parity of `⌊x⌋`.

`LgammaApprox.AllFacts` has thirteen statements.  Their tolerances run from `2^-60` relative for the hard cases to `2^-100` for the table of `sin (k π/128)/π` and the accurate path's split `ln 2`, and `RootFact` adds an absolute `2^-120` to its relative `2^-63`.  The 19 pieces and the fast path's Stirling fit carry `12 · 10^-20` and `7 · 10^-20` relative, which the rounding test needs within about twice CORE-MATH's `eps`, and the accurate Stirling fits on `[15/4, 48]` carry `2^-87`.  The tight tolerances of the accurate path come from the reflection's cancellation between -17 and -10.5, where `as_lgamma_accurate` has no polynomials about the zeros: a computation in 80-digit decimal arithmetic, recorded in the journal, gives `|lgamma x|` as small as `7.9 · 10^-9` at the double next to the zero near -11, against `lgamma (1 - x) ≈ 17.5`, a ratio of `2^31`.  `CancelFact` states the bound `2^32` on that ratio for every non-integer double from -21/2 down.  [The assembled proof](LgammaFacts.lean), `LgammaFacts.allFacts`, proves all thirteen statements using the results in the table.  Its axiom report lists only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

The fast path's fit from 16 on needed a new check. In 70-digit arithmetic the error reaches 0.62 of the allowed `7 · 10 ^ -20 lgamma x` near 96.

## Tests

[The accuracy check](../../../../tests/TestSupport/Numerics/LgammaAccuracy.lean) compares `Lgamma.lgammaR` natively with the double nearest to `ln |Γ x|` from [the gamma reference](../../../../tests/TestSupport/Numerics/GammaReference.lean), requires the sign that `signReference` gives, and fails at the first result more than one step away or with another sign.  Its 81,033 arguments are 30,001 evenly spaced values from -20 to 20 and 20,001 from 0.5 to 8.3, 30,000 hashed values over all exponents with both signs, the doubles around the integers and half-integers from -30 to 30.5, around the zeros 1 and 2 and the 17 centers of the accurate path's polynomials, around the 20 borders of the pieces, around ten thresholds of the code and their negations, the 19 hard cases and their neighbors, the six inputs of `WorstCases.lgamma`, and 15 special values.  Those six give the largest known `lgamma` errors in Tables 4 to 11 of Gladman, Innocente, Mather, Ozaki, and Zimmermann's report [10], whose Table 3 lists 0.500 units for GNU libc 2.44 and 4.45e15 for FreeBSD 15.1.  The check also calls `Lgamma.accurate` directly at every eighth finite argument below the overflow test, other than zero and the integers below 3, and at the 19 hard cases, 9,234 arguments.

The reference is that of [the gamma function](../Tgamma/README.md#tests): Stirling's series with 60 terms at `y + N ≥ 300`, the logarithm of the exact product of the shifts, and the reflection for negative arguments, with truncation errors stated in docstrings and not proved.  In a run of `tests/verified/run.sh`, all 81,033 results equal the reference with their signs, so the largest measured distance is 0 steps against the decided bound of 1, and the 9,234 results of the accurate path also equal the reference.  The first passing run gave the same results, and two earlier runs failed on defects of the check: the reference rounded the residue of the series at the exact zeros 1 and 2 instead of returning 0, and the direct calls of the accurate path included magnitudes from `0x1.006df1bfac84ep+1015` up, where `cr_lgamma` never calls it and where it returns NaN from `∞ - ∞`.

## Limits

The accuracy theorem, with the exceptional results of `LgammaSpecial` and the sign of `LgammaSign`, covers every input.  [The file theorem](LgammaFile.lean) states that `build/verified/lgamma.wasm` holds the compiled module.  `LgammaBytes.lgamma_bytes_accuracy` has the limits of [the exponential](../Exp/README.md#limits): Talos and the decoder are trusted, the file theorem trusts `binary_file%`, the theorems cover the canonical NaN argument only, and host actions between calls need their own theorem.  The port depends on `Fma.fma` on every path, which `FmaTheorem.fma_toBits` proves equal to IEEE 754's fusedMultiplyAdd with roundTiesToEven.

The proved bound permits one representable step from nearest-even rounding.  The signs at NaN, `-∞`, and the negative integers are CORE-MATH's choices where POSIX leaves `signgam` unspecified, and the sign of +1 at the negative integers from `0x1.754d9278b51a6p+1014` up differs from the -1 below it.  The port drops `errno` and the floating-point exceptions that C99 and POSIX specify at the poles and on overflow.
