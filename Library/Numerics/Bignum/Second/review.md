# Reviews of the statements

The reviews below were written when the library was developed in another repository.  This copy gives the present paths and names of the library and of the calculator, and the request and the design that the reviews cite stayed in that repository.

## Review of the WebAssembly statements

A fresh agent reviewed the statements of [`statements.md`](statements.md), with their proofs omitted, against the request, the first iteration's specification, and the programs, after writing its own reading of the request: the products equal to `limbsOf (value u * value v)` and `.ofInt (a * b)` and to the first iteration's `natMul` and `intMul`, the integer reader equal to `readSpec` and to `intOfText` with the same flags, a natural reader of the grammar `"0" / nonzero *digit` that rejects every negative text and `-0`, canonical results, closed forms linear in the lengths, a call theorem for each new export at the index of its name with the room of the bound whose closed form is stated, no premise on the depth, and the shift-carry loops equal to the first iteration's.  It found that every export meets this reading, and that no statement accepts a wrong result or a wrong program among those it tried: a product wrong only above some size or right only at the chosen threshold, an untrimmed product, a negative zero, a reader that accepts `-0`, `+1`, or a leading zero or keeps a sign on a rejected text, a natural reader that accepts a negative text or returns a value on a rejected one, and a reader right only for one bound `E`.  It built a model of the specifications in Node's `BigInt` and compared it with the module on 12,448 cases, among them products of 0 to 129 limbs in every pair, 20,000 × 20,000 limbs, thresholds from 0 to `2^64 − 1`, bounds `E` from 1 to `2^64 − 1`, and texts of up to 192,000 digits, with no difference, and it measured the rise of `top` on fresh instances within every closed form, the tightest 131,072 bytes against 131,104 for a product of 1,023 × 1,025 limbs.

## Review of the C form

A second fresh agent reviewed the statements of [`Native.lean`](Native.lean) and [`NativeRuntime.lean`](NativeRuntime.lean), with their proofs omitted, the scripts, and the emitted C text, against the request's decision 6 and the standard of the first iteration's C form and its review, after writing its own reading: for each new export, under every `Archi` and every semantics of external functions, from a C memory related to a store with the call theorem's precondition and room for the generated bound at 1,000 frames, a terminating call with `lx_status` 0, results that represent the specification's value, and `top` raised by at most the closed form, with a size form on the 256 MiB of `lx_mem` and no premise on the frames, together with the names of the symbols, the program's start, and the runtime's functions.  It found that the theorems of the exports 19, 20, 21, 25, and 26 match their call theorems and closed forms, that the size premise implies the generated room, that `native_names` matches the 27 functions of the emitted `bignum2.c` and its result globals, with each entry calling its body at depth 0, and that `native_start`, `alloc_room_native`, and `release_native` match the first iteration's corrected forms.  It found no wrong C program that the statements accept: a translation that swaps two C functions of one signature, a result left in another global, a trap, a rise of `top` beyond the closed form, a corrupted array, and a wrong start are each excluded.

## The user's decisions on the reviews' open points

On 2026-11-27 the user approved `natReadSpec` with the grammar `"0" / nonzero *digit`, `(true, limbsOf n)` on success, and `(false, limbsOf 0)` for negative texts and `-0`, with the flags of `readSpec`, and kept the readers' base case, Horner's rule on up to 768 chunks before the passes of Algorithm 1.25.  Neither decision changed a statement or a program, so no statement needed a new review.
