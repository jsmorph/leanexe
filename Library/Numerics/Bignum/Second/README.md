# Arbitrary-precision naturals and integers: Karatsuba's product and a divide-and-conquer reader

## Specification

This part of [the bignum library](../README.md) adds faster functions with the first part's specifications: Karatsuba's product above a threshold, carries by `>>> 32` and `&&& 0xFFFFFFFF`, and a reader of decimal text by divide and conquer.  Its module, [`Compiled.lean`](Compiled.lean), is separate from the first part's, and its exports `natMulKara`, `intMulKara`, `natOfTextDC`, and `intOfTextDC` sit beside the first part's `natMul`, `intMul`, and `intOfText`.  The products and the integer reader have the first part's specifications: `natMulKara u v = limbsOf (value u * value v)`, `intMulKara (.ofInt a) (.ofInt b) = .ofInt (a * b)`, and `intOfTextDC s = readSpec s`, with `readSpec`'s flag `true` for a text of Int64Text's grammar and `(false, .ofInt 0)` for every other array.  The natural reader has no function in the first part, so [`Spec.lean`](Spec.lean) adds `natReadSpec`: the grammar `"0" / nonzero *digit`, the same grammar without the sign, which returns `(true, limbsOf n)` for a text that denotes `n` and `(false, limbsOf 0)` for every other array, the texts of negative integers and `-0` included.  `natReadSpec_eq` states it as `readSpec` with every text that starts with `-` rejected.  [The review](review.md) records two independent readings of the statements, listed in [`statements.md`](statements.md), and their comparison with the reviewers' own models.

## The program

[The program](Program.lean) keeps the numbers of each product in one workspace, an array that every helper takes as an owned parameter and returns, so that each update `set!` writes in place.  The generated allocation bound charges every block and credits no freed one, so a Karatsuba product that allocated its sums and partial products at each level would have a bound of order `n^1.58`; in place, a product allocates its workspace and its trimmed result.  `addGen` and `subGen` add or subtract two segments of the workspace into a third, with carries and borrows by `>>> 32` and `&&& 0xFFFFFFFF`, and `baseMul` is Algorithm M in place.  `kara t W ap bp rp tp n` writes the product of the `n` limbs at `ap` and at `bp` to the `2 n` limbs at `rp`, with `6 n` limbs of scratch at `tp`: below `max t 16` limbs it calls `baseMul`, and above it writes `A0 B0` and `A1 B1` to the halves of the result, the sums `A0 + A1` and `B0 + B1` to the scratch, their product after them by a third call, subtracts the first two products, and adds the rest, `A0 B1 + A1 B0`, from limb `⌈n/2⌉` on.  `natMulKaraWith t u v` multiplies directly when the shorter operand has fewer than `max t 16` limbs; otherwise it pads the longer operand to chunks of the shorter one's length and adds each chunk's product to the result, by `kara`, or by `baseMul` for a last chunk shorter than the threshold.  `natMulKara` is `natMulKaraWith 48`.

`kara` is the program's one recursion, and the verified compiler gives a recursive function and its callers a frame count that traps at 1,000 frames.  A call on `n` limbs recurses on at most `⌈n/2⌉ + 1`, so `kara` nests at most 25 calls for the largest array of WebAssembly's 32-bit memory, `2^29` limbs.  Above `kara`'s calls, `natMulKaraWith` adds one frame, `natMulKara` two, `intMulKara` three, `intOfTextDC` and `natOfTextDCWith` three, and `natOfTextDC` four.  `kara_fits_all` proves the frame condition for every argument at 65 frames and more, so the call theorems carry no premise on the depth.

`decimalValueDC t E s start count` reads `count` digits in `C = ⌈count/9⌉` chunks of nine, padded with leading zeros as the first part's reader pads them, into `P2 = 2^K` base elements of `e0 = ⌈C/P2⌉ ≤ max E 1` chunks each, with `K` the least for which `E · 2^K ≥ C`.  `readInit` computes each base element's limbs in place by Horner's rule, over the length that each step's value needs, and `10^(9 e0)` after them, and each `readPass` turns the elements of `e` limbs into elements of `2 e` limbs, `l0 + 10^(9 e) l1` by `kara` and `addGen`, and squares the power when a later pass needs it: Brent and Zimmermann's Algorithm 1.25 from base elements of `e0` chunks.  The passes are a `repeatWhile` of at most 32 rounds, with no recursion.  `natOfTextDC` and `intOfTextDC` check the grammar as the first part's `intOfText` does and use `readBase = 768`, so a text of up to 6,912 digits is read by Horner's rule alone.  `decimalValueDC_eq` holds for every `E`, so the readers return the same results with any base case.  The constants `karaThreshold = 48` and `readBase = 768` come from timing sweeps of the compiled module in Node.

`addS`, `subS`, and `longDivS` are MultiPrecision's Algorithms A, S, and D with the carries and borrows by shift and mask and each word computed once, and `natAddS`, `natSubS`, and `natDivModCheckedS` are the first part's functions over them.  [`Loops.lean`](Loops.lean) proves each equal to its first-part function, so every first-part theorem holds for it.  They are not exports of this module, and the third part's division uses `divideS` below its threshold.

In the table, `m` and `n` are the operands' lengths in limbs and `s` the text's length in words.  `Verified.Call` states that the function, called from a ready store with arguments that represent `x`, the precondition, and room for `compiled.f.bound 1000 x` bytes, returns without a trap the representation of the specification's value, leaves a ready store with every borrowed array kept and every region of the heap unchanged, and raises `top` by at most that bound; `*_room` bounds it by the closed form.  The equations assume fewer than `2^32` limbs or words per array, which the call theorems take from WebAssembly's 32-bit memory.  `natMulKaraWith` treats every threshold below 16 as 16, which the scratch of `6 n` limbs requires, and `natOfTextDCWith` with `E = 0` takes `2^32` base elements, whose workspace no memory holds, so its call theorem's room cannot be met there.

The module also exports the helpers, which compute the Lean functions above: `isDigitW` (2), `addGen` (3), `subGen` (4), `baseMul` (5), `trimSeg` (7), `mulAddSeg` (11), `readInit` (12), `kara` (18), `readPass` (22), and `decimalValueDC` (23).  `Call.lean` states a theorem without a trap for each, with the generated bound for `trimSeg`, `readInit`, and `decimalValueDC` and no room for the others, which update their moved workspace in place and allocate nothing.  The axiom report of each theorem of the tables names only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; `Call.lean` prints them.

## The C form

[`Native.lean`](Native.lean) joins the C translation of the module to the claims of the six new exports, as the first part's does, at 1,000 frames with the frames discharged.  `f_native_size` states the premise as arithmetic on `lx_mem`: canonical arguments of fewer than `2^32` limbs or texts of fewer than `2^32` words, and a heap whose `top` plus the closed form is at most `2^28` bytes, so a product of two operands of 2.2 million limbs each has a call theorem in WebAssembly and none in C.  [`NativeRuntime.lean`](NativeRuntime.lean) states the C names, among them the functions whose code takes the call depth, `lxf_6`, `lxf_8` to `lxf_10`, and `lxf_13` to `lxf_17`, the program's start, the runtime's functions, and the C theorems of the helpers, and `natOfTextDCWith` has one for every bound `E` with the generated bound beside the closed form for `1 ≤ E ≤ 2^32`.  CompCert's theorem assumes that the machine stack does not overflow: within the 256 MiB of `lx_mem`, `kara`'s body `lxf_6` nests at most 17 calls, below at most five others.

## Limits

The product of unequal lengths pads its last chunk to the shorter operand's length when that chunk has at least 48 limbs, which can double the time of a product whose longer operand is slightly more than a multiple of the shorter.  Toom–Cook and FFT multiplication, Montgomery multiplication, gcd, bitwise operations, and 64-bit limbs are not in the library.
