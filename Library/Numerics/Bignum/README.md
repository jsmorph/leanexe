# Arbitrary-precision naturals and integers

## Specification

A natural number is an array of limbs, words below `2^32`, least significant first, and an integer is a `BigInt`, a sign and such a magnitude.  [The specification](Spec.lean) states every function as the canonical form of the exact result over `ℕ` or `ℤ`.  `limbsOf n` is the array of the base-`2^32` digits of `n` from Mathlib's `Nat.digits`, `Canonical u` states limbs below `2^32` and a nonzero top limb, and `BigInt.ofInt z` is the sign `z < 0` with `limbsOf |z|`, so zero has the one form `⟨false, #[]⟩`.  An equation with `limbsOf` or `BigInt.ofInt` states the value, the absence of a zero limb at the top, and the absence of a negative zero at once.  The library provides both truncated division, `Int.tdiv` and `Int.tmod`, and Lean's Euclidean `/` and `%` on `Int`.  Its divisions follow Lean at a zero divisor, `x / 0 = 0` and `x % 0 = x`, so a negative dividend keeps its sign in the remainder there, and each exported division returns an error status for a zero divisor instead.

`printSpec z` is the bytes of Lean's `toString z`, one byte to a word, and `readSpec` reads the grammar `"0" / ["-"] nonzero *digit` of [`Int64Text`](../Decimal/Int64Text/README.md) with no bound on the value, so that `-0`, `+1`, a leading zero, white space, and any word above 255 are rejected and each integer has one text.  Reading and printing are exact inverses: `readSpec` accepts a text exactly when it is `printSpec` of an integer, which it returns (`readSpec_print` and `print_of_readSpec`).  One text export per integer operation, `textNeg`, `textCmp`, `textAdd`, `textSub`, `textMul`, `textTDiv`, `textTMod`, `textEDiv`, and `textEMod`, reads decimal operands and returns a status and a decimal result.  The status is 0 for a result, 1 or 2 for a first or second operand outside the grammar, and 3 for a division by zero, and the division exports over limbs and records return 1 and two zeros for a zero divisor.  The library's theorems say nothing of arguments that are not canonical, for which the results have no meaning.  [The review](review.md) records independent readings of the specification and of the statements, with the answers to their points, and [the statements](statements.md) list the main theorems.

The library has three parts, each a compiled module of its own beside the earlier ones.  This directory holds the first, with the schoolbook algorithms: Knuth's Algorithms A, S, M, and D from [MultiPrecision](../MultiPrecision/README.md) and quadratic conversion to and from decimal text.  [The second part](Second/README.md) adds Karatsuba's multiplication and a divide-and-conquer reader of decimal text, and [the third](Third/README.md) adds Burnikel and Ziegler's recursive division and a divide-and-conquer printer.  The new functions of the later parts are proved equal to the first part's functions, so they meet the same specifications.  [The calculator demo](../../../Demos/BignumCalc/README.md) compiles the text exports into a program that reads a message such as `mul 123 -456` and writes `0 -56088`, on bare metal and as an AArch64 Image.

## The program

[The program](Program.lean) is written in leanexe's verified dialect, with `LeanExe.loop` and `LeanExe.build` and no recursion.  The natural functions apply MultiPrecision's `add`, `sub`, `mul`, and `divide` and trim the result with `natTrim`, which copies the limbs below MultiPrecision's `significant`, and `natCmp` applies Uscale's `le` in both orders.  `natDivMod` handles the empty divisor first, since `divide` cuts the remainder to the divisor's length.  The integer functions choose signs and call the natural ones, and `intEDivMod` corrects the truncated result once when the remainder is negative and the divisor is not 0.  `intToText` divides a copy of the magnitude by `10^9` in place, `n + n / 14 + 2` times for `n` limbs, and writes the chunks with Int64Text's `digitCount` and `digitByte`.  `intOfText` checks the grammar and multiplies an array of limbs by `10^9` in place for each chunk of nine digits, the first padded with leading zeros.  Both update an owned array in a loop's state, so their allocation is linear in the length.  The divisions of Lean's convention and the program's other helpers are `@[inline]`, so the compiler expands them into their callers.

## The module returns the canonical results

`verified_compile compiled := […]` in [the compilation](Compiled.lean) compiles the program, with the MultiPrecision, Uscale, and Int64Text functions that it calls, into one module.  Functions 20 to 43 of the table below are the library's interface.  The compiler exports every function that it compiles, so the module also exports the reused functions `width`, `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `significant`, `mulSmall`, `wordBits`, `shortDiv`, `tooBig`, `trial`, `longDiv`, `divide`, `limb`, `cmpStep`, `le`, `digitCount`, `digitByte`, and `isDigitW` as functions 2 to 19, beside the runtime's `alloc` and `release`.  [`Reused.lean`](Reused.lean) states a call theorem for each of the 18: MultiPrecision's specifications for `add`, `sub`, `mul`, and `divide`, Uscale's for `le`, and the Lean function itself for the others, whose leanexe theorems give its meaning.  `divide`, `shortDiv`, and `longDiv` follow MultiPrecision's conventions at a zero divisor, Lean's total convention, and return no error status.

In the table, `m` and `n` are the operands' lengths in limbs and `s` and `t` the texts' lengths in words.  `Verified.Call` states that the function, called from a ready store with arguments that represent `x`, the precondition, and room for `compiled.f.bound x` bytes, returns without a trap the representation of the specification's value, leaves a ready store with every borrowed array kept and every region of the heap unchanged, and raises `top` by at most that bound.  The comparisons cannot trap, so their theorems need no room.  The bounds count every allocation of a call and credit no freed block.  The equations assume fewer than `2^32` limbs per array, which keeps the word arithmetic on lengths exact, and the call theorems take the bound from the arrays' presence in WebAssembly's 32-bit memory, which holds fewer than `2^29` words in one array.

The equations follow one route: `eq_limbsOf` reduces an equation with `limbsOf n` to a canonical result with the value `n`, `natTrim_eq` provides both for the trimmed output of each of Knuth's algorithms, and the integer functions reduce to signed magnitudes.  The two text loops keep invariants in the style of MultiPrecision's `shortDiv_spec` and Uscale's `mulSmall_spec`, moved into the array that the loop updates, and `Int64Text`'s `digitWords_getElem?`, `digitCount_toNat`, and `digitByte_eq` read the digits of `toString`.  The axiom report of each theorem of the tables names only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; `Call.lean` prints them.

## The C form

[`Native.lean`](Native.lean) joins the C translation of the module to the claims.  `verified_compile` translates `compiled.module` to the subset of CompCert's Clight that leanexe's translator emits, with `lx_mem` of 256 MiB, and states `compiled.translation_eq`.  For each export of the interface, `f_spec_trapFree` adds the export's equation with the specification and the closed form of its bound to the generated theorem without a trap, from canonical arguments, and `f_native_spec` carries it to the C function `lxf_f` through `LeanExe.Native.ImplementsA.native`.  Under every `Archi` and every semantics of external functions, from a C memory related to a store whose heap meets the allocator invariant and has room for the generated bound, the call terminates with `lx_status` 0 and results that represent the Lean function's value, which equals the specification's value, and it raises `top` by at most the closed form.  `f_native_size` states the premise as arithmetic on `lx_mem`: canonical arguments of fewer than `2^32` limbs, or texts of fewer than `2^30` words (`2^32` for `intOfText`), and a heap whose `top` plus the closed form is at most `2^28` bytes.  The C memory of 256 MiB bounds the inputs that these theorems cover, where the WebAssembly module's memory can grow to 65,535 pages: a product of two operands of more than about 5.6 million limbs each has a call theorem in WebAssembly and none in C.

[`NativeRuntime.lean`](NativeRuntime.lean) completes the C form.  `native_names` states, by evaluation in the kernel, that the C function of each export is `lxf_` followed by its name, that `lx_init`, `lx_status`, `lx_mem`, and the result globals `lx_r0_l` to `lx_r4_l` are the identifiers that the theorems name, and that no two identifiers and no two names of the translation coincide.  `native_start` states that the program's start meets the C theorems' premises on the environment, the memory, and the heap, and `alloc_room_native` and `release_native` are leanexe's theorems of the runtime's functions at this translation, with the allocator invariant after each call.  The host's writes of an argument's words into the block that `lxf_alloc` returns have no theorem, so the premises on the arguments, their representation and their separation, rest on the host's code, which the tests exercise.  The C theorems state that a terminating run exists, and leanexe's uniqueness theorem for deterministic semantics of external functions, `LeanExe.Native.NativeA.unique`, is not instantiated here; the translation calls no external function.  `StoreRel` relates the translation's globals and `lx_mem`, so a C function that also wrote a global of the host would meet the theorems, which the translation's text, with no reference to other globals, excludes.

## Building, running, and testing

```sh
tools/leanrun --timeout 3h lake build Library.Numerics.Bignum.NativeRuntime \
  Library.Numerics.Bignum.Second.NativeRuntime Library.Numerics.Bignum.Third.NativeRuntime
tests/bignum/run.sh
tests/bignum/native.sh
```

The first command checks every theorem of the three parts.  [`tests/bignum/run.sh`](../../../tests/bignum/run.sh) builds the call theorems and the cases of [`TestSupport.Bignum.Cases`](../../../tests/TestSupport/Bignum/Cases.lean), which evaluate the divisions of Lean's convention that no module exports, emits the three modules to `build/bignum`, and validates them with `wasm-tools`.  [`tests/bignum/check.mjs`](../../../tests/bignum/check.mjs) compares every export of the first module with Node's `BigInt`, with no packages: pairs of naturals among 0, 1, `2^32 − 1`, `2^32`, `2^64 − 1`, runs of all-ones limbs, powers of ten, and limbs from MultiPrecision's `divInputs` that make step D3 decrease the trial digit twice and step D6 add the divisor back, pairs with equal operands, one-limb divisors, divisors whose top limb is 1 to 4, divisors longer than dividends, random pairs of 0 to 64 limbs, and pairs of 1,000 to 5,000 limbs; every sign combination of 900 of these pairs; arrays with zero limbs at the top for `natTrim`; and texts, among them the rejections that Int64Text tests and texts of up to 300 digits, for every text export.  It also checks that the comparison rejects three mutated results, a trim that leaves one zero limb, a sign left on zero, and a remainder with the divisor's sign, and that the module releases every block it allocates.  [`tests/bignum/Native.lean`](../../../tests/bignum/Native.lean) evaluates the specification on the cases in native Lean, whose `Nat` and `Int` use GMP, and the results must equal the module's.  The script then runs the tests of the second and third parts, which their guides describe.  [`tests/bignum/native.sh`](../../../tests/bignum/native.sh) writes the C translation of each module, has `clightgen` read it back and compares that reading with the theorems' program, compiles it with CompCert and the host [`tests/bignum/bignum.c`](../../../tests/bignum/bignum.c), and requires the host's output to equal the WebAssembly module's on every call.  The modules are registered in [the module registry](../../../tests/verified/modules.txt), so `tests/verified/run.sh` emits them and checks [the file theorems](File.lean), and [the list of other drivers](../../../tests/verified/no-cases.txt) names these scripts for their cases.
