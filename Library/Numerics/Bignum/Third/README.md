# Arbitrary-precision naturals and integers: recursive division and a divide-and-conquer printer

## Specification

This part of [the bignum library](../README.md) adds a subquadratic division and, with it, a subquadratic conversion to decimal text, with the first part's specifications.  Its module, [`Compiled.lean`](Compiled.lean), is separate from the earlier parts', and its exports `natDivModCheckedBZ`, `intTDivModCheckedBZ`, `intEDivModCheckedBZ`, `natToTextDC`, and `intToTextDC` sit beside the first part's `natDivModChecked`, `intTDivModChecked`, `intEDivModChecked`, and `intToText`.  The new functions have the first part's specifications, with no new definition: `natDivModCheckedBZ u v = natDivModCheckedSpec (value u) (value v)`, the status 1 and two empty arrays for a zero divisor and otherwise the status 0 with `limbsOf (a / b)` and `limbsOf (a % b)`; `intTDivModCheckedBZ (.ofInt a) (.ofInt b) = intTDivModCheckedSpec a b` and `intEDivModCheckedBZ (.ofInt a) (.ofInt b) = intEDivModCheckedSpec a b`; `intToTextDC (.ofInt z) = printSpec z`, the bytes of `toString z`; and `natToTextDC u = printSpec (value u)`, the text of the natural as an integer, which is `intToText ⟨false, u⟩`.  `natDivModCheckedBZ_eq_natDivModChecked`, `intTDivModCheckedBZ_eq_intTDivModChecked`, `intEDivModCheckedBZ_eq_intEDivModChecked`, `natToTextDC_eq_intToText`, and `intToTextDC_eq_intToText` state the equality with the first part's functions, so the first part's theorems hold for the new ones: `intToTextDC_toString`, `readSpec_intToTextDC`, and `intOfText_intToTextDC` restate the equality with `toString` and the round trip through the reader.  [The review](review.md) records two independent readings of the statements, listed in [`statements.md`](statements.md).  The statements of the interface state the results, the frames, and the allocation, so they would also hold of the first part's quadratic functions: the subquadratic growth rests on the program and on timings.

## The program

[The program](Program.lean) keeps every number of a division or a conversion in one workspace, an array that every helper takes as an owned parameter and returns, as the second part's product does, so that each `set!` writes in place and every helper's generated allocation bound is 0.  The verified compiler limits a function that takes the call depth to 32 positions, its depth, parameters, and locals, so the recursion's halves and the signs of the integer divisions are functions of their own.

`rdiv t T W ap bp qp sp n m` is Algorithm 1.8 of Brent and Zimmermann, RecursiveDivRem: it divides the `n + m` limbs at `ap`, for `m ≤ n`, by the `n` limbs at `bp`, normalized so that the top limb is at least `2^31`, writes the `m` quotient limbs to `qp` and returns the top quotient word, 0 or 1, and leaves the remainder in the dividend's low `n` limbs.  With `k = ⌊m/2⌋`, it divides the top `2 (m - k)` limbs by the top `n - k` limbs of the divisor by a recursive call, subtracts the product of that quotient half by the low `k` limbs of the divisor, `halfStep`, which computes the product by the second part's `kara` chunk by chunk (`mulAcc`, `mulChunk`) and corrects the quotient at most four times (`subFix`), and does the same for the low half (`lowHalf`).  The difference can be negative, and it is kept in `n + 1` limbs with one borrow word, which suffices because the quotient of each half is below `2 β^k`.  Below `max T 3` quotient limbs the base case `baseDiv` is Algorithm 1.6 with Knuth's quotient selection in place.  `udiv` is Algorithm 1.9, UnbalancedDivision: a dividend of `n + M` limbs is divided `n` quotient limbs at a time from the top, each block by `rdiv`, and a last block of `M' < n` quotient limbs at or above the threshold by `tdiv`, steps 3 to 5 of Algorithm 1.8 with the split at `n - M'`.  `divSeg` normalizes the divisor and the dividend into the workspace by the second part's `mulAddSeg`, calls `udiv`, and divides the remainder back by `divSmallSeg`.

`natDivModBZWith t T D u v` returns `(#[], natTrim u)` when `u` is shorter than `v`, the second part's shift-carry Algorithm D `divideS` when the divisor has fewer than `max D 2` limbs or the dividend fewer than `max D 2` limbs more than the divisor, and otherwise `divSeg` in a workspace of `3 m + 15 n + 11` words for operands of `m` and `n` limbs, with the quotient and the remainder trimmed by `trimSeg`.  `natDivModCheckedS_eq_natDivModChecked` of the second part, from `divideS_eq_divide`, proves that `divideS` returns the first part's results for every input.  `natDivModCheckedBZWith` adds the status of a zero divisor, `natDivModCheckedBZ` is `natDivModCheckedBZWith 48 32 192`, and the integer divisions choose the signs as the first part's do, in `signTDiv` and `signEDiv`.  `natDivModBZWith` and `natDivModBZ`, which follow Lean's convention at a zero divisor, are `@[inline]` and not exported.

`chunksDC` is Brent and Zimmermann's Algorithm 1.26 with `k` a power of two.  `levelsFor` takes the least `K` with `2^(K+1)` at least the first part's count of chunks, `n + ⌊n/14⌋ + 2` for `n` limbs, and `powInit` computes the powers `P_i = 10^(9·2^i)` for `i ≤ K` by squaring with `kara`, with a table of their positions and lengths.  `toChunks` at level `i` divides the number, below `P_i²`, by `P_i` with `divSeg`, converts the quotient at level `i - 1` into the upper `2^i` chunks and the remainder into the lower ones, and at level `L` or below, `leafChunks` divides by `10^9` repeatedly as the first part's printer does.  `textOfChunks` copies the `2^(K+1)` chunks and writes the text as the first part's `intToText` does from its chunks.  `natToTextDCWith t T L P u` calls the first part's `intToText` below `P` limbs, and `natToTextDC` and `intToTextDC` use `L = 5`, `P_5 = 10^288` of 30 limbs, and `P = 32`.  The constants `divBase = 32`, `divThreshold = 192`, `printLeaf = 5`, and `printThreshold = 32` come from timing sweeps of the compiled module in Node.

In the table, `m` and `n` are the lengths in limbs of the dividend and the divisor, or of the number printed.  `Verified.Call` states that the function, called from a ready store with arguments that represent `x`, the precondition, and room for `compiled.f.bound 1000 x` bytes, returns without a trap the representation of the specification's value, leaves a ready store with every borrowed array kept and every region of the heap unchanged, and raises `top` by at most that bound; `*_room` bounds it by the closed form, on arrays of fewer than `2^32` limbs, which the call theorems take from WebAssembly's 32-bit memory.  The division's bound is the first part's, because a long dividend by a divisor below 192 limbs takes Algorithm D; above the thresholds the workspace and the two results take at most `32 m + 120 n + 264` bytes.  The printer's bound counts a text of at most `9 · 2^(K+1) + 21` words, about twice the longest text.

The recursion's frames need no premise.  `rdiv` recurses on `⌈m/2⌉` and `⌊m/2⌋` quotient limbs and stops below 3, so it nests at most `j` calls for `m ≤ 2^j + 1` and needs `j + 71` frames with those of `lowHalf`, `halfStep`, `mulAcc`, `mulChunk`, and `kara`; `rdiv_fits_all` proves 135 frames enough for every argument.  `toChunks` descends from a level below 40, which `levelsFor_le` proves for every argument.  The export with the most frames, `natToTextDC`, needs 182, and every export's frame condition at 1,000 frames holds for every argument.

The module also exports the helpers, which compute the Lean functions above, among them `trialAt` (28), `mulSubSeg` (29), `baseDiv` (30), `subFix` (33), `divSmallSeg` (39), `signTDiv` (43), `signEDiv` (44), `leafChunks` (47), `textOfChunks` (50), `kara` (55), `mulChunk` (56), `mulAcc` (57), `halfStep` (58), `lowHalf` (59), `rdiv` (60), `tdiv` (61), `udiv` (62), `divSeg` (63), `powInit` (68), and `chunksDC` (70).  `Call.lean` states a theorem without a trap for each, in place with no room for those that update the workspace, with no premise on the depth.  `toChunks` (69) has none of its own: its level is an argument, and its frames are those of `chunksDC`, which starts it below level 40.  The axiom report of each theorem of the tables names only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; `Call.lean` and `NativeRuntime.lean` print them.

## The C form

[`Native.lean`](Native.lean) joins the C translation of the module to the claims of the seven new exports, as the second part's does, at 1,000 frames with the frames discharged.  `f_native_size` states the premise as arithmetic on `lx_mem`: canonical arguments of fewer than `2^32` limbs, and a heap whose `top` plus the closed form is at most `2^28` bytes, so a division of 1.4 million by 700,000 limbs has a call theorem in WebAssembly and none in C.  A natural division leaves its status, quotient, and remainder in the result globals `lx_r0_l` to `lx_r2_l`, and an integer division its status and the sign and magnitude of the quotient and of the remainder in `lx_r0_l` to `lx_r4_l`; a printer returns the address of its text.  [`NativeRuntime.lean`](NativeRuntime.lean) states the C names, among them the bodies of the functions whose code takes the call depth, the program's start, the runtime's functions, and the C theorems of the helpers.  CompCert's theorem assumes that the machine stack does not overflow, and no theorem bounds the stack in bytes: the frame theorems of `Bound.lean` bound the nesting of the bodies that take the call depth below an export by 182 frames for every argument, and the depth guard counts frames, not bytes.

## Building, running, and testing

[The first part's guide](../README.md#building-running-and-testing) gives the commands.  [`tests/bignum/third/check.mjs`](../../../../tests/bignum/third/check.mjs) compares `natDivModCheckedBZ`, `intTDivModCheckedBZ`, `intEDivModCheckedBZ`, `natToTextDC`, and `intToTextDC`, and `natDivModCheckedBZWith` and `natToTextDCWith` at other thresholds, with Node's `BigInt` and with the first module's `natDivModChecked`, `intTDivModChecked`, `intEDivModChecked`, and `intToText`.  The divisions: zero, one, small values, and a zero divisor in every pair; divisors of 190 to 194 limbs with quotients of 0 to 194 limbs, around the thresholds; dividends of twice the divisor's length and divisors with a top limb of 1 from 30 to 385 limbs; all-ones limbs, divisors with a top limb of 1, and dividends of the divisor's length and one limb more, from 2 to 2,000 limbs; dividends much longer than the divisor, up to 100,000 by 10 and 100,000 by 200 limbs, and quotients shorter than the divisor; random pairs of up to 2,000 limbs and of 20,000 by 10,000, 60,000 by 30,000, and 200,000 by 100,000 limbs; and every sign combination of ten integers, zero included.  The printers: zero, one, nine, ten, `10^9 - 1`, `10^9`, and the top values of one to three limbs; random and all-ones numbers of 1 to 2,000 limbs, around the lengths where the leaves and the levels change; the powers `10^(9·2^i) ± 1`, their squares, and `P (P - 1)` for `i` from 1 to 12; and random numbers of 5,000 to 100,000 limbs and all-ones of 100,000, with both signs.  It writes the cases of up to 2,000 limbs and the module's results, and [`tests/bignum/third/Native.lean`](../../../../tests/bignum/third/Native.lean) evaluates the new functions in native Lean beside the first part's functions and the specification.  [`tests/bignum/native.sh`](../../../../tests/bignum/native.sh) runs the C form with the host [`bignum3.c`](../../../../tests/bignum/third/bignum3.c), which also calls `lxf_natDivModCheckedBZWith` with the base case at 4 quotient limbs and the top-level threshold at 2, and `lxf_natToTextDCWith` with leaves at level 1 and no threshold, on every case.

## Limits

The integer divisions with Lean's convention at a zero divisor, the first part's `intTDivMod` and `intEDivMod`, have no new counterparts.  The division's recursion and the printer's divisions run at the pace of Karatsuba's product; Toom–Cook and FFT multiplication, Newton's division, and 64-bit limbs are not in the library.
