# Review of the specification

Three fresh agents reviewed [the specification](Spec.lean) and [the case inputs](../../../../tests/TestSupport/Modules/Int64Text.lean) against [the request](request.txt), from the files and the specification's output on every input, without running Lean.

The first review found no disagreement.  It traced `expectedPrint` through the `ToString` instance of `Int64` to `Int.repr` and `Nat.toDigits`, and an independent Python model of the request, a printer by repeated division and a reader by the regular expression `0|-?[1-9][0-9]*` with a range check, reproduced all 591 lines of the output.  It found every open case recorded with a reason: the leading `+`, leading zeros, `-0`, the report of rejection, words above 255, and white space.  It found one clause of the reason for rejecting `-0` inaccurate: the comment made binary64's signed zero the cause of RFC 8259's admitting `-0`, which its grammar admits because it allows the minus sign before every `int`.  The comment now states the two facts separately and cites the `(false, 0)` of the library's parsers for the report of rejection.  It found two comments of the case inputs inaccurate, a claim that half of the 40 arbitrary values are negative, where 19 are, and a claim of `+` in every position, where no input had `+` after a digit.  The first claim is gone, and the inputs now include `5+` and `1+2`.

The first review named two classes that no input covered and that a program could get wrong.  No input held a byte from 128 to 255, so a reader that rejects words above 255 and then masks each byte to 7 bits passed every input, and it would read `[0xB1]` as 1.  No input had 20 digits whose accumulation in 64 bits wraps to a larger word in range, so a reader that detects overflow only when its accumulator decreases passed every input, and it would read `25000000000000000000` as `6553255926290448384`.  The inputs now include the bytes 0xB0, 0xB1, and 0xB9, 0xAD before `1`, 0xB0 after `1`, 0xB1 after `-`, 128 and 255 alone, 255 after `1`, and 128 after `-`, and the texts `25000000000000000000`, `20500000000000000000`, and `27000000000000000000`, the first and last also with `-`.

The second review found no disagreement, and its independent model reproduced every line of the output.  It found the specification's list of rejected arrays incomplete where it read as exhaustive, since a sign out of place, as in `5-` or `1-2`, fell under none of its items, and it found that the comment called the flag a word where the code's flag is a `Bool`.  The list now gives examples among the rejected arrays and names a sign out of place, and the comment says that the module passes the `Bool` as the word 1 or 0.  It found three comments of the inputs inaccurate: they claimed every byte from 128 to 255 whose low 7 bits are a digit or `-`, white space between digits, and `20500000000000000000` with `-`.  The inputs now hold the eleven such bytes alone, `1 2` and `1` with a line feed before `2`, and `-20500000000000000000`.  It named as uncovered white space other than a space and a line feed, which the inputs now include as a tab, a carriage return, a vertical tab, and a form feed, and `-18446744073709551615`, which they now include.

The third review, of the specification and the inputs after these changes, found no disagreement, and its independent model agreed on all 253 printed values and all 370 read inputs.  It found the comments of the specification accurate.  It found that the comment of the inputs mentions the word 0 after a digit but not the arrays `[0, 49]` and `[45, 0]`, which it also holds.  It named two classes that no input covers, which this record lists without new inputs, as the review procedure does for its last review.  No input is a positive text of 19 digits above `2^63 - 1` that ends in a digit from 0 to 7, such as `9300000000000000000`, on which a reader that tests only the last digit against the bound would fail.  No input starts with `,` or `.`, the bytes on either side of `-`, on which a sign test written as a range or a mask would fail.
