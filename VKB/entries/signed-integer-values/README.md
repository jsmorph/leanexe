# Signed quantities as `Int64` values

A program that computes a signed integer, such as the exponent `k = (int64_t) tmp >> 52` of a logarithm or the shifted difference `k = (iy - ix) >> 20` of `atan2`, can hold it as an `Int64`, which compiles as [the entry on programs over `Int64`](../signed-int64/README.md) describes.  `k.toFloat` of a variable `k` compiles to the bytes of the same conversion written by hand on words.  The proof then states the integer of the value, such as `k.toInt = E - 1023`, instead of a word congruence `(k.toNat : Int) = (E - 1023) % 2 ^ 64` and a case split on the sign bit.

`LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64Value` gives the integer of each form.  `toInt_toInt64` reads a word as a signed integer, `toInt_shiftRight` states `a >>> b` as `a.toInt / 2 ^ b` for an amount below 64, and `toInt_of_word` concludes `a.toInt = n` from `(a.toUInt64.toNat : Int) = n % 2 ^ 64` and the bounds of `Int64`, while `toNat_toUInt64` gives the congruence of a value whose integer is known.  The word of a sum or difference of `Int64` values is the sum or difference of the words by `rfl`, so a `show` with the word expression, `UInt64.toNat_add` and `UInt64.toNat_sub`, and `omega` prove the congruence for `toInt_of_word`.  `toFloat_exact` gives `k.toFloat` the value `k.toInt` for `|k.toInt| ≤ 2 ^ 53`.  A comparison goes to the integers as [the entry on programs over `Int64`](../signed-int64/README.md) describes.  `omega` evaluates ground powers such as `2 ^ 52`, so the statements may keep them.

A proof's own definition of such a value can fail to elaborate where the program's `let` succeeded: `k0 + UInt64.toInt64 (xw >>> 32 >>> 20) - 1023` with `k0 : Int64`, the definition `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log10.Log10Bits.kWord10`, failed with `failed to synthesize HSub Int64 ℕ`, and the literal written `(1023 : Int64)` elaborates to the term that the program's unfolding gives, as [the entry on types that elaboration chooses](../elaborated-types/README.md) describes.

A kernel computation over many values of a conversion, such as the check of `log2 (2 ^ k) = k` for every `k`, evaluates `Wasm.IEEE64.convertI64S k.toUInt64` from `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64Wasm.toBits_toFloat` rather than Lean's model of `Int64.toFloat`.  A comparison on `Int64` in an `if` is a proposition, so a branch lemma takes `h : ¬ k > 0` or `h : k > 60` and closes the unfolded `if` with `ite_eq_right h` or `ite_eq_left h`, where the word form took a Boolean equation.  The proofs of `Library/Numerics/Float64/Log1p/Log1pTheoremCorr.lean` and `Library/Numerics/Float64/Log1p/Log1pTheoremReduce.lean` write `if_neg` and `if_pos`, which Lean's `Init/Core.lean` deprecates in favor of `ite_eq_right` and `ite_eq_left`, so they compile with a deprecation warning.

Worked examples: `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log.LogCoreBits.fields_upper` and `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log.LogCoreStep.core_within` for the shift and the exact conversion, `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log2.Log2Power.core2_power` for the kernel check, `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log10.Log10Bits.xr_fields` and `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log1p.Log1pTheoremReduce.main_reduce` for sums through `toInt_of_word`, `Library.Numerics.Float64.Log1p.Log1pTheoremCorr.k_cases` for a comparison in an `if`, and `Library.Numerics.Float64.Atan.AtanTwoBits.k_cases`, `big_cond`, and `tiny_cond` for the comparisons of `atan2`.
