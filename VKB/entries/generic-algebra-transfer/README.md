# One definition over ℤ and over words

The entry applies when a program computes with wrapping 64-bit words an algorithm whose specification is over ℤ.  In a CIC decimation filter the integrators' values grow with the input's length and leave the range of a word, while each output stays within a bound that the input's range and the filter's parameters fix.  A proof that relates the program to the specification step by step carries a cast through every operation and needs a bound on every intermediate value, which the integrators do not meet.

Define the algorithm once over a type with `Zero`, `Add`, and `Sub`, as `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cascade` defines the filter's integrators, decimation, and combs over lists (header of `Library/Numerics/Filters/Cic/Cascade.lean`).  At `UInt64` the operations are the wrapping operations of Lean's core library, which a program in the verified compiler's dialect uses, so the program's proof involves no cast and no integer: `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cic_toList` states that the dialect's filter computes `cascade 3 8 1` at `UInt64` on every input array, pass by pass.  Prove the definition at ℤ equal to the specification, as `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cascade_eq_outputs` states `cascade N R M xs = outputs N R M xs` for every list over ℤ and every value of the parameters.  Prove once that a map that preserves zero, addition, and subtraction commutes with the definition, as `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cascade_map` does by induction over each stage.

`UInt64.ofInt` preserves the three operations, so `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cascade_ofInt` follows from `cascade_map` and `cascade_eq_outputs`: the word cascade of the residues of an integer input is the list of residues of the outputs.  `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cascade_eq_map_ofInt` states the same for any input words through their signed readings, and `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.toInt_cascade` reads each output word as its exact output reduced by `Int.bmod` into [−2^63, 2^63).  A bound on the outputs alone then gives exactness: `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.toInt_cascade_of_bound` concludes that each output word reads as its exact output when the inputs lie in [−A, A'] with (R·M)^N·A ≤ 2^63 and (R·M)^N·A' < 2^63, which place the outputs in [−2^63, 2^63) by Hogenauer's bound `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.outputs_bound`, and `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.toInt_cascade_of_width` states the case of inputs of `B` bits under `(R·M)^N·2^(B−1) ≤ 2^63`.  The theorems place no bound on the integrators, whose words wrap, since each word is the residue of its value over ℤ (header of `Library/Numerics/Filters/Cic/Exact.lean`).

The method transfers only the operations that the map preserves.  Indices, counts, and parameters stay outside the element type and need no transfer, as `cascade` takes `N`, `R`, and `M` as natural numbers and the decimation selects elements by their indices.  A comparison, a division, or a right shift of a signed value does not commute with the reduction modulo 2^64, so a definition that uses one needs an argument of its own, such as the bounds that keep every word in range in [the entry on a machine over words against its specification](../word-machine-refinement/README.md).

The worked example is the CIC filter.  Its table's samples lie in [−2^15, 2^15), and (8·1)^3·2^15 = 2^24 ≤ 2^63, so the premise of `toInt_cascade_of_width` holds for the program's parameters (header of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Cic.lean`).  `TestSupport.A64.Cic.check_toInt` applies `toInt_cascade_of_width` after `cic_toList` at the program's table, whose range premise `decide +kernel` checks, and [the entry on AArch64 Image theorems](../a64-image-theorem/README.md) joins it with the Image's output.
