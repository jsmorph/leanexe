# Laws of random variables from moment-generating functions

The entry applies when a theorem must identify the law of a real random variable with a named distribution, such as the chi-square law of a quadratic form of a Gaussian vector, so that a program's enclosure of a distribution function or a tail becomes a probability under the request's model.  Mathlib at the pinned revision defines the Gaussian laws `ProbabilityTheory.gaussianReal` and `ProbabilityTheory.multivariateGaussian` and the Gamma law `ProbabilityTheory.gammaMeasure`, and `Demos.Gate.Model.chiSquare k` is the Gamma law of shape `k/2` and rate `1/2`.  Mathlib has no density of the standard Gaussian on a Euclidean space and no theorem that equal moment-generating functions give equal laws: `ProbabilityTheory.eqOn_complexMGF_of_mgf` needs the moment-generating functions equal on all of `ℝ`, and the module documentation of `Mathlib/Probability/Moments/ComplexMGF.lean` lists that uniqueness as a result still to prove.

Prove the equality of two probability measures on `ℝ` from their moment-generating functions on a half-line `(-∞, c)` with `c > 0`, as `Demos.Gate.Model.ext_of_mgf_Iio` does.  `ProbabilityTheory.analyticOnNhd_complexMGF` makes `complexMGF id μ` analytic on the half-plane `re z < c` once `exp (t * x)` is integrable for every `t < c`, and `convex_halfSpace_re_lt` makes the half-plane preconnected.  `AnalyticOnNhd.eqOn_of_preconnected_of_frequently_eq` then extends the equality at the real points `-1/(n + 1)`, which `ProbabilityTheory.complexMGF_ofReal` reduces to the real integrals, to the whole half-plane.  The imaginary axis lies in the half-plane, `ProbabilityTheory.complexMGF_id_mul_I` turns its points into values of the characteristic functions, and `MeasureTheory.Measure.ext_of_charFun` concludes.  Each side needs only the closed forms of its integrals for `t < c`: the Bochner integral of a function that is not integrable is 0 (`MeasureTheory.integral_undef`), so `MeasureTheory.Integrable.of_integral_ne_zero` derives the integrability from a positive closed form.  The Gamma side follows from `integral_withDensity_eq_integral_toReal_smul` and `Real.integral_rpow_mul_exp_neg_mul_Ioi` (`Demos.Gate.Model.integral_exp_mul_gamma`), the square of a standard normal variable from `ProbabilityTheory.integral_gaussianReal_eq_integral_smul` and `integral_gaussian` (`Demos.Gate.Model.integral_exp_mul_sq`), and a sum over independent coordinates from `MeasureTheory.integral_fintype_prod_eq_prod` under `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi`.

`ProbabilityTheory.multivariateGaussian` with mean `μ` and covariance `S` is by definition the image of the standard Gaussian under `x ↦ μ + √S x`, with `√S = CFC.sqrt S`, and `ProbabilityTheory.map_pi_eq_stdGaussian` gives the standard Gaussian on `EuclideanSpace ℝ ι` as the image of the product of `gaussianReal 0 1` under `WithLp.toLp`.  Two applications of `MeasureTheory.Measure.map_map` therefore carry a function of the Gaussian vector to a function on the product of independent standard normal variables, as `Demos.Gate.Model.map_mahalanobis` does.  `CFC.sqrt_mul_sqrt_self` gives `√S √S = S`, and `CFC.sqrt_nonneg` with `Matrix.nonneg_iff_posSemidef` gives the symmetry of `√S`, from which `Demos.Gate.Model.mahalanobis_sqrt` shows that the squared Mahalanobis distance of `√S z` is `Σ z_i²`.  `MeasureTheory.map_measureReal_apply` turns the equality of laws into the probability of an event, as `Demos.Gate.Model.gaussian_tail` does.

The notation `*ᵥ` and the order of matrices that `CFC.sqrt` needs are scoped.  Without `open scoped Matrix`, a statement with `S *ᵥ x` fails with "elaboration function for Mathlib.Tactic.subscriptTerm has not been implemented", and without `open scoped MatrixOrder`, `CFC.sqrt S` of a real matrix fails with `failed to synthesize PartialOrder (Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ)`.  The proof of `Demos.Gate.Model.mahalanobis_sqrt` checks under these two scopes without the scope of the operator norm, Matrix.Norms.L2Operator, which `Demos/Gate/Gaussian.lean` opens as Mathlib's file of the multivariate Gaussian does.  The statements of `Demos.Gate.Model.gaussian_tail` and `Demos.Gate.gate_miss` need neither scope, because `ProbabilityTheory.multivariateGaussian` fixes its instances in its definition, and the review restated and proved `Demos.Gate.Model.gaussian_tail` in a file that opens neither (finding 1 of the section "Review of the Gaussian link" of `Demos/Gate/review.md`).  A rewrite with `integral_withDensity_eq_integral_toReal_smul` whose measurability argument is `(measurable_gammaPDFReal a r).ennreal_ofReal` fails with "Did not find an occurrence of the pattern", because that argument's type fixes the density as the lambda `fun x => ENNReal.ofReal (gammaPDFReal a r x)` while the goal holds `gammaPDF a r`, so `Demos.Gate.Model.integral_exp_mul_gamma` names the density, `(f := gammaPDF a r)`.  For the chi-square law, Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.eqOn_complexMGF_of_mgf` would need, beside the integrals for `t < 1/2`, proofs that neither integrand is integrable for `t ≥ 1/2`.  A route through characteristic functions needs the Gamma integral with a complex rate, while `Real.integral_rpow_mul_exp_neg_mul_Ioi` has a real rate, and a route through the density of the standard Gaussian, polar coordinates, and the volume of the ball needs the density that Mathlib lacks.  The decision "Route of the Gaussian link" of `Demos/Gate/review.md` compares these routes, and [the entry on requirements for a safety theorem](../requirements-for-theorems/README.md) applies the comparison to requirements that fix a proof's route.

The worked examples are `Demos.Gate.Model.map_sumSq`, which identifies the law of a sum of squares of independent standard normal variables with the chi-square law, `Demos.Gate.Model.map_mahalanobis`, `Demos.Gate.Model.gaussian_chiSquare`, and `Demos.Gate.Model.gaussian_tail` for the squared Mahalanobis distance under `ProbabilityTheory.multivariateGaussian`, and `Demos.Gate.gate_miss`, which joins them with the program's soundness theorem through the positive definiteness that the program proves.  [The entry on derivatives, inverse functions, and differential equations of real models](../real-model-calculus/README.md) covers the calculus of `Demos/Gate/Model.lean`, which gives the chi-square law's distribution function in closed form.
