# Array literals, conditions on propositions, and structure patterns

A definition may return an array literal with elements, test a conjunction or disjunction of propositions, or match a structure with patterns that fix fields to constructors of enumerations, and the verified reflector reads these forms, so such a definition compiles unchanged.  The reading below decides the generated code and its bound.  Negation, implication, and equivalence of propositions still stop the reflector.

The reflector reads `#[a₁, …, aₙ]` as `((#[].push a₁).push …).push aₙ`, and the kernel checks the reading by unfolding `Array.push`.  The bound charges the empty block, `Verified.blockCost 0` or 56 bytes, the first push, `Verified.growCost 1 1` or 96 bytes, and each later push, `Verified.paidGrowCost 1` or 80 bytes, so a literal of two words costs 232 bytes and a literal of one word 152.  When nothing else allocates, `rw [compiled.f.bound_eq]; decide` closes the bound, as `Verified.Examples.Forms.pair_bound` and `Examples.Lookup.compute_bound` do, and `Verified.growCost_eq` and `Verified.paidGrowCost_eq` give the linear forms otherwise.  Wasmtime's counters show two allocations for a literal of one element and three for two or three elements, with one block live afterwards, and the first cases of the forms example, which assumed one allocation for a one-element literal, failed on that point.

`if p ∧ q` and `if p ∨ q` become tests of `decide (p ∧ q)` and `decide (p ∨ q)` by `Verified.Reflect.ite_decide_eq`, and the reflector reads those as `decide p && decide q` and `decide p || decide q` by `Verified.Reflect.decide_and_eq` and `decide_or_eq`.  Each part must be a comparison, an equation of `Bool`s or enumerations, or another conjunction or disjunction.  The rule holds in ordinary code, in builds, in loops, and in tail loops, whose conditions the reflector reads through `decide`, and the WGSL translation lowers the connectives, as `Examples.Binary32.condMix32_dispatch` shows.  The code evaluates both parts, as it does for `&&` and `||`, so a guard `i < n ∧ f i < k` calls `f i` when `i < n` fails, and a nested `if` keeps the call lazy.

`if ¬p`, implication, and equivalence stop with `unsupported condition` or `unsupported decision`.  When the definition may change, write `!decide p` or the negated comparison.  A port that must keep such a definition unchanged needs another reflector rule, which is a change to the compiler, and the worked examples of literals and connectives are the forms example in `Verified/Examples/Forms.lean`, `Examples.Lookup.lookup_call`, `Examples.Binary32.condMix32_dispatch`, and `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.PrimeFactors.primeFactors_call`.

A `match` with several alternatives on a structure with a `Flat` instance may fix fields to constructors of enumerations, as `Examples.Calc.Calc.undo` does with `{ value, steps, last := .add }`, `{ value, steps, last := .sub }`, and `_`.  The reflector reads it as `if` tests of the fixed fields' words in the order of the alternatives, with the tests of one pattern joined with `&&`, the last alternative untested, and the projections of the value for the bound fields.  `Verified.Reflect.structAlts` reads the patterns, and `Verified.Reflect.structMatchChain` builds the proof that the chain equals the match by cases on the structure and on each fixed field.  The kernel checks that proof inside the function's `compiled.f.denote_eq`, with one case for each combination of the fixed fields' constructors.  A pattern that fixes a field to a literal, a `Bool`, or a nested structure stops with ``verified_compile: a pattern fixes the field … to …, which is not a constructor of an enumeration with a `Flat` instance to words``, and a `match` on a `Bool` stops with `unsupported term`.  Lean's `UInt64` is a structure without a `Flat` instance, so a `match` with several alternatives on a `UInt64`, such as `match w with | 0 => … | _ => …`, does not reach the rule and stops with ``verified_compile: unsupported term match w with …``.  When the definition may change, test such a field with `if`, or match a nested structure in a nested `match`.  An `if` on `w = 0` compiles in place of the match on words.  The worked examples are `Examples.Calc.compiled.undo.implements` and `Verified.Examples.Patterns.compiled.bytes`, whose program fixes one field or two, fix every field, bind the value, cover every constructor without a wildcard, and match on a call, in a loop's body, and on a record with an array field.
