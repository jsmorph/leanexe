# Closed computations

Many steps of a program proof are closed facts: a table entry, a constant of a standard, the address of a table in the initial store, an export of a module, or the bytes of an emitted file.  `decide +kernel` proves such a fact by having the kernel evaluate it.  The elaborator's plain `decide` often stops at its recursion limit on the same goals.

Do not use `native_decide` or `bv_decide`.  Both add an axiom that trusts compiled code, and the library's theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.  The SHA-512/t initial values of the copied FIPS 180-4 transcription use `decide +kernel`, which takes 40 seconds.

When the kernel's evaluation reaches the recursion limit, raise the limit for one declaration with `set_option maxRecDepth N in`.  `Library.Hashing.Crc.crc32c_table` uses 10,000, because the address of the second CRC table depends on the size of the first.  The `file_theorems` command, which `LeanExe.Pipeline.elabFileTheorems` elaborates, sets 100,000 for both theorems that it states, because the bytes they compare nest as deep as the file is long.  `whnf`, `Nat.reducePow`, and `Int.reducePow` do not evaluate a power whose exponent exceeds `exponentiation.threshold`, 256 by default, and log a warning instead (the option's description and `checkExponent` in Lean's `Lean/Util/SafeExponentiation.lean`), and `whnf` can then unfold the power by its definition, so `rfl` or `simp` on a goal that holds `2 ^ 2098` reaches the maximum recursion depth.  A proof that needs such a power evaluated raises the option for its own declaration, as `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/FloatToInt.lean` raises it to 4,096 for the masks of 2,048 bits whose powers of 2 its walks evaluate and `mag_inf_eq` of `tools/sail/F7/FPCompare.lean` raises it to 2,100 to evaluate `2 ^ 2098`.  A raised threshold applies to every power that unification meets: a threshold of 4,096 set for the whole of `Library/Numerics/Float64/Trig/TrigLarge.lean` made unification evaluate `2 ^ 1278` and reach the recursion limit, which the default threshold avoids, and this remedy is uncertain.  A tactic can also evaluate a power that the proof needs to keep: in `Library.Numerics.Float64.Sinpi.SinpiTinySplit.split_spec`, `push_cast` and `field_simp` evaluated `2^1074` into a 324-digit numeral and exhausted 200,000 heartbeats, so its proof splits into `tail_le` and `tail_grid` and rewrites with `Nat.cast_mul`, `Nat.cast_pow`, and `mul_div_assoc`, which keep the power as a power (section "Proof structure" of `Library/Numerics/Float64/Sinpi/README.md`).

The kernel keeps the result of every reduction until it finishes checking a declaration, so the memory of a check grows with the work of its evaluation, as the section "Encoding and the image's bytes" of `docs/a64-bare-metal.md` describes.  A check that exhausts memory as one declaration can therefore pass as several declarations, each of which evaluates a part, and a lemma that joins their results.  `decide +kernel` adds its proof as an auxiliary theorem, which the kernel checks with caches of its own, so `refine ⟨?_, …, ?_⟩ <;> decide +kernel` holds the terms of one conjunct at a time, as the tactic `inst_ok` of `LeanExe/Native/ClightN/Instance.lean` does for the conditions of an instance of N (its docstring), at the price of computing again for each conjunct what the conjuncts share.  The kernel's check of the Cortex-M7 gateway's image does not fit in 16 GiB as one declaration and passes in parts (header of `LeanExe/M7/Parts.lean`), and [the entry on image files](../image-file-checks/README.md) gives the parts and their join lemmas.  [The entry on kernel checks over ℚ](../kernel-rational-checks/README.md) covers the kernel's checks of rational bounds, and [the entry on kernel evaluation costs](../kernel-evaluation-costs/README.md) covers a check whose cost comes from a comparison that forces the kernel to evaluate a term.  [The entry on definitions that the kernel evaluates](../kernel-evaluated-definitions/README.md) covers a check whose cost comes from the form of the definition that the kernel evaluates.  An equality of two compiled modules is a closed fact whose check evaluates both compilations, and a `decide +kernel` on the equality of two modules' encodings exhausted its job's memory.  Compare the emitted files instead, as `tests/verified/run.sh --check-bytes` compares the emitted modules with saved reference modules byte for byte (the section "Types" of `docs/verified-manual.md`).

The limit of heartbeats applies to a declaration as a whole, so the evaluations of every `decide +kernel` in the `have`s and the arguments of one theorem count toward one budget.  Closed facts about a large compiled module, such as the position of its allocator, its cap, its stack bounds, and `LeanExe.A64.bytesEntryOk`, therefore go in theorems of their own, which the main theorem takes as arguments.  As `have`s of `TestSupport.A64.RcvRuns.rcv_runs`, the facts about the tabulator's module exceed the default budget, while `alloc_eq`, `cap_le`, `stack_alloc`, `stack_tabulate`, and `entry_ok` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/RcvRuns.lean` each check within it.  `alloc_eq` states that function 0 of the module is the allocator by `rfl`, since `Wasm.Function` derives no `DecidableEq` and `decide` finds no instance for the equation.  `decide` also reports that it failed to synthesize `Decidable` for an equation of a product or a list whose components are abbreviations of nested products, such as `(1, [w]) = (1, [w])` with `w` of a type `W6` that abbreviates a product of six `UInt64`, although `DecidableEq W6` resolves alone, because the instance's search exceeds `synthInstance.maxSize`.  Instances declared by name for the abbreviations, `instance : DecidableEq W6 := inferInstance` and its siblings for the lists and the state, as in `clightn/lean/Euler.lean`, let the search succeed, and so does a raised `synthInstance.maxSize` for the declaration, as [the entry on one source for a real formula and its interval program](../enclosure-def/README.md) sets it for large tuples.

A goal that `decide +kernel` takes minutes to check usually states more than it needs.  Check constants as whole words rather than bit by bit: each SHA-3 round lemma, such as `Library.Hashing.Sha3.RoundProof.round_0`, checks its round constant as one list of 64 bits, after the elaborator's `decide` reached its recursion limit from round 10 on.  Prove a table's entries against a short definition once, rather than at every use: `Library.Hashing.Crc.Proof.crc32Table_of` unfolds `TableOf` and `Holds` and checks all 256 entries against eight bitwise steps of `lfsr`.  A fact over a bounded range of a natural number can take the form `∀ E, E < N → … = true`, which `decide +kernel` checks over the whole range, as `Library.Numerics.Decimal.PrintFloat.PrintFloatSteps.wide_binary64` does for 2,048 binades.  An export's membership in a module's export list is another closed fact, as `Library.Hashing.XxHash64.xxh64_export` shows.  So is a theorem that a property fails, which states a counterexample: `Demos.Rcv.Properties.monotonicity_fails` proves the constraints, the winners, and the eliminations of two contests of 17 ballots each by `decide +kernel`, one conjunct at a time, and gives the witnesses of its existential explicitly, with the equality of the two contests' structures by `rfl`.

A bound on a quantity over every run of a function is no closed fact, since it quantifies over the run's inputs: `LeanExe.M7.costRun` counts the instructions of a run by well-founded recursion on a fuel (`LeanExe/M7/Cost.lean`), and its bound must hold at every fuel, store, and argument list.  Compute the bound with a definition by structural recursion over the code, which the kernel evaluates, and prove once that it bounds the quantity.  `LeanExe.M7.staticCost` computes a bound on a function's instructions over its WebAssembly code in the pattern of `LeanExe.CallGraph.stackNeedOf` (header of `LeanExe/M7/Bound.lean`), `LeanExe.M7.costRun_le_staticCost` states that it bounds `costRun` at all of them, and `TestSupport.M7.echo_static` evaluates it for the echo's steps by `decide +kernel`.

When a closed fact concerns a compiled module, ensure that the kernel does not reduce the module as one term.  `compiled.initial` states the initial store of `Verified.compileWith` at the default literal pool, and given directly where `Verified.Ready compiled.module compiled.tables` is expected, it reaches the recursion limit for the modules of CRC and of the exponential.  `Verified.Ready.initial` takes the pool's equation as a premise, so that Lean matches `compiled.module` by its definition, and `Verified.Ready.initial rfl compiled.initial` checks without a raised limit, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.ExpTheorem.exp_bytes_accuracy` shows.  `compileWith` takes the pool as an optional argument and Lean otherwise inserts the default, so a rewrite that should use a supplied pool must name it, since rewrites that left it implicit selected the default pool.  [The entry on large folds and compiled terms](../large-term-unfolding/README.md) gives the related rule for rewriting a store before a kernel check.

`Verified.Prog.Covered prog pool` states that the pool holds every byte literal of the program, and it has no decidability instance.  For a program without literals, prove `prog.literals = []` by `decide +kernel` and close the coverage with `simp only [Prog.Covered, noLiterals, List.nil_subset]`, as `Verified.Examples.CompositionBase.add_pure` does.  The equation on `literals` is the closed computation, and the subset step needs no evaluation.
