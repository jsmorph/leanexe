# Corollaries over the layer events of a WASI executable

The entry applies when a WASI command has a theorem over its WASI events, and its Linux executable needs a corollary that states the request over the layer events of the shim, the calls that Linux answered.  The executable's theorem holds the layer events of a run of `main` and its exit under `Verified.Shim.linuxHost`, which [the entry on WASI hosts built on a lower layer](../wasi-host-from-layer/README.md) describes together with readAll's corollary.  The random histogram and the random choice add to that form the exclusion of trapped runs, the shape of the layer events, a statement that mirrors the command's specification, and a witness.

When the command's charge does not depend on the replies, a budget excludes the guest's trap for every reply sequence, as `Demos.RandomHistogram.linux_footprint_bounded` does, and the condition on the replies can be a predicate on the layer events of the finished run.  `Demos.RandomHistogram.InRange` states that the result of every `getrandom` and `write` lies in the range that Linux gives for its call and that the shim accepts: an error number or a count of at most the call's length, positive for a positive length for `getrandom` (`LeanExe.Linux.RandomCount`).  It excludes exactly the results at which the shim traps: `Demos.RandomHistogram.random_inRange` and `Demos.RandomHistogram.write_inRange` state that the image of a call that returned meets it, and `Demos.RandomHistogram.image_untrapped`, below, gives the converse.  A condition on single results that admits more therefore does not give the corollary, and one that admits less, such as a full count from every `getrandom`, leaves out runs that Linux gives.  `Demos.RandomHistogram.linux_exec_counts` proves `Demos.RandomHistogram.Counts` for every run whose layer events meet it and `Demos.RandomHistogram.Sized`, a run that ends at the accepted exit.  The trapped runs need excluding without a class of reply sequences: `Demos.RandomHistogram.main_trapped` shows by the structure of `main` that every trapped run has a trapped call among `args_sizes_get`, `args_get`, `random_get`, and the write, and `Demos.RandomHistogram.image_untrapped` shows that no layer events that meet `InRange` are the image of such a call.  A lemma over every action would also have to exclude a trapped exit inside `main`, which needs a predicate that `main` makes no exit, and the lemma over the structure of `main` needs none.  The file theorem `histogram_elf_counts` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesRandomHistogram.lean` concludes `InRange ls → Counts args ls (q.core.x 0)` for every reply sequence.

A corollary that states only the bytes that the calls returned and wrote admits extra calls, other sizes of calls, and other flags.  A WASI call that the shim answers with a loop of Linux calls has a group of layer events for its image.  The shim answers `random_get` with `getrandom` calls, each for the bytes that the calls before it did not return, at the address after them, until a call returns the bytes that remain or an error number, and it traps at a count of 0 or above the bytes asked for.  `Demos.RandomHistogram.RandomGroup` states the group of one `random_get` in those terms, and `Demos.RandomHistogram.random_group` proves it of every image under `LeanExe.Linux.RandomCount`, with the call's bytes `Demos.RandomHistogram.randomData g`, the concatenation of the calls' outputs, each cut at its count.  `Demos.RandomHistogram.RandomMirrors` ties a `random_get` to its group, its errno, and, for errno 0, its bytes.  `Counts` then states the draws as groups that mirror, one for one, the calls of the WASI specification's `Demos.RandomHistogram.Draws`, which fixes each call at `8 · min r 512` bytes, followed by writes of descriptor 1, each of which offered the bytes of the output that the writes before it did not take (`Demos.RandomHistogram.OfferedLayer`).  A statement of groups of 1 to 4,096 bytes admits programs that draw one word a call or that add a draw of fewer than 8 bytes (`Demos/RandomHistogram/review.md`).  The image relation leaves the length of an event's output free, so a short output before the last call would make the shim's buffer differ from the concatenation, and the proof needs `Demos.RandomHistogram.Sized`, that each `getrandom` holds as many output bytes as it asked for.  Every run gives it from `Verified.Shim.Carried` (`Demos.RandomHistogram.carried_sized`), so the file theorem keeps `InRange` as its only condition on the replies, as the manifest checker's `Demos.ManifestCheck.Carried.sized` does for reads.  The conditions of `InRange` hold of Linux's replies for the command's calls, by the facts that [the entry on hardware facts](../hardware-facts/README.md) cites.

A command whose specification over WASI events is an inductive relation of passes, such as `Demos.RandomChoice.Rounds` in `Demos.RandomChoice.Meets`, keeps that relation for the layer events.  Restating the passes over layer events would copy the inductive and every lemma about it.  `Demos.RandomChoice.Mirrors x g` instead states, for each kind of WASI event that the command makes, the layer events that the shim makes for it: the group of a `random_get` (`Demos.RandomHistogram.RandomMirrors`) or one `write`, and it is `False` for every other kind.  `Demos.RandomChoice.Choices` then states `ls = groups.flatten` and `List.Forall₂ Mirrors events groups` together with `Rounds` over random bytes that `Mirrors` ties to the groups of the successful calls, since a failed group may hold bytes of short reads that `Rounds` does not count, so the layer events are the passes of the command's own specification.  `Demos.RandomChoice.rounds_mirrors` proves it by induction on `Rounds`, with one lemma for each kind of event, `Demos.RandomChoice.random_mirror` and `Demos.RandomChoice.write_mirror`, and the output and cap facts of `Meets` transfer without a new proof, since `Verified.Shim.Images.writeData` equates the bytes that the layer's writes took with the WASI trace's.  The reviewer's model found that the clauses of `Choices` on status 0 and on the cap reject no run that the `Mirrors` and `Rounds` clause accepts, so they restate its consequences in the terms that `Demos.RandomChoice.output_count` takes.

A corollary of the form `∃ q ls, … ∧ (InRange ls → Counts …)` holds vacuously if no process meets its premises with layer events that meet `InRange`, so a witness shows that the premises hold together, as [the entry on satisfiable hypotheses and complete conclusions](../non-vacuous-statements/README.md) requires.  `Demos.RandomHistogram.short_run` follows the action under one reply sequence with a short read, `Demos.RandomHistogram.shortReplies`, which answers the `getrandom` of 8 bytes with 4 and the `getrandom` of the other 4 with them (`Demos.RandomHistogram.random_short`), without evaluating a run of the machine, and it concludes `Demos.RandomHistogram.ShortRead`, so the witness exercises a short read.  The replies are indexed by the call's position, so `Verified.Shim.Carried` gives each event's result once the proof knows the events before it, and `Demos.RandomHistogram.carried_split` splits `Carried` over an append.  `histogram_elf_witness` of the same file reuses the footprint lemma of the corollary, `histogram_elf_runs`, with a memory that holds the file's segments as `LeanExe.A64.ElfLoaded` reads them and an initial stack whose bytes `decide +kernel` checks.  `cases` on a `Verified.Shim.Images` whose index is `[] ++ []` fails by dependent elimination, which `Demos.RandomHistogram.images_nil` with a `show` avoids, and `congr 1` on `byteAt` of the file's number recurses into the literal, which `congrArg (byteAt histogramElfBytes)` avoids.  `Demos.RandomChoice.witness_run` and `choice_elf_witness` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesRandomChoice.lean` give the random choice's witness in the same way.  `tests/TestSupport/A64/ElfWitness.lean` holds what the witnesses of the histogram, the random choice, and the manifest checker share: the loader's memory `TestSupport.A64.ElfWitness.loadedMem`, which meets `LeanExe.A64.ElfLoaded` by `TestSupport.A64.ElfWitness.loadedMem_loaded`, the transfer of an initial stack to it, `TestSupport.A64.ElfWitness.loadedMem_initial`, and the initial stack of the two one-byte arguments `h` and `1`, which `TestSupport.A64.ElfWitness.loadedMem_stack` places.  A witness whose arguments are `h` and `1`, such as `manifest_elf_witness` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesManifestCheck.lean`, takes the memory and the stack from the module, and a witness with other arguments proves its own stack over `TestSupport.A64.ElfWitness.stackMem` by `decide` and takes the rest, as `witness_stack` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesRandomChoice.lean` does for the random choice's three arguments.

The worked examples are `Demos.RandomHistogram.linux_exec_counts`, `Demos.RandomChoice.linux_exec_choices`, and the file theorems `histogram_elf_counts` and `choice_elf_choices`, which `tests/linux/a64-wasi.sh` elaborates beside the executables.
