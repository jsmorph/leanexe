# Readers of line-oriented files

The entry applies to a reader of a text file whose specification splits a list of bytes at a separator with `List.splitOn` and parses the pieces in order.  It covers two goals: that a program in the dialect, which scans a `LeanExe.Bytes` buffer for separators by position, equals the specification on the buffer's bytes, and that the specification's error, which the reader reports at the first line that fails, equals a separate definition of the error by line number.  The ranked-choice reader of canonical ballot files has both theorems.

For the program, read the bytes from position `s` to position `e` as one list, `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.slice`, and prove once that the position that the scan finds splits that list as `List.splitOn` does.  `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.findByte_spec` states that `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.findByte` returns the first position of the byte, or `e`, and `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.findByte_splitOn` and `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.findByte_split` turn it into the first piece and the rest of the split of the slice, through `List.splitOn_append_cons_self_of_not_mem` and `List.splitOn_eq_singleton`.  `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.piecesFrom` gives the pieces of the file from a position on, and each loop of the program over lines or fields then follows the specification's recursion over pieces, as `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.readLine_pieces` does for one line.  `List.splitOn` is `List.splitOnPPrepend` with an accumulator that holds the piece read so far, so an induction that unfolds it against a loop of the program must state that accumulator at every step, which the lemma about a slice avoids.

For the error, state the pieces that the reader passes to line `j` as the pieces of the file from piece `j - 1` on, `Demos.Rcv.Spec.piecesAt`, and prove that the read of a line from them depends on line `j` alone, `Demos.Rcv.Spec.lineOf`, as `Demos.Rcv.Spec.takeLine_drop` and `Demos.Rcv.Spec.readLine_drop` do.  Each loop over lines then gets a lemma with two cases, as `Demos.Rcv.Spec.readCandidates_drop` shows: when the loop succeeds, every line that it read meets its role and the rest is `piecesAt` of the next line, and when it fails, the error's line lies in the loop's range, every earlier line meets its role, and the error's line fails it.  The main proof takes the header's lines one at a time, by cases on each line and each parse, and carries the facts that the earlier lines are not faulty in the sense of `Demos.Rcv.Spec.faulty`.

The worked examples are `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.readContest_eq`, which uses the lemmas on the lines of the file, `Demos.Rcv.ReadProgram.fieldsFrom_spec`, which uses them on the comma-separated fields of a ballot line, and `Demos.Rcv.Spec.read_error_first`, which proves T2(c) of the ranked-choice requirements.  The rewrites that leave a `match` unreduced in these proofs appear in [the entry on tactics whose failures mislead](../tactic-combinator-pitfalls/README.md), and [the entry on decimal text](../decimal-conversions/README.md) covers the parsers of numbers within a line.
