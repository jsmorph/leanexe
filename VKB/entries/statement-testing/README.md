# Testing a statement before its proof

The entry applies before the proof of a theorem whose statement is new: a safety theorem over a machine's history, a loop invariant, a count of the inputs that give each result, or an error figure.  A proof of a false statement ends at a goal that no lemma closes, after the lemmas that lead to it are written.  An executable model decides the statement on many inputs in little time, so test the statement on a model before the proof starts.

Build the model from the requirements or the request without the Lean specification, and evaluate the statement on it: on every input at reduced word sizes or small parameters, on random runs, and, for an invariant, on every state that the model reaches.  For a statement over a machine's history, an agent other than the specification's author builds the model from the requirements' transition rules, so that the model does not share the author's reading of them.  The review of the BMS requirements tested M1 on random runs of such a machine, and its follow-up found the statement of `Demos.Bms.Spec.Permitted` true on 10,964 answers with a nonzero permit, 879 of them after several starts (sections "Review" and "Follow-up review" of [the BMS review](../../../Demos/Bms/review.md)).  One review of Dijkstra's specification checked `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.RelaxInv` and `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.MainInv` on 3,839,201 states of its model (`Library/Graphs/Dijkstra/review.md`).  The reviews of the sampling programs checked the bounds `fewest` and `most` and those of Algorithms S and R exhaustively at small word sizes, where the bounds are reached, and gave the error figures that the specification states, such as `N(N - 1) / 2^65` for Algorithm S (`Library/Random/Sampling/review.md`).

`#guard` evaluates a term when its module builds, so it checks one part of a specification against another before any proof.  `Demos/Bms/Cases.lean` runs `Demos.Bms.Cases.mixed`, a run through every state, and requires each history term to equal the machine's field and the reset acceptances and starts to fall at the expected segments, which catches a wrong history definition before a proof attempt, as [the entry on safety theorems over a machine's history](../history-safety-theorems/README.md) describes.  The same module checks the requirements' test vectors and the expected answers of the fault-injection schedule, which the requirements state apart from the machine.

A test decides the statement only on the inputs that it reaches, and the theorem quantifies over every input, so a test can show a statement false and cannot show it true.  The tests therefore precede the proof and never replace it, and the sampling reviews record that the distribution of the results, which no case can test, rests on the proofs alone.  A proof can also find what the tests missed: the first program of the frames demo's `timeScales` tested `d + 1 < E` on words, which accepts `d = 2⁶⁴ − 1` because `d + 1` wraps to 0, and the proof of `Demos.Frames.timeScales_sound`, whose statement says `d.toNat + 1 < E.toNat`, failed on that wraparound before the review reported it, after which the program tests `d < E − 1` (finding 8 of `Demos/Frames/review.md`).  A model that judges the program's runs, such as the BMS's Python machine, is cross-checked against the specification as [the entry on executable mirrors of a specification](../mirror-cross-check/README.md) describes, and a model's verdicts count once it has rejected a statement known to be false, as [the entry on checks that can fail](../discriminating-checks/README.md) describes.

A statement over every function with stated properties needs inputs that range over that class of functions, and the program's own function is one member.  `Demos.Rcv.Batch.run_batch_winner` states T10(a) of the ranked-choice requirements for every interpretation of ballots that satisfies `Demos.Rcv.Stability.Closed` and `Demos.Rcv.Stability.Stable`, and `rcv-stability general` drew 200,000 random interpretations, each ballot with an order of preference over some candidates and a reason, a form that every interpretation with both properties takes (section "Stability, batches, and the county count" of `Demos/Rcv/README.md`).  An interpretation outside the class, one that counts a ballot for a candidate outside the set, made T10(a) fail on 1,216 cases, which shows that the test reaches the premise.

A test counts once it has rejected false variants of each statement, as [the entry on checks that can fail](../discriminating-checks/README.md) requires of every check.  Variants of the right shape include the statement with a weakened premise or a changed constant, a property that the procedure is known to fail, and the conclusion of a sibling module's theorem.  The tests of the ranked-choice properties rejected T12 with half of the continuing ballots in place of more than half, and the Condorcet winner criterion and monotonicity, which instant runoff does not meet.  The Maine count's T12 carries over from the Oakland count with a changed conclusion, and the Oakland conclusion that the count ends after round 1 failed for 80,946 holders of a majority.  `Demos/Rcv/README.md` and `Demos/Rcv/Maine/README.md` record each variant and the number of cases on which it failed.

Keep each statement test as an executable of `lakefile.lean` whose modes read input files or draw seeded random inputs, print the number of cases that each statement covered, and exit with status 1 when a statement fails, as `rcv-rounds`, `rcv-stability`, `rcv-properties`, `rcv-maine`, and `rcv-maine-properties` do.  A module that evaluates the statements with `#eval` during its elaboration and reads files has no build target, so a change to a definition that it reads goes unnoticed until someone runs it by hand.  Each of these executables declares `main` at the root of its module, and two modules that declare a root `main` cannot be imported together, so each test is a module of its own.  Where the theorem's proposition is decidable, evaluate that proposition rather than a Boolean restatement, which can differ from it: `Demos/Rcv/Maine/Native.lean` derives `Decidable` instances for `Demos.Rcv.Maine.Eliminates` and `Demos.Rcv.Maine.Concludes`, whose quantifiers range over lists, by `infer_instance` and decides them, while `Demos/Rcv/RoundsNative.lean` tests a function `eliminates` that restates T4's description by hand.

A form of M1 whose item 4 forces `j` to the last start, and whose item 5 then requires `STANDBY` through a `PRECHARGE`, fails for a run with two starts after one reset acceptance: sync, reset, `RUN` 1, `RUN` 0, `RUN` 1.  Random runs of the reviewer's machine find that run (finding 1 of the BMS review), while a proof attempt would end at the unprovable case.  `Demos.Bms.Spec.Permitted` takes `i` and `j` as the last reset acceptance and the last start, requires `STANDBY` only of `a_i` and `a_(j-1)`, and excludes `RESET_REQUIRED` and `FAULT` from `a_i` to `a_n`.

The worked examples are `Demos.Bms.Spec.m1` with the checks of `Demos/Bms/Cases.lean`, `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.dijkstra_spec` with its invariants `RelaxInv` and `MainInv`, and `Library.Random.Sampling.shuffle_count`, whose bounds the reviews checked exhaustively at small word sizes.  The `review.md` files of `Library/Graphs/Dijkstra` and `Library/Random/Sampling` record each model and the inputs or states that it covered.  [The entry on independent review](../independent-review/README.md) describes the reviews that build and run these models.
