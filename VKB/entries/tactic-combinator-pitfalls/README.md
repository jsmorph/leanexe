# Tactics whose failures mislead

The entry applies when a proof built from tactic combinators fails at a step other than the faulty one, or does not fail where it should: a `first` that took an alternative which did not finish, a discharger that closed a goal with `sorry`, a `try` that did not stop a recursion-depth error, a `split` or `ite_self` that does not apply to an `if`, a `split` that does not apply to a `match` under `have`, a conditional `simp` lemma that does not fire, a rewrite or a destructuring whose result makes a later step fail, a rewrite that closes a goal with metavariables, a case split whose names fall on other hypotheses than intended, a `repeat' split` whose cases multiply, or a `tauto` whose search doubles with each premise in its context.  The symptom appears as unsolved goals at a later step, as an error that a discharger logs while the proof continues on a false branch, as exhausted memory, or as a message that names a cause other than the defect.  The combinators behave as Lean's source defines them, so each remedy below changes the form of the proof rather than the combinator.

`first | t₁ | t₂` takes the first alternative that succeeds, and an alternative succeeds when it makes progress, whether or not it closes the goal, so a partial result of `t₁` keeps `t₂` from running.  End each alternative that must close its goal with `done`: the walks of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/FloatToInt.lean` try `(apply Eq.symm; xreg_end; done)` before `fp_side`, and the last alternative of `div_sgn` in `tools/sail/F7/FPDiv.lean` ends a `simp only` with `done`.  `first`, which `Lean.Elab.Tactic.evalFirst` implements, joins its alternatives with `Lean.Elab.Tactic.orElse`, which runs its left side without error recovery, so every alternative but the last fails as a whole, while the last runs with error recovery and can log its error and close the goal with `sorry`.  A discharger whose last alternative may fail therefore ends in `fail`, as `cond_disch` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean` does, and [the entry on instruction forms against Isla](../isla-form-proofs/README.md) records the wrong branch that a walk took without it.  `try t` is `first | t | skip`, and neither catches the recursion-depth error or the heartbeat error, which Lean's `Lean.Core.tryCatch` rethrows as runtime exceptions (`Lean.Exception.isRuntime`), so a step that can exceed either limit needs a cheaper form before it, as `reg_end` compares values with `with_reducible rfl` before it compares them as natural numbers (the same entry).

`split` and `ite_self` rebuild an `if`'s `Decidable` instance, so they do not apply to an `if` whose instance is another term, such as the cost of a WASI effect body's status, whose instance still holds the body's environment.  State the step as a lemma over any instance, proved by cases on the instance or by `ite_eq_left` and `ite_eq_right` with the instance named.  Such a lemma names the instance as a variable, so `rw` and `refine` apply it whatever instance the goal holds.  `Demos.OrderBook.Process.ite_le_zero` bounds an `if` with any decision procedure, and `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.ite_bound` bounds an `if` of costs by an `if` of step results on the same condition with any two instances, which `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.step_le` applies down the trees of the step's cost and result with `refine`.

`split at h` also fails, with `Could not split an if or match expression`, on a `match` whose discriminant is bound by `have`, as unfolding `Demos.Rcv.Spec.rule` leaves its tied set `Z`.  Prove one evaluation lemma for each branch of such a definition, which unfolds the definition, rewrites the discriminants with the branch's hypotheses, and closes with `simp only [h0, ↓reduceIte]` and `rfl`, as `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_majority`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_zero`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_batch`, and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_lowest` do.  A proof then cases on each discriminant with `cases hx : e` and rewrites with the branch's lemma, as `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_order` does.

A `simp` lemma with a condition rewrites only where the discharger proves the condition, and otherwise leaves the term without a message.  Where the condition concerns a state that the proof builds step by step, state the step as an unconditional equation into an `if` on the condition, with an irreducible form in the other branch.  `F7.bind_fa` of `tools/sail/F7/Base.lean` rewrites the run of a bind over IsFullA64Enabled to an `if` whose first branch continues the run and whose second is `stuckRun`, `sail_simp` reduces the condition with the hypotheses that it is given, and a call from a state that it cannot show to satisfy the condition stays in the goal as `stuckRun` (its docstring).

Some steps succeed and leave a goal on which a later step fails.  `rw` fixes a lemma's implicit arguments at the first match in the goal and rewrites only the instances of that match, so `rw [ite_eq_left h]` reduces the first `if` on the condition and leaves an `if` on the same condition with other branches, while `simp only [h, ↓reduceIte]` reduces both, as in `Demos.Rcv.Refine.batchOf_spec`.  A `by` block among the lemma's arguments runs after that match, on the instance that it fixed, so `rw [List.filter_cons_of_pos (by …)]` must prove the condition of the first `filter` of a cons in the goal, and the arguments that select another occurrence must be given explicitly.  `rw [h] at hyp` with `h : lineOf b j = some l` leaves `match some l with …` unreduced in `hyp`, and a later `rw` with the parse of `l` finds no instance inside the `match`, while `simp only [h] at hyp` rewrites and reduces the `match`, as the proof of `Demos.Rcv.Spec.read_error_first` does for each header line.

`rw` ends with `with_reducible rfl` on the main goal, wherever it rewrites, so `rw [e] at h` inside a `by` block whose goal holds the unassigned implicit arguments of a lemma, such as `?a * 3 = ?b * 3`, closes that goal by assigning `?b := ?a`, and the lemma then states the trivial `a = a`.  Lean reports the unassigned metavariable or that the next tactic has no goals, so state the hypothesis with its type, `have hb : b • w = b' • w := by …`, before the lemma takes it.  `rw [← h]` with `h : n ^ 2 * a ^ 3 = μ` rewrites every occurrence of `μ`, those inside applications such as `semimajor μ r v` included, after which the lemmas about those applications no longer match.  Name such applications with `generalize` or `set` before the rewrite, or derive the equation that the step needs and rewrite with it, as `Demos.Orbit.lambert_newton` rewrites `conicAdvance_time` at the time `t` into `hadv` and uses `hadv` in place of the hypothesis on `t`.

`split_ifs` on a goal that still holds a program's `let` bindings, after `unfold`, leaves goals that `omega` does not close, and `dsimp only` before it removes the bindings.  The names of `split_ifs with h1 h2 …` go to the conditions in the order of the splits, those of an `if` inside a let-bound value included, so such an `if` shifts the names of the later hypotheses.  `Demos.Orbit.signOf` moves the selection of a sign out of the program's body into a definition of its own, and `Demos.Orbit.lambertBox_sound` applies `dsimp only`, generalizes the large interval terms of the unfolded program to variables, and then splits.  `cases h with | ctor a b …` on a hypothesis of an inductive family whose indices are local variables expects fewer names than the constructor has fields, `Too many variable names provided at alternative`, and a name that falls on a field that the unification of the indices removes names nothing, so a later `rename_i` or a later name meets other hypotheses than the proof expects.  `match h with | .ctor _ b _ hab hc => …` takes one pattern for each explicit field.  A pattern variable whose name resolves to several constants of the open namespaces, such as `Inv`, stops a `match` with `ambiguous pattern, use fully qualified name`, and a lower-case name avoids it.

Some messages name a cause other than the defect.  `decide` refuses a goal that mentions a local variable with `Expected type must not contain free variables`, even when the proposition reduces to a closed one, such as `¬({ found := 0, lo := x } : Br).found = 1`, and `absurd (show (0 : UInt64) = 1 from h) (by decide)` restates the hypothesis in closed form first.  A literal of `Float` and `(k : UInt64).toFloat` reduce in the kernel, so `absurd (congrArg Float.toBits h) (by decide +kernel)` refutes `h : (2.0 : Float) = 1.0`, and a read of an array literal that holds a computed value is reduced first with a lemma proved by `rfl`, such as `Demos.Screening.lit5_get0`.  `obtain ⟨R, hR, -⟩ := hu` clears `hu`, so a later use of `hu` fails as an unknown identifier, and `obtain … := id hu` keeps it, as `Demos.Conjunction.Model.radiusIn_of` does.  In a module that imports Mathlib, `not_le` and the other lemmas of Mathlib's linear orders do not apply to `UInt64`, and the error is a type mismatch between `@LT.lt ?α LinearOrder.toPartialOrder.toLT ?b ?a` and `@LT.lt UInt64 instLTUInt64 b a`, while `UInt64.not_le`, `UInt64.lt_iff_toNat_lt`, and `UInt64.le_iff_toNat_le` apply.  `Library.Numerics.Bounds.finite` is an abbreviation, so `simp` with `decide_eq_true_eq` turns `finite s = true` into a comparison of `Library.Numerics.Bounds.absBits`.  Field notation such as `h.cosh` on `h : HasDerivAt f f' x` in a module that does not import `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Trigonometric.DerivHyp` reports that the environment does not contain the field `cosh` of `HasFDerivAtFilter`, the unfolded type, where the cause is the missing import.  A line that continues a tactic of a `by` block inside a term must start to the right of that tactic's column, and a continuation to its left ends the block, with an unexpected identifier at the continuation, so a long tactic inside a term goes into a `have` of its own.

`simp only []` turns a component `a = a` of a conjunction into `True`, so a following anonymous constructor with `rfl` for that component fails and `trivial` fits it.  `subst h` with `h : a = b` between two variables eliminates `b`, so `Demos.Rcv.Properties.trace_not_loser` states `ℓ = i` to keep the name `ℓ` that its later steps use.  `obtain ⟨-, h0⟩ := h` on `h : ∃ hr : 0 < l.length, l[0] = 3`, whose second component depends on the first, clears the first component and `h0` with it, without an error, so a later use of `h0` fails as an unknown identifier.  `obtain ⟨_, h0⟩` keeps both, as `Demos.Rcv.Maine.locality` does with `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.head`.

`repeat' split` splits the branches of every copy of a term separately, so a statement that holds several copies of one term multiplies its cases.  A disjunction over copies of `frameTick f v`, split in this way with alternatives that tried `rfl` on terms that unfold into word arithmetic, exhausted the job's memory.  `LeanExe.Requests.TickCase` states the cases of one window's result on a single copy, and `LeanExe.Requests.frameTick_cases` proves it with `split` and an explicit `next` for each branch.

Mathlib's `tauto` turns each hypothesis `p → q` with a decidable `p` into `¬p ∨ q` and splits it, so every such premise in the context doubles its search, and a call with premises that the goal does not need can exhaust the declaration's heartbeats.  Clear those premises before the call, as `Verified.tail_ite_specX`, `Verified.tail_let_specX`, and `Verified.tail_letPair_specX` clear `hAl` and `hRA` before their calls of `tauto` and `flag_tac` in `Verified/AlignedTailBody.lean`.  The clearing halved the time of the tactics of `Verified.tail_let_specX` and kept the theorems within the default limit of heartbeats.

The worked examples are `LeanExe.Requests.frameTick_cases`, `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.step_le`, and the uses of `Demos.OrderBook.Process.ite_le_zero` in `Demos/OrderBook/ProcessCharges.lean`.  The first states the cases of a term once and splits them one branch at a time, the second applies a lemma over two instances down a tree of `if`s, and the third bounds the cost of a status whose instance holds an environment.  `Verified.tail_let_specX` clears the premises that its calls of `tauto` do not need.  The walks of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/FloatToInt.lean`, `cond_disch` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean`, and `fpDiv_run` of `tools/sail/F7/FPDiv.lean` with `div_sgn` lie in modules that the repository's build does not compile, so `entry.json` lists none of them.
