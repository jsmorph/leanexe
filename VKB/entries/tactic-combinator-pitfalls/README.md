# Tactics whose failures mislead

The entry applies when a proof built from tactic combinators fails at a step other than the faulty one, or does not fail where it should: a `first` that took an alternative which did not finish, a discharger that closed a goal with `sorry`, a `try` that did not stop a recursion-depth error, an `ite_self`, a `split`, or a `rw` that does not apply in a goal that is type-correct only at the default transparency, a `split` that does not apply to a `match` under `have`, a conditional `simp` lemma that does not fire, a rewrite or a destructuring whose result makes a later step fail, a rewrite that closes a goal with metavariables, a rewrite of an `if`'s condition whose motive is not type correct, a `congr` that unfolds a hypothesis while it looks for an assumption, a definition whose body begins with implicit binders, a unification of `?f ?a` with an application that assigns the application's last argument to `?a`, an auto-bound implicit whose name resolves to a declaration of the namespace that the declaration's name opens, a structure that unification does not recover from its projection, a case split whose names fall on other hypotheses than intended, a `repeat' split` whose cases multiply, a `tauto` whose search doubles with each premise in its context, a `simp` whose normal form a hypothesis does not share, an `omega` that takes one quantity as two atoms, a `simpa` that compares two forms at reducible transparency, a pattern variable that names a constructor, a field notation that misses a definition declared in another namespace, a structure field that hides a parameter of the same name, a parse that a dependency's token, a line break, or a comma inside an anonymous constructor changes, a `revert` that reverts in another order than its list, an `obtain` whose term leaves implicit arguments unassigned or whose `-` clears the hypotheses that depend on a witness, the join points of a `do` block after `unfold`, an address in two syntactic forms, a `cases` on an equation of constructor applications whose arguments are not constructors, a `generalize` that misses an occurrence inside a structure's fields, a name that a reintroduced hypothesis shadows, or a `split` that takes an outer `match`.  The symptom appears as unsolved goals at a later step, as an error that a discharger logs while the proof continues on a false branch, as exhausted memory, or as a message that names a cause other than the defect.  The combinators behave as Lean's source defines them, so each remedy below changes the form of the proof rather than the combinator.

`first | t₁ | t₂` takes the first alternative that succeeds, and an alternative succeeds when it makes progress, whether or not it closes the goal, so a partial result of `t₁` keeps `t₂` from running.  End each alternative that must close its goal with `done`: the walks of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/FloatToInt.lean` try `(apply Eq.symm; xreg_end; done)` before `fp_side`, and the last alternative of `div_sgn` in `tools/sail/F7/FPDiv.lean` ends a `simp only` with `done`.  `first`, which `Lean.Elab.Tactic.evalFirst` implements, joins its alternatives with `Lean.Elab.Tactic.orElse`, which runs its left side without error recovery, so every alternative but the last fails as a whole, while the last runs with error recovery and can log its error and close the goal with `sorry`.  A discharger whose last alternative may fail therefore ends in `fail`, as `cond_disch` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean` does, and [the entry on instruction forms against Isla](../isla-form-proofs/README.md) records the wrong branch that a walk took without it.  `try t` is `first | t | skip`, and neither catches the recursion-depth error or the heartbeat error, which Lean's `Lean.Core.tryCatch` rethrows as runtime exceptions (`Lean.Exception.isRuntime`), so a step that can exceed either limit needs a cheaper form before it, as `reg_end` compares values with `with_reducible rfl` before it compares them as natural numbers (the same entry).

`ite_self` takes the `if`'s instance as an implicit argument `{d : Decidable c}` (`Init/SimpLemmas.lean` in the toolchain's sources), and `split` reduces the branches with `ite_eq_left` and `ite_eq_right` applied to the goal's instance (`reduceIte'` in `Lean/Meta/Tactic/SplitIf.lean`), so `simp only [ite_self]`, `rw [ite_self]`, and `split` apply to an `if` whose instance is any term of type `Decidable c` for its condition `c`, such as a local variable or a term that holds an environment.  A rewrite of the condition by `simp` or `dsimp` with an equation proved by `rfl` or with a definition's equations, such as those of `Verified.Expr.denote`, or by a `dsimproc`, replaces the condition and keeps the instance, while the tactic `unfold` gives the `if` an instance of the new condition.  `simp` and `rw` check the type of each term that they assign to a lemma's variable, such as the instance `d` of `ite_self`, at the `implicit` transparency, which unfolds only definitions marked `@[reducible]`, `@[instance_reducible]`, or `@[implicit_reducible]` (`checkTypesAndAssign` and the option `backward.isDefEq.respectTransparency.types` in `Lean/Meta/ExprDefEq.lean`, and `Lean.Meta.TransparencyMode` in `Init/MetaTypes.lean`).  When the old and the new condition are equal only through a definition that this transparency does not unfold, the kept instance's type matches `Decidable` of the new condition only at the default transparency, and `ite_self` does not apply.  In the same call of `simp` the `if` stays and Lean warns that `ite_self` is unused, a later `simp only [ite_self]` reports that it made no progress, with a note that the target expression is not type-correct under the `implicit` transparency level, and `rw [ite_self]` finds no occurrence of its pattern.  `split` fails with `Could not split an if or match expression in the goal`, or, when the new condition holds an `if` of its own, splits that inner `if`.  After a propositional rewrite of the condition, `simp` gives the `if` an instance of the new condition, and `ite_self` applies.  The cost of a WASI effect body's status is such an `if`, since the `simp` that unfolds `Verified.Expr.denote` in its condition keeps the instance that holds the body's environment.  A lemma over any instance, proved by cases on the instance or by `ite_eq_left` and `ite_eq_right` with the instance named, closes the step with `exact` or `refine`, which unify at the default transparency.  `Demos.OrderBook.Process.ite_le_zero` bounds an `if` with any decision procedure, `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.ite_bound` bounds an `if` of costs by an `if` of step results on the same condition with any two instances, which `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.step_le` applies down the trees of the step's cost and result with `refine`, and [the entry on WASI effect charges](../wasi-effect-charges/README.md) removes the budget term `if c then b else b` of the `ite` rule before a `simp` that unfolds its condition.

The same note accompanies a `rw` in a goal over a command's meaning in an environment that `Verified.Wasi.ResultTy.bindEnv` builds.  After the bind of the error continuation in `Verified.Wasi.Reflect.withOpen_rel`, the goal holds the meaning of the last command `errorLast` in such an environment, with the command's context written by `Verified.Wasi.ResultTy.extend`, and `rw [hErrorLast]`, an equation over `Verified.Env.cons`, fails there with `Did not find an occurrence of the pattern`.  The proof's `show` restates the environment as `.cons` and the context as a list, which Lean checks at the default transparency, and the `rw` still fails after it, since the bound value `flat` keeps the type `(ResultTy.value type).denote`, which equals `type.denote` only at the default transparency.  After `subst same` replaces `flat` by `flatten native`, of type `type.denote`, the `rw` succeeds.  The proof applies the equation by `exact` through the private `rel_pure_of_eq` of `Verified/Wasi/ReflectBracket.lean`, which takes it as an argument and unifies at the default transparency.

`split at h` also fails, with `Could not split an if or match expression`, on a `match` whose discriminant is bound by `have`, as unfolding `Demos.Rcv.Spec.rule` leaves its tied set `Z`.  Prove one evaluation lemma for each branch of such a definition, which unfolds the definition, rewrites the discriminants with the branch's hypotheses, and closes with `simp only [h0, ↓reduceIte]` and `rfl`, as `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_majority`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_zero`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_batch`, and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_of_lowest` do.  A proof then cases on each discriminant with `cases hx : e` and rewrites with the branch's lemma, as `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.rule_order` does.

A `simp` lemma with a condition rewrites only where the discharger proves the condition, and otherwise leaves the term without a message.  Where the condition concerns a state that the proof builds step by step, state the step as an unconditional equation into an `if` on the condition, with an irreducible form in the other branch.  `F7.bind_fa` of `tools/sail/F7/Base.lean` rewrites the run of a bind over IsFullA64Enabled to an `if` whose first branch continues the run and whose second is `stuckRun`, `sail_simp` reduces the condition with the hypotheses that it is given, and a call from a state that it cannot show to satisfy the condition stays in the goal as `stuckRun` (its docstring).

Some steps succeed and leave a goal on which a later step fails.  `rw` fixes a lemma's implicit arguments at the first match in the goal and rewrites only the instances of that match, so `rw [ite_eq_left h]` reduces the first `if` on the condition and leaves an `if` on the same condition with other branches, while `simp only [h, ↓reduceIte]` reduces both, as in `Demos.Rcv.Refine.batchOf_spec`.  `rw [h]` with an equivalence `h : p ↔ q` whose left side is the whole condition of an `if` fails with `motive is not type correct`, since the abstracted condition leaves the `if`'s instance of type `Decidable p`.  A rewrite with `ite_eq_left (h.mpr hq)` or `ite_eq_right (mt h.mp hnq)` decides the `if`, as `Verified.ShimA64.closeStub_sim` and `Verified.ShimA64.run_handleChecked` do with `Verified.ShimA64.held_byte`, and `simp only [h]` rewrites the condition together with its instance.  The toolchain deprecates `if_pos`, `if_neg`, `dif_pos`, and `dif_neg` in favor of `ite_eq_left`, `ite_eq_right`, `dite_eq_left`, and `dite_eq_right` (`Init/Core.lean` in its sources).  A `by` block among the lemma's arguments runs after that match, on the instance that it fixed, so `rw [List.filter_cons_of_pos (by …)]` must prove the condition of the first `filter` of a cons in the goal, and the arguments that select another occurrence must be given explicitly.  `rw [List.take_add_one]` rewrites the first `List.take (n + 1) l` that it finds, and a numeral such as the `8` of `List.take 8` matches `n + 1`, so `Demos.RandomHistogram.tally_eq` states the equation for the intended `take` as a `have` and rewrites with it.  `rw [h] at hyp` with `h : lineOf b j = some l` leaves `match some l with …` unreduced in `hyp`, and a later `rw` with the parse of `l` finds no instance inside the `match`, while `simp only [h] at hyp` rewrites and reduces the `match`, as the proof of `Demos.Rcv.Spec.read_error_first` does for each header line.

`rw` ends with `with_reducible rfl` on the main goal, wherever it rewrites, so `rw [e] at h` inside a `by` block whose goal holds the unassigned implicit arguments of a lemma, such as `?a * 3 = ?b * 3`, closes that goal by assigning `?b := ?a`, and the lemma then states the trivial `a = a`.  Lean reports the unassigned metavariable or that the next tactic has no goals, so state the hypothesis with its type, `have hb : b • w = b' • w := by …`, before the lemma takes it.  A `by` block among a lemma's arguments meets the same `rfl` when its goal holds implicit arguments of the lemma that neither an earlier argument nor an expected type fixes.  In `have ih := lemma n (a + 1) fun j hj => by rw [e]; exact …`, the block runs at the end of the term's elaboration, its goal is `?m' x = ?m x`, the `rfl` of its `rw` closes it by assigning one metavariable to the other, and `exact` fails with `No goals to be solved`.  As the argument of an outer `rw`, the block runs after the rewrite has fixed `?m'` from the goal, the same `rfl` sets `?m` to it, `exact` again reports no goals, and the outer rewrite leaves the goal as it was, which the end of the enclosing proof reports unsolved.  Naming the implicit arguments or giving the `have` its type fixes the block's goal before it runs, as the recursive call in the proof of `LeanExe.A64.readList_congr` passes `(m := m) (m' := m')`.  The same `rfl` leaves open a goal that holds only after a definition unfolds: after `rw [h]` with `h : M.core q' = { M.core q with pc := p }`, the goal `(M.core q').x r = (M.core q).x r` reads `LeanExe.A64.State.x`, a definition, of a structure update, and `rw [h]; rfl` closes it, since the tactic `rfl`, unlike the one that `rw` runs, unfolds definitions at the default transparency, as both branches of `LeanExe.A64.Machine.sim_iff` do.  `rw [← h]` with `h : n ^ 2 * a ^ 3 = μ` rewrites every occurrence of `μ`, those inside applications such as `semimajor μ r v` included, after which the lemmas about those applications no longer match.  Name such applications with `generalize` or `set` before the rewrite, or derive the equation that the step needs and rewrite with it, as `Demos.Orbit.lambert_newton` rewrites `conicAdvance_time` at the time `t` into `hadv` and uses `hadv` in place of the hypothesis on `t`.

`simp` rewrites `List.headD l d` to `l.head?.getD d`, so a hypothesis stated with `headD` no longer matches the goal that `simp` leaves, and `exact` fails with a type mismatch between the two forms, while `simpa using h` brings the hypothesis to the same normal form.  It rewrites `l.tail.headD d` to `l[1]?.getD d` in the same way, so `Verified.Shim.handleRead_contract` names the pointers of a request with `generalize pointers.tail.headD 0 = p1 at element first apart ⊢` before it simplifies, and its bounds and the goal keep one variable.  `simp` also rewrites `(UInt32.ofNat c.toNat).toUInt64` to `c % 4294967296`, which leaves a goal such as `c % 4294967296 = c`, and `apply UInt64.toNat_inj.mp`, `simp only [UInt64.toNat_mod, UInt64.reduceToNat]`, and `omega` close it from the bound on `c`.  `Verified.Wasi.OracleSys.decodeArgs_lower` uses both remedies.  `generalize h : e = x at h'` replaces `e` in the goal as well as in `h'`, as the docstring of `Lean.Parser.Tactic.generalize` states, so a later step that expects `e` in the goal finds `x`.  It replaces only the occurrences that it finds in the terms as written, so an expression that occurs only inside the fields of a hypothesis whose type is a structure, such as `Demos.ManifestCheck.Hashed`, stays as it is, and `Demos.ManifestCheck.hashed_finish` extracts the fields with `have` before it generalizes.  A hypothesis given to `simp` as a lemma keeps its form, while `simp` reduces `2 ^ 32` to `4294967296` in the term that it simplifies, so in `simp [large] at head` the hypothesis `large : ¬x < 2 ^ 32` does not decide the `if x < 2 ^ 32` of `head`, and `Verified.Wasi.flatArgsWith_fit` applies `simp only [Nat.reducePow] at large` first.  `simp only` runs only the simprocs that it lists, and it rewrites with a lemma before it runs a simproc on the same term, so with `UInt64.toNat_ofNat` in its set `(256 : UInt64).toNat` becomes `256 % 2 ^ 64` before `UInt64.reduceToNat` can compute it, and `omega` then takes each product with that factor as an atom and fails.  `Verified.Wasi.littleEndian_read16` leaves the lemma out, lists `UInt64.reduceToNat`, and states the value of each `UInt64.ofNat x` in an equation of its own with `UInt64.toNat_ofNat_of_lt'`.

Two equations in one `simp only` call can match the same term, and `simp` then rewrites with either.  `Verified.testBit_shiftLeft_eq` states `Nat.testBit (a <<< k) = shift k a.testBit` and also matches `Nat.testBit (1 <<< k)`, for which the specification's selector tests `i == k`, the form that `Verified.testBit_one_shiftLeft_eq` gives.  With both equations in one call, the proof of `Verified.Expr.codeMask_eq` failed on every case whose mask held one variable, with `shift k (Nat.testBit 1)` left in the goal, so the proof rewrites with `testBit_one_shiftLeft_eq` in one call and with `testBit_shiftLeft_eq` in a later one, as the docstring of `Verified.testBit_one_shiftLeft_eq` states.  When a `simp only` call leaves a term in an unexpected form, look for a second lemma whose left side matches the same term, and split the call.

`omega` replaces a variable of a tactic `let` by its value where the variable stands alone as an integer or a natural number, and it leaves the variable inside a term that it takes as an atom.  It identifies atoms up to the canonical forms of `Lean.Meta.Canonicalizer`, which hashes a local variable by its identity, so after `let pointer := f root` the terms `pointer.toNat` and `(f root).toNat` are two atoms, and facts stated of the second do not reach a goal over the first: `omega` fails with a counterexample that names both.  A type ascription on `obtain` that states the facts in terms of `pointer`, `obtain ⟨low, high⟩ : root.toNat ≤ pointer.toNat ∧ … := spec …`, gives the goal and the facts one atom, and so does a proof that names the value itself in place of a `let`, as `Verified.Wasi.wp_sys_record_accept_with` names `UInt64.ofNat ABI.callArea.1` and states `(UInt64.ofNat ABI.callArea.1).toNat = ABI.callArea.1` once.  The goal of `decreasing_by` for a recursive call inside `let rep := replies k` names the call's argument with the value, `(replies k).ret.toNat`, while the condition of the `if` names `rep.ret.toNat`, so `omega` fails with a counterexample that names both atoms.  The condition stands among the hypotheses of `decreasing_by` whether or not the `if` names it, and `Verified.Shim.randomLoop` writes its condition and its call with `(replies k).ret.toNat`, which `omega` reads as one atom.  `omega` takes `UInt64.size` as an atom, and some rewrites leave it in a goal, so `omega` fails on a fact that it proves with `2 ^ 64`.  `UInt64.toNat_ofNat_of_lt'` states its premise with `UInt64.size`, and a wrapper whose premise is `n < 2 ^ 64`, such as `Demos.ManifestCheck.toNat_ofNat`, or a hypothesis `UInt64.size = 2 ^ 64` proved by `rfl` lets `omega` prove it.  `omega` also relates `2 ^ 64 - k.toNat` to `k` only when the context states `k.toNat < 2 ^ 64`, as in `Demos.RandomChoice.digitByte_word`.

`split_ifs` on a goal that still holds a program's `let` bindings, after `unfold`, leaves goals that `omega` does not close, and `dsimp only` before it removes the bindings.  The names of `split_ifs with h1 h2 …` go to the conditions in the order of the splits, those of an `if` inside a let-bound value included, so such an `if` shifts the names of the later hypotheses.  `Demos.Orbit.signOf` moves the selection of a sign out of the program's body into a definition of its own, and `Demos.Orbit.lambertBox_sound` applies `dsimp only`, generalizes the large interval terms of the unfolded program to variables, and then splits.  `split_ifs` also splits the `if`s inside the structure literals of a result, so its goals do not follow the branches of the program's tests.  After `unfold Demos.PassiveSafety.march` and `dsimp only`, it leaves two goals for an accepted panel, one for each side of the step's `if h2 ≤ e then h2 else e`, and without `dsimp only` it splits the rejected branch's test `m.h ≤ hmin` first and leaves the test of `Demos.PassiveSafety.accepts` under its binding in both goals.  `by_cases` on each outer test with `ite_eq_left` or `ite_eq_right` keeps one goal for each branch, and tests factored into functions of their own, such as `Demos.PassiveSafety.accepts` and `Demos.PassiveSafety.record`, keep their `if`s out of any split, as `Demos.PassiveSafety.march_covered` shows.  `cases h with | ctor a b …` on a hypothesis of an inductive family whose indices are local variables expects fewer names than the constructor has fields, `Too many variable names provided at alternative`, and a name that falls on a field that the unification of the indices removes names nothing, so a later `rename_i` or a later name meets other hypotheses than the proof expects.  `match h with | .ctor _ b _ hab hc => …` takes one pattern for each explicit field.  A pattern variable whose name resolves to several constants of the open namespaces, such as `Inv`, stops a `match` with `ambiguous pattern, use fully qualified name`, and a lower-case name avoids it.  A name that resolves to one constructor is that constructor, lower-case or not: where `Verified.Wasi.CommandResult` is an open namespace, a pattern variable `terminal` is the constructor `Verified.Wasi.CommandResult.terminal`, and without its arguments the pattern fails with `Invalid pattern: Not enough arguments`.  A definition's name opens its namespace in its body, so in a definition of the namespace `Verified.Wasi.Command`, such as `Verified.Wasi.Command.handleCheck`, a pattern variable `call` is the constructor `Verified.Wasi.Command.call`, while the same pattern elaborates in a definition outside that namespace, and the arm of `handleCheck` for a `sys` command names the call `operation`.  A pattern variable therefore takes a name that resolves to no constant.

Some messages name a cause other than the defect.  `decide` refuses a goal that mentions a local variable with `Expected type must not contain free variables`, even when the proposition reduces to a closed one, such as `¬({ found := 0, lo := x } : Br).found = 1`, and `absurd (show (0 : UInt64) = 1 from h) (by decide)` restates the hypothesis in closed form first.  A projection of a structure with free fields fails in the same way, and `Verified.ShimA64.readStub_sim` proves `(readEvent L mem 0 p n r).call ≠ Linux.closeCall`, whose projection reduces to `LeanExe.Linux.readCall`, by `show Linux.readCall ≠ Linux.closeCall by decide`.  A type ascription does not restate it: `absurd (h : (0 : UInt64) = 1) (by decide)` fails with the same message, because `(h : T)` checks `h` against `T` and leaves the term `h`, whose inferred type keeps the literal, while `have h0 : (0 : UInt64) = 1 := h` records the closed type, as `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.march_covers` does.  A literal of `Float` and `(k : UInt64).toFloat` reduce in the kernel, so `absurd (congrArg Float.toBits h) (by decide +kernel)` refutes `h : (2.0 : Float) = 1.0`, and a read of an array literal that holds a computed value is reduced first with a lemma proved by `rfl`, such as `Demos.Screening.lit5_get0`.  `obtain ⟨R, hR, -⟩ := hu` clears `hu`, so a later use of `hu` fails as an unknown identifier, and `obtain … := id hu` keeps it, as `Demos.Conjunction.Model.radiusIn_of` does.  A `-` clears the hypothesis that it matches and every hypothesis that depends on it (the docstring of `rcases` in `Init/RCases.lean` of the toolchain's sources), so `-` on an existential witness, as in `obtain ⟨_, _, -, s, -, r⟩ := …`, also clears `r`, whose type names the witness, and `_` in place of that `-` keeps `r`, as the uses of `Verified.Wasi.Resource.CommandAlong.split` in `Demos.ManifestCheck.body_bounded` do.  In a module that imports Mathlib, `not_le` and the other lemmas of Mathlib's linear orders do not apply to `UInt64`, and the error is a type mismatch between `@LT.lt ?α LinearOrder.toPartialOrder.toLT ?b ?a` and `@LT.lt UInt64 instLTUInt64 b a`, while `UInt64.not_le`, `UInt64.lt_iff_toNat_lt`, and `UInt64.le_iff_toNat_le` apply.  `Library.Numerics.Bounds.finite` is an abbreviation, so `simp` with `decide_eq_true_eq` turns `finite s = true` into a comparison of `Library.Numerics.Bounds.absBits`, to which `Library.Numerics.Interval.finite_iff` no longer applies.  `simp only [Bool.and_eq_true]` alone keeps `finite s = true`, and `of_decide_eq_true` then reads each comparison, as `Demos.PassiveSafety.midpoint_ok` does.  Field notation such as `h.cosh` on `h : HasDerivAt f f' x` in a module that does not import `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Trigonometric.DerivHyp` reports that the environment does not contain the field `cosh` of `HasFDerivAtFilter`, the unfolded type, where the cause is the missing import.  A definition `def StringVector.strings …` written inside the namespace `Verified.ShimA64` takes that namespace as a prefix of its name, while field notation `v.strings` on `v : LeanExe.A64.StringVector` looks for `LeanExe.A64.StringVector.strings` with the current namespace left out (`findMethod?` in `Lean/Elab/App.lean` of the toolchain's sources), so it reports `Invalid field` and that the environment does not contain that name, and `def _root_.LeanExe.A64.StringVector.strings` declares the definition where field notation looks, as `Verified/ShimA64/Strings.lean` does.  In a `structure` whose field has the name of a parameter, such as a field `scratch : ∀ k < 8, …` of a structure with the parameter `scratch : Nat`, a later field that names the parameter reads the field, and the error is an application type mismatch of a `Prop` where a `Nat` is expected, while `LeanExe.A64.StubEntry` gives that field the name `saved`.  The elaborator of an application unfolds the result type with `Lean.Meta.whnfForall` to look for further binders, so a term whose type is a definition whose body begins with implicit binders, such as `∀ {trace residual after}, Prefix … → …`, receives those implicit arguments as metavariables when nothing follows.  `have h := f x` then fails with `don't know how to synthesize implicit argument`, and field notation on `f x`, whose type is then a function type, looks in the namespace `Function` and reports `Invalid field` with the field's name in that namespace. In a declaration whose name opens a namespace, such as `theorem CommandAlong.body`, the name `first`, meant as an auto-bound implicit, resolves to the theorem `Verified.Wasi.Resource.CommandAlong.first` of that namespace, and the error names a `Prop` where a `Ty` was expected, so `Verified.Wasi.Resource.CommandAlong.body` names the pair's types `left` and `right`.  Unification does not recover a structure from its projection, so a lemma whose premise names `pure.funs` and whose conclusion names neither `pure` nor another of its fields takes `(pure := .ofProg …)` at each use.  A line that continues a tactic of a `by` block inside a term must start to the right of that tactic's column, and a continuation to its left ends the block, with an unexpected identifier at the continuation, so a long tactic inside a term goes into a `have` of its own.  The syntax `Lean.Parser.Tactic.location` saves the column of `at` and continues its list of hypotheses only with a term that starts to the right of that column, so a hypothesis on the next line that starts left of `at` fails to parse, whether it starts at the tactic's column or to its right.
