# Programs over `Int64`

The entry applies when a program of the dialect takes, returns, or keeps in its state `Int64` values and its specification states the results over `Int` through `.toInt` or through WebAssembly's signed conversions, and when such a program must also run as an A64 Image or a Cortex-M7 step.  An `Int64` is its word: the instance `Flat Int64 UInt64` of `LeanExe/Pipeline/Int64.lean` flattens it to `x.toUInt64`, so the generated theorems pass and return `.i64 x.toUInt64`.  The reflector writes `+`, `-`, `*`, `&&&`, `|||`, `^^^`, `<<<`, `==`, and negation as word operations by the equations of the last section of `LeanExe/ProofKit/Signed64.lean`, such as `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.add_word` and `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.neg_word`.  It writes `/` and `%` as `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.div` and `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.rem`, the unsigned division of the magnitudes with the sign fixed afterwards (`LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.div_eq`, `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.rem_eq`), so the module executes neither `i64.div_s` nor `i64.rem_s` and gives Lean's values for a zero divisor and for `minValue / -1`.  The order comparisons are the source comparisons `slt` and `sle` of the words, which `Verified.Reflect.slt_apply` and `Verified.Reflect.sle_apply` equate with Lean's `decide (a < b)` and `decide (a ≤ b)` and which compile to `i64.lt_s` and `i64.le_s`, and `>>>` is the arithmetic shift `sar`, which compiles to `i64.shr_s` (docstring of `Verified.Reflect.reflect.reflectInt64?`).  The conversions between `Int64` and the floats compile through the word forms `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.toFloat`, `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.toFloat32`, `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.ofFloat`, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.ofFloat32` of `LeanExe/ProofKit/Signed64Float.lean`, which use the unsigned conversions, as [the manual's section on expressions](../../../docs/verified-manual.md#expressions) describes.

Prove the specification at `.toInt` with core lemmas and leave the word forms to the reflector's equations, which the generated theorems already contain.  `Int64.toInt_div_of_ne_left` and `Int64.toInt_div_of_ne_right` state `(a / b).toInt` as `Int.tdiv` outside `minValue / -1`, `Int64.toInt_mod` states `(a % b).toInt` as `Int.tmod`, and `Int.fdiv_eq_tdiv` corrects the truncating quotient to the floor quotient.  `Int64.lt_iff_toInt_lt`, `Int64.le_iff_toInt_le`, and `Int64.toInt_inj` move comparisons and equalities to the integers, a literal's integer follows by `decide`, as in `show (60 : Int64).toInt = 60 by decide`, and `omega` closes the goals, and a difference that cannot wrap takes `Int.bmod_eq_of_le` after `Int64.toInt_sub`.  A proof about bits goes to `BitVec`: `BitVec.sshiftRight_eq'` and `BitVec.toInt_sshiftRight` state the arithmetic shift, and `BitVec.toInt_eq_msb_cond` splits each value on its sign bit, as `Examples.Floor.average_spec` does for `(a &&& b) + ((a ^^^ b) >>> 1)`.  `Examples.Floor.toInt_min` and `Examples.Floor.toInt_max` state `min` and `max` over the integers.  A program that holds a signed quantity computed from words, such as a logarithm's exponent, proves its integer with the lemmas of [the entry on signed quantities as `Int64` values](../signed-integer-values/README.md).  The specification states its exclusions: `Examples.Floor.floorDiv_spec` assumes a nonzero divisor and excludes `minValue / -1`, whose floor quotient `2^63` lies outside `Int64`.

A conversion's specification states the result through WebAssembly's signed conversions as Talos defines them, which do not mention Lean's model of floats.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64Wasm.toBits_toFloat`, `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64Wasm.toBits_toFloat32`, `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64Wasm.toInt64_eq`, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64Wasm.toInt64_eq32` prove such a specification in one step each, as `Examples.Convert.toDouble_spec` and `Examples.Convert.ofDouble_spec` show.  The word forms round as Lean's model does because `LeanExe.ProofKit.Signed64.fromInt_neg` proves that the rounding of `-n` is the negation of the rounding of `n`.  At binary32 the 64-bit magnitude rounds once, and `Examples.Convert.special_values` checks `2^62 + 2^38 + 1`, whose rounding through binary64 would give a different single.

The call theorem applies the generated `pure` theorem and states the result word `w` by `w.toInt64.toInt = expected`.  `Examples.Floor.floorDiv_call` takes `(compiled.floorDiv.pure env store (a, b)).mono`, supplies `(floorDiv a b).toUInt64` as the word, and closes the list equation with `simpa [Scalar.values, Flat.flat] using congrArg List.reverse hv`, and [the entry on specification call theorems](../spec-call-theorem/README.md) describes the general form.

The C translator and both machine back ends compute the three signed instructions with forms that they have for other instructions.  `LeanExe/Native/Translate.lean` casts both operands to `long long` before C's comparison or `>>`.  The A64 code generator follows `SUBS` with `CSINC` on GE or GT, which `LeanExe.A64.cset_ltS` relates to `x.toInt64 < y.toInt64`, and computes `i64.shr_s` by `LeanExe.A64.sarCode`, the logical shift between two copies of the sign mask, with the simulation lemmas `LeanExe.A64.sim_ltSI64`, `LeanExe.A64.sim_leSI64`, and `LeanExe.A64.sim_shrSI64` ([the AArch64 document's section on the code generator](../../../docs/a64-bare-metal.md#the-code-generator)).  The Cortex-M7 code generator adds 2^31 to both high words with `LeanExe.M7.biasOps` before the unsigned comparisons and wraps the logical shift of a pair in exclusive ors with the sign mask in `LeanExe.M7.sarOps`, with the simulation lemmas `LeanExe.M7.sim_ltSI64`, `LeanExe.M7.sim_leSI64`, and `LeanExe.M7.sim_shrSI64` ([the Cortex-M7 document's section on the code generator](../../../docs/m7-bare-metal.md#the-code-generator)).  [The entry on instruction forms against Isla](../isla-form-proofs/README.md) weighs such sequences against a new machine form.

An A64 Image of a function that returns an `Int64` writes the result's word, and `TestSupport.A64.Floor.writes_int64` gives the bytes of that word from the compiler's theorem, as `TestSupport.A64.Floor.floorDiv_runs` uses it.  `TestSupport.A64.ImageFiles.floordiv_image_spec` and `TestSupport.A64.ImageFiles.average_image_spec` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesFloor.lean` join the Images with the specification, `TestSupport.A64.ImageFiles.ofdouble_image` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesConvert.lean` does the same for a conversion, and [the entry on AArch64 Image theorems](../a64-image-theorem/README.md) describes the method.  A Cortex-M7 step whose state holds an `Int64` takes the theorem of [the entry on Cortex-M7 board theorems](../m7-board-theorem/README.md), with the field's word in the state's word list, as `TestSupport.M7.Signed.sgWords` names `s.acc.toUInt64`.

`<<<` and `>>>` take the amount's low 6 bits, so `x >>> 65` is `x >>> 1` and `x >>> (-1)` is `x >>> 63`, and a specification over the integers restricts the amount or states the same reduction.  For a zero divisor, `Examples.Floor.floorDiv` gives 0, or -1 for a negative dividend, where `Int.fdiv` gives 0, so its specification leaves the case out (`Examples/Floor/README.md`, section "Theorems").  A table of a wrapper stays an `Array UInt64`, which a program reads with `.toInt64`, and a recursive type has no array field of `Int64`.

A field of a record of a recursive type may hold an `if` on a condition that adds a large literal to a word, such as the biased comparison `a + 2^63 < b + 2^63`.  A kernel check that compares such a field's value with the record's family at its index unfolds the `if` first and evaluates the condition's `Decidable` instance in a number of steps linear in 2^63, which exhausts the heartbeats of `verified_compile`.  The reflector therefore proves the family equations for variables in place of the values and applies them to the values, which the kernel substitutes without comparing them (docstring of `Verified.Reflect.assignFamilyEquation`), and `tests/verified/TreeFieldConditions.lean` compiles such a record for each kind of field and in a match alternative under 20,000 heartbeats.  A proof by hand whose goal compares such a term with its unfolding meets the same evaluation, which [the entry on kernel evaluation costs](../kernel-evaluation-costs/README.md) describes with its remedies.

The worked examples are `Examples.Floor.floorDiv_spec`, `Examples.Floor.average_spec`, and `Examples.Floor.floorDiv_call` for division and the shift, `Examples.Convert.ofDouble_spec` and `Examples.Convert.scale_spec` for the conversions, `TestSupport.A64.Floor.floorDiv_runs` for an A64 Image, and `TestSupport.M7.Signed.board_loop` for the STM32F767ZI microcontroller, an Arm Cortex-M7, on ST's NUCLEO-F767ZI development board.  [The `Int64` fixture](../../../Verified/Examples/Signed.lean) compiles every form of the dialect.
