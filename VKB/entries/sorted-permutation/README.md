# Sorting and selection through the unique sorted permutation

A program that sorts an array of words, or that selects the word of rank `k`, has the reference sort as its specification: `(xs.toList.mergeSort fun a b => a ≤ b).toArray`, or that array's word at position `k`.  The program's method differs from merge sort, so the proof does not follow the reference's computation.  Exactly one list holds the input's words in nondecreasing order, so the proof needs only two properties of the result: it is in order, and it is a permutation of the input.

Prove `r.toList.Pairwise (· ≤ ·)` and `r.toList.Perm xs.toList` as separate facts, then finish with `unfold expected` and `rw [← Library.Sorting.eq_mergeSort_of_sorted_perm hs hp, Array.toArray_toList]`, as `Library.Sorting.Heapsort.heapsort_eq` does.  A proof that states the order by positions, `∀ p q, p ≤ q → q < r.size → r[p]! ≤ r[q]!`, converts it with `List.pairwise_iff_getElem`, as `heapsort_eq` does with the order that `heapsort_sorted_perm` gives.  `Library.Sorting.pairwise_mergeSort_le` states that the reference sort is in order, for a proof that assembles a sorted list from sorted pieces.  The order lemmas of words, `Library.Sorting.Heapsort.wle_trans` and its neighbors, move each comparison to `toNat`.

The order comes from the structure of the method.  Straight insertion keeps the first `k + 1` words in order, a permutation of the input's first `k + 1` words, and the rest of the input unchanged, under `LeanExe.Dialect.loop_induction` (`Library.Sorting.StraightInsertion.straightInsertion_sorted_perm`), with one insertion stated on lists as `List.orderedInsert` (`shift_toList`).  Insertion into an accumulated array keeps that array in order and a permutation of the words read so far (`Library.Sorting.Insertion.sortInsert_sorted_perm`), and heapsort keeps a heap on a prefix, a sorted suffix, and every word of the prefix at most every word of the suffix (`heap-sift-invariant`).  Merge sort is proved on a list abstraction: `Library.Sorting.MergeSort.pass_toList` and `sortLoop_toList` show that the array passes compute `passList` and `sortList`, and `sortList_spec` proves the order and the permutation on lists.  A pass of radix sort places the word at `j` at `pos s src j`, a map of `Fin src.size` that `pos_injective` shows injective and `Finite.injective_iff_bijective` therefore bijective, so `Equiv.Perm.ofFn_comp_perm` gives the permutation, and the pass's stability gives the order modulo `2^(8k)` after `k` passes (`Library.Sorting.RadixSort.pass_spec`, `passes_spec`).

A method whose last phase sorts every array reduces to that phase.  Quicksort and Shellsort end with straight insertion, so `straightInsertion_sorted_perm` gives a sorted permutation of the earlier phases' result, and those phases need only to permute (`Library.Sorting.Quicksort.stages_perm`, `Library.Sorting.Shellsort.passes_perm`).  Selection keeps a partition invariant instead: FIND leaves at position `k` a word at least every word before it and at most every word after it (`Library.Sorting.Find.Around`, `find_spec`).  `Library.Sorting.Find.rank_of_around` sorts the words on each side with `List.mergeSort`, places the word at `k` between them, and identifies the resulting sorted permutation with the reference, whose word at `k` is therefore the program's.

Every exchange of `Library.Sorting.Heapsort.swap` permutes the array (`swap_perm`), and `swap_get` and `swap_size` give its words and size, so a program built from exchanges composes their permutations with `List.Perm.trans`.  Quicksort, FIND, Shellsort, Dijkstra's heap, and the next permutation reuse heapsort's `swap` and these lemmas.  Insertion proves its permutation with `List.perm_insertIdx`, and straight insertion with `List.perm_orderedInsert`.  `Library.Combinatorics.NextPermutation.isNext_of_greatest` applies the same uniqueness to identify the arrangement that follows the last one.

The theorems of quicksort and Shellsort hold for any earlier phase that permutes the array, so they do not show that the partitions or the increments order anything: that order determines only the running time of the final insertion (`Library/Sorting/Quicksort/Proof.lean`, header).  The proofs record no failure.  Most sorting proofs restate `xs.size.toUInt64.toNat = xs.size` inline as `hN`, and merge sort copies heapsort's order lemmas, while `LeanExe.Dialect.size_toUInt64` states the size fact once and `Library.Sorting.RadixSort.radixSort_eq` uses it.

The worked examples are `Library.Sorting.Heapsort.heapsort_eq`, `Library.Sorting.Quicksort.quicksort_eq`, `Library.Sorting.Shellsort.shellsort_eq`, `Library.Sorting.MergeSort.mergeSort_eq`, `Library.Sorting.RadixSort.radixSort_eq`, `Library.Sorting.Insertion.sortInsert_eq`, and `Library.Sorting.Find.select_eq`.  Each states the equality for arrays of fewer than `2^64` words, which every array in memory is.  Their compiled modules join the equality with the generated theorem, as `spec-call-theorem` describes.
