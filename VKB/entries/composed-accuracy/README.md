# Accuracy of numerical functions applied in sequence

The entry applies when a theorem bounds the result of two compiled numerical functions applied one after the other, such as a transform and its inverse, and each function already has an accuracy bound in a master theorem.  The worked example is the round trip of the discrete Fourier transform, `Library.Numerics.Fourier.inverse_dft`, which bounds each float of `inverse (dft xs)` against the float of `xs` at its index.  The bound composes the error of the first function, the error of the second on an input that carries the first error, and an exact identity over the reals that relates the two exact functions.

A `Verified.Master` statement names the module's function only under an existential, so a composition cannot take its bound from the master theorem.  State each function's bound as a theorem about its Lean definition, as `Library.Numerics.Fourier.dft_spec` and `Library.Numerics.Fourier.inverse_spec` do with `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64Approx.FApprox` for each output float, and prove the master theorem from it by projecting the conjunctions, as `Library.Numerics.Fourier.dft_master` and `Library.Numerics.Fourier.inverse_master` do.  The execution theorems state that the exports compute the Lean definitions, so a bound on `inverse (dft xs)` holds for the floats that one export returns on the result of the other.  State the composition over sequences of reals with the two bounds as hypotheses, as `Library.Numerics.Fourier.roundtrip_real` does, which keeps array terms out of the error arithmetic.  The theorem about the program then instantiates the sequences with functions such as `fun j => value xs[2 * j]!.toBits`, and `exact` accepts the hypotheses' beta-redexes by definitional unfolding.

The second function's domain constrains the first function's output.  `inverse_spec` requires `Σ_j (|a_j| + |b_j|) ≤ 2 ^ 1000` of its input, and `Library.Numerics.Fourier.dft_mass` bounds that sum for the transform's output by `2 n (M + E)`, where `M` is the input's sum and `E` the forward error.  The premise of the composition therefore tightens, to `M ≤ 2 ^ 960` for `inverse_dft` with fewer than `2 ^ 30` floats.  The closed form of the final bound comes from bounding the error factor `γ_(n-1) = (n - 1) / (2 ^ 53 - (n - 1))` by `(n - 1) / 2 ^ 53 + 2 ^ -47` for `n ≤ 2 ^ 29`, which `Library.Numerics.Fourier.gamma_le` proves with `div_le_iff₀` and `nlinarith`.

The exact identity of the discrete Fourier transform rests on the orthogonality of its factors, `Library.Numerics.Fourier.sum_cos_sin`: for `j, m < n`, the sum over `k < n` of `cos (2 π k (j - m) / n)` is `n` when `j = m` and 0 otherwise, and the sum of the sines is 0.  The proof writes the terms as powers of `z = exp (2 π i (j - m) / n)` with `Complex.exp_nat_mul`, sums them with `geom_sum_eq`, gets `z ^ n = 1` from `Complex.exp_int_mul_two_pi_mul_I`, and excludes `z = 1` with `Complex.exp_eq_one_iff`, which leaves an integer `N` with `j - m = N n`.  `Complex.re_sum`, `Complex.exp_ofReal_mul_I_re`, and `Complex.exp_ofReal_mul_I_im` then give the real and imaginary parts.  `Library.Numerics.Fourier.inversion` expands the double sum with `Real.cos_sub` and `Real.sin_sub`, exchanges the sums with `Finset.sum_comm`, and closes with `Finset.sum_ite_eq'`.  `Library.Numerics.Fourier.abs_sum_trig` bounds a sum of such terms by `Σ_j (|a_j| + |b_j|)`, both for the exact transform's magnitude and for the forward error's effect on the exact inverse.

Three steps of the example failed as first written.  A hypothesis obtained by instantiating a lemma after `set M := Σ …` contains the sum or a beta-redex in place of `M`, so `linarith` treats the two as different atoms, and `Library.Numerics.Fourier.dft_mass` takes `M` with an equation `Σ … = M` that the caller proves by `rfl`.  `positivity` does not prove `0 ≤ γ_(n-1)`, since the denominator `2 ^ 53 - (n - 1)` is a difference, so the proofs use `div_nonneg` and `linarith` or the first conjunct of `gamma_le`.  Rewriting `↑(n - 1) + 1` backward with `Nat.cast_succ` produces `↑(n - 1).succ`, which `Nat.sub_add_cancel` does not match, and `Nat.cast_sub` followed by `ring` proves `(n : ℝ) = ↑(n - 1) + 1`.
