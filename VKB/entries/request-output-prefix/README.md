# Output of request programs

The entry applies when a program with requests runs on METAL or M7 and the goal states the bytes that its windows write to a UART's transmit register as a prefix of a specification's output for the bytes that they read from the receive register, `dataOut B u w <+: spec (dataIn B u w)` for the trace `w` of the first `N` windows.  The goal concerns the step function alone.  `LeanExe.Requests.reqTrace B tick s0 σ` gives the access of each window for every sequence `σ` of the values read and every trace type with its `LeanExe.Requests.Bus`, and `LeanExe.Requests.windows` joins the first `N` windows, so one lemma serves the C path's `LeanExe.Native.regBus` and the Cortex-M7's `LeanExe.M7.devBus devBase`.  A program theorem whose trace is those windows then carries the lemma to the program, as `TestSupport.M7.Gw.board_prefix` does with `obtain` on the loop theorem of [the entry on Cortex-M7 board theorems](../m7-board-theorem/README.md).

For a step that frames records of words, `LeanExe.Requests.frameTick u kin kout mach` is the reference step: it reads records of `kin` words through the registers `u : LeanExe.Requests.Uart`, runs the record machine `mach` on each, and writes the machine's record of `kout` words, each as 8 bytes with the most significant first.  `LeanExe.Requests.frame_prefix` states that the bytes that its windows write begin `LeanExe.Requests.outBytes mach m0 (LeanExe.Requests.decode kin (LeanExe.Requests.dataIn B u w)).recs`, from the invariant `LeanExe.Requests.FrameInv` that `LeanExe.Requests.frameInv_run` carries over the windows.  The compiled step keeps the records in structures with `Flat` instances, so the proof defines an abstraction `abs` from the step's state to `LeanExe.Requests.Framing` and proves `frameTick u kin kout mach (abs s) v = (abs (tick s v).1, (tick s v).2)`.  `Demos.Energy.gwTickWith_abs` proves it by `unfold frameTick gwTickWith`, a `simp only` with the abstraction and equations such as `UInt64.ofNat (18 - 1) = 17`, and `split_ifs <;> simp_all` with the machine's and the records' definitions.

`rw [← LeanExe.Requests.reqTrace_map abs habs]` then replaces the step by `frameTick` from `abs s0`, which must reduce to `LeanExe.Requests.frame0 kin kout m0`, and `frame_prefix` closes the goal with its premises on the registers and the record lengths by `decide` and the machine's output length by `fun m r _ => rfl`, as `Demos.Energy.gw_prefix` does.  `TestSupport.M7.Sha.sha_windows_prefix` takes the abstraction's equation as a hypothesis and holds for every bus and every register map that satisfies the register premises of `frame_prefix`, registers below 65536 and distinct receive and status registers, so one proof serves the board's step and QEMU's.  `LeanExe.Requests.frame_reads` bounds the bytes read by half the number of windows, and `LeanExe.Requests.decode_length` and `LeanExe.Requests.decode_lengths` give the records' number and lengths.  For bytes below 256, which `LeanExe.Requests.dataIn_lt` gives for every byte read, `LeanExe.Requests.decode_take` states the bytes of the first `i` records as the first `8 * kin * i` bytes read, and `LeanExe.Requests.decode_recs` states the records as the bytes up to the last complete record.  `LeanExe.Requests.flatMap_recordBytes_inj` states that records of `kin` words are determined by their bytes.  `TestSupport.M7.Gw.board_bytes` and `TestSupport.M7.Sha.sha_digests_bytes` restate the gateway's and the SHA-256 step's outputs over the bytes read with these lemmas.

A step that frames no records keeps its own invariant over the windows.  `TestSupport.M7.Crc.crc_replies` proves, for every bus and every register map that satisfies the register premises of `frame_prefix`, that the replies begin `crcReplies` of the bytes read, the low byte of CRC-32 of each nonempty prefix, and that at most one byte read waits for its reply.  Its invariant, `TestSupport.M7.Crc.CrcAt`, relates each phase of the state to the bytes read and written, and it relates the register in the state's upper 32 bits to the division's state through `Library.Hashing.Crc.Proof.byteStep_holds`.  A join with a standard follows the prefix: `TestSupport.M7.Sha.shaSpec_fips` states each answer of the record machine as the SHA-256 digest of FIPS 180-4 of the blocks so far, for fewer than 2^55 blocks, by `Library.Hashing.Sha256.sha256_spec`, and `TestSupport.M7.Sha.sha_digests` composes it with `sha_windows_prefix` for fewer than 2^62 windows, with `frame_reads` and `decode_lengths` bounding the blocks.

For a step that frames records of bytes with COBS and a `0x00` after each, `LeanExe.Requests.delimTick u maxLen mach` is the reference step: it stores a segment's bytes up to `maxLen`, calls the machine `mach : M → Except LeanExe.Requests.SegErr (List UInt8) → M × List UInt8` once for each nonempty segment, and writes the encoded answer and a `0x00`.  `LeanExe.Requests.delim_prefix` states that the bytes written begin `(LeanExe.Requests.delimAnswers mach maxLen m0 ((dataIn B u w).map UInt64.toUInt8)).map UInt8.toUInt64`, and `LeanExe.Requests.delim_answered` that they equal that list when the window after them reads a byte, so that no complete segment waits for its answer, which a prefix alone does not state.  Its invariant, `LeanExe.Requests.DelimInv`, follows `LeanExe.Requests.FrameInv` with the split `LeanExe.Requests.split` of the bytes read in place of the record decoder's state.  `LeanExe.Requests.segments_append_zero` states that the segments after a `0x00` do not depend on the bytes before it, and `LeanExe.Requests.cobsDecode_eq_some_iff` that the decoder accepts exactly the encodings, so a statement in terms of `cobsDecode` rests on `LeanExe.Requests.cobsEncode`.  A compiled step that packs the segment into words commutes with `delimTick` only on the states of an invariant, such as one that keeps a count of bytes to write positive while the step writes, so the proof states the commutation under the invariant and rewrites with `LeanExe.Requests.reqTrace_mapOn` in place of `reqTrace_map`, as `TestSupport.M7.Delim.dl_prefix` does with `TestSupport.M7.Delim.dlPhases_abs`.  [The entry on byte buffers in words](../byte-buffer-passes/README.md) gives the decoder and the encoder in words that such a step calls.

The theorems state the bytes that the windows exchange in the model, and their use on hardware adds a condition on the far end, which no proof covers.  USART3 holds one received byte, and the framing reads no byte in the window of a record's last byte or while it sends the answer, so the far end must send a record only after it has read the whole answer to the record before, as the section "The final theorems" of `docs/m7-bare-metal.md` states.  The SHA-256 board theorem bounds the windows below 2^62 so that each message stays within the length that FIPS 180-4 allows.

The worked examples on the C path are `Demos.Energy.gw_prefix` and `Demos.Energy.gw_usart3_prefix`, with `Demos.Energy.gwTickWith_abs`, and `TestSupport.Bare.sum_prefix`, with `TestSupport.Bare.sumTick_abs`.  On the Cortex-M7 they are `TestSupport.M7.Gw.windows_prefix`, `TestSupport.M7.Sha.sha_windows_prefix` with `TestSupport.M7.Sha.shaTickWith_abs`, `TestSupport.M7.Crc.crc_replies`, `TestSupport.M7.Sha.shaSpec_fips`, and for delimited records `TestSupport.M7.Delim.dlPhases_abs`, `TestSupport.M7.Delim.dl_usart3_prefix`, `TestSupport.M7.Delim.dl_usart3_answered`, and `TestSupport.M7.Delim.board_prefix`.  The board theorems that join them with the runs, `gw_board_prefix` and `gw_board_bytes` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/GwBoardFile.lean`, `crc_board_prefix` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/CrcBoardFile.lean`, `sha_board` and `sha_board_bytes` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/ShaBoardFile.lean`, and `dl_board_prefix` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/DelimBoardFile.lean`, read the image files and lie outside the build.
