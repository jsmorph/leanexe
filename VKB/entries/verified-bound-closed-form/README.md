# Closed-form allocation bounds

`verified_compile` gives each function `p.f.bound`, the number of bytes by which a call can raise the allocator's `top`, and `p.f.trapFree`, which excludes a trap when that many bytes fit under the memory cap.  The generated bound is a Lean term that follows the program through its loops, calls, copies, and built arrays.  A theorem that a call cannot run out of memory, or the room condition of a process, needs that term bounded by a closed form in the sizes of the input.

Rewrite the bound with its generated equation `p.f.bound_eq` and with the equations of the callees it names, then bound each part.  `Verified.blockCost_eq`, `growCost_eq`, and `paidGrowCost_eq` turn the block charges into linear terms, and `omega` closes the arithmetic.  A loop's charge is `Verified.loopCost`: `loopCost_const` evaluates it when every pass costs the same, `loopCost_le` bounds it from an invariant of the index and the state, and `loopCost_le_upto` needs the cost and the step only at the passes the loop reaches.  `Verified.loopCost_le_last` covers a loop whose passes cost at most `c` except one costly pass after which the condition fails.  `loopCost_repeatWhile_le` and `repeatWhile_count_le` bound a counting search by the count at which it stops, and `loop_push_size` gives the size of the array in the first component of a loop's state when each pass extends it by one element.

A loop whose state is a pair or a record has a bound of the form `min unpaid paid`, the smaller of two accountings of its arrays.  Take the paid side with `Nat.min_le_right` and bound it, as `Demos.RunningSum.consumeInput_bound_le` does before it applies `loopCost_le_last`.  Give each bound lemma a companion for the size of the result that callers pass on, as `Demos.RunningSumFp.mulSmall_size` accompanies `mulSmall_bound_le`.  When the closed form is a constant, `ImplementsA.mono` turns `p.f.trapFree` into a statement with that constant, as `Demos.Slew.Bound.slew_trapFree` does with `plan_bound`.

Do not evaluate a bound with `#eval compiled.f.bound x`.  `bound` runs the interpreter of the reflected program, and for the regex matcher at a pattern of one word it ran for 10 minutes before it was stopped.  Take a tuple argument whole wherever a caller passes the result of a call, and split the branches of a bound in order rather than with `first`, for the reasons that the `large-term-unfolding` entry records.  The bound ignores the reuse of freed blocks, so a bound over a whole run grows with every allocation, and a process needs the bounds per step of the `wasi-process-total` entry.

`Demos.Slew.Bound.plan_bound` bounds the slew planner by 68,020,488 bytes, `Demos.Drone.Verified.compute_bound` bounds the drone planner by 2,251,064 bytes, and `Library.Regex.matchWith_bound` gives a bound linear in the pattern's length.  The slew proof bounds the search's rounds with `loopCost_le` and an invariant of the search, and the drone proof rewrites with the equations of `compute` and its three callees before it bounds the number of tables that `forward` builds.  `Demos.RunningSumFp.print64_bound_le` and `parse_bound_le` bound a binary64 printer and parser through one lemma per callee, from `mulSmall_bound_le` upward, and the integer running sum does the same in `Demos/RunningSum/Charges.lean`.

`Verified.blockCost_eq` gives `8 * words + 56` and `paidGrowCost_eq` gives `32 * ext + 48`.  A build of `count` elements therefore costs `8 * count.toNat + 56`, a bound on `count.toNat` closes it with `omega`, and `UInt64.le_iff_toNat_le.mp` gives that bound in the branch where the count passed its test.  `Verified.sumBelow_const` evaluates the sum that `loopCost_le` leaves when every pass has the same bound.  The finished lemma `∀ x, compiled.f.bound x ≤ B` goes to `trapFree.mono`, with `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Within.mono` for the premise and `Nat.add_le_add_left` for the conclusion, as `Demos.Drone.Verified.drone_trapFree` does.  `Examples.Increment.compute_bound` and `Examples.RemoveZero.compute_bound` bound builds, `Examples.Below100.compute_bound` bounds pushes in a `repeatWhile`, and `Library.Random.Xorshift.jump_bound` bounds a loop of equal passes.

Two tactics mislead on these goals.  `split` on a step charge of the form `CA s + if C s then BA k s else 0` can split an `if` inside `C s`, such as the test of the input's size in the loop's count, instead of the charge's own condition, so `Examples.Below100.compute_bound` proves a lemma about `if c = true then a else 0` and applies it.  `generalize` keeps no fact about the term it replaces, so prove a bound on a count before generalizing it, as `Examples.RemoveZero.compute_bound` does.

A loop whose number of passes the run's values decide, such as a solver's retries of a rejected timestep, gives a bound with no closed form in the sizes of the input alone.  Count its passes with `Verified.loopCost` itself, with the generated condition and step and a charge of one for each pass, and compare the bound with a multiple of that count through `Demos.Euler.Verified.loopCost_le_mul`, under an invariant of the step such as the size of the array in the loop's state.  `Demos.Euler.Verified.advanceStep_bound` bounds the Euler solver's loop of tries in this way by `2 (48 c + 56)` bytes for each try of a timestep on `c` cells, with `Demos.Euler.Verified.stepTries` counting the tries.  The generated condition and step match on the state's tuple, while a count written with projections differs from them, so `loopCost_le_mul` takes both and their equality, which `rintro ⟨_, _, _⟩; rfl` proves.  Apply it with `refine` and named holes, so that Lean takes the generated condition and step from the goal before it elaborates the proofs about them.  Such a closed form can exceed the 65,535 pages of memory for large inputs, since the bound ignores the reuse of freed blocks: a run of the solver that builds two grids for each try fits in 65,535 pages on the 800 grid only for at most 69 tries.  A loop that writes into arrays of its state has a bound that does not depend on its passes, as the `verified-source-costs` entry describes, and `Demos.Euler.Verified.solve_bound` bounds the buffered solver by `3 (48 c + 56) + 16 c + 88` bytes.  The lemma is local to that module, and moving it beside `Verified.loopCost_le` is a separate change.

A bound with no free variable, such as that of a function whose only allocation is an array literal, closes with `rw [compiled.f.bound_eq]; decide`, as `Examples.Lookup.compute_bound` and `Verified.Examples.Forms.pair_bound` do.

A fold's bound is `Verified.foldCost xs init step charge`, the sum of each step's charge along the fold.  A fold whose state pairs a word with an array that each step pushes onto has a bound of the form `min unpaid paid`, and the paid side is `blockCost 0 + creditCost 0 + foldCost … (fun _ _ _ => paidGrowCost 1)`: the empty array's block, its credit, and 80 bytes for each push.  `Examples.RunningMax.foldCost_const` sums a constant charge `c` to `c * xs.size.toUInt64.toNat`, from `Examples.RunningMax.fold_charge_const`, an induction on `Nat.fold_succ` over a fold whose first component gains `c` at each step.  `Examples.RunningMax.compute_bound` takes the paid side with `min_le_right`, rewrites with `foldCost_const`, `Verified.blockCost_eq`, and `Verified.paidGrowCost_eq`, and closes `80 * xs.size + 72` with `omega` and `Nat.mod_le` for `xs.size.toUInt64.toNat ≤ xs.size`.  The two lemmas are local to that example, and moving them beside `Verified.foldCost_zero` is a separate change.
