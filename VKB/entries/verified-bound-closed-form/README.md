# Closed-form allocation bounds

`verified_compile` gives each function `p.f.bound`, the number of bytes by which a call can raise the allocator's `top`, and `p.f.trapFree`, which excludes a trap when that many bytes fit under the memory cap.  The generated bound is a Lean term that sums the charges of the program's loops, calls, copies, and built arrays.  A theorem that a call cannot run out of memory, or the room condition of a process, needs that term bounded by a closed form in the sizes of the input.

Rewrite the bound with its generated equation `p.f.bound_eq` and with the equations of the callees it names, then bound each part.  `Verified.blockCost_eq`, `growCost_eq`, and `paidGrowCost_eq` turn the block charges into linear terms, and `omega` closes the arithmetic.  A loop's charge is `Verified.loopCost`: `loopCost_const` evaluates it when every pass costs the same, `loopCost_le` bounds it from an invariant of the index and the state, and `loopCost_le_upto` needs the cost and the step only at the passes the loop reaches.  `Verified.loopCost_const_le` bounds a loop whose condition costs nothing and whose passes each cost at most `c` by `n * c`, and `Verified.loopCost_le_last` covers such a loop with one costlier pass after which the condition fails.  `loopCost_repeatWhile_le` and `repeatWhile_count_le` bound a counting search by the count at which it stops, and `loop_push_size` gives the size of the array in the first component of a loop's state when each pass extends it by one element.

A loop whose state is a pair or a record has a bound of the form `min unpaid paid`, the smaller of two accountings of its arrays.  Take the paid side with `Nat.min_le_right` and bound it, as `Demos.RunningSum.consumeInput_bound_le` does before it applies `loopCost_le_last`.  Give each bound lemma a second lemma for the size of the result that callers pass on, as `Demos.RunningSumFp.mulSmall_size` is for `mulSmall_bound_le`.  When the closed form is a constant, `LeanExe.Pipeline.ImplementsA.mono` turns `p.f.trapFree` into a statement with that constant, as `Demos.Slew.Bound.slew_trapFree` does with `plan_bound`.

A bound that depends on the size of an array that a loop of the program builds needs that size, which a lemma of the refinement states over the refinement's own form of the loop.  `Demos.Rcv.Refine.rounds_size` bounds the result words of the ranked-choice count's rounds over `roundsGo`, while the generated bound holds the program's `repeatWhile`, and the two forms are equal by unfolding.  `Demos.Rcv.Program.count_bound` states the arithmetic as lemmas over variables, `Demos.Rcv.Program.min_bound` for `min unpaid paid` plus the final growth and an `omega` lemma applied by `exact` for the last term, so that unification matches the generated terms with the variables and the size lemma's term with the program's by unfolding, where `rw` and `omega` would need the terms to agree as written.

The cost of a function that the compiler turns into a tail loop is `Verified.Tail.costAt` of the loop's step and charge, which the generated `cost_eq` states.  Bound it by induction on a count that each continuing step lowers, such as a fuel argument: rewrite with `cost_eq` and `Verified.Tail.costAt_rank_eq`, with `Verified.Tail.rank_lt` applied to the generated `wellFounded` for the decrease, state the step's result in each branch as an equation that `simp` proves from the generated `step` and the branch's conditions, and fold the rest back with `← compiled.f.cost_eq` so that the induction hypothesis applies.  `Demos.Rcv.TabulateProgram.fieldsFrom_cost` bounds the reader's loop over the fields of a line in this way.  `grw` with a callee's bound replaces the callee's cost in a goal `… ≤ c` without restating the callee's arguments, as `Demos.Rcv.TabulateProgram.pushRoundLines_cost` does with the bounds of the printer's callees.

Two compilations of the same functions generate the same bounds, and a closed form proved for one transfers to the other by an equation between the bounds.  Prove the equation by rewriting both sides with the generated `cost_eq` and `bound_eq` equations of the two compilations, the callees' equations first, as `Demos.Rcv.TabulateProgram.count_bound_eq` does for the count in the tabulator's compilation and in its own.  For a tail loop both sides are a `Verified.Tail.costAt` of steps that the compilations define alike, and `Demos.Rcv.Maine.TabulateProgram.tail_cost_congr` equates them whatever the proofs of well-foundedness, after which `Demos.Rcv.Maine.TabulateProgram.tabulate_bound` reuses the Oakland reader's `Demos.Rcv.TabulateProgram.readContest_bound`.  A proof of such an equation by `rfl` unfolds the compiled programs and exceeded the heartbeat limit for the Maine module (header of `Demos/Rcv/Maine/TabulateBound.lean`), while `Demos.Rcv.Process.tabulate_bound_eq` closes by `rfl` the equation between the WASI compilation's bound of `tabulate` and the pure compilation's.

A closed-form lemma can take the generated equations as a hypothesis in place of one compilation's names.  `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.Equations` collects the `cost_eq` and `bound_eq` equations of the order book's `run` and of the functions whose costs its cost includes, for any functions, so that `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.run_bound_le` applies to every compilation of the program that generates them (header of `Demos/OrderBook/Bound.lean`).  `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.compiled_equations` gives the equations of `Demos.OrderBook.Verified.compiled`, and `Demos.OrderBook.Process.runBound_74` gives those of the pure functions of the order book's WASI process.  `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.ite_bound` bounds an `if` of costs plus a constant by an `if` of step results on the same condition, with any two `Decidable` instances, and `Demos.OrderBook.Bound.step_le` applies it with `refine` down the trees of a step's cost and result.

Do not evaluate a bound with `#eval compiled.f.bound x`.  `bound` runs the interpreter of the reflected program, and for the regex matcher at a pattern of one word it had not finished after 10 minutes.  To evaluate a bound at fixed arguments, rewrite it with `bound_eq` and the callees' equations and close the comparison by `decide +kernel`, as `TestSupport.A64.Gpt32.heap_room` does for the room of a call of `layerCheck`, which [the entry on AArch64 Image theorems](../a64-image-theorem/README.md) describes.  Take a tuple argument whole wherever a caller passes the result of a call, and split the branches of a bound in order rather than with `first`, for the reasons that [the entry on large folds and compiled terms](../large-term-unfolding/README.md) records.  The bound assumes that no freed block is reused, so a bound over a whole run grows with every allocation, and a process needs the bounds per step of [the entry on WASI process totality](../wasi-process-total/README.md).

`Demos.Slew.Bound.plan_bound` bounds the slew planner by 68,020,488 bytes, `Demos.Drone.Verified.compute_bound` bounds the drone planner by 2,251,064 bytes, and `Library.Regex.matchWith_bound` gives a bound linear in the pattern's length.  The slew proof bounds the search's rounds with `loopCost_le` and an invariant of the search, and the drone proof rewrites with the equations of `compute` and its three callees before it bounds the number of tables that `forward` builds.  `Demos.RunningSumFp.print64_bound_le` and `parse_bound_le` bound a binary64 printer and parser through one lemma per callee, starting at `mulSmall_bound_le`, and the integer running sum does the same in `Demos/RunningSum/Charges.lean`.

`Verified.blockCost_eq` gives `8 * words + 56` and `paidGrowCost_eq` gives `32 * ext + 48`.  A build of `count` elements therefore costs `8 * count.toNat + 56`, a bound on `count.toNat` closes it with `omega`, and the forward direction of `UInt64.le_iff_toNat_le` gives that bound in the branch where the count passed its test.  `Verified.sumBelow_const` evaluates the sum that `loopCost_le` leaves when every pass has the same bound.  The finished lemma `∀ x, compiled.f.bound x ≤ B` is the argument of `trapFree.mono`, with `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Within.mono` for the premise and `Nat.add_le_add_left` for the conclusion, as `Demos.Drone.Verified.drone_trapFree` does.  `Examples.Increment.compute_bound` and `Examples.RemoveZero.compute_bound` bound builds, `Examples.Below100.compute_bound` bounds pushes in a `repeatWhile`, and `Library.Random.Xorshift.jump_bound` bounds a loop of equal passes.  A bound with no free variable, such as that of a function whose only allocation is an array literal, closes with `rw [compiled.f.bound_eq]; decide`, as `Examples.Lookup.compute_bound` and `Verified.Examples.Forms.pair_bound` do.

Two tactics have unexpected effects on these goals.  `split` on a step charge of the form `CA s + if C s then BA k s else 0` can split an `if` inside `C s`, such as the test of the input's size in the loop's count, instead of the charge's own condition, so `Examples.Below100.compute_bound` proves a lemma about `if c = true then a else 0` and applies it.  `generalize` keeps no fact about the term it replaces, so prove a bound on a count before generalizing it, as `Examples.RemoveZero.compute_bound` does.

A loop whose number of passes the run's values determine, such as a solver's retries of a rejected timestep, gives a bound with no closed form in the sizes of the input alone.  Count its passes with `Verified.loopCost` itself, with the generated condition and step and a charge of one for each pass, and compare the bound with a multiple of that count through `Demos.Euler.Verified.loopCost_le_mul`, under an invariant of the step such as the size of the array in the loop's state.  `Demos.Euler.Verified.advanceStep_bound` bounds the Euler solver's loop of tries in this way by `2 (48 c + 56)` bytes for each try of a timestep on `c` cells, with `Demos.Euler.Verified.stepTries` counting the tries.  The generated condition and step match on the state's tuple, while a count written with projections differs from them, so `loopCost_le_mul` takes both and their equality, which `rintro ⟨_, _, _⟩; rfl` proves.  Apply it with `refine` and named holes, so that Lean takes the generated condition and step from the goal before it elaborates the proofs about them.  Such a closed form can exceed the 65,535 pages of memory for large inputs, since the bound assumes that no freed block is reused: a run of the solver that builds two grids for each try fits in 65,535 pages on the 800 grid only for at most 69 tries.  A loop that writes into arrays of its state has a bound that does not depend on its passes, as [the entry on source forms and costs](../verified-source-costs/README.md) describes, and `Demos.Euler.Verified.solve_bound` bounds the buffered solver by `3 (48 c + 56) + 16 c + 88` bytes.  `loopCost_le_mul` is local to the Euler module, and moving it beside `Verified.loopCost_le` is a separate change.

A fold's bound is `Verified.foldCost xs init step charge`, the sum of each step's charge along the fold.  A fold whose state pairs a word with an array that each step pushes onto has a bound of the form `min unpaid paid`, and the paid side is `blockCost 0 + creditCost 0 + foldCost … (fun _ _ _ => paidGrowCost 1)`: the empty array's block, its credit, and 80 bytes for each push.  `Examples.RunningMax.foldCost_const` sums a constant charge `c` to `c * xs.size.toUInt64.toNat`, from `Examples.RunningMax.fold_charge_const`, an induction on `Nat.fold_succ` over a fold whose first component increases by `c` at each step.  `Examples.RunningMax.compute_bound` takes the paid side with `min_le_right`, rewrites with `foldCost_const`, `Verified.blockCost_eq`, and `Verified.paidGrowCost_eq`, and closes `80 * xs.size + 72` with `omega` and `Nat.mod_le` for `xs.size.toUInt64.toNat ≤ xs.size`.  These lemmas are local to that example, and moving them beside `Verified.foldCost_zero` is a separate change.
