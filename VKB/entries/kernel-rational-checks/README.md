# Bounds checked by kernel evaluation over ℚ

A proof of a numerical function often needs a bound that holds at every real point of an interval: the distance between the program's polynomial and a Taylor polynomial, the tail of a series, a table entry against the function it approximates, or the distance of every double of a binade from a multiple of `π/2`.  Such a bound follows from a checker over ℚ or ℤ that `decide +kernel` evaluates, together with a soundness theorem that turns the checker's `true` into the statement over ℝ.  The entry applies when the bound has no short symbolic proof and its constants are rationals or binary64 words.

`LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyBound.check p lo hi ε d` shifts the polynomial `p`, a list of rationals with the lowest degree first, to the center of `[lo, hi]`, accepts when `Σ |q k| h ^ k ≤ ε` for the half-width `h`, and otherwise bisects at most `d` times along each branch.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyBound.check_sound` gives `|eval p x| ≤ ε` for every real `x` of the interval, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyBound.ratOfWord` writes a binary64 word's exact value as a rational, so that a program's coefficients enter the polynomial as `ratOfWord 0x…`.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Trig.TrigKernelPoly.sin_check` checks the difference between the Taylor polynomial `sinCoeffs 12` and fdlibm's sine kernel in this way, and the Taylor remainder completes the bound.

`LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyChecks.Covers p ε d lo hi ms` is the conjunction of the checks over the intervals that the breakpoints `ms` cut from `[lo, hi]`, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyChecks.bounded_of_covers` turns it into a bound over the whole interval.  Prove each conjunct as its own declaration and assemble the conjunction from them, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Atan.AtanKernelPoly.atan_part_covers` assembles `atan_part_1` to `atan_part_16`.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyChecks.abs_le_weighted` gives a relative bound `|A| ≤ ε W` from two one-sided checks, where `W` is the denominator of a rational approximation or the cosine of `tan = sin/cos`.

For a function that is not a polynomial, `LeanExe.ProofKit.TaylorModel.tm_sound` proves that every Taylor model that `tm` computes encloses its expression, which may combine rationals, polynomials, sums, products, inverses, `exp`, `log (1 + a)`, `sin`, `cos`, and `π`.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.TaylorCheck.exprOk c E ε depth` computes the model of `E` and checks its polynomial with `LeanExe.ProofKit.PolyBound.check`, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.TaylorCheck.exprOk_sound` gives `|E.eval t| ≤ ε` for `|t| ≤ c.r` from the caller's bounds on `π`.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Tgamma.TgammaExpTables.expErr_checked` checks `exp (X log 2/1024)` against its polynomial on `|X| ≤ 5/8` in this way, and `exp_poly` states the result over ℝ.  With `c.r = 0` the check encloses a constant, such as a table entry.

`decide +kernel` can check a finite table at every index in one proposition.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.ExpTableBounds.checked_table_bounds` unfolds `Finite` and checks, for every `j : Fin 128`, that the head and tail words are finite and that the table's scale lies within a relative `2 ^ -100` of `2 ^ (j/128)`, by comparing 128th powers over ℚ.  The proposition names `rationalScale`, the scale computed over ℚ, because the kernel cannot evaluate the real `scale`, and `rationalScale_cast` and `scale_enclosure` in the same module turn the check into the enclosure of `Real.exp (j log 2/128)`.

The kernel normalizes the result of every rational operation, which makes a long shift of coefficients over ℚ slow, and coefficients scaled to integers avoid that cost.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Erfc.ErfcSeriesZ.shiftZ` shifts the series' integer coefficients, `rowBoundZ_eq` proves the integer form equal to the rational one, and the module's header records the cost of one rational row.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.BesselRatio.ishift` does the same for the Bessel functions.

A certificate that a script finds and the kernel can check enters the proof as a literal.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Trig.TrigDistance` holds, for each exponent field from 1022 to 2046, a convergent `(p, q)` computed offline, `certs_checked` checks all of them, and `check_sound` turns a checked certificate into a bound on the distance of `m R / 2 ^ K` from every integer.  `tools/bessel/genpq.py` writes `Library/Numerics/Float64/Bessel/BesselPQ0.lean` and `Library/Numerics/Float64/Bessel/BesselPQ1.lean`, and `tools/arc/asin/mkratio.py` and `mkmid.py` write `Library/Numerics/Float64/Asin/AsinTheoremRatio.lean` and `Library/Numerics/Float64/Asin/AsinTheoremMid.lean`, whose bounds the kernel checks, so that the theorems do not depend on the scripts.

The kernel's cost for one `decide +kernel` grows faster than the work, so one check over a whole interval costs more than its parts checked as separate declarations (`LeanExe/ProofKit/PolyChecks.lean`, header).  A module with too many checks stops at a build's limits.  `Library/Numerics/Float64/Lgamma/LgammaAccSmallH.lean` holds checks of at most 62,000 coefficient operations of the Python model, because modules with more stopped at the runner's memory limit, and `Library/Numerics/Float64/Lgamma/LgammaRootA0.lean` to `Library/Numerics/Float64/Lgamma/LgammaRootA2.lean` prove one range each, because the module of all three passed the 20-minute limit of a build (`Library/Numerics/Float64/Lgamma/LgammaRootA.lean`, header).

Kernel checks elaborated in parallel each hold a large reduction cache and together exhaust the build machine's memory, so `Library/Numerics/Float64/Log2/Log2Power.lean` sets `Elab.async false` to elaborate its declarations in order.  The log-gamma root-range proofs need `--memory-high 10G`, and on a host with about 12 GiB of memory a cold build runs the heaviest gamma and log-gamma checks one worker at a time (`docs/verified-manual.md`, sections "The full check" and "Memory-heavy numerical proofs").  Powers with literal exponents above 256 need `exponentiation.threshold`, as [the entry on closed computations](../closed-computations/README.md) states.

The worked examples are the sine and cosine kernels (`TrigKernelPoly`), the arctangent kernel in parts (`AtanKernelPoly`), the gamma function's tables through Taylor models (`TgammaExpTables`), the exponential's table (`ExpTableBounds`), and the reduction's certificates (`TrigDistance`).  `Library/Numerics/Float64/Trig/README.md`, section "Kernel-checked facts", lists every kernel check of the sine's proof with its declaration.  Most other function READMEs of `Library/Numerics/Float64` have a section of that name, which lists the checks of that function.
