# Accuracy against real functions

A function of the numerical library, such as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.exp`, `Library.Numerics.Float64.Trig.sin`, or `Library.Numerics.Float64.Asin.asin`, returns a binary64 value, and its accuracy theorem states that the value's word lies within a number of representable steps of the nearest-even rounding of a Mathlib function at the input's value.  The theorem then transfers to the compiled module: a call of the export returns the Lean function's word, and the bytes of the emitted file decode to the module.  The entry applies to a new function of this kind and to a program that composes such functions and states their accuracy.

`LeanExe.ProofKit.F64PositiveRounding.roundNearestEven y` is the nearest-even word of a real `y ≥ 0`, with positive infinity from the overflow threshold `2 ^ 1024 - 2 ^ 970` on, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64SignedRounding.roundNearestEven` extends it to negative `y` by negation.  A function with nonnegative results states one step as two inequalities on the encodings, `c.toNat ≤ (roundNearestEven y).toNat + 1` and its converse, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.ExpTheorem.exp_one_step` does for `Real.exp`.  A function with signed results states `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64SignedRounding.WithinSteps n y w`: `w` is not a NaN and lies within `n` steps of the signed reference, counted on a line on which both zeros lie at 0.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Trig.TrigSinCos.sin_within` states `WithinSteps 1 (Real.sin (value x.toBits)) (Trig.sin x).toBits` for every finite `x`.

The proof bounds the distance between the computed value and the function in two parts.  The rounding errors of the operations follow `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64Approx.Approx` through the rules of `F64Approx` or of `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64ErrorTrack`, such as `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64ErrorTrack.add_approx`, as [the entry on binary64 error bounds](../float-error-tracking/README.md) describes.  The approximation error of the polynomial or series comes from a Taylor remainder and a kernel check of the program's polynomial against the Taylor polynomial, which [the entry on kernel checks over ℚ](../kernel-rational-checks/README.md) describes.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.TaylorRemainder` bounds the remainders of `sin`, `cos`, `exp`, `log (1 + x)`, and `arctan`, as `LeanExe.ProofKit.TaylorRemainder.sin_remainder` does for `sin`.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.RealExpError.taylor_error_one` bounds the exponential's remainder on `|x| ≤ 1`, `LeanExe.ProofKit.AsinRemainder.asin_remainder` bounds the arcsine's on `|x| ≤ 1/2`, which Mathlib lacks, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.LogRatioRemainder.log_ratio_remainder` bounds that of fdlibm's `log (1 + s) - log (1 - s)`.  The section "Error budget" of `Library/Numerics/Float64/Trig/README.md` lists such a budget stage by stage, with the lemma of each stage.

A closing criterion turns the total error into steps.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64FullStep.one_step_binades` takes a word and a number between the starts of binades `a` and `b`, where binade 2047 starts at positive infinity, and an error below one and a half spacings of binade `a`, and it gives the two inequalities of one step, overflow included.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64RelativeStep.within_one_relative` applies it to a finite word within `11/8` units of roundoff of `y`, relative to `|y|`, for `|y| ≥ 2 ^ -1000`, which leaves `3/8` of a unit beside the rounding of the last operation.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64UnitStep.unit_step` covers a number of magnitude from `1/2 + 2 ^ -50` to 1 with an error below one and a half spacings of `[1/2, 1)`, and `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64SelfStep.self_step` covers a nonzero word within `|c| / 2 ^ 54` of the number, as when fdlibm's `sin` and `tan` return a tiny input unchanged.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.ExpOneStep.one_step_of_error` is the exponential's criterion: from the range of `exp` that table entry `j` gives and an error below `(7/5) u 2 ^ q H j`, a finite word, or positive infinity when the value reaches `2 ^ 1024`, lies within one step.

The transfer to the module composes three results.  The entry call, which [the entry on specification call theorems](../spec-call-theorem/README.md) describes, gives the Lean function's word from every ready store, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.ExpTheorem.exp_entry_call` does for a module with a table and `Library.Numerics.Float64.Asin.AsinBytes.asin_entry_call` does, with `.ofA` and `nofun`, for a module without one.  Entry calls of a module whose tables lie after large tables raise `maxRecDepth`, as that entry states for the Bessel functions.  `Verified.Ready.initial rfl compiled.initial` makes the initial store ready, and a module with several byte theorems states it once, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Asin.AsinBytes.ready_initial` does.  The byte theorem, such as `exp_bytes_accuracy`, takes the encoding and the decoding from the generated `compiled.bytes`, the export from a lemma proved by `decide +kernel`, and the accuracy theorem under `Finite x.toBits`, and it attaches the accuracy to each call's result.  A module with several exports states one byte theorem for each and their conjunction, such as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Asin.AsinBytes.asin_module_accuracy`, which `file_theorems asin Asin.compiled "…" from AsinBytes.asin_module_accuracy` in `Library/Numerics/Float64/Asin/AsinFile.lean` names, so that the file theorem's axiom report covers every export, as [the entry on file theorems](../file-theorems/README.md) describes.

The native checks of `tests/TestSupport/Numerics` test the same functions.  `tests/TestSupport/Numerics/ExpAccuracy.lean` and its siblings compare the Lean function with references computed in integer arithmetic, such as those of `tests/TestSupport/Numerics/Reference.lean`, which work at 400 bits after the binary point for arguments of magnitude at least 1.  The argument sets include the inputs of the largest known errors of other libraries, listed in `tests/TestSupport/Numerics/WorstCases.lean`.  Each function's README gives its argument sets, the measured distances, and the proved bound (`docs/verified-manual.md`, section "Accuracy checks").

An equation between definitions built from IEEE operations, proved by `rfl`, makes the definitional check unfold `Wasm.IEEE64.add` and the other operations, while `simp only` with the definitions proves it, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Tan.TanOdd.smallW_odd` shows (`Library/Numerics/Float64/Tan/README.md`, section "Proof structure").  On the whole body of `lgammaWith`, `dsimp only` and `simp only` did not finish either, and `extract_lets` names the body's `let` bindings as local definitions, which keeps the term's size (`Library/Numerics/Float64/Lgamma/LgammaBranches.lean`, header).  [The entry on large folds and compiled terms](../large-term-unfolding/README.md) gives the general rule.

Large numbers need lemmas of their own.  `omega` exceeds its recursion limit on facts with coefficients near `2 ^ 52`, and `Library/Numerics/Float64/Tanh/Expm1SubOne.lean` proves them with monotonicity lemmas.  Powers such as `2 ^ 1088` in the context exceed the recursion depth of `omega`, `positivity`, `congr`, `generalize`, and `rw`, and the sine's proof states lemmas over a named variable and proves them by `subst` (`Library/Numerics/Float64/Trig/README.md`, section "Proof structure").  The ProofKit's lemmas for nearest rounding stop at `2 ^ 1022` or `2 ^ 1023`, so a function that rounds in the top binades needs its own, as `Library.Numerics.Float64.Tgamma.TgammaTop.add_top` and `div_top` are for the gamma function.

The exponential's chain is complete in `Library/Numerics/Float64/Exp/ExpTheorem.lean`, with `exp_one_step`, `exp_entry_call`, and `exp_bytes_accuracy`, and `Library/Numerics/Float64/Exp/ExpFile.lean` beside it states the file theorem.  The sine's `sin_within` closes through `Library.Numerics.Float64.Trig.TrigStep.sin_final`, which applies `within_one_relative` and `unit_step`, and through `self_step` for tiny inputs, and its README tabulates the budget.  `Library/Numerics/Float64/Asin/AsinBytes.lean` shows a module without tables that has two exports.
