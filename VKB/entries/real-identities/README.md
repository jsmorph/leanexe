# Identities and certificates over the reals

The entry applies to an identity of a real model that holds only under constraints, such as unit length and orthogonality of vectors, a quadratic relation between two variables, or `sin² φ + cos² φ = 1`, and to a sign property of a matrix that a program certifies from interval data.  Such an identity closes by `linear_combination` once its coefficients are known, and the work lies in finding the coefficients or in a substitution that removes the constraint.  `ring` and `field_simp` alone do not use the constraints, so a goal that holds only under them stays open after either.

The coefficients of `linear_combination e` are those of the identity `goal.lhs - goal.rhs = Σ cᵢ (hᵢ.lhs - hᵢ.rhs)` over the hypotheses `hᵢ`, and for vector identities they follow by hand from the expansions of the vector products, such as `r × (a × b) = a (r · b) - b (r · a)`.  `Demos.Attitude.cross_perp` states that the cross product of two vectors perpendicular to a unit vector `r` is a multiple of `r`, and the coefficients of its unit length and its two orthogonality hypotheses in each component are the components of `a × b`, `a × r`, and `b × r`.  `Demos.Attitude.act_turn` and `Demos.Attitude.Frame.Good.expand` close in the same way, with polynomial coefficients of degree three.  After `field_simp`, the coefficients depend on the normal form that `field_simp` produces, so a goal with divisions takes `linear_combination (norm := skip) e`, followed by `field_simp` and `ring`.  Its certificate `e` then has the form of the goal as written, with its divisions, as in `Demos.Orbit.time_scalar`, which multiplies the equation `kt` by `t / (1 - x ^ 2)`.

An identity in which `sin φ` and `cos φ` appear only through `sin² φ + cos² φ` has two arrangements that avoid a coefficient read from `field_simp`'s output.  The first states the identity exactly, with the term `k (sin² φ + cos² φ - 1)` written out, proves it by `field_simp` and `ring` with `sin φ` and `cos φ` as atoms, and then rewrites with `Real.sin_sq_add_cos_sq`, as `Demos.Avoidance.norm_identity` of `Demos/Avoidance/TwoBody.lean` does.  The second compares the numerators over one denominator with `div_left_inj'` and gives `linear_combination` a coefficient of `Real.sin_sq_add_cos_sq` computed by hand, as `Demos.Avoidance.sinw_hasDerivAt` and `Demos.Avoidance.versw_hasDerivAt` of the same file do.

A rational parametrization removes a constraint that is a conic with a rational point, after which `field_simp` and `ring` close each identity without coefficients.  Izzo's relation `y² = 1 - λ² + λ² x²` with `q = y + λ x` gives `x = (q² - (1 - λ²))/(2 λ q)` and `y = (q² + 1 - λ²)/(2 q)`, which `Demos.Orbit.xy_of_q` states.  `Demos.Orbit.izzo_energy` generalizes `y + λ x` to `q`, substitutes both equations with `subst`, and closes with `field_simp` and `ring`, and `Demos.Orbit.key_f` rests on the same substitution.  The parametrization also gives the converse direction, since it produces the `x` of a prescribed `q`, as `Demos.Orbit.orbit_izzo` uses.

A symmetric matrix with exactly one positive eigenvalue has a certificate in the signs of the pivots of a symmetric factorization with pivoting, without eigenvalues or Sylvester's law of inertia.  `Demos.Attitude.Sym4.OnePos` states that the quadratic form is positive at some vector and negative at every nonzero vector of some hyperplane.  `Demos.Attitude.Sym4.onePos_of_neg` keeps the property from the Schur complement under a negative pivot, `Demos.Attitude.Sym4.onePos_of_pos` derives it from a negative definite Schur complement under a positive pivot, and `Demos.Attitude.Sym4.negDef_of` keeps negative definiteness, each with siblings for `Demos.Attitude.Sym3` and `Demos.Attitude.Sym2`.  `Demos.Attitude.Sym4.onePos_swap` transfers the property across an exchange of the first index with another, so `Demos.Attitude.onePos_of_pivots` holds for every choice of pivots, and a program that chooses its pivots from interval data needs no proof about the choice: its pivots enclose `Demos.Attitude.pivots p1 p2 p3 A` for the indices that it chose, which `Demos.Attitude.QMethod.pivots_enc` states.  The structures have explicit entries, and the completions of the square close by `field_simp` and `ring`.

The worked examples are `Demos.Attitude.cross_perp`, `Demos.Orbit.time_scalar`, `Demos.Orbit.izzo_energy` with `Demos.Orbit.xy_of_q`, `Demos.Avoidance.norm_identity`, and `Demos.Attitude.onePos_of_pivots` with `Demos.Attitude.QMethod.pivots_enc`.  The proofs of `Demos/Avoidance/TwoBody.lean` lie outside the modules that the full check's first step builds, so `entry.json` lists their names under `unimported`.  [The entry on derivatives, inverse functions, and differential equations of real models](../real-model-calculus/README.md) uses these identities to close the equations of derivatives.
