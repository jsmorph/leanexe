# Lemmas that close goals of structure literals

The entry applies when a lemma about an updated structure must close, by `exact` or `apply`, a goal whose side is one structure literal, such as `{ regs := s.regs, ..., pc := s.pc + 4, ..., io := io }`, while unification supplies the lemma's arguments.  `simp` flattens nested updates and reduces the projections of literals, so the goals that a `simp` call or the walk of a trace leaves take this form.  A tactic that ends a walk, such as `dev_end` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/LdrW.lean`, closes these goals with lemmas whose arguments it cannot name.  The entry also applies when a proof states many facts about states after nested updates, whose literals it can keep out of its goals.

State the lemma's side as one update of a variable, `{ s with pc := s.pc + 4, io }`, as `LeanExe.A64.Isla.devRead_io_31` does.  That side elaborates to the literal `{ regs := s.regs, ..., pc := s.pc + 4, ..., io := io }`, and unification with the goal compares the two field by field: `State.regs ?s` against `State.regs s` assigns `?s`, and the field `io` assigns `?io`.  The goal and the lemma's side then have the same constructor and fields, so `exact` succeeds whether or not the lemma's arguments are given.

A nested update, `{ { s with io } with pc := s.pc + 4 }`, elaborates to `have __src := { regs := s.regs, ..., io := io }` followed by a literal whose fields read `__src`.  `exact` of a lemma with that side, with the state and the UART's state left to unification, fails against the flat goal with `Type mismatch`, and the message shows the lemma's `have __src` form against the goal's literal.  The same lemma closes the goal when the UART's state is given and fails when only the state is given, so the failure lies in the assignment of `?io`: unification compares `__src.regs` with `s.regs` through their arguments, solves `{ ?s with io := ?io } =?= s` by structure eta, and so assigns `?io := s.io`, after which the comparison of the field `io` fails and unification does not revisit the assignment.

A proof that states many facts about states after updates keeps the literals out of its goals by naming each update.  `LeanExe.A64.Proc.ret` of `LeanExe/A64/Linux.lean`, and `LeanExe.A64.Proc.enter` and `LeanExe.A64.Proc.goto` of `LeanExe/A64/Proof/Linux.lean`, give a process after the kernel's return from a call, after the first instruction of a call routine, and after a branch, and a lemma for each field, such as `LeanExe.A64.Proc.enter_x`, `LeanExe.A64.Proc.ret_mem`, and `LeanExe.A64.Proc.goto_pc`, turns each fact about the process after a call into one `rw` chain.  `generalize` names the process that a chain of operations builds, and later steps prove facts of the name from its equation, as `LeanExe.A64.run_readPass` and `LeanExe.A64.run_writeLoop'` of `LeanExe/A64/Proof/LinuxMain.lean` do, and [the entry on raw Wasm locals](../local-frame-projection/README.md) names the states of long `wp` proofs in the same way.

The worked example is the macro `dev_end` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/LdrW.lean`, which closes the paths with `Rt` = 31 by `devRead_io_31` with its arguments left to unification.  `LeanExe.A64.Isla.devRead_io_eq`, whose side applies `setRegAt` to `{ s with io }` before the update of the program counter, closes the other paths in the same macro.  `TestSupport.A64.Isla.dev_tcg` of that file rests on lemmas that end with `dev_end`, and [the entry on instruction forms against Isla](../isla-form-proofs/README.md) describes the walk that leaves these goals.
