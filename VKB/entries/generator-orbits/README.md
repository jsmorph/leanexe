# Periods, equidistribution, and jump-ahead of generators

A generator whose step is a map on words, applied `n` times by `LeanExe.loop`, needs theorems about its period, about how often each value occurs over a period, and about a jump-ahead function that computes `n` steps with one pass per bit of `n`.  The linear congruential generator, Park and Miller's generator, and xorshift64 of `Library/Random` prove these properties, and the sampling algorithms of `Library/Random/Sampling` count the word arrays that give each outcome.  The methods below serve a new generator whose step is an affine map modulo 2^64, a multiplication modulo a prime, or a map that distributes over xor.

`Library.Random.Orbit.loop_eq_iterate` turns `LeanExe.loop n x (fun _ s => f s)` into `f^[n.toNat] x`, and each generator's `advance_eq_iterate` restates its loop this way, after which Mathlib's `Function.minimalPeriod` and its lemmas serve.  For the LCG, `2^k` steps of `affine a c` form the affine map with the coefficients `doubled a c k`, which repeated squaring computes (`Library.Random.Lcg.iterate_two_pow`).  Under Hull and Dobell's conditions that map is the identity at `k = 64` and adds `2^63` at `k = 63`, so Mathlib's `minimalPeriod_eq_prime_pow` gives the period `2^64` (`Library.Random.Lcg.minimalPeriod_affine_iff`).  For Park and Miller's generator, Mathlib's `lucas_primality` proves the modulus `2^31 - 1` prime with the witness 16807, `orderOf_eq_of_pow_and_pow_div_prime` gives the multiplier's order in `ZMod (2^31 - 1)`, and `reduce_mod_char` evaluates the powers that both need by repeated squaring (`Library.Random.ParkMiller.prime_modulus`, `Library.Random.ParkMiller.orderOf_multiplier`).  For xorshift, `Library.Random.Orbit.minimalPeriod_eq_of_primes` gives the minimal period `N` of a point that returns after `N` steps and after no `N / q` steps for each prime `q` that divides `N`, and one `decide +kernel` checks those returns for the state 1 (`Library.Random.Xorshift.orbitCheck_eq`, `Library.Random.Xorshift.minimalPeriod_one`).

The kernel computes xorshift's iterates by jump-ahead over tables.  `Library.Random.Xorshift.applyList_cols` states that a map on numbers that distributes over xor is the xor of its columns, its images of the powers of 2, at the set bits of its argument, and `Library.Random.Xorshift.jumpList_pows` computes `f^[m]` by one product per set bit of `m` from the tables of the map's repeated squares.  The tables hold natural numbers, since the kernel computes `^^^`, `/`, and `%` of natural-number literals directly (`Library/Random/Xorshift/Columns.lean`, header), and `jumpList_tables` relates them to the step on words through `stepNat`.

The count of each value over a period follows from a bijection of the counts onto a set of states.  When the orbit of `x` stays in a finite set with as many elements as the minimal period of `x`, `Library.Random.Orbit.existsUnique_iterate` reaches each element at exactly one count below the period, and `Library.Random.Orbit.card_topBits` counts the words with given top bits.  `Finset.card_bij` from the counts to the states then gives the LCG's and xorshift's `advance_topBits`.  A jump-ahead program is proved by induction on its passes: after `k` passes the LCG's state holds the coefficients of `step^[n % 2^k]` and of `step^[2^k]`, and xorshift's holds `step^[n % 2^k] x` and the columns of `step^[2^k]` (`Library.Random.Lcg.jumpPasses_spec`, `Library.Random.Xorshift.jumpPasses_spec`), and `jump_eq` follows at the last pass.

A search that a script runs offline enters a proof as a certificate that the kernel checks.  `Library.Random.Lcg.le_of_certificate` bounds the squared length of every nonzero vector of the LCG's dual lattice from integer matrices `U`, `B`, and `A` with `U B = C` and `B A = δ I`, a bound on each coordinate, and the check of the short combinations within those bounds.  `Library/Random/Lcg/certificates.py` computes the matrices and bounds, `decide +kernel` checks each fact in `spectral_two` and the other spectral theorems, and the theorems therefore do not depend on the script.  For sampling, `Library.Random.Sampling.scale_card` states that each index `i < m` of `scale u m` comes from between `⌊2^64 / m⌋` and `⌈2^64 / m⌉` words, and `Library.Random.Sampling.shuffle_count` multiplies those bounds over the steps of the shuffle through `Fintype.card_piFinset`.

A rewrite of `2^(64 - j)` by the count of words with given top bits also rewrites the `2^(64 - j)` inside the filter's predicate and sends the elaborator past its recursion limit, so the LCG's and xorshift's `advance_topBits` end with `Eq.trans` against `Library.Random.Orbit.card_topBits` instead.  In `Library/Random/Sampling/Scale.lean`, `omega` did not prove bounds whose divisions by the variable `m` stood in the goal, and `Library.Random.Sampling.ceil_diff_bounds` names each division with `generalize` before it calls `omega`.  The same lemma states a general numerator `A`, which `scale_card` instantiates at `2^64`, because `generalize` on the literal `2^64` reached the recursion limit.  `Library.Random.ParkMiller.check_value` rewrites `advance` as an iterate of `step` before `decide +kernel`, since the kernel's evaluation of `LeanExe.loop` takes about eight times as long when the count doubles (its docstring).

The worked examples are `Library.Random.Lcg.step_minimalPeriod`, `Library.Random.ParkMiller.step_minimalPeriod`, and `Library.Random.Xorshift.step_minimalPeriod` for the periods, `Library.Random.Lcg.advance_topBits` and `Library.Random.Xorshift.advance_topBits` for the counts, and `Library.Random.Lcg.jump_eq` and `Library.Random.Xorshift.jump_eq` for jump-ahead.  `Library.Random.Lcg.spectral_two` shows a checked certificate, and `Library.Random.Sampling.shuffle_count` the count of word arrays for each arrangement.  [The LCG's README](../../../Library/Random/Lcg/README.md), [xorshift's README](../../../Library/Random/Xorshift/README.md), and [the sampling README](../../../Library/Random/Sampling/README.md) state each theorem.
