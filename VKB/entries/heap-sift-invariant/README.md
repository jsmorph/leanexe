# Sift invariants of array heaps

A heap in the prefix `a[0, n)` of an array, with node `i` the parent of nodes `2i + 1` and `2i + 2`, changes by sifts: a word moves down by exchanges with its larger child, or up by exchanges with its parent.  A sort, a priority queue, or a graph search built on the heap needs, after each sift, heapify, insertion, or removal, that the prefix is a heap again, that its words are a permutation of those before, and that the rest of the array is unchanged.  The order holds at every node except near the moving word while the sift runs, so the invariant states that exception.

`Library.Sorting.Heapsort.HeapFrom a n L` states the order at every node at or after `L`: each word at `j < n` whose parent `(j - 1) / 2` is at least `L` is at most that parent.  `Library.Sorting.Heapsort.siftDown_spec` proves `HeapFrom (siftDown i n a) n.toNat L` from two premises: the order holds at every node at or after `L` except at the moving node `i`, and, when `L < i`, the children of `i` are at most the parent of `i`.  Its proof recurses with the program's own `termination_by (n - i).toNat`, reads the program's branches through `maxChild` (`siftDown_eq`), and proves both premises for the child at each recursive call.  `siftDown_frame` states the other facts in a separate theorem: the size, the permutation, the words at `n` and beyond, and every upper bound on the words of `a[0, n)`.  `heapify_spec` runs `LeanExe.Dialect.loop_induction` with the invariant `HeapFrom acc n.toNat (n.toNat / 2 - k)`, so the order holds from node `n / 2 - k` after `k` sifts, and `heap_root` gives that the root of a heap is its largest word.

`Library.Queues.BinaryHeap.siftUp_spec` is the dual for an insertion: the order holds everywhere except between the moving node and its parent, and the children of the moving node are at most its parent.  The binary heap's `insert_spec` and `remove_spec` state each operation as the queue's words with one word added or with one copy of the largest word removed, and the removal reuses heapsort's `siftDown_spec`, `siftDown_frame`, and `heap_root` unchanged.  Dijkstra's heap keeps the least key at the root and stores keys and vertices in two arrays: `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.MinHeap` states the order on the keys, `pairs hk hv len` lists the entries as pairs, and `pairs_swap` shows that an exchange of two entries permutes that list through `Equiv.swap` on `Fin len`.  Its own `siftUp_spec` and `siftDown_spec` restate the two-premise form for the reversed order, and `push_spec` and `pop_spec` state the operations.

The proofs record no failure.  The binary heap's order and layout match heapsort's, so it imports heapsort's lemmas, while Dijkstra's heap, with the reversed order and the entries in two arrays, restates both sifts and reuses heapsort's exchange and order lemmas and the binary heap's `parent_toNat` and `sub_one_toNat`.  One sift lemma over an order and a list of entries would serve all three heaps.  Heapsort's sorting invariant combines the heap with a sorted suffix, which [the entry on sorting and selection](../sorted-permutation/README.md) describes, and the binary heap's loop over its operations is in [the entry on operation streams](../operation-stream-refinement/README.md).

The worked examples are `Library.Sorting.Heapsort.heapsort_sorted_perm`, `Library.Queues.BinaryHeap.insert_spec` and `remove_spec`, and `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.push_spec` and `pop_spec`.  `heapsort_sorted_perm` takes the root to the end of the prefix with `swap`, bounds every word of the new prefix by the old root with the last conjunct of `siftDown_frame`, and restores the heap with `siftDown_spec` at `L = 0`.  `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.dijkstra_spec` keeps `MinHeap` in the invariants of its relaxation and main loops and applies `push_spec` and `pop_spec` at each step.
