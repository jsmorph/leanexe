# Total execution of a WASI process

A WASI process runs `begin` and then a step until the step stops, and its total theorem states that the compiled module exits with the specified output for every input within stated limits.  `Verified.Wasi.compiled_process_total_run` gives that theorem from `ProcessTotalPremises`, a host environment that satisfies `hostSpec host`, a well-founded relation on ghost states, and the facts about the initial heap.  The integer and binary64 running sums reach their final theorems this way, and a new process follows the same path.

The compiled process compacts its state after `begin` and after each step: it copies the state, moves the copy down to the heap's base, and resets the top, so that between steps the heap holds the literal pool and one state.  `Verified.Wasi.ProcessStored` states that arrangement and `wp_process_compact` proves that the compaction produces it.  `ProcessTotalPremises` therefore asks for room one step at a time: `beginRoom`, `stepRoom`, and the conditions inside `beginReplies` and `stepReplies` compare the starting top plus a step's charge and the copies of the states before and after it with the memory cap.  `beginBounded` and `stepBounded` are the prefix bounds of the `wasi-effect-charges` entry.  The state's type must satisfy `Verified.Ty.compactable`, which excludes trees, and `Ty.copyCost_mod` shows that the copy of such a state costs a multiple of 8 bytes.

Each application states its charges as an intermediate structure under an invariant and derives it from limits on the input.  `Demos.RunningSum.RunCharges` holds a charge for each state, a charge for `begin`, their prefix bounds, and the room conditions, for the states where an invariant holds.  `Demos.RunningSum.limitCharges` builds it from `InputLimits L D` and the inequality `heap.top + limitMemory L D ≤ 65536 · cap`, through `Sized.copyCost`, `step_sized`, and `limitMemory_room`.  `Demos.RunningSum.initial_terminates_integer` then states the theorem over the limits with that one inequality, and `Demos.RunningSumFp.initial_terminates_fp` does the same for lines of at most `L` bytes.

`Demos.RunningSum.Termination.target_terminates` takes `StepAdvances`, which gives for each step a represented ghost of the final world at which the invariant holds and the rank falls.  The ghost must be the one at which the rank falls, because the host's representation relation is not functional and two replies may give different positions for one world.  The stream lemmas that produce a position therefore also state the fall of the rank at that position.

A budget for a whole run cannot be proved.  The heap model charges every allocation against `top` and credits no release, so the charge of a run grows with every read, and with a host that returns one byte per read no budget below `2^32` bytes covers an input above about one million bytes.  The earlier premise `RunBudget` had this form, and no proof supplied it for any input.  The compaction and the room conditions per step replaced it.
