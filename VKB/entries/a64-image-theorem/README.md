# AArch64 Image theorems

The entry applies when a program that `verified_compile` compiled runs as an arm64 Image of the AArch64 back end and the goal is the Image theorem.  That theorem starts from a boot state whose memory holds the Image file at a base `B`, a multiple of 4096 inside the RAM GiB, with the program counter at `B` and the MMU off.  It concludes that the boot model reaches PSCI's `SYSTEM_OFF` with every store to the UART complete, after the UART received the result's bytes and the status byte 0, or a status byte alone where the program's theorem allows a trap.  The entry also applies when the Image theorem must name a specification, such as Mathlib's `Real.exp` or a filter's outputs over ℤ, in place of the Lean function that the program computes.

The Image's `main` calls one entry of the module on fixed argument words, and the proof first states that run as `LeanExe.A64.ProgramRuns m k args room cap o out T`, where `o` is `.word` or `.array`, `out` is the bytes written, and `T` admits the statuses that may replace the result.  The entry is either a wrapper that builds its inputs from literal arguments, such as `TestSupport.Bare.crcCheck` or `TestSupport.A64.Gpt.scoresCheck`, or a library entry on argument words with equations such as `a.toUInt64 = wa`, as `TestSupport.A64.Floor.floorDiv_runs` takes them.  `LeanExe.A64.program_runs_bounded` gives `T = fun _ => False` from a compiler theorem without a trap and the premise `stackBound m k ≤ 4096 ∨ stackBound m k ≤ room.toNat`.  `LeanExe.A64.program_runs_trapFree` gives `fun c => c = 4`, an overflow of the stack, from the same theorem without that premise, and `LeanExe.A64.program_runs_implements` gives `fun c => c = 1 ∨ c = 4` from `p.f.implements` of a program that may reach `unreachable`.  `LeanExe.A64.ProgramRuns.mono` weakens `T`.

Each of these theorems takes the heap at the module's initial store, `compiled.initial.1`, the precondition at `initStore m cap` under `initialPages m ≤ cap`, and an output lemma: `LeanExe.A64.writes_word`, `LeanExe.A64.writes_float`, or `LeanExe.A64.writes_array` for a result of type `UInt64`, `Float`, or `Array UInt64`, and `TestSupport.A64.Floor.writes_int64` for an `Int64`.  The last argument, `hok : LeanExe.A64.entryOk m k ts args.length o = true`, defaults to `decide +kernel`, and `entryOk` compares the function's parameter types with `ts`, whose default gives `i64` to every argument.  An `f64` argument therefore passes `[.f64]` and proves `hargs : argValues ts args = Scalar.values x` from the word's equation `x.toBits = w`, as `TestSupport.A64.Exp.runs` does, where `hok` follows as the proved fact `TestSupport.A64.Exp.entry_ok`.  The proofs of `runs` for the programs with tables, the Euler solver, the GPT-2 kernels, and the binary32 conversions set `maxRecDepth` to 100,000 for that declaration, as `TestSupport.A64.Crc.runs` does, and the Image files set it for the whole file.

A compiler theorem without a trap, `compiled.f.trapFree`, requires room for the entry's allocations in its precondition.  The proof states `heap_room : (Verified.initialHeap compiled.tables).top.toNat + compiled.f.bound x ≤ 65536 * initialPages compiled.module` and proves it by `decide +kernel`, after a rewrite with the generated `bound_eq` equations, as `TestSupport.A64.Gpt32.heap_room` does, or after a closed-form bound, as `TestSupport.A64.Euler.heap_room` does with `Demos.Euler.Verified.solve_bound`.  `LeanExe.A64.room_initStore` carries the fact to the cap, and a recursion with the depth's guard also proves `compiled.f.fits d x = true` by `decide +kernel`, as `TestSupport.A64.Gcd.rec_fits` does.  For an entry whose theorem names tables of the initial store, `Verified.ImplementsTables.implementsA` turns the theorem into `ImplementsA`, and `Verified.borrowed_nativeStore` applied to `compiled.initial.2.2 i` gives table `i` borrowed at the cap, as `TestSupport.A64.Crc.runs` shows.  An entry whose stack bound exceeds 4096 bytes takes a premise on the room, `5280 ≤ room.toNat` in `TestSupport.A64.Sha.runs` and `stackBound compiled.module 28 ≤ room.toNat` in `TestSupport.A64.Euler.runs`, and the Image theorem discharges it at the Image's room by `decide` or `decide +kernel`.

The Image theorem lies in a module that reads the Image file at elaboration, outside the build, and it applies `LeanExe.A64.image_run_at` to the kernel's check of the file, `bytesOk (genImage m k args room cap o imageBase) XBytes XSize = true`, which [the entry on image files](../image-file-checks/README.md) describes, and to `runs room cap`.  The alignment and size of the room and the check's base close by `decide`, and the premises on `B` and on the boot state are the Image theorem's own.  The statement names the code map and the protected bytes through `LeanExe.A64.codeWords` and `LeanExe.A64.protectedBytes` of `LeanExe.A64.imageProgLength`, and the upper bound on `B` through `LeanExe.A64.imageSize`, so the kernel evaluates them for each program.  `image_run_at` ends in a disjunction of the output `b.core.out ++ out ++ [0]` and a status `c` with `T c` alone.  Where `T` is `fun _ => False`, `rcases ho with ho | ⟨_, hf, -⟩` and `hf.elim` remove the second case, and where `T` admits statuses, `ho.imp` restates them as bytes, as `gcdrec` and `depth` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFiles.lean` do.

A Lean-level equation of the wrapper's value joins the output with a specification through `▸`.  `TestSupport.A64.Gpt.scoresCheck_eq` states `scoresCheck` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/GptScores.lean` as the words of `Demos.Gpt.scores`, and `gptscores` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesGpt.lean` ends with `Gpt.scoresCheck_eq 3 ▸ ho`, as `gptlayer32` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesF32.lean` does with `TestSupport.A64.Gpt32.layerCheck_eq`.  `TestSupport.A64.Exp.one_step` restates `Library.Numerics.Float64.Exp.ExpTheorem.exp_one_step`, an accuracy theorem over every finite double, for a word whose real value is a number `a`, `TestSupport.A64.Sin.within_one` restates `Library.Numerics.Float64.Trig.TrigSinCos.sin_within` in the same way, and the Image theorem existentially quantifies the word that the UART carries.  At the literal input, `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64Bits.toBits_ofBits` and `decide +kernel` give the word of `Float.ofBits`, `unfold CodeLib.IEEE64.Finite; decide +kernel` its finiteness, and `value_of_scaled` with a scaled value checked by `decide +kernel` its real value, as `exp1_accuracy` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesExp.lean` and `sin10_accuracy` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesSin.lean` do.  An exactness theorem whose premise the kernel checks joins the same way: `TestSupport.A64.Cic.check_toInt` applies `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.toInt_cascade_of_width` after `Library.Numerics.Filters.Cic.cic_toList`, and `cic_outputs` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFiles.lean` states the outputs over ℤ.  A specification theorem of the module's own entry applies at the literal arguments with its exclusions by `decide`, as `floordiv_image_spec` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesFloor.lean` applies `Examples.Floor.floorDiv_spec`.

`program_runs_bounded` does not apply to a recursion.  `LeanExe.A64.stackBound` follows calls as deep as the module has functions, so a cycle of calls leaves it at 2^64, as `TestSupport.A64.Gcd.rec_stack_bound` states, and `TestSupport.A64.Gcd.rec_runs` therefore uses `program_runs_trapFree`, whose Image theorem allows the byte 4.  The section "The final theorem" of `docs/a64-bare-metal.md` notes that a bound from the depth guard's limit on calls would remove that status.  The integer form of CIC's input, `TestSupport.A64.Cic.input_toInt`, lies off the chain of `cic_outputs`, since `check_toInt` checks the range premise of `toInt_cascade_of_width` on the words by `decide +kernel`.  `TestSupport.A64.Exp.value_of_scaled` and `TestSupport.A64.Sin.value_of_scaled` are copies of one lemma.

For a program whose `main` reads its arguments from the UART, `LeanExe.A64.InputRuns` takes the place of `ProgramRuns`.  The model describes the PL011's receiver by the platform's record `LeanExe.A64.Rx`, whose functions of the byte's index and of the count of polls give the word of each byte and the value of each read of the flag register, `LeanExe.A64.Rx.fr`, while the state's `LeanExe.A64.Io` holds the output and counts the bytes read and the polls since the last byte.  `LeanExe.A64.Rx.Delivers` and `LeanExe.A64.Rx.Flagged` are predicates on any record, so the theorem's two cases, bytes delivered without an error flag and a flagged byte after unflagged ones, which ends the run with status 65, hold for every timing of the input.  `LeanExe.A64.run_poll` follows the poll loop by induction on the polls that remain before the byte arrives, and `LeanExe.A64.run_readByte` the reads of one byte.  The generator `LeanExe.A64.genProgramWith` takes the entry routine as a parameter, `LeanExe.A64.genProgram` and `LeanExe.A64.genInProgram` are its instances, and `LeanExe.A64.programWith_sim` gives the run of every program of that form from the effect of its entry routine, so the reads join the simulation of the call without a second proof of it.  `LeanExe.A64.input_runs` and `LeanExe.A64.input_runs_bounded` give `InputRuns` from the compiler's theorem, and `LeanExe.A64.image_input_run` joins it to the startup as `image_run` joins `ProgramRuns`.  `TestSupport.A64.Uart.uart_runs` states GCD on two words read from the UART, and `gcd_uart` and `gcd_uart_flag` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesUart.lean` are its Image theorems for delivered bytes and for a flagged byte.  The sections "The simulation theorem" and "The machine model" of `docs/a64-bare-metal.md` describe the entry routine and the receiver's model, which covers the runs in which every byte that the program reads arrives.

The worked examples are `TestSupport.A64.Gcd.runs` and `TestSupport.A64.Gcd.rec_runs` for the bounded and the recursive forms, `TestSupport.A64.Fold.runs` for `program_runs_implements`, `TestSupport.A64.Crc.runs` and `TestSupport.A64.Cic.runs` for borrowed tables, `TestSupport.A64.Euler.runs` and `TestSupport.A64.Sha.runs` for a premise on the room, and `TestSupport.A64.Exp.runs` and `TestSupport.A64.Floor.floorDiv_runs` for argument words.  The Image theorems lie in `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFiles.lean`, `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesEuler.lean`, `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesGpt.lean`, `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesF32.lean`, `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesExp.lean`, `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesSin.lean`, and `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesFloor.lean`, which `tests/baremetal/a64-images.sh` elaborates in the directory of the Images.  Among them, `gcd`, `gcdrec`, `depth`, and `euler` show the forms of `T`, and `cic_outputs`, `gptscores`, `exp1_accuracy`, `sin10_accuracy`, `floordiv_image_spec`, and `average_image_spec` show the joins with specifications.
