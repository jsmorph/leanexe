# Requirements for a safety theorem

The entry applies when a demo or a protocol program starts from a design in English and its requirements must give the theorems that the proofs will state: a protection machine, its framing, and the parties around it with their timing.  Requirements that leave a term undefined, a main property unstated, or an initial value open hand the statement to the prover, and a reviewer then has nothing to compare it with.  `Demos/Bms/requirements.md` and its record of decisions and review, `Demos/Bms/review.md`, are the worked example.

Define each term that a theorem uses, such as good, passing the communication checks, synchronizing, age, start, and reset acceptance, over the history of records and answers up to the one it describes, so that the statement does not restate the machine's code ([the requirements' section on the machine's theorems](../../../Demos/Bms/requirements.md#machine)).  State the main property as a theorem with numbered items, each over segments and answers, as M1's items are, and let a structure with one field for each item hold the conclusion, as `Demos.Bms.Spec.Permitted` does, so that a reviewer compares fields with items.  Every check that an item covers needs a history definition, as check 17 has through the relays' ages (finding 12 of the review).  [The entry on safety theorems over a machine's history](../history-safety-theorems/README.md) gives the Lean definitions of such terms and the state that a history term can need beyond the requirements' tables.

Derive the timing margins in a table from requirements on the other parties, and compare each proved bound with the requirement that it serves.  The bridge's requirements BR1 to BR7 are premises of every statement about the relays, and the table of cases gives the answers and leases after the sampling of a record with the margin of each lease ([the bridge's requirements](../../../Demos/Bms/requirements.md#the-bridges-requirements)).  A bound on every window is dominated by the window that decodes and answers, and it gives no usable baud rate for the polling windows, which must fit in half a byte time, 16,667 cycles at 4,800 baud (finding 6).  B3a therefore bounds the polling windows apart: `TestSupport.M7.Bms.board_loop_poll` gives each of them at most 3,301 steps of the model, by the method of [the entry on bounds for a step's phases](../m7-phase-bounds/README.md).

Check the design against the repository before the requirements are written, and list each gap with its resolution and reason, as [the table of the resolution of the gaps](../../../Demos/Bms/review.md#resolution-of-the-gaps) does for gaps that include the sequence number after a fault, a missing answer, the main safety theorem, the previous `RESET` bit after a board reset, USART3 after an overrun, and the virtual COM port.  A requirement that fixes the route of a proof rests on what the libraries prove for its objects, such as the moment-generating and characteristic functions of a distribution in Mathlib, so list those facts before estimating the proof's cost.  For the law of the gating demo's Mahalanobis distance, the route through a density of the standard Gaussian, which Mathlib lacks, was estimated at 600 to 900 lines, while moment-generating functions, whose integrals Mathlib proves, gave the proof in the 219 lines of `Demos/Gate/Gaussian.lean` (the decision "Route of the Gaussian link" of `Demos/Gate/review.md` and [the entry on laws of random variables](../laws-of-random-variables/README.md)).  Keep the trusted items consistent with the theorems.  B1 includes the initialization's accesses, so the trusted item covers them and B2 confines both the initialization and the windows (finding 7).  B1 and B5 hold for every sequence of values read, so the virtual COM port belongs to the black channel and no property of the bytes that reach USART3 enters a safety claim (gap 6).

A requirement stated over the machine's state restates the program, which is why M1 names only the segments and the answers (gap 3).  A proved bound chosen without the requirement it serves can hold and still serve none, as B3's single bound does, and a trusted base written apart from the theorems can omit what they include, as finding 7 shows for the initialization's accesses.  Before the proof, test the main statement on a model built from the requirements, as [the entry on testing a statement before its proof](../statement-testing/README.md) describes, since the review's model found a run that falsifies a form of M1.

The worked examples are `Demos.Bms.Spec.m1` with `Demos.Bms.Spec.Permitted`, `TestSupport.M7.Bms.board_loop_poll` for B3a, and `TestSupport.M7.Bms.board_safe` for B5, which composes the board's prefix with M1.  The section "Theorems" of `Demos/Bms/requirements.md` gives the framing's, the machine's, and the board's theorems with their codes, and the sections "Trusted base" and "What lies outside the theorems" give what the theorems assume and what they leave out.  The sections "Review" and "Follow-up review" of `Demos/Bms/review.md` give each finding with its verification and response.
