# Proofs over large folds and compiled terms

A proof that a program meets its specification often holds a term that is cheap to state and expensive to reduce: an 80-round fold of a compression function, the padded blocks of a message, the module that `verified_compile` builds, or a call inside a generated bound.  Lean's elaborator and kernel reduce such a term whenever a step asks them to check two forms for definitional equality.  The hashing, regex, and running-sum proofs met this as recursion-depth errors, heartbeat timeouts, and builds that exhausted their memory, and the steps below avoided it.

`rw` tries `rfl` after it rewrites, and on a goal that holds a long fold this `rfl` can reach the recursion limit, where `simp only` with the same equation does not.  A `show` that restates a `match` on a fold, and the closing `rfl` of `congr 1`, unfold the fold as well.  Abstract the fold with `generalize` before a case split, as `Library.Hashing.Sha512.compress_toVec` does with its `LeanExe.loop` of 80 rounds, and replace `congr 1` by `congrArg` applied to the outer function, as `SHS.Equiv.SHA512.Foldl.Fused.spec_compress_eq_fused` does with `congrArg (addH H)`.  State a lemma about a fold for an arbitrary step function rather than the concrete one: `Library.Hashing.Sha256.foldl_append` takes any `c`, and its first version, stated for `Impl.compress`, unfolded the compression until the heartbeat limit.  Write the program's additions in the order of the specification or of the implementation it is compared with, because `ac_rfl` timed out reconciling two orders on the terms of the SHA-512 round.

A few rewrites need the opposite choice.  `simp` cannot rewrite a count inside `Nat.fold` when the function's type depends on the count, while `rw` can, as `Library.Hashing.Sha256.hashFrom_iv` rewrites the block count with `rw [bytes_size] at e`.  `simp [f]` does not unfold a definition by well-founded recursion applied to a cons, while `unfold f` does, as the regex proofs found with `Spec.run`.  `simp only` with `↓reduceIte` reduces a chain of tests on literals only with `decide := true`.

Split a proof that mixes word arithmetic, the regions of a string, and lengths into lemmas that each do one of them.  The first proof of SHA-3's padding did all three at once, and `set` and `simp` rewrote the lengths into forms that no longer matched.  The accepted proof states the zeros of pad10*1 in closed form (`Library.Hashing.Sha3.SpongeProof.pad101_zeros`), equates the padded string with any bit function that agrees on three regions (`padded_of_regions`), and gives the program's bit with the byte count written as `n = 8 Q + ρ` for `omega` (`bit_padded`).  The SHA-3 step lemmas split over the 25 lanes, one case takes 1.4 seconds of `simp`, and the four lemmas raise `maxHeartbeats` to 1,000,000 locally.

A compiled module or a generated bound must never reach the kernel as a term to reduce.  Take a tuple argument whole in a lemma over a generated bound when callers pass the result of a call: `Demos.RunningSumFp.sciLayoutW_bound_le` takes its pair `p` whole, and its first form, over `(neg, t, kb)`, made the elaborator match the caller's `shortest Q E 1074 low` by structure eta, after which the kernel reduced `shortest` on a symbolic word.  Split the branches of a bound in order rather than with a `first` combinator that tries `Nat.zero_le`, which made the elaborator reduce `decRound`'s bound toward 0 until the heartbeat limit (`Demos.RunningSumFp.decRound_bound_le`).  Rewrite a store with `Verified.Wasi.PureTransport.erase_restore` before a kernel check rather than restating it with `show`, as `Demos.RunningSumFp.initial_memoryCap` does, because the `show` made the elaborator unfold the compiled module past its recursion limit.  State a frame lemma for an arbitrary array, as `LeanExe.ProofKit.BorrowedKept.borrowed_kept` is, so that checking it never evaluates the size of a literal table.

When a step fails this way, look first for a lemma applied at a term that the kernel must reduce to match it.  The bisection of `print64_bound_le` in scratch files exceeded its 12 GiB memory limit even with every hypothesis of the failing branch replaced by `sorry`, because the cause was the application of the layout's lemma, not a hypothesis.  Run such bisections under a memory limit.

The kernel can fail the same way inside `verified_compile`.  For `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.PrimeFactors.countFactors`, defined by well-founded recursion with a measure that multiplies by `2 ^ 64`, the command reported `(kernel) deep recursion detected` while every generated declaration seemed to exist, because Lean checks theorems asynchronously and keeps a theorem that fails the kernel as an axiom.  `#print axioms` of a later theorem then names generated theorems such as `p.f.meaning_eq` and `p.f.fixed`, and listing the program's constants whose `ConstantInfo` is an `axiomInfo` names every failure.  The kernel had unfolded the source function before the reflector's definitions, whose height was 1.  The reflector now gives each meaning definition a height above its value's with `Verified.Reflect.addBoundDefinition` and states the tail equations through `Sum.elim_inl`, `Sum.elim_inr`, and `Verified.Tail.fixed_restate`.  A proof that relates a generated constant to a well-founded function should do the same, rewriting with an equation lemma instead of closing by `rfl`, and `set_option maxRecDepth` and `set_option Elab.async false` did not help.
