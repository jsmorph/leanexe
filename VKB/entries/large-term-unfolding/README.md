# Proofs over large folds and compiled terms

A proof that a program meets its specification often holds a term that is cheap to state and expensive to reduce: an 80-round fold of a compression function, the padded blocks of a message, the module that `verified_compile` builds, or a call inside a generated bound.  Lean's elaborator and kernel reduce such a term whenever a step requires them to check two forms for definitional equality.  Such reductions cause recursion-depth errors, heartbeat timeouts, and excessive memory use, as in the hashing, regex, and running-sum proofs, and the steps below avoid them.

`rw` tries `rfl` after it rewrites, and on a goal that holds a long fold this `rfl` can reach the recursion limit, where `simp only` with the same equation does not.  A `show` that restates a `match` on a fold, and the closing `rfl` of `congr 1`, unfold the fold as well.  Abstract the fold with `generalize` before a case split, as `Library.Hashing.Sha512.compress_toVec` does with its `LeanExe.loop` of 80 rounds, and replace `congr 1` by `congrArg` applied to the outer function, as `SHS.Equiv.SHA512.Foldl.Fused.spec_compress_eq_fused` does with `congrArg (addH H)`.  State a lemma about a fold for an arbitrary step function rather than the concrete one: `Library.Hashing.Sha256.foldl_append` takes any `c`, and a version stated for `Impl.compress` unfolds the compression until the heartbeat limit.  Write the program's additions in the order of the specification or of the implementation it is compared with, because `ac_rfl` times out reconciling two orders on the terms of the SHA-512 round.

A few rewrites need the opposite choice.  `simp` cannot rewrite a count inside `Nat.fold` when the function's type depends on the count, while `rw` can, as `Library.Hashing.Sha256.hashFrom_iv` rewrites the block count with `rw [bytes_size] at e`.  `simp [f]` does not unfold a definition by well-founded recursion applied to a cons, while `unfold f` does, as in the regex proofs about `Spec.run`.  `simp only` with `↓reduceIte` reduces a chain of tests on literals only with `decide := true`.

Split a proof that mixes word arithmetic, the regions of a string, and lengths into lemmas that each do one of them.  In a proof of SHA-3's padding that does all three at once, `set` and `simp` rewrite the lengths into forms that do not match.  The library's proof states the zeros of pad10*1 in closed form (`Library.Hashing.Sha3.SpongeProof.pad101_zeros`), equates the padded string with any bit function that agrees on three regions (`padded_of_regions`), and gives the program's bit with the byte count written as `n = 8 Q + ρ` for `omega` (`bit_padded`).  The SHA-3 step lemmas split over the 25 lanes, one case takes 1.4 seconds of `simp`, and these lemmas raise `maxHeartbeats` to 1,000,000 locally.

The kernel must never have to reduce a compiled module or a generated bound.  Take a tuple argument whole in a lemma over a generated bound when callers pass the result of a call: `Demos.RunningSumFp.sciLayoutW_bound_le` takes its pair `p` whole, and a form over `(neg, t, kb)` makes the elaborator match the caller's `shortest Q E 1074 low` by structure eta, after which the kernel reduces `shortest` on a symbolic word.  Split the branches of a bound in order rather than with a `first` combinator that tries `Nat.zero_le`, which makes the elaborator reduce `decRound`'s bound toward 0 until the heartbeat limit (`Demos.RunningSumFp.decRound_bound_le`).  Rewrite a store with `Verified.Wasi.PureTransport.erase_restore` before a kernel check rather than restating it with `show`, as `Demos.RunningSumFp.initial_memoryCap` does, because a `show` makes the elaborator unfold the compiled module past its recursion limit.  State a frame lemma for an arbitrary array, as `LeanExe.ProofKit.BorrowedKept.borrowed_kept` is, so that checking it never evaluates the size of a literal table.  When a step fails this way, look first for a lemma applied at a term that the kernel must reduce to match it.  A bisection of `print64_bound_le` that replaces every hypothesis of the failing branch by `sorry` fails in the same way, because the cause is the application of the layout's lemma rather than a hypothesis.

The kernel can fail the same way inside `verified_compile`.  If the reflector's definitions had height 1, the kernel would unfold a source function defined by well-founded recursion before them, and for `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.PrimeFactors.countFactors`, whose measure multiplies by `2 ^ 64`, the command would report `(kernel) deep recursion detected` while every generated declaration seemed to exist, because Lean checks theorems asynchronously and keeps a theorem that fails the kernel as an axiom.  In such a failure, `#print axioms` of a later theorem names generated theorems such as `p.f.meaning_eq` and `p.f.fixed`, and listing the program's constants whose `ConstantInfo` is an `axiomInfo` names every failure.  The reflector therefore gives each meaning definition a height above its value's with `Verified.Reflect.addBoundDefinition` and states the tail equations through `Sum.elim_inl`, `Sum.elim_inr`, and `Verified.Tail.fixed_restate`.  A proof that relates a generated constant to a well-founded function should do the same, rewriting with an equation lemma instead of closing by `rfl`, and `set_option maxRecDepth` and `set_option Elab.async false` do not help.
