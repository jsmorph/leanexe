# Claims and the theorems that state them

The entry applies to every sentence of a README, a docstring, a comment, a guide, or a report that says what a program or an artifact does: that it computes a result, stays within an allocation bound, never stops, or keeps a safety property.  A reader takes such a sentence as proved, so a sentence that says more than its theorem makes an unproved claim.  The reviews of the library's specifications check each comment and docstring against its theorem, as [the entry on independent review](../independent-review/README.md) describes.

Each claim names the theorem that states it and follows the theorem's wording: "at most" for an upper bound, and the premises of the call theorem, such as room for the generated bound and the memory cap.  `Library.Sorting.Quicksort.quicksort_implements` bounds the rise of `top` by the generated bound, and `Library.Sorting.Quicksort.quicksort_bound` states that bound as the cost of one block of 128 words, so a claim about the storage beyond the array names both, as `Library/Sorting/Quicksort/README.md` does.  A property that no theorem states names its basis instead.  `Library.Trees.Avl.insert_implements` bounds the rise of `top` by a generated bound that charges no block for a node that a step or a rotation rebuilds and 80 bytes for the new node, and no theorem relates the blocks of the result to those of the argument.  The header of `Library/Trees/Avl/Compiled.lean` therefore attributes the loops that keep the call stack from growing to the compiler's rules, which the manual states, and says of the blocks only what the generated bound charges.

A property proved of the Lean function reaches an artifact's theorem through a lemma and never through the reader's reasoning.  `Demos.Bms.Program.m1_prefix` states M1 for every complete answer in a prefix of the bytes that the framing writes, and `TestSupport.M7.Bms.board_safe` composes it with the board's prefix theorem, so B5 states M1 of the bytes that leave the board (section "Further decisions" of `Demos/Bms/review.md`).  Without that lemma the safety claim about the board would rest on a reader's joining of two theorems whose terms differ: the specification's run over segments and the board's run over register accesses.

A claim about a running artifact separates what the kernel checked, what is trusted with its evidence, and what tests showed.  [The trusted base](../../../docs/trusted-base.md) lists for each kind of artifact what its theorem depends on without proof and how the project checks each item, and a theorem's conclusion holds of the running artifact only if every listed item does what its table says.  [The BMS requirements' table of what lies outside the theorems](../../../Demos/Bms/requirements.md#what-lies-outside-the-theorems) names in the same way what no theorem covers, among them the bridge, the residual errors of the CRC, replayed records, physical quantities, and real time.

The reviews in `Library/Sorting/Quicksort/review.md`, `Library/Trees/Avl/review.md`, and `Library/Graphs/Dijkstra/review.md` record the kinds of sentence that say more or less than a theorem.  A storage limit attributed to the bound's equation alone omits the call theorem that applies the bound, and a statement that a module allocates only the new node or reuses the tree's nodes has no theorem behind it.  A docstring that states a conclusion its theorem lacks, as the Dijkstra review records of an empty heap, or that calls positions arcs, misleads a reader who does not read the statement.  A sentence that begins "in particular" after a theorem's items claims a consequence of them, which the reviewer checks against the items (finding 12 of `Demos/Bms/review.md`).

The worked examples are `TestSupport.M7.Bms.board_safe` with `Demos.Bms.Program.m1_prefix`, the README of quicksort with `quicksort_implements` and `quicksort_bound`, and the header of the AVL module's compilation.  [The entry on requirements for a safety theorem](../requirements-for-theorems/README.md) states the requirements' claims as numbered theorems, and [the entry on the output of request programs](../request-output-prefix/README.md) gives the prefix theorems that a board's claim composes.
