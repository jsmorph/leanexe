# Comparisons that make the kernel evaluate

The entry applies when the elaborator accepts a declaration and the kernel's check of it runs out of time or memory, reports `(kernel) deterministic timeout` or `(kernel) deep recursion detected`, or passes once `set_option debug.skipKernelTC true` skips it.  The cost then comes from a comparison of two terms that the kernel settles only by evaluating one of them, and a large literal or a large block of code under the evaluated term makes the evaluation expensive.  The code records such comparisons in the reflector's proofs, in the Isla walks of the Cortex-M7 model, in the sign proof of `lgamma`, and in the Sail proofs of the floating-point equations.  [The entry on large folds and compiled terms](../large-term-unfolding/README.md) covers the elaborator's own reductions, which skipping the kernel's check does not remove.

The kernel compares two projections of structures through the projected terms, without reducing those terms first, and a flattening by `LeanExe.Pipeline.Flat.flat` puts the terms of a structure under such projections.  When one side is a beta redex, whose head is a lambda, and the other is its reduction headed by a definition such as `ite`, the kernel unfolds `ite` before it reduces the redex, and the iota reduction that follows evaluates the condition's `Decidable` instance (`Verified.Reflect.reflect`, its comment on beta redexes).  When the two sides have different head constants, the kernel unfolds the head of greater definitional height first, and both at equal heights.  A type hint that `Lean.Meta.mkExpectedTypeHint` builds is `id` of the term, so the kernel compares a hint with an `if` by unfolding the `if`, of height 2, before `id`, of height 1, and evaluates the condition's instance (docstring of `Verified.Reflect.assignFamilyEquation`).  A typed `let`, `let v : T := e; v`, states a term at its type with no head to unfold, since the kernel removes the `let` by zeta reduction before it compares, and `Verified.Reflect.reflect` passes the arrays of a recursive record to their coercion lemmas under a `let` for that reason: against `id` of the value, the kernel would unfold `Verified.Expr.denote`, whose height is greater, and evaluate the field's source, such as an `if`'s condition (its comment at that coercion).  A `simp` call that reduces projections of a structure instance forces the same work when the kernel checks the reduced type against the unreduced one (`LeanExe/M7/Isla/Arith.lean`, header).  A projection such as `r.2.2.2` compared with a different term makes the kernel unfold the projection and evaluate `r` with `whnf` (the section comment before `Library.Numerics.Float64.Lgamma.LgammaSign.sgnOf`).  A `simp` lemma proved by `rfl`, such as `Nat.add_zero`, leaves no proof step, so the kernel checks the rewritten term by unfolding (docstring of `LeanExe.A64.Isla.nat_add_zero`).

The kernel computes natural-number arithmetic directly only on literals, so a term that adds a large literal to a variable, or multiplies a variable by a large power, evaluates one successor at a time.  For an `if` on `UInt64` words, the reflector's comment counts the steps of the instance's evaluation as linear in the value of a literal added to a word, and a structure-valued `if` on `a + 2^63 < b + 2^63` with a redex on one side of the comparison times out.  [The Sail README's section on the proofs](../../../tools/sail/README.md#the-proofs) records the same evaluation of `N * 2 ^ 1074` with a variable `N`, which reached the kernel's recursion limit, and the M7 walks met it in the overflow terms `r + r / 2^31 * (2^128 - 2^32)` of `AddWithCarry` and in the products of a branch offset's fields and powers of 2.  The forcing comparison causes the evaluation and the literal sets its cost, so look for the comparison before rewriting the literals.

A closed term of natural numbers forces the same evaluation.  Before it compares the arguments of two applications with the same head, the kernel computes a term without free variables as a literal where it can, and it puts both operands of `Nat.add` in weak head normal form, so two closed sums that differ anywhere inside, by the binder type of a lambda or by a beta redex in a type that the kernel infers, are evaluated in full.  The bound pass of `verified_compile` met this in sums that hold `Verified.loopCost`, whose evaluation derived every state of the loop again from the start, so a lemma about such a sum takes its costs as variables and states the term it rewrites, as `Verified.loopCost_eq_zero` does with the hypotheses `∀ s, CA s = 0` and `∀ i s, BA i s = 0` (its docstring).  A proof by `rfl` that the reflector builds for variables carries its statement: `Eq.refl` of a match's alternative has the type `alt … = alt …`, so an equation built from it ends at the substituted body, and a later step relates that body to the match by unfolding the matcher and evaluating the discriminant.  `Verified.Reflect.casesEq` avoids that step by attaching its equation with `Lean.Meta.mkExpectedTypeHint` (its comment at the proof by `refl`).

Two loops whose steps are compiled pattern matches compare cheaply only when the matches are the same terms.  The kernel's check of `Library.Numerics.Float64.Bessel.BesselYnTheorem.yn_eq` ended in deep recursion, and under a raised limit ran for 15 minutes without finishing, when the code's `LeanExe.repeatWhile`, whose step matches on the state, met a restated loop, and also when `dsimp` with its default structure eta rewrote those matches (its docstring).  The proof reduces the `let`s with `dsimp (config := { etaStruct := .none }) only`, turns the code's final match into an equation, and replaces the step through `Library.Numerics.Float64.Bessel.BesselYnTheorem.step_ext`, whose hypothesis holds by `rfl` on each state.  A raised `maxRecDepth` does not help such a check, since the depth comes from the evaluation that the comparison forces.

To keep the comparison out of the kernel's path, state an equation outside the flattening or the projection, where the kernel reduces a redex before it compares the sides, and apply the flattening or the projection afterwards with `congrArg`, as `Verified.Reflect.meaningAt` proves `φ (name ps) = φ (F y)` from `name ps = F y`.  Prove an equation over variables in place of the forcing values and apply it to the values, which the kernel substitutes without comparing them, as `Verified.Reflect.assignFamilyEquation` does for the fields of a record of a recursive type, whose check compared each field's `if` with the record's family at its index and timed out (its docstring, and `tests/verified/TreeFieldConditions.lean`).  Give a proof argument the binder type that the kernel checks, the lemma's binder with the earlier arguments substituted without beta reduction, which `Verified.Reflect.LemmaApp.argType` computes and `Verified.Reflect.LemmaApp.assignHint` uses as a type hint, since a hint stated with the reduction makes the kernel compare it with the redex.  Rewrite a forcing term during the proof with a lemma stated over variables, as `LeanExe.M7.Isla.awc_v` rewrites the overflow test of `AddWithCarry` into a condition on the signs of 32-bit values.  Name a large computed quantity by a variable with an equation and rewrite the term to the name, as the branch theorems of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean` do with the offset and the target, whose equations `LeanExe.A64.Isla.Opaque` hides from `omega`.

To keep definitional unfolding away from a projection of a large block, name the projected component by a regular definition, which the kernel compares through its argument, and prove lemmas that read it through the block's branches.  `Library.Numerics.Float64.Lgamma.LgammaSign.sgnOf` names the last component of `lgamma`'s fast-path block, and `ite_sgn`, `m1_sgn`, `m3_sgn`, and the tactic `sgn_step` read it through the block's `if`s and its pair and triple matchers without reducing a discriminant.  Replace a `rfl` lemma of a `simp` set by the same statement with a proof, as `LeanExe.A64.Isla.nat_add_zero` proves `n + 0 = n` by `omega`.  The replacement has a limit: `simp` rewrites an argument on which a later argument's type depends with `dsimp`, which applies only lemmas proved by `rfl` (`Lean.Meta.Simp.congrArgs`), so a lemma proved by `omega` leaves `n + 0` in such an argument.  `isla_simp` of `LeanExe/A64/Isla/Check.lean` therefore keeps `Nat.add_zero`, so that its rewrites still reach those arguments, while the simp sets of the walks use `nat_add_zero`.  A projection lemma of a state proved by `rfl` costs the same unfolding when `simp` rewrites with it, so the lemmas of `LeanExe/M7/Proof/Straight.lean`, such as `LeanExe.M7.State.r_withPc` and `LeanExe.M7.State.zFlag_setNZCV'`, split the state with `cases s` before `rfl`, and `simp` uses each as an equation.  An unfolding would leave the kernel to check that a field of the state equals the value that the lemma names, and when the value is a sum of a word and a large numeral, as `CMP` with `#0` gives, that check can unfold the numeral one successor at a time (the comment before `r_withPc`).  On Talos's side of the Sail proofs, equations of words, which the kernel compares argument by argument, replace definitions that it would evaluate (`fromInt_nat` and `tSqrtU` in the section on the proofs).

The kernel caches nothing from one declaration to the next, so an evaluation that one comparison forces recurs in every generated theorem that makes the comparison, and the check of each stays fast while the module's total grows.  A comparison of a list of `Verified.Sig` literals with a list of the form `f.sig :: S`, which the type of `Verified.Prog.cons` gives, evaluates `Verified.Func.sig`, and with it `Verified.Expr.aborts` and `Verified.Expr.depthCalls` over the function's body.  The check of an explicit chain of `Verified.Prog.cons` evaluates the signature of every function below its top, and the lemmas of `Verified.Reflect.tableChain`, stated over `.cons f rest`, evaluate the signature of every function above the one they reach.  The bound definitions, `p.f.trapFree`, and `p.f.bound_eq` therefore each evaluate a share of the program, and the kernel's time of `verified_compile` grows with the number of functions times the program's size: the checks of `Demos.Conjunction.Verified` sum to 566 s and those of `Demos.Sgp4.BoxVerified` to 6,479 s on one thread.  While `Verified.Reflect.addDefinition` gave the generated definitions height 1, every `p.f.implements` and `p.f.aligned` also evaluated the byte literals of the whole program (`Verified.Expr.literals` at each node), because the kernel unfolded the compiler's functions before the generated constants.  The options `trace.profiler` and `trace.profiler.threshold` print each declaration whose check takes longer than the threshold as `[Kernel] typechecking declarations [NAME]`, and `Lean.Kernel.enableDiag` with `Lean.Kernel.getDiagnostics` counts the definitions that the kernel unfolds while it checks one declaration.

To locate the step, elaborate the declaration with `set_option debug.skipKernelTC true`, for diagnosis only: when it then passes quickly, the kernel's check is the cost.  Then check in the kernel, one at a time and under a small heartbeat limit, each argument type of the proof term that differs syntactically from its binder's type, until one fails.  A bisection by truncated proofs or by `sorry` misleads when a hypothesis that no later step uses drops out of the proof term, since the kernel then never checks the step that built it, so each truncated proof must use its last hypothesis.  In the bisections of `lgamma`'s sign proof and of the M7 walk of `SUB{S}`, truncated proofs that left such a hypothesis unused passed and pointed away from the failing step.

`Library.Numerics.Float64.Lgamma.LgammaSign.sign_code` reads the sign of `lgamma`'s fast path through `sgn_step`, the worked example of a wrapped projection.  `subReg_agrees` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/AddWithCarry.lean` walks with `awc_v_c`, and `bcond_agrees` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean` walks with the named offset and target.  Both modules read their traces at elaboration, and [the entry on instruction forms against Isla](../isla-form-proofs/README.md) describes them.  `Verified.Reflect.reflect`, `Verified.Reflect.meaningAt`, and the reflector's uses of `Verified.Reflect.LemmaApp.assignHint` show the same rules inside `verified_compile`.
