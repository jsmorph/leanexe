# Derivatives, inverse functions, and differential equations of real models

The entry applies to the real model of a program: a function's derivative, the inverse of a strictly monotone function and its derivative, the uniqueness of a solution of an ordinary differential equation, and the monotonicity or continuity of a function that an integral defines.  These facts carry the theorems of a program to statements about the physical quantities of its request, such as Newton's equation for a two-body motion, and they use Mathlib's calculus without reference to binary64.  The interval programs and their enclosure theorems meet these facts only through the real functions that both name, so a fact of this layer serves every program over the same model.

Combine derivatives with the forms of Mathlib's lemmas for functions written as lambdas, and close with an equation of the derivatives.  `HasDerivAt.mul` states the derivative of the product of functions `c * d`, and `HasDerivAt.fun_mul`, which the attribute `@[to_fun]` generates in `Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/Deriv/Mul.lean`, states it for `fun y => c y * d y`, as `HasDerivAt.fun_add`, `HasDerivAt.fun_sub`, and `HasDerivAt.fun_inv` do for their operations.  `HasDerivAt.congr_deriv` then takes an equation of the derivatives, which `ring`, or `field_simp` followed by `linear_combination`, proves, and Lean checks the equality of the functions by definitional unfolding, as `Demos.Orbit.Newton.e_deriv` and `Demos.Gate.Model.G_succ` show.  `HasDerivAt.neg` states the derivative of the function `-f`, so the goal holds `(-fun t => …) x`, which `Pi.neg_apply` reduces, as `Demos.Gate.Model.G_one` does.  `convert h using 1` against a goal whose function is a definition leaves, besides the equations of the functions and of the derivatives, goals that equate two instances, such as `Real.instAddCommGroup = Real.normedCommRing.toAddCommGroup`.

Define the inverse of a continuous, strictly increasing, surjective function `f : ℝ → ℝ` as `Function.invFun f`.  `Function.invFun_eq` gives `f (g y) = y` from surjectivity, `Function.leftInverse_invFun` gives `g (f x) = x` from injectivity, and `Monotone.continuous_of_surjective` gives the continuity of `g`, which is monotone and surjective.  `HasDerivAt.of_local_left_inverse` then gives the derivative `(f' (g y))⁻¹`, with `Filter.Eventually.of_forall` applied to the first equation as the local inverse, as `Demos.Orbit.conicAdvance_hasDerivAt` does for the advance of the universal variable at a time.  `Demos.Avoidance.root_hasDerivAt` of `Demos/Avoidance/TwoBody.lean` does the same for the root of Kepler's equation, and `Demos.Orbit.conic_newton` composes such a derivative with `HasDerivAt.comp` to show that a closed form solves Newton's equation.

Prove the uniqueness of a solution of an ordinary differential equation from Mathlib's theorems for Lipschitz fields.  A linear field on `Fin 6 → ℝ` becomes a continuous linear map by `LinearMap.toContinuousLinearMap`, whose `lipschitzWith` gives the Lipschitz premise of `ODE_solution_unique_univ`, as `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.flow_unique` shows, and `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.flow_add` follows from that uniqueness rather than from trigonometric identities.  A field that is continuously differentiable where the solutions stay, such as Newton's `(r, v) ↦ (v, -μ r/|r|³)` away from `r = 0`, is Lipschitz on a neighborhood of each state by `ContDiffAt.exists_lipschitzOnWith`, and `ODE_solution_unique_of_eventually` makes the set of times at which two solutions agree open.  `isClosed_eq` makes the set closed, and `IsClopen.eq_univ` on a connected interval finishes the proof without a global Lipschitz constant, as `Demos.Orbit.newton_unique` does.  A structure without a norm, such as `Demos.Orbit.V3`, enters through an embedding into `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)`: `Demos.Orbit.toE` applies `WithLp.toLp`, `Demos.Orbit.norm_toE` relates the norms through `EuclideanSpace.norm_eq`, and `Demos.Orbit.hasDerivAt_toE` carries derivatives of the coordinates through `hasDerivAt_pi` and `EuclideanSpace.equiv`.  `Demos.Orbit.newton_unique` holds for solutions on every open, preconnected set of times, for the reason that [the entry on satisfiable hypotheses and complete conclusions](../non-vacuous-statements/README.md) gives.

Taylor's theorem of order two with the Lagrange remainder follows from Rolle's theorem applied twice, without Mathlib's iterated derivatives.  `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.taylor_two` applies `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.rolle_between`, which takes `exists_hasDerivAt_eq_zero` for two points in either order, first to the remainder of the expansion and then to its derivative.  A trajectory with impulsive burns has a velocity that jumps at each burn, so a form that needs derivatives applies only between burns.  On a panel with no burn time inside, `Demos.PassiveSafety.path_smooth` replaces the trajectory by `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.smooth`, the closed form from the initial state plus the closed forms of the burns at or before the panel's start, which has derivatives at every time.  `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.Motion` states the six derivative equations of any functions of time, `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.smooth_motion` proves them for `smooth` from `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.flow_hasDerivAt` and `HasDerivAt.fun_sum`, and `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.sep_derivs` derives the first and second derivatives of the squared distance from `Motion` alone.  The program decides which burns to include by comparing their times with the panel's ends, and `Demos.PassiveSafety.straddles_window` turns a loop that finds no burn strictly inside the panel into `Demos.PassiveSafety.Window`, the hypothesis of `path_smooth`.  `Demos.PassiveSafety.smooth_panel` joins these facts with the interval forms that [the entry on interval inclusion proofs](../interval-enclosures/README.md) describes.

An integral representation whose integrand is monotone in the parameter proves the monotonicity of a function that closed forms with branches define.  `intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt` proves the representation of each branch in `Demos/Orbit/Lambert.lean`, and `intervalIntegral.integral_lt_integral_of_continuousOn_of_le_of_exists_lt` compares the integrals, as `Demos.Orbit.Lambert.tof_strictAntiOn_pos` does.  For continuity at a point where the closed forms are `0/0`, replace the integrand by one that is continuous on the whole plane and equal to it near the point, such as `Demos.Orbit.Lambert.integrandSafe` with the denominator `√(max q (1/2))`, apply `intervalIntegral.continuous_parametric_intervalIntegral_of_continuous'`, and conclude with `ContinuousAt.congr`, as `Demos.Orbit.Lambert.tof_continuousAt_one` does.  `Demos.Orbit.Lambert.strictAntiOn_join` joins two strictly monotone pieces on each side of a point through continuity at it.  A set integral that increases strictly with a set follows from `MeasureTheory.setIntegral_sdiff₀`, which writes the difference of the integrals as an integral over the difference of the sets, and from `MeasureTheory.setIntegral_pos_iff_support_of_nonneg_ae` with a product of two open intervals inside that difference, whose measure `MeasureTheory.Measure.prod_prod` and `Real.volume_Ioo` compute, as `Demos.Conjunction.Model.pc_strictMonoOn` does.

Definitions that make identities hold by definition keep a model's proofs uniform across cases.  `Demos.Orbit.U2` and `Demos.Orbit.U3` are `(1 - U0)/β` and `(χ - U1)/β` for `β ≠ 0`, with the polynomial forms at `β = 0`, so `Demos.Orbit.U0_add_U2` and its sibling for `U1` and `U3` hold by the definitions, and only the derivatives of `U0` and `U1` and `Demos.Orbit.U1_sq` need a proof by cases on the sign of `β`.  The lemmas of the path, the distance, the time, and Newton's equation then hold for ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas by one algebra (the row "Time along an orbit" of `Demos/Orbit/review.md`), and the motion relative to a state, without orbital elements, covers circular orbits, at which elements are undefined.

The worked examples are `Demos.Orbit.newton_unique`, `Demos.Orbit.conicAdvance_hasDerivAt` with `Demos.Orbit.conic_newton`, `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.flow_unique` with `Demos.PassiveSafety.Model.flow_add`, `Demos.Orbit.Lambert.tof_strictAntiOn_pos` and `Demos.Orbit.Lambert.tof_continuousAt_one`, `Demos.Conjunction.Model.pc_strictMonoOn`, and the derivatives of `Demos/Gate/Model.lean` and `Demos/Orbit/Newton.lean`.  `Demos.Orbit.newton_unique` and `Demos.Orbit.conic_newton` together imply that the closed form of a conic is the one solution of Newton's equation from its state.  [The entry on identities and certificates over the reals](../real-identities/README.md) proves the algebraic steps that these proofs leave to `ring` and `linear_combination`.
