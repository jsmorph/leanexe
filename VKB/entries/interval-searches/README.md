# Searches over interval enclosures

The entry applies to a dialect program that locates a radius, a root, a time, or a region by repeated interval evaluations: a loop that grows a bracket, a bisection, an adaptive march over a window, a subdivision of cells level by level, a queue of cells under a budget of splits, or a branch-and-prune search over boxes.  Its theorem states that an accepted result contains every point of interest, or that a dropped cell holds none, for the real model to which [the entry on interval inclusion proofs](../interval-enclosures/README.md) and [the entry on one source for a real formula and its interval program](../enclosure-def/README.md) relate each evaluation.  The program decides each step from the endpoints of accepted intervals, so the loop invariants concern those intervals and the real points that they contain, never the binary64 values that the search visits.

Prove the search once for any evaluator and any real function, and equate each compiled search with the generic one.  `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.Encl H E P` states that an accepted `E r` belongs to a finite `r ≥ 0`, contains `P (value r)`, and implies the model's facts `H`, and the loops of `Demos/Conjunction/AccuracySoundness.lean` take the evaluator `E` as a parameter.  `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.growWith_inv`, `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.raiseWith_below`, `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.lowerWith_above`, and `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.search_sound` prove the invariants once, and `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.diskSearch_eq` and `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.lineSearch_eq` equate the circular and the linear searches, which call concrete evaluators, with the generic loops by `rfl`.  The input tests around a search are proved once in the same way.  `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.guarded` states the tests of the circular error around any search, `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.circularErrorWith_eq` and `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.circularErrorAdaptWith_eq` state by `rfl` that the programs with equal and with adaptive panels are such tests around their searches, and `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.guarded_sound` proves the tests and the exchange of axes for any search whose accepted results hold the radius.  The theorem of the program with adaptive panels therefore changed neither the statement of `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.circularError_sound` nor the bytes of the module `accuracy` (the decision "Input tests of the circular error" of the section "Adaptive panels" of `Demos/Conjunction/review.md`).  The existence of the root needs no limit of the model function at infinity when the program certifies one point below the target and one at or above it.  `Demos.Conjunction.Model.root_between` takes a function continuous and strictly increasing on `[0, ∞)` and gives the root between the two points from the intermediate value theorem and its uniqueness from the monotonicity, and `Demos.Conjunction.Model.radiusIn_of` packages both as `Demos.Conjunction.Model.RadiusIn`.

For a strictly monotone function whose enclosure decides the side of the target only at some points, run two bisections: one raises the point at which the enclosure shows the value above the target, and the other lowers the point at which it shows the value below.  Each loop keeps one invariant about one point, which `LeanExe.Dialect.loop_induction` proves, as `Demos.Orbit.raise_spec` and `Demos.Orbit.lower_spec` show, and when the points that the enclosure cannot decide form one interval around the root, the two loops end at its edges.  Each search treats an undecided probe as lying on its other side, so an undecided region away from the root moves a point past the root.  Izzo's closed forms lose their precision near `x = 1` over a region up to `3·10⁻⁵` wide, and a first midpoint there gave intervals of `x` up to 0.63 wide.  `Demos.Orbit.bracket` therefore evaluates the enclosure once at `x = 1`, where `T = (2/3)(1 - λ³)` holds exactly, and starts both searches from the half of the bracket on the root's side, which `Demos.Orbit.bracket_spec` proves (rows "Search" and "Starting bracket" and finding N2 of `Demos/Orbit/review.md`).  A single bisection that stops at the first undecided midpoint is sound and returns intervals up to 690 times wider, which only a test with a limit on the width detects (finding 7 of the same review).  The program tests `finite m` and `a < m ∧ m < b` at each midpoint and tests its brackets at run time, so that `Library.Numerics.Interval.lt_iff_value` turns each comparison into one of reals without a lemma about comparisons of values that are not finite.

An adaptive march over a window that accepts or refines one panel at each step needs one invariant for soundness: the covered part `[0, t]`, with `t` finite, bounds the function from below.  `Demos.PassiveSafety.search_covered` proves it for every panel function whose interval encloses the function on the panel, so one lemma serves the searches over time and over phase.  Two tests of the program keep the invariant free of floating-point facts: `Demos.PassiveSafety.panelEnd` returns `t + h` only when the sum is finite, and `Demos.PassiveSafety.march` accepts a panel only when its end lies at or above its start.  Without the tests the proof would need the monotonicity of the rounded sum `t + h`.  A march that accepts a panel as soon as its lower end meets the threshold returns a bound near the threshold whatever its enclosure: for the passive-safety check's safety ellipse it returned 50.06 m against a sampled minimum of 92.59 m.  `Demos.PassiveSafety.accepts` therefore also requires the lower end `ℓ` of a panel above the smallest step to reach `(√M − acc)²` for the caller's accuracy `acc`, where `M`, which `Demos.PassiveSafety.record` lowers, is the least lower end of the panels' midpoint intervals over every search of the same kind, so that the returned bound is at least `√M − acc`.  `Demos.PassiveSafety.march_covered` uses only the threshold, so the accuracy and the record stay outside the invariant and its proof.  A record shared across the scenarios' searches needed 23 to 50 percent fewer panels than a record for each search at accuracies of 1 m and 0.1 m, because a scenario whose minimum lies far above another's accepts its panels at once.  An accuracy of 0 refines every stretch of decreasing midpoint bounds to the smallest step and exhausts any fuel (the decisions "Refinement past the threshold", "Reference of the accuracy", and "Accuracy 0" and finding 3 of the section "Review of the panel enclosure and the accuracy" of `Demos/PassiveSafety/review.md`).

An adaptive march also encloses an integral without an array of panels.  Each of the marches of `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.pcAdaptWith` accepts a panel whose interval is at most a tolerance `τ` wide and otherwise shortens it, so its panels have about equal widths, which minimizes the total width for a fixed number of panels when a panel's width grows as the square of its length.  A march's state is the covered end `t`, the next length, the sum, and two counters, and one invariant, `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.Covers`, states that an accepted sum encloses the integral over `[-r, t]`.  `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.marchStep_covers` proves it for every tolerance, length, and number of steps, without a fact about their binary64 values, because the program tests `t ≤ b` before it accepts a panel and takes `b = t + h` only when the sum is finite and below the end, as the passive-safety march does.  The last permitted step accepts the panel from `t` to the end whatever its width, so a march of `F` steps evaluates at most `F` panels and ends with `[-r, r]` covered or stopped.  The tolerance for a budget of `K` panels comes from an earlier march: a panel of width `w` splits into about `√(w/τ)` panels at the tolerance `τ`, so `τ = (Σ √w / K)²`.  The estimate fails in either direction when the earlier march was coarse, so the program runs a coarse march and two estimated ones, `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.marches_used` bounds their panels by the budget, and the result is the part common to the sums of the last two, which `Demos.Conjunction.Adaptive.meet_sound` proves to contain the integral.  In the design's model, the third march ran out of steps on 36 of 144 inputs at 256 panels, and with the common part the width stayed within 1.26 times that of equal panels, against 16.5 times without it.  In the same model, a tolerance estimated from the number of the earlier march's panels left up to 96 percent of the budget unused, because the panels that the march lengthened across the tails stay one panel each at every tolerance (the section "Measurements" of the adaptive panels in `Demos/Conjunction/requirements.md`).

Equal widths give the least total width among partitions into `N` panels, but they change only the constant of the total's decrease as `1/N`, which the order of the panel enclosure sets.  Where a panel of length `L` has about the width `L² g` for a rate `g`, equal widths give a total of about `(∫ √g)² / N` against `2R (∫ g) / N` for equal panels, and the ratio of the two, at most 1 by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, is small only when `g` is concentrated.  For the probability of a disk without a bias the ratio is 0.95 at `p = 0.9` and 0.89 at 0.99, and the narrowest single march was 4 to 11 percent narrower than equal panels (the section "Adaptive panels" of `Demos/Conjunction/requirements.md`).  Measure the ratio on a model before choosing adaptive panels over a panel enclosure of higher order.

A search that refines an array of cells level by level, appends the children of each kept cell, and drops a cell by a proved test instantiates `Demos.Screening.loop_cover`.  For `LeanExe.loop cells.size.toUInt64 #[] fun i acc => step cells[i.toNat]! acc` with a step that keeps the accumulator as a prefix, `Demos.Screening.Extends`, the lemma states that every element of the result has a property that each step gives the elements it appends, and that a cell with a property `In` leaves an element with a property `Out` when each step that reads such a cell appends or keeps one.  `Demos.Screening.loop_size_le` bounds the result's size by the number of cells times the most elements that one step appends.  The bisection in time, `Demos.Screening.isolateLevel_inv`, and the branch-and-bound over two anomalies, `Demos.Screening.pathLevel_inv`, instantiate both lemmas, with `In` the property that a cell holds a close point and `Out` that it holds the point.  The conversion `cells.size.toUInt64.toNat = cells.size` needs `cells.size < 2 ^ 64`, and a test of a level's new size against a cap compares the size modulo `2 ^ 64`, so the invariant of each search carries a bound of `2 ^ 20` on the size, which keeps the next level's size, at most four times as large, within both.

A search that splits cells under a budget of splits needs a running count of them, which the step of `Demos.Screening.loop_cover` cannot keep, and a level-by-level loop copies every kept cell.  `Demos.Avoidance.search` keeps the cells in an append-only array with the index of the next cell to evaluate, in a `LeanExe.repeatWhile` whose fuel `2S + 1` suffices to evaluate every cell, since `S` splits append `2S` cells to the first.  Each child carries the interval of its parent's evaluation, so the search may stop at any step: `Demos.Avoidance.finish` adds the intervals of the cells left unevaluated to the hull, and no theorem about finishing is needed.  The invariant `Demos.Avoidance.Inv` states that every point of the box lies in a cell at or after the next index or is covered by the hull, `Demos.Avoidance.search_inv` proves it with `LeanExe.Dialect.push_get_lt` and `LeanExe.Dialect.push_get_size` for the appended cells, and `Demos.Avoidance.finish_sound` gives the inclusion (the decisions "Order of the search" and "Iteration bound" of `Demos/Avoidance/review.md`).  A cell's lower end reaches a threshold only where the function varies little over the cell, so a search that shows a value at or above the threshold only by a cell's lower end spends its budget on a wide box: with an earlier cell evaluator, 4,096 splits of the box of `±1` m/s never showed it.  The interval at the cell's center, a point box, showed it in 25 evaluations when each split cell had one and in 19 when every third depth had one, and its point is the witness of the theorem that verdict 1 means a value at or above the threshold, `Demos.Avoidance.authorize_above`.  For a cube, three splits of the longest side halve each side once, so a probe at every third depth probes once for each halving of every side, and the longest-side split with such probes took 257, 443, and 1,065 evaluations where an octree split with a probe in each split cell took 235, 469, and 1,009 (the decisions "Split rule" and "Unsafe burns" of the same review).

A root of `g x = 0` whose left side rises with slope at least `s > 0` has an enclosure that needs no accuracy of the approximation: evaluate the residual over the input box at any binary64 point `e`, and widen `e` by the residual's magnitude over a lower bound of the slope.  `Demos.Frames.Geodetic.bijective_of_growth` gives the root's existence and uniqueness from continuity and the growth bound `s (x₂ − x₁) ≤ g x₂ − g x₁`, `Demos.Frames.Geodetic.bound_of_growth` gives `|x − x₀| ≤ |g x|/s` for the root `x₀`, and `Demos.Frames.widen_encloses` turns them into an enclosure for every `e`, as `Demos.Sgp4.Kepler.root.encloses` does for Kepler's equation.  `Demos.Frames.Geodetic.lag_mono` and `Demos.Frames.Geodetic.ratio_mono` derive the growth bound of a function with a square root in a denominator by cases on signs, without derivatives.  The interval quotient `e − K/S` of a residual `K` and a slope bound `S` is no enclosure, since the bound can lie far below the slope, and the reviewer of the frames requirements found a point at which it excludes the root (finding 2 of `Demos/Frames/review.md`).

The soundness of a branch-and-prune search does not depend on its rule for splitting a box, so the rule can follow the geometry of the region.  The geolocation search measures a box's longitude by the largest `|cos φ|` of the box, so that the box's size bounds its extent on the ground.  With the longitude's extent times the equatorial radius alone, the search split boxes near a pole far below the resolution and stopped at its cap (the row "Size of a box" and finding 7 of `Demos/Geolocation/review.md`).  A theorem about a search can state its conclusions for every function within the margins that the search tests, so that one proof covers the model and its perturbations.  `Demos.Events.Parts` and `Demos.Events.Sound` state an event's functions, their rate bounds, and their margins, the theorems of `Demos/Events/Isolation.lean` hold for every function within the margins of the event's functions on the window, and `Demos.Events.events_near` applies them to the shadow of every trajectory within the padding.

The number of panels that a fuel-bounded search evaluates equals the least fuel with which it finishes, since more fuel leaves a finished search unchanged, so a binary search over the fuel counts them with the program's own functions, as `TestSupport.PassiveSafety.Cases.used` does without instrumenting the program.  `TestSupport.PassiveSafety.Cases.panels` repeats the scenario loop of the check's `scanStep` with these counts and passes each search's record to the next.  A comparison of methods by the least panel count at which a search returns status 0 overstates a gain when an accepted result can still be wide.  At 64 panels the adaptive radius search for a bias of 100 standard deviations returned `[100.9, 572.6]` around a radius of 101.29, because the lower end of the probability's enclosure stayed below `p` from the radius out to 604, so the second bisection never lowered its upper end.  `TestSupport.Conjunction.AdaptiveCases.leastPanels` therefore reports both the least count with status 0 and the least with `hi - lo ≤ 0.01 lo` (finding 1 of the section "Adaptive panels" of `Demos/Conjunction/review.md`).

The worked examples are `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.diskSearch_sound` with `Demos.Conjunction.Accuracy.search_sound`, `Demos.Orbit.bracket_spec` with the two bisections, `Demos.PassiveSafety.search_covered`, `Demos.Screening.isolateLevel_inv` and `Demos.Screening.pathLevel_inv` with `Demos.Screening.loop_cover`, and `Demos.Frames.widen_encloses`.  The isolation of event windows in `Demos/Events/Isolation.lean` applies `Demos.Screening.loop_cover` and `Demos.Screening.loop_size_le` to cells that are spans of time.  `Demos/Conjunction/AdaptiveSoundness.lean` proves the adaptive panels, `Demos/Avoidance/Soundness.lean` the queue, and `tests/TestSupport/PassiveSafety/Cases.lean` and `tests/TestSupport/Conjunction/AdaptiveCases.lean` count panels.  These files, `Demos/Events/Isolation.lean`, `Demos/Events/Soundness.lean`, and the programs of the adaptive panels and the avoidance search lie outside the modules that the full check's first step builds, so `entry.json` lists their names under `unimported`.
