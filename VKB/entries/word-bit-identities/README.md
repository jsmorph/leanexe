# Identities of words built from shifts and masks

Programs for the verified compiler compute on `UInt64` words, while their specifications often state the same computation on `BitVec`, on 32-bit words, or one bit at a time.  The proofs then require equations between words built from shifts, masks, ors, and xors: a rotation, a byte swap, a masked byte, or a 32-bit operation computed in the low half of a 64-bit word.  The hash libraries and the regex matcher prove such equations, and the methods below recur across them.

For an identity at fixed amounts, reduce it to bits.  The forward direction of `UInt64.toBitVec_inj`, or of its `UInt32` and `UInt8` forms, turns the goal into an equation of bit vectors, `BitVec.eq_of_getLsbD_eq` turns that into one goal per bit `i`, and `simp only` with the `toBitVec` lemmas of each operation followed by `interval_cases i <;> simp` closes it.  `Library.Hashing.Words.bswap_eq`, `byteOf_masked`, `byteOf_marked`, and `byteOf_low` are proved this way, and each raises `maxHeartbeats` to 800,000 or 1,600,000 for its 64 cases.  Do not use `bv_decide`.  It adds an axiom that trusts compiled code, while the library's theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

The programs rotate with two shifts and an or: `Library.Hashing.Words.rotr` in SHA-512, `Library.Hashing.Sha256.rot` in SHA-256, and a `rotl` in each of the SHA-3, SipHash, and XXH64 programs.  A transcription that rotates with `BitVec.rotateLeft` matches the program through one lemma per amount, proved by `apply UInt64.toBitVec_inj.mp` and `simp [rotl, Spec.rotl, BitVec.rotateLeft_def]`, as `Library.Hashing.SipHash.Proof.rotl_13` and `Library.Hashing.XxHash64.Proof.rotl_1` are.  `sipRound_eq` and `round_eq` then rewrite with those lemmas.  For a variable amount, `SHS.Equiv.SHA512.Bridge.toBitVec_rotr` gives the bit vector of the shift form and `Library.Hashing.Sha3.RoundProof.bit_rotl` gives each bit of the rotated word, both for an amount strictly between 0 and 64.  At 0 the form gives `x ||| x`, because Lean and Wasm take shift amounts modulo 64.

A 32-bit algorithm runs on 64-bit words without a change to the verified compiler.  `Library.Hashing.Sha256` holds each 32-bit word in the low half of a `UInt64` and ignores the high half.  Truncation by `UInt64.toUInt32` commutes with addition and the bitwise operations, so only a right shift or a rotation needs the high half cleared first, which `Library.Hashing.Sha256.low32` does.  `low32_toUInt32`, `shr_toUInt32`, `rot_toUInt32`, and `xor_ones_toUInt32` state the truncations, and `bigSigma0_toUInt32` shows how a sigma function that masks its argument once becomes the 32-bit function.

A word that joins bit fields that do not overlap may join them with `+` in place of `|||`, and then each byte of the result reduces to arithmetic on `toNat` that `omega` decides without a case split on bits.  `LeanExe.Requests.push40` joins `(w <<< 8) + (v >>> 56)`, and `LeanExe.Requests.toNat_shl8_add` and `LeanExe.Requests.toNat_shl56_add` state such joins as `w.toNat % 2 ^ 56 * 256 + z.toNat` and `y.toNat % 256 * 2 ^ 56 + w.toNat / 256` through `UInt64.toNat_add`, `UInt64.toNat_shiftLeft`, and `UInt64.toNat_shiftRight`.  `LeanExe.Requests.wordBytes8_shl` and `LeanExe.Requests.wordBytes8_shr` then prove each of a word's eight bytes by `UInt8.toNat_inj`, `simp` with `UInt64.toNat_toUInt8` and `Nat.shiftRight_eq_div_pow`, and `omega`, which gives `LeanExe.Requests.push40_bytes` and `LeanExe.Requests.unshift40_bytes` without `bv_decide`.  `omega` does not reason about `|||`, so the same joins with `|||` need a lemma that equates the or of disjoint fields with their sum before it applies.

For a word that holds a set of positions, `Library.Regex.Bits.bit` reads one position through `Nat.testBit`.  `bit_or`, `bit_and`, and `bit_one_shiftLeft` give the members of a union, an intersection, and a singleton, `shiftRight_and_one` turns the compiled membership test `(s >>> q) &&& 1 == 1` into `bit`, and `ext` proves two words equal from their members.  `Library.Hashing.Sha3.RoundProof.bit` is a second copy of `bit`, with `bit_xor` and `bit_complement`.  These lemmas serve statements over a variable position, where a case split on 64 bits does not apply.

A specification that states the bits of natural numbers as arithmetic, bit `j` of `n` as `n / 2 ^ j % 2`, reaches the bitwise operations through one lemma.  `ClightN.Proof.bit_cases` of `LeanExe/Native/ClightN/Laws.lean` takes numbers `A`, `B`, and `C` whose bits satisfy `C.testBit j = g (A.testBit j) (B.testBit j)` for a Boolean function `g`, which `Nat.testBit_and`, `Nat.testBit_or`, and `Nat.testBit_xor` give for `&&&`, `|||`, and `^^^`, and a relation `f` that holds of every two Booleans and their image under `g`, read as 0 and 1.  It concludes `f` of the three bits `A / 2 ^ j % 2`, `B / 2 ^ j % 2`, and `C / 2 ^ j % 2`.  `ClightN.Proof.land_bits`, `ClightN.Proof.lor_bits`, and `ClightN.Proof.lxor_bits` apply it with the relations `z = x * y`, `z + x * y = x + y`, and `z = (x + y) % 2`, each checked on the four pairs by `cases x <;> cases y <;> rfl`, and close with `omega`.

A program may keep a set of numbered items as a mask, with bit `i - 1` for item `i`, while its specification keeps an increasing list of the items.  `Demos.Rcv.Refine.testBit_mask` states that bit `j` of `Demos.Rcv.Spec.mask s` is set exactly when `j + 1 ∈ s`, for an increasing list of positive numbers, through Mathlib's `Nat.bitIndices_sum_map_two_pow`, and `Nat.eq_of_testBit_eq` then compares two masks bit by bit.  `Demos.Rcv.Refine.members` lists the items of a mask in increasing order, `Demos.Rcv.Refine.bit_test` turns the program's test `(m >>> i) &&& 1 != 0` into `m.toNat.testBit i`, and `Demos.Rcv.Refine.loop_members` and `Demos.Rcv.Refine.loop_members_filter` state that a `LeanExe.loop` over the positions below `n`, whose step changes the state only at the members of the mask, or at those that meet a predicate, is a fold over those members.  `Demos.Rcv.Refine.members_remove` gives the members after the program removes a set with `&&&` and the complement of its mask.  `Demos.Rcv.Refine.decideRound_eq` and `Demos.Rcv.Refine.go_eq` use these lemmas to equate the ranked-choice program's rounds over masks with the specification's rounds over lists.

A program that computes a map that is linear over GF(2) one bit at a time equals a table program for every argument when its step is linear and the table holds the map on each byte, with no evaluation over the arguments.  `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.bitStep` takes a step of CRC-32's division without a branch, `(r >>> 1) ^^^ ((r &&& 1) * 0xEDB88320)`, and `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.select_xor` proves the product linear: `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.and_one_cases` splits each operand's low bit into 0 or 1, and `decide` closes the four closed goals.  `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.bitStep_xor` follows by `UInt64.shiftRight_xor` and `ac_rfl`, and `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.iterate_xor` extends it to `bitStep^[j]`.  `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.iterate_even` states through `toNat` that steps of a register whose low `j` bits are zero divide it by `2 ^ j`, and `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.iterate_table` checks the 256 entries of the table by `decide +kernel`.  `Library.Hashing.Crc.Bitwise.crc32Reg_eq` then splits the register `x` as `(x ^^^ (x &&& 0xFF)) ^^^ (x &&& 0xFF)` and equates eight steps with `Library.Hashing.Crc.byteStep crc32Table`.  The step case of `iterate_xor` rewrites with `Function.iterate_succ_apply` by `rw`.  With `simp only [Function.iterate_succ_apply]` before the same `rw [bitStep_xor, ih]`, the kernel's check of the theorem exhausted its memory and reported `(kernel) deterministic timeout`, for a cause not located, while the same `simp only` call on the equation of the iterates alone passed.

A lemma stated for `UInt64.ofNat n` does not rewrite a literal such as `1377 : UInt64`, which Lean elaborates as `OfNat.ofNat 1377` rather than as `UInt64.ofNat 1377`.  State such a lemma for a word variable, which a literal instantiates, as `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaRound.lit_le` restates `UInt64.le_iff_toNat_le` with explicit arguments for a comparison of a word with a literal.  The FMA's rounding proofs reach a literal's value through `lit_le` and `UInt64.toNat_sub_of_le`, as `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaRound.assemble_spec` does with 2047 and `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaRoundPack.normal_right` does with 1377.
