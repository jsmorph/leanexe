# Identities of words built from shifts and masks

Programs for the verified compiler compute on `UInt64` words, while their specifications often state the same computation on `BitVec`, on 32-bit words, or one bit at a time.  The proofs then meet equations between words built from shifts, masks, ors, and xors: a rotation, a byte swap, a masked byte, or a 32-bit operation carried in the low half of a 64-bit word.  Five hash families and the regex matcher prove such equations, and the recipes below recur across them.

For an identity at fixed amounts, reduce it to bits.  `UInt64.toBitVec_inj.mp`, or its `UInt32` and `UInt8` forms, turns the goal into an equation of bit vectors, `BitVec.eq_of_getLsbD_eq` turns that into one goal per bit `i`, and `simp only` with the `toBitVec` lemmas of each operation followed by `interval_cases i <;> simp` closes it.  `Library.Hashing.Words.bswap_eq`, `byteOf_masked`, `byteOf_marked`, and `byteOf_low` are proved this way, and each raises `maxHeartbeats` to 800,000 or 1,600,000 for its 64 cases.  Do not use `bv_decide`.  It adds an axiom that trusts compiled code, while the library's theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

The programs rotate with two shifts and an or: `Library.Hashing.Words.rotr` in SHA-512, `Library.Hashing.Sha256.rot` in SHA-256, and a `rotl` in each of the SHA-3, SipHash, and XXH64 programs.  A transcription that rotates with `BitVec.rotateLeft` meets the program through one lemma per amount, proved by `apply UInt64.toBitVec_inj.mp` and `simp [rotl, Spec.rotl, BitVec.rotateLeft_def]`, as `Library.Hashing.SipHash.Proof.rotl_13` and `Library.Hashing.XxHash64.Proof.rotl_1` are.  `sipRound_eq` and `round_eq` then rewrite with those lemmas.  For a variable amount, `SHS.Equiv.SHA512.Bridge.toBitVec_rotr` gives the bit vector of the shift form and `Library.Hashing.Sha3.RoundProof.bit_rotl` gives each bit of the rotated word, both for an amount strictly between 0 and 64.  At 0 the form gives `x ||| x`, because Lean and Wasm take shift amounts modulo 64.

A 32-bit algorithm runs on 64-bit words without a change to the verified compiler.  `Library.Hashing.Sha256` holds each 32-bit word in the low half of a `UInt64` and ignores the high half.  Truncation by `UInt64.toUInt32` commutes with addition and the bitwise operations, so only a right shift or a rotation needs the high half cleared first, which `Library.Hashing.Sha256.low32` does.  `low32_toUInt32`, `shr_toUInt32`, `rot_toUInt32`, and `xor_ones_toUInt32` state the truncations, and `bigSigma0_toUInt32` shows how a sigma function that masks its argument once becomes the 32-bit function.

For a word that holds a set of positions, `Library.Regex.Bits.bit` reads one position through `Nat.testBit`.  `bit_or`, `bit_and`, and `bit_one_shiftLeft` give the members of a union, an intersection, and a singleton, `shiftRight_and_one` turns the compiled membership test `(s >>> q) &&& 1 == 1` into `bit`, and `ext` proves two words equal from their members.  `Library.Hashing.Sha3.RoundProof.bit` is a second copy of `bit`, with `bit_xor` and `bit_complement`.  These lemmas serve statements over a variable position, where a case split on 64 bits does not apply.
