# Region frame of a call or a step

The entry applies when a proof needs the caller's arrays, buffers, or trees after a call of a compiled function, or composes steps that allocate, release, or write.  `ImplementsA`, `Verified.ImplementsTables`, and `Verified.ImplementsStatic`, the statements of `p.f.implements` and `p.f.trapFree`, end with the same region clause.  Every region of the heap before the call that lies below `top` and outside every free block (`LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Region`), has positive length, and lies apart from the consumed blocks (`Apart`) keeps its bytes, remains such a region of the final heap, and lies apart from the blocks of the result (`LeanExe.Pipeline.Represent.outside`).

The guard `0 < r.2` excludes empty regions.  An empty region on the boundary between a released block and a free block is a region before a release that merges the two blocks, and not after it.  The regions that the proofs keep have positive length: an array's words because of its length word, an object's block because of its header, and a record's slots when the record has a slot.

Inside proofs the clause takes the form `Heap.Keeps heap initial gone heap' store fresh`, with the consumed blocks in `gone` and the new blocks in `fresh`.  `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.implements` converts the clause of a theorem to this form, with `gone` the blocks of the moved pointers and `fresh` the result's blocks, and `apart_blocks` converts `Apart` over pointers to apartness from their blocks.  `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.refl` starts a chain, `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.trans` and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.transBoth` compose two steps when the second consumes only blocks that the first leaves fresh or consumes, and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.mono` changes the lists.  `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.release` is the frame of a release, which `Verified.releaseStep` uses.

`LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.borrowed` and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.owned` recover the caller's arrays from a frame, with the same contents and, for an owned array, the same capacity.  They depend on `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Borrowed.keepIn` and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Owned.keepIn`, which need the kept bytes, the region in the new heap, and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.At` of the new store, and no bound on pages.  `LeanExe.ProofKit.BorrowedKept.borrowed_kept` applies `keepIn` to the region clause directly, and `Verified.ImplementsTables.call` uses it to keep every array borrowed before a call, the program's tables among them.

`LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.node` recovers an owned value of a recursive type with the same blocks, and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.nodeBorrowed` a borrowed one, whose slot regions (`LeanExe.Pipeline.Node.slotRegions`) must have positive length and lie apart from the consumed blocks.  The slot regions are each record's slots, with the length word and elements of each array and the block of each buffer that the records hold.  `LeanExe.Pipeline.Node.slotRegions_pos` gives the positive lengths for a value whose every record has a slot (`LeanExe.Pipeline.Node.Slotted`), and `LeanExe.Pipeline.EncodeSlotted.slotRegions_pos` for a type with an `EncodeSlotted` instance, which `List UInt64`, `Words`, `KeyTree`, and the verified compiler's `Verified.Tree shape` have.  `LeanExe.Pipeline.NodeBorrowed.regions` gives each slot region as a `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Region`, and `LeanExe.Pipeline.NodeBorrowed.frame` keeps the value when its slot regions keep their bytes and remain regions.

The premise `Separate` of `ImplementsA` keeps the consumed blocks pairwise apart and apart from `LeanExe.Pipeline.Represent.reads`: an array argument's words, the block of a buffer argument whose root is nonzero, and a borrowed tree argument's slot regions.  A borrowed value passed together with a consumed one therefore starts apart from it, and the region clause keeps the borrowed value's regions through the call.  A proof that composes two calls by hand converts both clauses with `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.implements`, composes them with `LeanExe.Pipeline.Heap.Keeps.trans`, and reads the values back with the keep lemmas above.

The verified compiler's own proofs state the frame in `Verified.Step heap store keep heap' store' fresh` of `Verified/HeapStep.lean`.  `Step` adds the allocator invariant and the unchanged caps and names the kept regions by a predicate `keep` instead of a list of consumed blocks.  `Verified.Step.refl`, `Verified.Step.trans`, `Verified.Step.transBoth`, and `Verified.Step.mono` compose steps, `Verified.Step.allocate` is an allocation, and `Verified.Step.borrowed` and `Verified.Step.owned` recover an array through `keepIn`.  `Verified.Step.nodeBorrowed` keeps a borrowed tree through a step that keeps every region, from `LeanExe.Pipeline.NodeBorrowed.regions`, `LeanExe.Pipeline.Node.slotRegions_pos`, and `LeanExe.Pipeline.NodeBorrowed.frame`.

A byte buffer keeps its representation through a step by `LeanExe.Pipeline.Bytes.Rep.step`, which needs the buffer's block in `keep` and states that block apart from the step's fresh blocks, and `LeanExe.Pipeline.Bytes.Rep.toAt` then gives the buffer's bytes in memory.  The block of an object, `LeanExe.Pipeline.block`, starts 48 bytes before the root and holds those 48 bytes and the payload capacity that the object's header records, `LeanExe.Pipeline.capacityAt`, so the separation of two blocks gives the separation of their payloads by `omega` on the facts that bound each payload within its block.  `Verified.Wasi.input_at_call` derives in this way the separation of an input of a `sys` command from the call's output buffer, across the buffer's allocation, with `keep` the regions apart from the scratch area and the bounds from `object.base` and `capacity` of the input's buffer and from `ReadReady` of the output buffer.  The input's two locals, its root and its length, pass through the code that sets the command's scratch locals by `Verified.Wasi.inputsHeld_transfer`, which needs both locals unchanged.  Its callers derive that from a premise that places each input's two locals below or above the scratch locals, `place + 2 ≤ base ∨ base + 6 ≤ place` in `Verified.Wasi.wp_sys_flat_inputs_with`.

The WASI compiler's command proofs compose a command's step with the release of the variables that die after it.  `Verified.Wasi.command_sys_spec_with` runs the code of a `sys` command to a result whose `Verified.Wasi.After` keeps the command's input variables live, `fun i => (inputs.any …) || live i`, appends the code that releases the dying inputs with `Wasm.wp_append`, applies `Verified.Wasi.wp_release_context_with` to the field `holds` of that `After`, and joins the two with `Verified.Wasi.After.release`, giving it the release's evolution narrowed to the live set by `Verified.Evolves.mono`.  `Verified.Wasi.After.release` composes the two steps with `Verified.Step.transBoth` and keeps the result's representation through the release by the field `apart`, which keeps the result's blocks apart from those of the variables.  `Verified.Wasi.wp_call_after_arguments_with` composes a call of an effect function with its release in the same way.  A trap needs no release: `Verified.Wasi.CommandResult.trap_live` states that the classification of a trap does not depend on the live set, so the trap of the command's code is the trap of the whole.
