# Instruction forms against Isla traces

A form that the A64 or the Cortex-M7 code generator starts to emit needs a theorem that the model's `exec` agrees with Isla's trace of the form in Arm's specification, for every value of the opcode's fields and every state.  An A64 form has such a theorem, `LeanExe.A64.Isla.Agrees` or `LeanExe.A64.Isla.AgreesAll`, in the profiles `tcg` and `kvm` of `LeanExe/A64/Isla/Profile.lean`, and the floating-point forms also in `sme`, while a Thumb-2 form of the M7 model has `LeanExe.M7.Isla.Agrees` in the profile `LeanExe.M7.Isla.sys`.  The theorems validate the models and take no part in a program's theorem, and [the AArch64 document's section on Isla](../../../docs/a64-bare-metal.md#isla) and [the Cortex-M7 document's section on the Isla check](../../../docs/m7-bare-metal.md#the-isla-check) describe the traces, the profiles, and each form's premises.  Each module of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla` and `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla` reads its traces at elaboration with `isla_trace%` and imports no other module of its directory.  `tests/isla/theorems.sh` and `tests/isla/m7-theorems.sh` elaborate each module with `lake env lean`, since Lake does not track the trace files, and fail on any axiom beyond `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.  A new WebAssembly form does not always need a new machine form.  A sequence of forms that have theorems can compute it at the cost of a few instructions and of a simulation lemma, with no Isla trace, Isla theorem, encoder case, or co-simulation case: on AArch64, `LeanExe.A64.sarCode` computes `i64.shr_s` with the logical shift between two copies of the sign mask in place of `ASRV`, and on the Cortex-M7, `LeanExe.M7.biasOps` adds 2^31 to both high words before the unsigned comparisons for `i64.lt_s` and `i64.le_s`, and `LeanExe.M7.sarOps` wraps the logical shift of a pair in exclusive ors with the sign mask, 3 and 7 instructions more than the unsigned forms (the table of [the Cortex-M7 document's section on the code generator](../../../docs/m7-bare-metal.md#the-code-generator)).  That cost weighs against the new form's trace, its theorem in each profile, its encoding with a `decode_encode` case, its assembler samples, and its co-simulation cases.  [The entry on encodings and co-simulation of machine forms](../machine-form-validation/README.md) covers the encoding, the samples, and the co-simulation, and [the entry on code around an executable reference](../reference-harness-audit/README.md) covers the harness functions, configurations, and IR edits on which the traces rest.

An A64 theorem states `Agrees P (tcg.sys g u) W F fields instr s` for a form with one outcome, where `g` names the group of traces, such as `.tcr`, `.memory`, or `.fp`.  A form that writes a D register states `AgreesAll P A (tcg.sys g u) W F fields instr s` instead, because `V_set` zeroes or keeps the bits of `_Z` above bit 127, a CONSTRAINED UNPREDICTABLE choice of which the trace takes both branches.  A form whose trace holds an abstract call also states `AgreesAll`, even with one outcome, since only `AgreesAll` takes the meanings `A` that the call's equation constrains: `fcvtzu_tcg` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/FloatToInt.lean` writes a general register and `fcmp_tcg` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/FloatCompare.lean` the flags, and both list FPSR in `W`, since Arm's FPToFixed and FPCompare set FPSR's bits, which the abstract call leaves out of the trace (the modules' headers).  `W` lists the registers outside the model that the trace may change, such as `SEE` and `__BranchTaken`, with `_Z` and `FPSR` for a floating-point form, and the constraints that the trace's `assume` events check enter as premises in the model's terms.  The proof starts with `refine ⟨by decide, by decide, LeanExe.A64.Isla.decode_word ?_, fun σ hσ s' hs => ?_⟩`, proves the opcode's word with `simp only [Opcode.word, Opcode.wordFrom, …]` and `omega`, and unfolds `exec` in `hs`.  For a register form, `isla_reads` turns the premise `hσ` into one equation for each read of the trace, `isla_fwd h using omega with [hσ]` takes every path to its outcome, and `gpr_end` compares that outcome with `exec`'s state.  A read at a symbolic register number evaluates to Isla's chain of `ite` expressions over the registers, which `LeanExe.A64.Isla.chain_eq` and `LeanExe.M7.Isla.chain_eq`, rules of `isla_simp` and `t32_simp`, fold into the indexed register.  The backward direction splits on register 31 for each field and builds the path with `isla_bwd` and `isla_gprs`.  When the TCG and KVM traces of a form are equal, the kernel checks the two footprints equal by `rfl`, and `LeanExe.A64.Isla.Agrees.of_reads` carries the TCG theorem to `kvm`, which agrees with `tcg` on the registers that the trace reads.

A load, a store, or a floating-point form has a trace of hundreds of events, and its module walks it with the tactics of `LeanExe/A64/Isla/Walk.lean`.  `isla_nodes F` defines a name for each node of the trace, `theorem F_n0 : F.trace = F.n0 := rfl` restates the trace through those names, and `isla_ctx F π` defines `ctx` in the namespace `F`, the premise on `σ` with one equation for each register read, wrapped in `LeanExe.A64.Isla.Opaque`, and proves `ctx_of`, which derives it from the premise.  For each case of the conditions on the fields that the trace tests, such as register 31, a forward lemma walks every path with `mem_fwd` or `fp_fwd` and a backward lemma builds one path with `mem_bwd` or `fp_bwd`, and the floating-point walks end with `dreg_end` or `xreg_end`.  The theorem joins the lemmas: the backward lemma gives an outcome, and the forward lemma states that it and every other outcome are `exec`'s state.  Such a module declares `LeanExe.A64.Isla.run_congr` and `runSome_congr` as local congruence lemmas, runs `set_option Elab.async false`, and raises `maxRecDepth` to 100,000 for the node definitions and the walks.  The TCG and KVM traces of these forms, and of the branches, differ, so each profile has its own proof.  A floating-point form takes the equation of its abstract function as a hypothesis, such as `hA : LeanExe.A64.Isla.FPAddEq A`, whose derivation [the entry on floating-point equations from Sail](../sail-fp-equations/README.md) covers.

A Thumb-2 theorem states `LeanExe.M7.Isla.Agrees P δ (sys u) W F fields instr m`, which adds the sequence `δ` of device values, decodes the opcode's halfwords with the opcode's length (`LeanExe.M7.Isla.decode_halves`), and lets each outcome hold some upper words of X0 to X14, since the A-profile model clears or keeps bits 63 to 32 of a written register by a fresh Boolean.  Every theorem except those of the 32-bit `B`, `BL`, and `BX LR` takes the premise that the program counter plus the opcode's length lies below 2^32.  The register forms walk with `t32_reads`, `t32_fwd`, and `t32_bwd` or `t32_gprs`, the A64 tactics over the M7 semantics, and end with `t32_reg_end` and `t32_reg_bwd` of `LeanExe/M7/Isla/Check.lean`.  A decode proved inside a theorem's tactic block, with `simp only` on the opcode's word and `omega`, can elaborate and still overflow the kernel's stack when the kernel checks it, as the decode of MOV.W with an immediate does, and the remedy states the opcode by `rfl`, proves the word from that equation, and gives the decode as a term of `LeanExe.M7.Isla.decode_halves`: `movImmT_opcode`, `movImm_word`, and `movImm_decode` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Immediate.lean` (the comment before `movImmT_opcode`).  A form that computes `AddWithCarry` rewrites the trace's overflow test during the walk with `LeanExe.M7.Isla.awc_v`, `awc_v_c`, or `awc_v_b` and compares the outcome through `LeanExe.M7.Isla.awc_eq` with `t32_awc_end` and `t32_awc_bwd`.  Branches, loads, and stores walk with `m7_fwd` and `m7_bwd` of `LeanExe/M7/Isla/Walk.lean`, which take apart a conjunction, an existential, a disjunction, or an `if` at the top of the hypothesis or goal before they unfold the next event, as a trace whose decode clauses fork, such as a branch's, requires.  A branch's theorem names the offset's number and the target's number as variables, rewrites the trace's computations of them to the names with equations that `Opaque` hides from `omega`, and closes the conditions of `B<cond>.W` with a discharger whose last alternative is `fail`.  The proofs of every module of both directories except the A64 branches run inside `without_info`.

The command-line driver keeps the info tree of every command until the file ends, and the info trees of a walk's steps hold its goals and contexts.  Under `tests/isla/theorems.sh`, whose `MemoryHigh` is 8 GiB, the immediate forms reached a resident set of 8,576 MiB with the info trees and 5,262 MiB inside `without_info` (`tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Immediate.lean`, header).  `simp` applies a lemma proved by `rfl`, such as `Nat.add_zero`, without a proof step, so the kernel checks the rewrite by unfolding, and the docstring of `LeanExe.A64.Isla.nat_add_zero` records the cost of that check on the remainder of a 128-bit term, the table's address in the walk of a store at the UART.  The simp sets of `mem_simp`, `t32_simp`, and `m7_simp` therefore use `nat_add_zero`, proved by `omega`.  `rfl` on the value that SUB writes unfolds `UInt64` subtraction and exceeds the maximum recursion depth, an error that `try` does not catch, so `reg_end` compares the values with `with_reducible rfl` before it compares them as natural numbers (`tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Register.lean`, docstring of `reg_end`).

On a hypothesis that holds the trace's literal, each step of a walk costs the size of the remaining trace, since `rw` and `simp` traverse it, and the names of `isla_nodes` reduce a step's cost to the size of one event (`LeanExe/A64/Isla/Walk.lean`, header).  The facts that a walk introduces, hundreds of them with terms of the address translation, make each call of `omega` slow, and `mem_omega` runs `omega` without them, which `m7_simp` uses as its discharger (docstrings of `mem_omega` and `m7_simp`).  A load that runs byte by byte inserts its bytes into a word one at a time, and rewriting each insertion during the walk would double the term at each byte, so `ins_chain` gives the word's value at the end of the path (`tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Ldr.lean`, header).  `set_option Elab.async false` makes the lemmas of a module elaborate one after another, so that the peak of memory is that of one lemma's walk (`tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Ldr.lean` and `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Float.lean`).

With the overflow terms of `AddWithCarry`, `r + r / 2^31 * (2^128 - 2^32)`, in a flag, `simp` reduces projections of the machine's structure instance and the kernel checks the reduced type against the unreduced one.  The kernel's check of the walk of `SUB{S}` then exceeded an 8 GiB cap, and with `awc_v`, `awc_v_c`, and `awc_v_b` rewriting the test into a condition on the signs of 32-bit values it checks in 2.2 GiB (`LeanExe/M7/Isla/Arith.lean`, header).  With the products of a branch offset's fields and powers of 2 in place of named variables, the kernel's check of the walk's steps evaluates those products and the job runs out of memory (`tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean`, header), and [the entry on kernel evaluation costs](../kernel-evaluation-costs/README.md) describes both failures.  `t32_fwd` unfolds the next event before it splits, so in a trace whose decode clauses fork the hypothesis collects every branch of the fork as one formula, and `omega` in the discharger case-splits its disjunctions until the job runs out of memory, which `m7_fwd` avoids by splitting first (`LeanExe/M7/Isla/Walk.lean`, header).

`first` runs its last alternative with error recovery.  A discharger whose last alternative was a tactic that can fail logged the failure and closed the condition with `sorry`, and the walk, which proves the condition of a disjunction's left branch in a `by` block, took a branch whose condition is false.  `cond_disch` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean` ends in `fail` to keep that tactic out of the last position (its docstring).  [The Cortex-M7 document's section on the Isla check](../../../docs/m7-bare-metal.md#the-isla-check) gives each M7 module's elapsed time and peak memory under `tests/isla/m7-theorems.sh` with 4 CPUs and an 8 GiB cap.

The worked examples lie in modules that read their traces, which no module imports, so `entry.json` lists none of them.  In `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla`, `addReg_tcg` and `addReg_kvm` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Register.lean` show a register form and its transfer to `kvm`, `fadd_tcg` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Float.lean` shows a floating-point form with its equation, and `ldr_tcg` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/Isla/Ldr.lean` shows a load with the premises of the translation table and the address.  In `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla`, `andReg_agrees` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Register.lean`, `subReg_agrees` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/AddWithCarry.lean`, and `bcond_agrees` of `tests/TestSupport/M7/Isla/Branch.lean` show a register form, a form with flags from `AddWithCarry`, and a conditional branch.
