# Correct rounding of software arithmetic

Some programs return the correctly rounded word of an exact value: the library's fused multiply-add, `Library/Numerics/Arithmetic/Fma`, rounds `a · b + c` once, the library's decimal parser rounds the value of a decimal string, and Talos's rational roundings, which the parser's tests use, round a quotient.  The theorem of such a program is an equality of words with a reference defined over ℝ, without an error bound.  The entry applies to a new operation or conversion of this kind and to a change of the rounding code of an existing one.

Count in units in which every operand is an integer, so that the rounding becomes a statement about natural numbers.  The FMA counts in units of `2 ^ -2400`, and `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaGrid.RoundsTo Y E Q` states that `Y` lies in binade `E` and that `Q` is the integer nearest to `Y` divided by the binade's spacing, the even one at a tie.  The decimal conversions count in units of the least subnormal, and `Library.Numerics.Decimal.Shared.StrconvGrid.RoundsTo f y E Q` states the same for a real `y` and a format `f` given by its precision and the exponent field of its infinities, `b64` or `b32`.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaNearestEven.positive_reference` then equates `F64PositiveRounding.roundNearestEven (Y / 2 ^ 2400)` with the packed word `FmaGrid.packWord 0 E Q`, and `Library.Numerics.Decimal.Shared.StrconvGrid.positive_reference` equates the format's reference `StrconvGrid.refPos f y` with `StrconvGrid.packInf f E Q`, infinity included.

The code rounds a value that differs from the exact one, and a transfer lemma shows that both round alike.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaGrid.roundsTo_transfer` moves `RoundsTo` from `Y1` to any `Y2` closer than `2 ^ w` when `Y1` is an odd multiple of `2 ^ w` and the spacing is a multiple of `2 ^ (w + 2)`, which is the argument for the FMA's sticky bit.  `Library.Numerics.Decimal.Shared.StrconvTruncate.refPos_transfer` shows that two reals in an open interval that holds no half-grid point of the format have the same reference, which lets the parser keep the first 768 significant digits, 113 for binary32, and replace the rest by half a unit of the last kept place (`truncate64`).  `Library.Numerics.Decimal.Shared.StrconvRational.roundsTo_quotient` gives `RoundsTo` for Talos's `roundQuotient` of `N / D` in a binade that holds the quotient, and `Library.Numerics.Decimal.ParseFloat.ParseFloatRound.roundUnits_spec` reads the parser's rounding off its dropped bits and sticky flag as `refPos`.

Give each multiword routine a natural-number specification before the rounding proof uses it.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaWords.pair h l` is `h · 2 ^ 64 + l`, and `FmaWords` states `mul128`, `sticky128`, and the other routines on it.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Uscale.UscaleTheorem.val` reads an array of 32-bit limbs as a natural number, and `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Uscale.UscaleTheorem.mulSmall_spec` is the pattern of a loop over limbs.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaRoundPack.roundPack_spec` then shows that the code's rounding and packing produce the `E` and `Q` of the value it rounds.

The final theorem names a reference on words.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaTheorem.fmaReference` defines IEEE 754's fusedMultiplyAdd with roundTiesToEven in the style of `Wasm.IEEE64.add`, with `LeanExe.ProofKit.F64SignedRounding.roundNearestEven` for finite operands and the sign rules of a zero result, and `fmaBits_spec` and `fma_toBits` prove the code equal to it for all operands.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaSpecTheorem.fma_nearest` derives `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaSpec.FmaNearest`, the nearest property that error proofs take as a hypothesis, as [the entry on binary64 error bounds](../float-error-tracking/README.md) describes.

Instantiate a lemma over formats for binary64 first.  The parser's lemmas once assumed `F · 2 ^ (p - 1) < 2 ^ 62`, which binary32 meets and binary64, with `2047 · 2 ^ 52 = 2 ^ 63 - 2 ^ 52`, does not, so the binary32 theorems passed while binary64 exposed the hypothesis.  `roundUnits_spec` assumes the bound `2 ^ 63`, and it derives `F < 2 ^ 62` from `2 ≤ 2 ^ (p - 1)` in the one step that needs it.

In the first version of `positive_reference`, a rewrite of a literal significand into the rounding's hypotheses with `rw … at`, followed by `exact`, elaborated until its module timed out without a diagnostic.  `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaNearestEven.strict_top` proves that step for an arbitrary spacing `S` in a lemma of its own.  `set` introduces local definitions whose bodies `omega` and `rw` unfold, such as `(Fma.msb64 (sigWord c)).toNat` or `2 ^ (r - 1)`, and the proof then stops at the recursion or heartbeat limit.  `generalize` and `obtain` with opaque variables avoid the unfolding, as `roundPack_spec` generalizes `Fma.msb128 h l`.

A proof of `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaParts.fmaBits_eq` by `rfl` exceeded the heartbeat limit, while `dsimp only` proves it, because it turns the pattern-matching `let`s of the code into projections on both sides alike.  `rw` tries `rfl` after rewriting, and in `Library.Numerics.Arithmetic.Fma.FmaParts.addend_spec` that attempt evaluated the exponent arithmetic to the recursion limit, so the step is a term built with `congrArg` and `Eq.trans`.  `linarith` and `nlinarith` reached the recursion or heartbeat limit while preprocessing hypotheses that mention `0x4000000000000000`, and `roundUnits_spec` uses `linarith only` with the hypotheses it needs and explicit bounds proved by `omega`.

`Library/Numerics/Arithmetic/Fma/README.md`, section "Proof", describes the FMA's proof module by module, from `FmaWords` to `FmaSpecTheorem`.  The parser's rounding is `Library.Numerics.Decimal.ParseFloat.ParseFloatRound.roundUnits_spec`, which [the entry on decimal text](../decimal-conversions/README.md) places in the parser's proof.  `StrconvRational` gives the binary64 and binary32 instances of Talos's rational and dyadic roundings (`rational64`, `dyadic64`).
