# Independent review of specifications and designs

The entry applies when a specification and its sample inputs, or a design note for an increment, are complete and nothing has been built on them.  A theorem that equates a program with its specification says what the specification says, and [the trusted base's section on specifications](../../../docs/trusted-base.md#specifications) trusts each such definition to say what its name says.  A review by an agent that did not write the specification checks that trust before a program and a proof depend on it, and a design review checks the claims of a plan before code depends on them.

[Step 5 of the verified-request skill](../../../.claude/skills/verified-request/SKILL.md#stage-1-the-specification) gives a fresh agent only the request, the specification, the samples, and the samples' outputs, and none of the author's reasoning or plans.  The reviewer decides for each case of the input whether the specification gives what the request asks, names every case that the request leaves open, and checks each sample's output by hand from the request.  It may build its own executable model from the request alone and compare it with the samples and on random inputs, and it runs no build or test of the repository's Lean code, so its wrong programs and wrong methods run in its model.  The two reviews of Dijkstra's specification each built a model of the program at 64-bit words and an oracle of the request, the first oracle Dijkstra's algorithm on exact integers checked against Bellman and Ford's and the second a search of every walk of at most `n` arcs, and compared model and oracle on 503,042 and 222,300 inputs (`Library/Graphs/Dijkstra/review.md`).

The reviewer runs wrong programs on the cases, and a wrong program that passes every case gets a case that detects it, or the review records that no input can and why.  A Dijkstra program without the test `s < n` passes every case in which each source of `n` or more reads empty ranges of `first`, so the cases include a source of `n` with a range of one arc.  A program without the skip of done vertices computes the same result on every input, so only the program text shows the skip that the request asks for.  In quicksort, step Q9 sorts every array, so a defect of steps Q1 to Q8 that keeps a permutation and finishes changes no result, and the theorems do not exclude it, since `Library.Sorting.Quicksort.quicksort_eq` depends on `Library.Sorting.Quicksort.stages_perm`, which states only that those steps permute the array (`Library/Sorting/Quicksort/review.md`).  `Library/Combinatorics/DancingLinks/review.md` pairs each wrong program with the inputs that detect it in one table and lists the classes that no case covers, with the reason, in another.  A premise on a host's replies gets wrong premises in place of wrong programs, and the model also runs the premise on runs with the replies that the host gives, which measures the class of runs that the premise excludes.  The review of `Demos.ManifestCheck.StatAlong`, `Demos.ManifestCheck.StatxBounded`, and `TestSupport.A64.ImageFilesManifestCheck.StatxAfterCheck` of `tests/TestSupport/A64/ImageFilesManifestCheck.lean` (the section "Review of the premises on the manifest's `statx`" of `Demos/ManifestCheck/review.md`) modelled the command, the shim's images, and Linux's replies with faults, and ran 19 variants of the definitions on 438 cases: indices one before or after the `statx`, a bound over every reply, no exception at `2^32` bytes, the size of Linux's record without the shim's tests of the times, and others.  Its cases told 14 variants from the Lean text, and the review records why each of the other five gives the Lean text's verdict on every case, such as that the shim holds the manifest's descriptor from its `path_open` to its `fd_filestat_get`.  `StatxAfterCheck` failed on exactly the runs from whose manifest's `statx` the shim reads errno 0 and a size of more than 1 MiB and fewer than `2^32` bytes, the class that [the entry on WASI hosts built on a lower layer](../wasi-host-from-layer/README.md) names, and a bound over every reply also failed on runs with Linux's replies whose last `read` returns 0 over a window whose first 256 bytes form the `statx` record of a large file.  The review of the random demos' premise `Demos.RandomHistogram.InRange` (the section "Review of the premise on short reads" of `Demos/RandomHistogram/review.md`) ran the commands, 10 variants of the premise, and wrong programs and shims on 24,574 cases.  A premise of full counts from every `getrandom` excluded 5,586 runs of the histogram and 6,690 of the random choice whose replies getrandom(2) allows, and a premise without "positive for a positive length", or one that reads −4096 as an error number, admitted 1,582 runs of the histogram and 1,887 of the random choice at which the shim traps.  The same review found that a statement that gives each draw a range of sizes, where the specification fixes the size, admits programs that draw one word a call, or the remainder's words first, on every run on which they differ from the right program, and `Demos.RandomHistogram.Counts` mirrors the specification's calls one for one.  A claim that a premise excludes exactly the results at which a host traps needs both directions as theorems, as `Demos.RandomHistogram.random_inRange`, `Demos.RandomHistogram.write_inRange`, and `Demos.RandomHistogram.image_untrapped` state them.

A specification made only of properties of the result admits a wrong method that has those properties, so it also states the equation with the algorithm that the request names.  An index `u mod (j + 1)` or a loop with increasing `j` meets the shuffle's other properties, so the first field of `Library.Random.Sampling.Shuffles` states the equation with Knuth's Algorithm P, `Library.Random.Sampling.algorithmP`.  The reviews of Algorithms S and R found wrong methods that meet the other three properties, so the specifications state `Library.Random.Sampling.algorithmS` and `Library.Random.Sampling.algorithmR` (`Library/Random/Sampling/review.md`).  Properties that determine the result exclude every other output, as `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.distances_unique` shows of `Library.Graphs.Dijkstra.Distances`.  The properties of `Library.Trees.Avl.SearchTree` do not determine the tree, since another balanced insertion with the same keys and height meets them, so the AVL specification fixes the result by `Library.Trees.Avl.algorithmA` and proves the properties in `Library.Trees.Avl.algorithmA_props`.  `Library.Trees.AvlDelete.Removes` states properties of the result of `Library.Trees.AvlDelete.deleteA`, and its field `unchanged` states the request's sentence that an absent key leaves the tree unchanged, without which another balanced tree with the same keys and height meets the other fields.

The reviewer computes the coverage of the cases over the combinations that the method distinguishes.  The AvlDelete reviews found that the ten trees missed eleven of the 105 combinations of location, side, case, and depth that balanced trees admit, and two key sequences give the missing double rotations, which a model confirms against the 105 combinations (`Library/Trees/AvlDelete/review.md`).  The reviewer also checks each comment and docstring against the theorem it cites, as [the entry on claims and the theorems that state them](../claims-and-theorems/README.md) describes.  For each disagreement the author corrects the specification or the samples, or records in `review.md` why the request supports the specification, and a new agent reviews the result (steps 6 to 8 of the skill).

A design note for an increment gets the same review before code depends on it.  Its claims cite code, probes, and primary sources.  A fresh agent checks each citation, estimate, and hardware fact against the tree, with a model where the claim can be computed, and the revision records for each finding whether it is adopted and why.  A note that specifies code against a reference, such as the checks that a host makes before a call, gets a model of that code: the reviewer writes the specified code as a program and runs it beside the reference on cases that combine the conditions that the code tests, since a case with one fault meets only the first check that it fails, and the author's revision of the note runs on the reviewer's cases and on the note's own.  `Verified.Shim.openCheck` makes Wasmtime 44's checks of `path_open` in an order that only such cases distinguish from another, as [the entry on hardware facts](../hardware-facts/README.md) states.  A check of the reference that an invariant of the code decides forms a class of wrong programs that no case meeting the invariant detects, which the review records with its reason, as the paragraph on wrong programs above asks, and code may omit such a check when its proof states the reference at the invariant's bound.  The stubs of the arguments and the environment test the guest's regions against the memory's size alone, since `LeanExe.A64.StoreRel` bounds the memory below 65,536 pages, and `Verified.ShimA64.sizesInvoke_eq` and `Verified.ShimA64.stringsInvoke_eq` state the specification's functions at that bound, where its tests against 2^32 follow from those against the memory's size ([the AArch64 document's section on WASI commands](../../../docs/a64-bare-metal.md#wasi-commands)).  The design review of Algorithms S and R compared both programs with Knuth's steps and Vitter's array form in exact rationals on 40,000 cases, and it noted that Knuth's Algorithm R keeps a table of indices and sorts it at the end, so the request names Vitter's array form.  The review of the BMS requirements recomputed the vectors, the CRCs, the line times, and the fault table with its own encoder and zlib, read the datasheet's pages, and tested M1 on its own machine, and [its table](../../../Demos/Bms/review.md#review) gives for each finding the verification and the response, a declined finding with its reason among them.

A review of the stubs of system calls finds wrong stubs that no run against Linux detects, since they differ from the right stub only at replies that Linux never gives.  Linux's `getrandom` returns an error or a positive count for a positive length, its `clock_gettime` a nanosecond count below 10^9 (`LeanExe/Linux/Replies.lean`), and a negated error number lies between −4095 and −1.  A stub of `random_get` without its trap at a count of 0, a stub of `clock_time_get` with another bound on the nanoseconds, and a stub whose test of the negated result against 4095, before its call of `errnoRoutine`, also accepts −4096 therefore give the right output on every run, and only the theorems of the stubs and of the startup's check, such as `Verified.ShimA64.clockStub_run`, `Verified.ShimA64.randomStub_run`, and `LeanExe.A64.run_preopenCheck`, which state the run for every reply, exclude them.  A change to a register that no statement constrains passes the theorems as well, and the review names both classes with their reasons, as the paragraph on wrong programs above asks.  Where Linux answers a wrong request as the shim's refusal does, a check of the trace detects what the outputs hide: a stub that passes `close` a descriptor that the shim does not hold gets `EBADF`, which the shim returns as WASI's errno 8 (`badf`), the errno of its own refusal, so `tests/linux/wasi/files-names.py` checks the run's `strace` log against `Verified.Shim.NamesHeld`, the property that `Verified.Shim.footprint_namesHeld` states of every run.

A restatement of a WASI specification over Linux events ties each alternative that makes no layer event to the shim's condition for it, since no event records the alternative's outcome.  An open that the shim refuses without a call carries its errno only in the WASI event, whose image holds no layer event, and the review of the manifest checker's executable found that an alternative that accepts any errno other than 0 for such an open lets a program that exits with status 1 without a call meet `Demos.ManifestCheck.NMeets` for every argument list (the section "Review of the executable's theorems" of `Demos/ManifestCheck/review.md`).  `Demos.ManifestCheck.Opening` therefore gives an open without a call the errno of the shim's checks, `Demos.ManifestCheck.nameCheck`, which depends on whether the shim holds descriptor 3, and `Demos.ManifestCheck.spares3_three` states that events that spare descriptor 3, with results in Linux's range, keep it among the numbers that the shim holds or away from them, so the shim holds it throughout the run exactly when the startup's check granted it.  `0 < e.ret.toNat` holds for a negated error number as well as for a count, so the `more` constructors of `Demos.ManifestCheck.NManifestReads` and `Demos.ManifestCheck.NFileReads`, which take a read that returned bytes, also require `LeanExe.Linux.isError e.ret = false`.  A review of a statement over Linux events therefore checks each alternative without layer events against the shim's condition and each comparison of a result with 0 against the negated error numbers.

A statement of a loop's output that names only the bytes written admits wrong loops.  The first statements of the random histogram's write loop, that the bytes written form a prefix of the output and that status 0 holds exactly when the prefix is whole, admitted a loop that writes one byte at a time, one that writes aligned chunks, and one that returns the status of exhausted passes after two passes, and Linux gives no reply that tells the last kind from the right loop, so no run detects it.  The review's model ran `writeAll` and 20 wrong write loops under every host of at most five writes (the section "Review of the write loop and the witness" of `Demos/RandomHistogram/review.md`), and the statements then stated that each write offers the bytes that the writes before it did not take, `LeanExe.Wasi.Offered` and `Demos.RandomHistogram.OfferedLayer`, and that the status of exhausted passes follows one write for each pass.  Those statements admitted a write of no byte after the output is whole, which offers the empty rest and returns 0, and a program that adds it met `Demos.RandomHistogram.Counts` on 2,002 of the 2,179 runs on which it differs from the right program (the section "Review of the premise on short reads").  They also admitted a loop with more passes than `writeAll`: for an output of B bytes, a loop of B + 2 passes met them on 285 of the model's 338 small runs on which it differs, and a loop without a cap on all 376, each run ending with status 0 or an errno after more than B + 1 writes (the section "Review of the writes of no byte").  `Demos.RandomHistogram.Written` and `Counts` therefore state as well that each write offers at least one byte, `LeanExe.Wasi.WroteSome`, and that the writes number at most the loop's passes, which `LeanExe.Wasi.WriteAllRun.shape` proves of every run of the loop that returns or exits.  The manifest checker's `Demos.ManifestCheck.LineWrites` states both, and the random choice's `Demos.RandomChoice.Writes` excludes both, since each of its constructors that makes a write requires bytes to remain and takes one of the passes.  A statement of a loop's output therefore names what each step offers, that a step runs only while bytes remain, the number of steps, and when the loop's status of exhaustion occurs.

A review of a numerical program builds its own model of the arithmetic and runs its wrong programs through the final tests.  The reviewers of the passive-safety and gating requirements modelled the interval library's outward rounding from `Library/Numerics/Bounds/Program.lean` and `Library/Numerics/Interval/Program.lean` and found that it moves an exact 0 below 0, where a square root or a comparison with a zero threshold then rejects, before the modules ran (finding 3 of `Demos/PassiveSafety/review.md` and finding 1 of `Demos/Gate/review.md`).  The reviewer of the screening requirements kept its 24 wrong programs as variants of one model that computes the output of the interface, so that each variant ran through the checks of `tests/screening/oracle.py`, which measured what the final tests detect rather than what the plan describes.  The checks rejected 23 of them, among them a status 0 at the cap that the reviewer expected no check to detect, and only the proof excludes the last (the section "Wrong programs" of `Demos/Screening/review.md`).  An oracle that shares the method of the program under test agrees with that method's mistakes, so the gating demo's test computes the least cost of an assignment by enumeration, by dynamic programming over subsets, and by successive shortest paths with the Bellman–Ford method, none of which computes the dual potentials that its solver's certificate holds (the section "Tests" of `Demos/Gate/requirements.md`).  An oracle of a cover by boxes needs consistent samples of every parameter, and the geolocation oracle covers the region for each parameter by a Lipschitz subdivision, which drops a cell when a residual at its center exceeds the bound by more than a bound of the residual's change over the cell, after the review found that samples around the central parameter missed the consistent set of the parameter that produced the measurements (findings 3 and 13 of `Demos/Geolocation/review.md`).  The output of a search that reports only a status and a hull does not show which cells it took: of the 32 wrong programs of the avoidance search's reviewer, 7, among them a probe over the padding, a round-robin split, and no split of rejected cells, returned the correct output bit for bit on every case.  A call with `S = 0` and a cell as its box evaluates that cell alone, so the oracle replays the search of the requirements with the module's own evaluations and requires the output bit for bit (`replay` in `tests/avoidance/oracle.py`).  The replay tests the steps, splits, budget, stops, and hull, while the other checks test the evaluations, and it names the witness of each verdict 1, at which the oracle evaluates the probability.  With the replay and the reviewer's inputs, the checks detected 30 of the 32 wrong programs, and the other two give the correct output on every case (finding 1 and the section "Wrong programs of the search" of `Demos/Avoidance/review.md`).  A model reproduces a program's floating-point results only when it ports the library's functions: the reviewer of the adaptive panels ported `exp` and `erf` and reproduced every result it compared, while a model with the platform's `erf`, which differs from the library's on 10,159 of 200,021 arguments, reproduced the sample of `pc` but only 9 of 26 results of `pcAdaptive` with at most 256 panels (finding 8 of the section "Adaptive panels" of `Demos/Conjunction/review.md`).  The same reviewer checked the design's claimed optimum, the column "One march, best tolerance" of the requirements' measurements, with a grid of `log₁₀ τ` and a refinement, and found single marches 4 to 8 percent narrower at the inputs that it reported, after which the requirements took the table from the grid and restated the gains (finding 4 of the same section).

A review that receives the author's reasoning reads the request through it, so the skill withholds the reasoning and plans.  A review without a model cannot find a wrong program that passes every case, a statement that a run falsifies, or a combination that no case reaches, and the reviews above recorded defects of each kind.  A wrong program that passes every case and that the review does not record leaves the next reader unaware that the cases admit it, so `review.md` names each such class.  The model and the cases are checks, to which [the entry on checks that can fail](../discriminating-checks/README.md) applies.

The worked examples are the `review.md` files of `Library/Random/Sampling`, `Library/Sorting/Quicksort`, `Library/Graphs/Dijkstra`, `Library/Trees/Avl`, `Library/Trees/AvlDelete`, and `Library/Combinatorics/DancingLinks`, `Demos/Bms/review.md`, and `Demos/Screening/review.md`.  [The entry on transcriptions of standards](../spec-transcription/README.md) covers specifications that transcribe a published standard, whose review compares the transcription with the standard, and [the entry on specification call theorems](../spec-call-theorem/README.md) gives the uniqueness theorems that make a property specification exclude every other output.  [The entry on testing a statement before its proof](../statement-testing/README.md) applies the reviewer's model to new theorems.
