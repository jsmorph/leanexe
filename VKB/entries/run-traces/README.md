# Traces of fuel-bounded runs

The entry applies when a function runs rounds by recursion on a fuel, each round either ending the run or continuing from a smaller state, and theorems state properties of the rounds that it returns: a property of every round, of the rounds after a given one, or of the run on a changed input.  A proof by induction on the fuel carries the fuel into every lemma, and statements about the rounds from round `r` on or about two runs on different inputs do not follow the recursion.  `Demos.Rcv.Spec.run` counts the rounds of a ranked-choice contest in this way, and `Demos.Rcv.Spec.count` gives it a fuel of one more than the number of counted candidates.

Define an inductive predicate of the rounds and the result, with a constructor for the round that ends the run and a constructor for a round that continues followed by the trace from the next state, and relate it to the function once.  `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace` has the constructors `last` and `step` and takes the decision of a round as a parameter, so its lemmas hold for every decision that meets `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Ends`: an elimination removes a member of the continuing set, and a set of at most one member ends the count.  `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.run_trace` proves by induction on the fuel, with the decrease that `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.eliminate_removes` gives, that `run` returns a trace whenever the fuel is at least the size of the set, and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.run_eq` proves that every fuel at least the trace's length gives the trace's count.  `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.run_fuel` follows: the count does not depend on the fuel once the fuel suffices.  `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.mem`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.head`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.next`, and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.last_round` state facts of every round, of the first, of consecutive rounds, and of the last, by induction on the trace and with no fuel in their statements.

`Demos.Rcv.Stability.trace_unique` derives from `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.run_eq` that a set has one trace, which equates a trace that a proof builds by another route with the count's own.  `Demos.Rcv.Stability.trace_suffix` states that the rounds from any round on form the trace from that round's continuing set, for rounds written `rds = p ++ rd :: q`, which names a round without an index and gives the rest of a count the lemmas of a whole count.  A statement about a changed input transfers the original trace: `Demos.Rcv.Properties.trace_ballots` gives the original rounds as a trace of the changed ballots when each round's counts are the same for both, and `trace_unique` with the trace of the changed input's own count then equates the two counts.  The counts need agree only against the sets that the original trace reaches, which is all that `Demos.Rcv.Properties.later_no_harm` can show of a changed ballot, through `Demos.Rcv.Properties.tally_replace`.  A change that keeps the counts against every set, such as a permutation of the ballots, gives equal runs by induction on the fuel, as `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.run_perm` does for `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.invariance`.

`cases` on a trace whose rounds are a term such as `p ++ rd :: q` fails with a dependent-elimination error, since the term does not unify with the rounds of a constructor, so `trace_suffix` takes the trace over a variable and the equation `rds = p ++ rd :: q` as a separate hypothesis.  A fact of a round chosen by its position is stated once as a lemma by induction on the trace, as `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.next` and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.last_round` are, rather than by `cases` at each use.  A walk through several rounds goes by induction on the number of rounds left, with the trace's set as a variable, as `Demos.Rcv.Batch.walk` follows the rounds that eliminate the members of a batch one at a time.

The worked examples are `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.count_trace`, the trace of the Oakland count, and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.locality`, which states T6 of the ranked-choice requirements through `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.head`, `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.mem`, and `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Trace.next`, together with `Demos.Rcv.Properties.later_no_harm` and `Demos.Rcv.Batch.walk`.  The Maine count has its own decision, and `Demos.Rcv.Maine.count_trace` and `Demos.Rcv.Maine.locality` reuse the same lemmas through `Demos.Rcv.Rounds.Ends`.  Every example lies in the ranked-choice demos, so the method's reach beyond counts of rounds is untested.  [The entry on tail-recursive and fuel-bounded functions](../tail-recursive-spec/README.md) covers functions whose theorems concern the result alone, and [the entry on counts over nested sets](../nested-set-counts/README.md) compares the counts of consecutive rounds.
