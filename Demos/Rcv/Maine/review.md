# Maine ranked-choice tabulator: decisions and review

[The requirements](requirements.md) specify the tabulator's Maine module, the first step of increment 4 of [the Oakland requirements' plan](../requirements.md#plan).  This record gives the way each of the user's decisions applies to Maine, each difference between the statute and the rules with the text that the module follows, each reading of the governing texts with its reason, the details decided while writing the requirements, the departures of official practice with their evidence, the recount of the corpus, and the findings of a fresh agent's review with the verification and response for each.  The readings are mine, stated as readings of the texts and not as legal opinions.  The figures come from `tests/rcv/maine/recount.py` on the files of [the Maine corpus manifest](../../../tests/rcv/maine/manifest.json), except where a paragraph names a scratch computation.

## The statute and the rules

§723-A(1)(H-1) makes the count "the ranked-choice counting process described in this section and in rules adopted by the Secretary of State", so the module reads both texts.  Where they differ, the statute governs, since the rules exist under §723-A(5-A) to administer the count that the statute describes.  The differences (a), (d), (e), and (f) arose from amendments of 2023 and 2025, and (b) from PL 2019 c. 320, §13, which struck the drawing by lot for a final-round tie from §723-A(3) and sent the tie to §732, all after the rules' filing of 2018, while the statute's provisions behind (c) and (g) date from the initiative of 2015, IB 2015 c. 3, so the rules departed from them from the start.  Where the statute is silent, the rules govern.  I decided each difference by the standard procedure, considering the texts, the records, the theorems, and the evidence of practice, and the table gives the result.

| Difference | Statute | Rules | The module | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Batch elimination | One last-place candidate per round, §723-A(2)(B), since PL 2023 c. 304 repealed (1)(A) and (G) | §4(2)(A), (B)(6), and (B)(7) permit it by reference to the repealed paragraphs | One candidate per round | The rules' provisions rest on definitions that no longer exist, so they cannot operate |
| (b) Tie in the final round | "Decided as provided in section 732": an automatic recount, then a drawing by lot in a primary and a special election in other elections, except for the offices that §732(2)(B) leaves to the constitutions, among them United States Senator and Representative to Congress | §4(2)(B)(6): by lot, the candidate chosen by lot defeated | The tied pair is an elimination by order, marked, and the survivor wins in the next round | The order is the lot of §732(1) and of the rules, and the mark tells the reader that the order decided.  For a general election §732(2) provides no lot, and the module cannot tell a primary from a general election, so the requirements state that the module's winner after a marked final tie rests on the order alone and matches §732 only in a primary |
| (c) Overvote | (1)(D): an exhausted ballot "contains an overvote at the highest continuing ranking" | §4(2)(B)(1): an overvote "invalidates the overvoted rankings and all subsequent rankings" | The rules' reading: the examination ends at the first multiple position that it reaches | See the paragraph after the table |
| (d) Excluded candidates | (1)(C) and (C-1), (2-A), and §371(5) and (6) and §374-A(3)(C) | Silent | The statute's provisions | The rules predate them |
| (e) Initial test | More than 50% of "all ballots cast for the particular office", blank first rankings, overvotes, and excluded first rankings included | §4(2)(A): more than 50% of "the first choice votes based on the election returns" | The statute's test, computed from the records | The statute fixes the denominator, and the records are the ballots cast.  The input of the official test is a practice, which [the departures](#departures-of-official-practice) record |
| (f) Continuing candidate | Not removed from consideration and not excluded | §2(2): not defeated | The statute's definition | The rules' definition predates excluded candidates |
| (g) End of the count | "2 or fewer continuing candidates" | §4(2)(A): "when only 2 continuing candidates remain" | The statute's | The statute also covers a contest with one counted candidate or none, which the rules do not |

The statute's overvote provision admits two readings, and each breaks a property that the module states.  Judged afresh in each round, it exhausts the ballot `[{B, C}, A]` against `{A, B}`, since its highest continuing ranking holds the overvote, and counts it for `A` once `B` and `C` are gone, so an exhausted ballot returns and T8 fails.  Judged as a status that lasts from the round in which it arises, it makes the ballot `[A, {B, C}, D]` inactive if `A` falls while `B` continues and a vote for `D` if `B` and `C` fall first, so the count depends on the order of the eliminations and T6 fails.  The rules' reading keeps both properties, and it is the only one that the records support: the Secretary of State's spreadsheets write `overvote` without the marked candidates, so whether an overvote holds a continuing candidate cannot be known.  RCTab's 2018 Maine test configuration uses `exhaustImmediately`, the rules' reading, as evidence of practice.

## Readings of the governing texts

| Provision | Reading | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| §723-A(2): initial test | A candidate is "assigned ranking number one" on a ballot whose first position marks it alone | The statute lists ballots with an overvote at ranking one among those counted only in the denominator, so a multiple first position assigns ranking one to no candidate |
| §723-A(2): "all ballots cast for the particular office" | Every ballot line of the file, `N` | The file holds one line for each ballot card that carries the contest, blank ballots included, as the statute's list includes ballots "on which ranking number one is blank" |
| §723-A(2), §371(5) and (6), §374-A(3)(C): an excluded candidate in the test | The test elects only a member of `K`, and when an excluded candidate is ranked first alone on more than half of the ballots the count proceeds in rounds | "A candidate who has died or been disqualified cannot be determined the winner", and in the same words for a withdrawn candidate.  The statute's condition for rounds, "if no candidate has been assigned ranking number one on more than 50%", is then false of the excluded candidate, and the module reads it with these provisions, since the only alternative would end the count with no winner, which no text provides |
| §723-A(2)(A) | With at most two continuing candidates the count ends, naming the one of more votes, the one alone, or no one when none continues | "The candidate with the most votes is declared the winner" |
| §723-A(1)(F), (2)(B), (3) | The candidate with the fewest votes is eliminated, and a tie among the fewest is decided by the order, marked | Decision 3.  The order encodes any sequence of drawings, since each loser leaves the count, and it is the drawing "recorded and reused in the event of a recount" |
| §723-A(1)(D) and (K), §4(2)(B)(2) and (3): skipped rankings | An empty position ends the examination when the next position is also empty and some later position is marked, and any other empty position passes | The statute's skipped ranking requires a later ranking, "ranks a candidate at a subsequent ranking", so trailing empty positions skip nothing, and the examination reaches the run only after the earlier positions, which the statute's "before its highest continuing ranking" requires.  Without the rule, the recount of the 2026 State Senate District 4 and House District 58 primaries differs from the reports.  A scratch reading that ends a ballot at any such run, wherever it lies, makes 6 more ballots inactive in round 1 of State Senate District 4 and 660 more in round 1 of the Republican primary for Governor than the reports show, and gives every figure of House District 58 |
| §371(5) and (6), §374-A(3)(C): an excluded candidate's ranking | A marked position: it passes like an eliminated candidate's ranking, ends a run of empty positions, and makes a position multiple when another mark shares it | "A ranking for a candidate who has withdrawn is considered a ranking only for the purpose of determining whether the ballot is exhausted under section 723-A", and §723-A(2-A) counts the ballot for its next continuing candidate |
| §696(2)(E): an undeclared write-in | The `aggregate-writein` kind is uncounted, and its ranking is a marked position like an excluded candidate's | The statute forbids counting "the vote for that office", and a voter who writes a name has not "left a ranking blank" under (1)(K).  Read as voiding the ballot's whole vote in the contest, the provision would leave a ballot that ranks an undeclared write-in and then a candidate inactive, which the Secretary of State's RCV FAQ contradicts: for such a ballot "the voter's 2nd choice candidate would be used".  The 2024 count equals the recount with write-ins passed as rankings, Golden 197,151, Theriault 194,445, and 11,340 inactive, and with write-ins passed as empty positions the recount gives 197,139, 194,435, and 11,362.  Counting `Write-in` as a candidate eliminated in round 1 gives the report's figures in a second round, and the records do not separate the declared write-in's votes from the others, so the count shows the treatment of the whole aggregate and not of undeclared write-ins alone.  The same FAQ says that undeclared write-ins "will be recorded as blanks" and that with "Two blanks in a row" the ballot "is considered exhausted", so it would exhaust the ballot `W,,A` with `W` an undeclared write-in, which the module counts for `A`.  The first statement fits the municipal first-choice tally: the 2024 returns give the write-ins 421 first choices and 11,997 blanks, against 2,021 first-ranked write-ins and 10,502 empty or multiple first positions in the records |
| §1(11-A), §722-A: declared write-in | `named-writein`, counted like a listed candidate | §1(27-C) counts declared write-ins among the candidates that make a contest a ranked-choice contest, (C-1) includes them among excluded candidates, and §737-A(2-A) treats one, for recounts, "the same as any candidate whose name is printed on the ballot", so they are candidates of the count |
| §4(2)(B)(4): duplicate rankings | No rule of its own | Passing each ranking of an eliminated candidate gives the rule's results, including exhaustion when the voter ranked no one else.  Rules 1 and 3 still apply to the later positions, so `A,A,,,B` with `A` eliminated is exhausted under §4(2)(B)(3), where the last sentence of (4) alone would count `B` |
| §4(2)(B)(5): no continuing candidate ranked | The reasons `blank`, `uncounted`, and `exhausted` of a ballot whose examination passes its last position | "The ballot is deemed exhausted", and the report keeps the three apart |
| No continuing ballot | No rule of its own: the count proceeds, every tie at zero votes is decided by the order and marked, and the count names a winner whenever `K` is not empty | The statute provides no other end, and the Oakland charter's silence, which led that module to end with no winner, has no counterpart here.  The marks show that the order decided every elimination |
| §723-A(4)(A): rankings | The module accepts any number of positions from 1 to 63 | The limit constrains the ballot, and the count of the records does not depend on it.  The 2026 ballots offered one ranking more than the number of candidates |
| §1(27-C), §723-A(5-B) and (7) | The module counts any contest file, and whether a contest is a ranked-choice contest is outside it.  Presidential electors and primaries are out of scope | §1(27-C) depends on the number of qualified candidates, a fact about filings, and (5-B) and (7) add statewide and district tabulations and slates of electors |
| Reasons | Each reason is part of the statute's exhausted ballot and of the Secretary of State's single category | (1)(D) covers a ballot without a continuing candidate, an overvote, and skipped rankings, and the 2024 report defines Ballots Exhausted as undervotes, overvotes, and ballots exhausted after an elimination |

## Departures of official practice

| Departure | Text | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The declared write-in of 2024 had no round | §723-A(1)(C) and (2): a declared write-in candidate is a continuing candidate in round 1, and with three continuing candidates the last-place candidate is removed in a round of its own | The Secretary of State's release of 2024-11-07 states that the race had "two named candidates and one declared write-in candidate", Diane Merenda, and that the second choices of her voters and of blank first rankings "will be added to the first-choice votes" of Golden and Theriault.  The summary report has one round with the two listed candidates, and the spreadsheets write every write-in as `Write-in` without the name.  The winner is unchanged: had the records named her votes, she would have been eliminated in round 1, the returns giving the write-ins 421 first choices and FairVote reporting 420 for her, and the next round would have the report's figures, since the module passes her rankings in that round as it passes the aggregate's |
| The initial test's input | §723-A(2) counts the ballots cast, which the records hold.  §4(1) and (2)(A) of the rules use the municipal returns | The Secretary of State's 2024 page describes its first-choice file as "1st Choice results reported by the municipalities", which gives Golden 196,388 first choices where the records give 196,349, and the RCV FAQ says that a candidate must win "more than 50% of the first-choice votes as counted on Election Day".  The 2026 first-choice files, titled "RCV Central Count first choice votes", have the records' totals and, for State Senate District 4 and House District 58, the records' first rankings, but they differ from the spreadsheets' first rankings in the larger contests: Charles 49,100 against 49,094 in the Republican primary for Governor and Jackson 45,906 against 45,920 in the Democratic.  Near 50% the official input and the records could decide the test differently.  In the 2026 Democratic primary for House District 23, which the test decided, Kornfield had 668 first choices of 1,321, seven more than the 661 that the test requires |

The first departure changes the count's rounds and not its winner, and the module can reproduce the official round only from records that do not name the declared write-in, which the requirements' kinds forbid without a command that names the comparison.  The second concerns the test's input, and T11(c) shows that the test changes no winner, so it can change only whether the count has rounds.  RCTab's 2018 Maine test configuration also counts `Write-in` as a candidate and uses batch elimination, both under the text of 2018, and the 2024 count shows that practice has since changed.

## Facts behind the requirements

`recount.py` gives the figures in the table.  Each of the five 2026 contests has every elimination and winner of its summary report, and no contest has a tie or an excluded candidate.  The records of the five 2026 contests hold no write-in, and the 2024 records hold the declared write-in's votes only inside the unnamed aggregate.

The differences of the last three contests come from the records.  For each of the three, the summary report's figures minus the recount of every spreadsheet but the third manual-entry spreadsheet are nonnegative in every round, sum to that spreadsheet's 18, 78, and 77 rows, and do not decrease from round to round for any continuing candidate or for the inactive ballots, which `recount.py --residual manual-3` tests.  Those spreadsheets carry "Updated 20260623" in their names, after the tabulation that the Secretary of State announced on 2026-06-19, and the Wayback Machine holds copies of 2026-07-07 identical to the posted ones.  In the Republican primary, one ballot of that spreadsheet ranks Wessels first and McCarthy second, which matches the recount's one extra vote for Wessels in rounds 1 and 2 and its move to McCarthy in round 3.  The evidence fits a correction of the manual entries after the tabulation, which the posted files do not confirm.

The reading of skipped rankings decides the small contests.  Without rule 1, `recount.py --no-skip-rule` gives one vote more to each of Perkins, Guerin, and Wood in State Senate District 4 and to Harris in House District 58 than the reports.  The initial test elects no one in the corpus's six counted contests: the largest first-ranking share is Golden's 196,349 of 402,936 in 2024.  The two 2026 contests that the test decided are the Democratic primaries for United States Senator, where Platner had 156,084 first choices of 222,405, and for House District 23, where Kornfield had 668 of 1,321, and the Secretary of State posted no cast vote record for either.  The Secretary of State's list of withdrawals before the 2026 primary, as of 2026-05-22, names no candidate of the counted contests.

The statements of the theorems were tested on a scratch model of the requirements, written by their author, before the review, and the statements that the review changed were tested again after it.  T8 held on all 680,400 pairs `S′ ⊆ S` over five candidates of every kind and every ballot of up to four positions with empty, single, and multiple positions.  On 30,000 random contests of up to six candidates of every kind, five positions, and 25 ballots, T3, T4, T5, T11(a) to (d), T12, T13, and T14 held, with 1,734 contests decided by the initial test, 4,781 tied final pairs, 8,453 ties at the fewest votes among three or more candidates, 18,470 tests of T11(a), and 13,547 Condorcet losers.  T11(a) and T12 were first tested after the review's finding 2, and T11(c), T11(d), and T12 in the statements that findings 1, 11, and 12 produced.  The model's author also wrote the requirements, so the review's own model is the independent check.

### Final check

The same agent checked the second revision and found the remaining disagreement and the five notes resolved.  It found that PL 2023 c. 628 changed only a term of §723-A(3), "defeated" to "removed from consideration", so that the record's dating of difference (b) to c. 628 was wrong, and that the sentence on the statement tests named finding 2 as producing a statement. The reviewer confirmed both edits against the printed law and added one note: c. 320 also struck "Election officials may resolve prospective ties between candidates before the election", so a tie-break order drawn before the election is not the statute's procedure.  The requirements and decision 3 now say that the order encodes the recorded drawings and that each drawing follows the tie that the report marks.  A further check found that drawing every marked tie at once can draw a tie that never arises: with four listed candidates, two positions, the order `1 2 3 4`, and the ballots `4,2`, `1,3`, `1,1`, `1,4`, `3,`, `3,1`, `1,`, `,`, and `2,1`, the report marks ties in rounds 1 and 2, and the order `1 3 4 2`, which draws the other way in round 1, leaves no tie in round 2.  I confirmed the example on my model.  The requirements and the open item now draw the earliest undecided marked tie, rerun, and repeat, and the requirements' references list PL 2019 c. 320 and PL 2023 c. 628, which they cite.  The reviewer confirmed these edits and found no other disagreement.
