# Oakland ranked-choice tabulator: decisions and review

[The requirements](requirements.md) specify the first module of the ranked-choice tabulator from the design document `rcv-tabulation-design.md`, the research on its open questions, the review of that research, and the decisions that the user made from them.  This record gives each decision with the way the requirements apply it, each reading of the charter and of state law with its reason, the details decided while writing the requirements, the departures of county practice with their evidence, and the findings of a fresh agent's review with the verification and response for each.  I computed the figures with scratch scripts from the files listed under [sources](#sources), and the scripts of [the Oakland RCV corpus](../../tests/rcv/README.md), `reports.py`, `recount.py`, and `district4.py`, give the same figures from the corpus's pinned copies.  The statements of the theorems were tested on a model of the requirements before the review.

## Readings of the governing texts

The readings below are mine, stated as readings of the texts and not as legal opinions.  Each settles a case that the text leaves to the reader, and the requirements state the result.  The review checked each reading against the City's copy of the charter and the state's texts.  Its findings 6 and 12 changed the reading of the kinds, finding 11 that of the undervote, and finding 14 that of the first choice.

| Provision | Reading | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| §1105(a)(3), (d)(2): majority | A candidate holds a majority when twice its votes exceed the continuing ballots of the round | "More than fifty percent of the votes coming from continuing ballots", with decision 4 |
| §1105(d)(1): first choice | The first position, after empty and uncounted positions are passed, that marks a single counted candidate, unless the examination first reaches a multiple position and stops there.  This is the interpretation against every counted candidate | §1105(g) applies "in the first or any round" |
| §1105(d)(2): advance | A ballot passes positions that mark a single eliminated candidate, so a duplicate ranking needs no rule of its own | "Advanced to the next-ranked continuing candidate" |
| §1105(e): lot | A pre-drawn permutation, with the tied candidate latest in it eliminated and each use marked | Decision 3.  A permutation can encode any sequence of official drawings, since each loser leaves the count, and T9 shows which ties the order decided |
| §1105(d)(2), (e): final tie | Two candidates with equal votes hold no majority, so (d)(2) eliminates the one with the smallest number of votes and (e) breaks the tie, and the survivor holds every continuing ballot in the next round | Both candidates "tie for the smallest number of votes".  §1103 applies state law only where the charter does not provide, so Cal. Elec. Code §15651's lot or special runoff does not apply, as the review of the research also concluded |
| §1105(f): qualifying candidate | Any continuing candidate `X` whose votes exceed the sum of the votes of the candidates with strictly fewer votes, when there is at least one | "Any candidate has more votes than the combined vote total of all candidates with fewer votes" |
| §1105(f): several sets | The largest qualifying set | Decision 5 |
| §1105(f): sets of one | Rule 3 applies only to sets of two or more, and a set of one is the unique candidate with the fewest votes, which rule 4 eliminates in the same way | The outcome is the same, and the report then names the elimination by its ground in (d)(2) |
| §1105(f) and (e) | When several candidates tie for the fewest votes and all of them fall in a qualifying set of two or more, the batch eliminates them together without a drawing | (f) says "shall be eliminated simultaneously", and (e) concerns the choice of one candidate to eliminate |
| §1105(f): timing | The batch test runs only after the majority test fails, in any round including the first | "During the elimination stage of any round" |
| §1105(g) | Any number of empty positions, consecutive or not, are passed | "That ballot shall immediately be advanced to the next ranking" |
| §1105(h): overvote | A ballot is an overvote when its examination reaches a position with two or more marks, whether or not the marked candidates continue | "Reaches a ranking with more than one candidate indicated".  The reading keeps the stability that "shall remain so" states, it is the only reading that records without the marked candidates' names support, and Alameda's certified 2022 District 4 count follows from it |
| §1105(h), §15342(d): write-in in an overvote | A position that marks a candidate together with a write-in, counted or not, is multiple | §15342(d) counts neither vote when the voter indicates more names than candidates to elect "by a combination of marking and writing" |
| §1105(h): exhausted | A ballot that passes its last position after marking a counted candidate at some single position | "Cannot be advanced because no further candidates are ranked", where the ranked candidates have been eliminated |
| §1105(h): undervote | `blank` and `uncounted` are both undervotes | A ranking of an uncounted write-in is passed over like an empty ranking under §15341 and decision 6, so such a ballot has no counted candidate indicated.  Alameda's Blanks figure includes these ballots: 4,403 in District 4 is 4,269 blank ballots plus 134 that rank only write-ins.  The report keeps the two reasons apart, so the reading affects only the mapping.  The overvote reading counts the same uncounted mark as a name indicated, by §15342(d), so both readings apply per ranking: a ranking with one uncounted mark has no counted candidate and is passed, and a ranking with two or more marks of any kind is an overvote |
| §1105(h): "shall remain so" | No separate status: the examination gives every inactive ballot the same reason in every later round | T8 proves it of the rules |
| §15341: kinds | `listed` and `named-writein` candidates are counted, and `aggregate-writein` and `excluded` candidates never are | §15341 counts a written name only for a candidate who complied with Part 3 of Division 8, which holds the statement of write-in candidacy of §8600 and the deadline of §8601.  An aggregate write-in in Alameda's records names no one, and `excluded` is a named person who did not comply.  No other basis for `excluded` applies in Oakland |
| No continuing ballot | When a round has no continuing ballot, the count ends with no winner | The charter is silent.  By T8 no later round can have a continuing ballot, so the eliminations that (d)(2) would continue could reach no majority and would mark ties that decide nothing |
| §1105(c), (k)(1): rankings | The module accepts any number of positions from 1 to 63 and does not compare it with the number of candidates | The limit on rankings constrains the ballot, and the count of the records does not depend on it |
| §1105(k) | No change applies | The search found no published determination of the City Clerk.  §1105(k)(4)'s first-choice tally, which the Clerk may authorize, is therefore not part of the count |

## Details decided here

| Detail | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Format lines | `rcv-ballots 1` and `rcv-report 1` | A reader and a script can tell the two files apart and their versions |
| Indices | From 1, in the order of the candidate lines, with bit `i` − 1 for candidate `i` | The report and the file read naturally for a person, and the shift is a single step in the program |
| Separators and symbols | `,` between fields, `|` within an overvote, `*` for an unnamed overvote | Every field then has one form, and no name or identifier appears on a ballot line |
| Identifier | Optional on each line, recognized by the field count `k` + 1, with 1 to 64 bytes of letters, digits, `-`, `_`, `.`, and `:` | The count of fields decides without inspecting the field, and the alphabet holds the identifiers of D. Otis's Alameda files and ES&S's record numbers, into which a converter can map any other identifier.  An empty identifier is excluded so that each line has one form |
| Names | 1 to 255 bytes, each from `0x20` to `0x7E` or `0x80` to `0xFF`, unvalidated as UTF-8 | The count never reads names, and the converter writes UTF-8 |
| Limits | `n` and `k` up to 63, `N` up to 2^32 − 1 | 63 candidates leave bit 63 free, and the research found no contest near the limit |
| Indices in a field | Strictly increasing, at least two | Each position word then has one field, which T2 needs |
| Errors | The first line that fails its role, with the role's name | The line number and the role are a function of the header, so every malformed file has one error |
| Report | The input's header lines repeated, per-round lines, an elimination line naming its ground, and `winner none` when no ballot continues | Each line is one fact that a script can parse, and the figures that §1105(i)(1)'s summary report requires are present |
| Rule identifier | The ASCII line of [the governing texts](requirements.md#governing-texts), with a revision number | It names each text, its version, and the date read, and the revision distinguishes a change of reading without a change of text |
| Word forms | A contest array and a result array, with `[0]` for an invalid contest | The first increment's call theorem needs a stated form before the reader exists, and every array then has one result |
| County count | Rule 4 alone, no majority stop, ending at the first round with two candidates or fewer, with no winner when the two tie | Every county report read ends at its first round with two candidates or fewer, and the report calls the one with more votes "elected because all other candidates have been eliminated".  No report in the corpus shows how the county decides a tie of the last two, so the definition names none |
| Faulty setting | Round 1 suspends a ballot whose first position is empty or holds one uncounted candidate and whose interpretation counts for a candidate or is `overvote` | It reproduces every figure of the county's certified 2022 reports whose settings record the suspension and whose contests have a record file, including the Suspended and Overvotes rows.  A form that suspended only ballots counting for a candidate matched the candidates' figures and missed each of those two rows by 145 ballots for the mayor and 25 for District 6 |
| T3(c) and T11(c) | The tied final round is the only way the last round can hold one candidate after round 1, and the county count then ends at the tied pair | A batch never removes the candidate that qualifies it, and a batch that left only that candidate would mean that it held a majority, which rule 1 tests first.  A round with one candidate therefore follows a round with two that tie |
| T15 | Two ballots raise `A` | Raising `A` on one ballot of the smallest examples found makes a tie in round 1, and the counterexample should not depend on the order |
| Scripts | The corpus's scripts, with no figure script of this task committed | The corpus's `recount.py` and `district4.py` compute every figure that the requirements cite from records, with the same interpretation, and a second recount script in `tests/rcv/` would duplicate them |

## Review

A fresh agent reviewed the draft against the City's copy of the charter, the state's texts, the design, the decisions, the research, and its review.  It built its own model from the requirements alone, with the reader, the writer, the count, the report, the word forms, the single count, the county count, and the faulty setting, and it did not read the author's scripts.  It tested T3 to T14 on 103,000 random contests of up to six candidates of every kind and exhaustively on 978,991 small contests, T9 over every order for up to five candidates, T2 on 412,000 mutated files, and the word forms on 20,000 arrays, and its model rejected a non-strict batch test and an overvote reading that counts only continuing marks.  It found the quotations exact, the grammar unambiguous with the field count as its only choice, the error rule well defined, the example's and District 4's reports exact byte for byte, T15's contests as stated, and every figure of the requirements and of this record that it recomputed from the reports and D. Otis's files correct.  Each finding below was checked against the texts, the data, or a model before the response.

## Hand check of the edge cases

A reviewer who had read neither the reference nor the demo's Lean files worked out by hand, from these requirements alone, the reports of 85 well-formed edge-case files and the error lines of 83 malformed files, and compared each with the reference's output.  The well-formed files include every kind of case that the requirements' edge-case list names, the 15 edited files that the reader accepts, and the exhaustive files for `k` up to 4, whose figures the reviewer computed by formula.  Every report and error line agreed byte for byte, as did the results of the 17 word arrays.  The reviewer found two loose wordings, the inactive counts called cumulative and the reasons said to depend on the ballot alone, which the report table and the interpretation now state exactly.  The generator lacked a leading zero in the order line, an index above `n` inside a multiple field, and a ballot count of 2^32 − 1 on a valid line, and it now writes all three.

## Review of the specification

[The specification](README.md#specification) states the count of these requirements in `Contest.lean` and `Spec.lean`.  The table gives the details that the requirements leave to it, each decided by its author.

| Detail | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bytes and words | Identifiers and names are lists of bytes, and each position is its 64-bit word | A name may hold bytes from `0x80` to `0xFF` that need not form UTF-8, so a Lean string cannot hold it, and the word is the form that the requirements' word column, the word forms, and the program use |
| Arguments of the count | `count` takes the kinds, the order, and the position words of the ballots | The count reads nothing else, so the word form decodes into the same arguments, and identifiers and names cannot reach it |
| Number of rounds | `run` allows one more round than there are counted candidates, and gives no rounds and no winner when its allowance runs out | A round that does not end the count eliminates a member of its continuing set, and a set of one ends the count, so the case is never reached, which T3(d) is to prove |
| Inputs outside the constraints | Every definition gives some value, such as the first member of a tied set when the order lacks every member, and the kind `excluded` for a code above 3 | The requirements define results only for well-formed structures and valid arrays, and the module docstring of `Spec.lean` states that no requirement or theorem concerns the other values |
| Native runs | `rcv-spec` parses a canonical file with a parser of its own and accepts it only when `write` prints the result as the file's bytes and it meets `Contest.WellFormed` | The reader belongs to the second increment, and a structure that meets the constraints and prints as the file is the file's structure, so a defect of the parser can reject a file but cannot change a count |
| District 4 file | Written for the first run by a scratch script from D. Otis's CSV, one line per row with the `ballotID` as identifier, after a check of its rankings against `recount.ballots` | The converter of `tests/rcv/`, which came later, writes the same file apart from the contest identifier, and `rcv-spec district4` gives the same result on both |

### Review

A fresh agent reviewed the specification against these requirements, the decisions, and this record.  It wrote a model of the count, the report, the writer, and the word forms from the requirements before it read `Spec.lean`, and the model reproduced the worked example's file, report, and words byte for byte.  It ran `rcv-spec` on 128,519 contests, 78,176 of them exhaustive over small contests of every kind, order, and position form, 50,000 random with up to 20 candidates and 400 ballots, and 342 at the limits of 63 candidates and 63 positions or with nested qualifying sets.  The specification's reports and words equaled the model's in all 244,916 rounds, among them 18,941 batch rounds, 25,257 rounds at the equality of the strict test, 14,264 tied final rounds, and 29,052 counts with no winner.  The 11,034 word arrays, 4,712 valid and 6,322 invalid, gave the model's results, `Contest.WellFormed` rejected each of 32 files that break one constraint and accepted 10 at the limits, and the District 4 file gave the requirements' rounds.  The reviewer checked every expected value of `Cases.lean` by hand and found none wrong.

| Finding | Verification | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The docstring of `Words` says that a missing word reads as 0, which holds for `n`, `k`, and `N` and not for the kinds, the order, and the positions | Confirmed | The docstring says that the lists are shorter when the array ends before them |
| 2. The module docstring says "over lists and natural numbers", while positions and arrays are words | Confirmed | It names the 64-bit position words |
| 3. `eliminationLines` claims increasing index, which only `rule` provides | Confirmed | The docstring says that the lines follow the lists, which `rule` gives in increasing index |
| 4. The requirements say that the count reads `k`, and `countContest` does not | Confirmed.  No result changes, since each ballot of a well-formed structure holds `k` words | The docstring of `countContest` says that the count reads `k` through the ballots |
| 5. No case has a contest identifier of 1 or 64 bytes | Confirmed: the cases' contest identifiers are `case` and `example-1` | Cases of 1, 64, and 65 bytes, with the file's and the report's lines |
| 6. The values for structures that break the constraints, such as a word with bit 63 and another bit, which `field` prints as `*`, a mark above `n`, or a ballot with other than `k` words, are recorded nowhere | Confirmed | The module docstring states that the definitions give such values and that no requirement or theorem concerns them |

The reviewer could not run the `#guard`s, which need a build, or test the bound on `N` with an array of matching length, which it checked by reading.

### Comparison with the reference

After the first review I compared `rcv-spec` with the reference of `tests/rcv/`, which another agent wrote from the requirements without the specification.  On the files that `tests/rcv/edges.py` writes, the specification's reports equal the reference's byte for byte for all 283 files that the reference reads, over 655 rounds, and its result words equal the reference's for all 300 word arrays.  `rcv-spec` rejects each of the 349 files for which the reference gives an error report.  On the 19 canonical files that `tests/rcv/check.py` converts from D. Otis's records, 1,015,392 ballots in all, the reports and the result words are equal as well.

The corpus run found that `Words.ballots` took the ballots by dropping `b · k` words for ballot `b`, which is quadratic in `N` and ran for minutes on the 2022 mayoral contest.  `chunks` now takes them in one pass, and the 19 arrays take 1.9 s in all.

### Second review

A second fresh agent reviewed the revised specification and cases in the same way, with a model that it wrote before it read the specification.  Its model and `rcv-spec` gave the same bytes on 77,105 contests over 150,954 rounds, 40,300 of them random with up to 63 candidates and 63 positions and 36,797 exhaustive over three ranked candidates and over every pair and triple of kinds, and on 10,042 word arrays, 5,066 valid and 4,976 invalid, which fail each condition of the validity test first.  The rounds included 5,452 batches, 36,516 eliminations by order, 8,473 tied final rounds, and tied sets of up to 24 members.  A contest of 63 candidates, 63 positions, and 20,000 ballots took 2.2 s, and one of 10 candidates and 100,000 ballots 0.6 s.  The reviewer found no disagreement, no wrong expected value, and no docstring that misstates its definition, and it found every edge case of the Tests paragraph covered, apart from the malformed files.

| Note | Response |

| Rule 4 with an empty continuing set gives `.order [] 0` from the defaults of `fewest` and `latest`, and no comment records it | Unreachable: rule 2 ends every round with `Tᵣ` = 0, and a round with a continuing ballot has a continuing candidate.  Recorded here |

Neither reviewer could run the `#guard`s, which need a build, and both checked by reading the parts of `Spec.lean` that apply only outside the constraints.
