The energy controller on a NUCLEO-F767ZI: one simulated day

The energy controller's gateway, written in Lean and compiled through the verified compiler, WebAssembly, C, and CompCert, ran on a NUCLEO-F767ZI board for 1,440 one-minute ticks. A host program on the attached computer played the site around it over the board's USART3 at 4,800 baud: load and PV from a day of NIST's minute data, a battery, an inverter, and an optimizer that proposes limits every five minutes. It sent one input record each tick and checked every output record, and native Lean, given the same input records, produced the same output records.

The day in numbers

The host proposed limits 285 times. Of the 187 proposals of the full range, −5,000 to +5,000 W, all 187 were rejected with the status export cap, because the lower limit would let the site export more than the 2,000 W cap. Of the other 98 proposals, whose limits were narrower, 94 were accepted and 4 were not considered, because they arrived on ticks that requested fallback. No proposal arrived after tick 1,420. The gateway sent 94 commands, each with a revert timeout of 870 s, and the inverter reported a command in force for 1,124 of the 1,440 minutes.

Battery power ranged from −3,824 W to 2,627 W. The simulated stored energy, the middle of the interval that the gateway receives, ranged from 2.995 to 6.250 kWh. It lay below the floor of 3.0 kWh in 45 minutes, between 09:10 and 09:54, by at most 5.4 Wh, while the inverter was in command or fallback mode and the battery's power lay between 0 and 149 W.

The gateway requested fallback 4 times: tick 0 (00:00), first tick; tick 180 (03:00), load spike inside a window; tick 540 (09:00), load spike inside a window; tick 900 (15:00), load spike inside a window. Load spikes that drew no request: tick 1,260 (21:00), no window in force, since the last command's window of 900 s had ended.

How to read the chart

All four panels share one time axis, in hours from the start of the run, and a pointer over the chart shows every value at the nearest tick. The top panel is the measured load and PV that the gateway receives; four times in the day the host adds 5,000 W to the measured load, which the optimizer's load interval does not cover, to test the gateway's monitoring. The second panel is the battery power, positive while it charges, inside the limits that the inverter reports back: a command's limits while one is in force, and the fallback interval of −50 to +50 W otherwise. The third panel is the stored energy interval that the gateway receives, the host's simulated energy ±0.1 kWh, between the site's floor of 3.0 kWh and ceiling of 9.5 kWh. The host's battery model charges and discharges at 95 % efficiency and loses 10 W at all times (tests/energy/replay.py), so its energy falls slowly while the battery holds zero power. The bottom panel is the gateway's decisions: each proposal accepted as a command or rejected with the authorizer's status, the minutes in which a command is in force, and each request to return to fallback.

What ran, and what the theorems cover

The board ran the program whose theorems are gw_usart3_program and gw_usart3_prefix in Demos/Energy/Loop.lean. The first states that the program runs forever and that each window performs the one register access that the gateway's framing requests; the second states that the bytes written to USART3 begin the gateway's output records for the input records that the bytes read encode. The authorizer's theorems, such as authorize_sound, state that under accepted limits every trajectory the model admits keeps the site's energy and grid limits or stays no further outside them than a return to fallback would. The program's two theorems hold in the Lean copy of CompCert's Clight, and the Rocq composition carries such a run to CompCert's Asm; their trusted base is code B of docs/trusted-base.md. The site in this run is a simulation: the battery, inverter, and optimizer are the host's models, and only the gateway's program ran on the board.
