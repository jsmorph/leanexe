# Initial orbit determination: decisions and review

[The requirements](requirements.md) specify Gibbs' method over position boxes and Lambert's problem for zero revolutions in Izzo's formulation.  This record gives each decision with its reason, the findings of the independent reviews of the requirements and of the transfer theorems with the response to each, and the open items.

## Decisions

| Question | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Model of a two-body orbit | The pair `(h, e)` with the polar equation of its path, a lemma that a state lies on its own orbit, and a lemma that Newton's equation conserves `h` and `e` | It covers ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas, needs no orbital elements, and connects to Newton's equation through `newton_conserves`.  The SGP4 demo's Kepler orbit covers ellipses only and is parametrized by elements, which are undefined for circular and equatorial orbits. |
| Time along an orbit | The universal form of Kepler's equation relative to the state, with Lagrange's coefficients `f` and `g` (`conicPos`, `conicTime`) and the universal functions `U0` to `U3` of `β = 2μ/|r| - |v|²`, defined by the sign of `β` | One motion covers ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas, and the proofs of the path, the distance, the time, and Newton's equation use only the derivatives of `U0` to `U3` and two identities that hold for every conic.  Separate motions by the eccentric, hyperbolic, and parabolic anomalies would repeat each lemma three times and need a transfer defined by cases.  The form needs no perifocal axes, so it covers circular orbits, and for an ellipse `√β χ` is the advance of the eccentric anomaly. |
| First arrival | `ConicTransfer` requires an advance `χ > 0` that reaches `r'` in the time `t` and no smaller positive advance that reaches `r'` | The clause states the first arrival without a period, which a parabola and a hyperbola lack.  `conicPos_inj` shows that Izzo's advance is the first, and two first arrivals at one position are equal by the clause alone. |
| Statement of Lambert's theorem | `lambert_transfer`, an equivalence between a transfer along a conic in the direction of `k` and `v = v1(x*)`, and `lambert_newton`, the same for solutions of Newton's equation with `v' = v2(x*)` | One equivalence states existence and uniqueness for every `x* > -1`.  The converse `orbit_izzo` of the geometry carries the uniqueness, with `izzo_no_arrival` for the orbits with `x ≤ -1`, which pass through both positions in the direction of `k` but do not reach `r2` after `r1`. |
| Orbits with `x ≤ -1` | `izzo_no_arrival` from the coefficients of `r2` in the basis `r1`, `v1`: reaching `r2` needs `U1 = 2 c0 c1`, which is negative, while `U1 > 0` at every positive advance for `β ≤ 0` | Constructing the negative advance at which the motion passes `r2` would need two more cases of the time equation.  The sign argument needs only `c0 < 0` and the monotonicity of `U1`. |
| Uniqueness for Newton's equation | `newton_unique` by Mathlib's `ODE_solution_unique_of_eventually` on the product of two copies of `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)`, with `ContDiffAt.exists_lipschitzOnWith` for Newton's field and a connectedness argument on an open preconnected set of times | The field is continuously differentiable away from the origin, so it is Lipschitz near each state, and Mathlib's local theorem applies without a global Lipschitz constant.  The proof takes about 110 lines against the estimate of 200 to 300 for a Grönwall argument on `V3`, and `V3` keeps its definitions, so the compiled modules are unchanged. |
| Domain of a Newton transfer | `NewtonTransfer` takes a solution of `NewtonOn` on an open interval of times that contains `[0, t]` | A transfer that begins with a manoeuvre at `r1` follows another orbit before the time 0, so its motion solves Newton's equation only from 0 on, and a definition over the whole line would leave it out (review finding 6).  The interval is open so that the derivatives at 0 and `t` are two-sided. |
| Shape of an orbit | `orbitOf_ecc`: `e · h = 0` and `|e|² = 1 - β |h|²/μ²` | The model defines the shape by `|e⊥|`, while the documents read it from the sign of `β`, and the identity relates the two (review finding 5). |
| Statement of the program theorem | `lambertBox_transfer` states that exactly one pair `(v, v')` has a `NewtonTransfer` in the direction of `k` and that the velocity intervals contain it | The statement names only `NewtonOn`, `NewtonTransfer`, the vector operations, `axisOf`, `InBox` with `Contains`, and the program's result, so a reader needs no definition of Izzo's formulas or of the universal functions to read it.  L4 keeps the interval of `x`. |
| Proofs of the scalar identities | The basis `r1`, `r2` of `izzo_basis` and the substitution `x = (q² - (1 - λ²))/(2 λ q)`, `y = (q² + 1 - λ²)/(2 q)` with `q = y + λ x` | The substitution removes the relation `y² = 1 - λ² + λ² x²`, so each identity is one of rational functions that `field_simp` and `ring` prove without coefficients written by hand. |
| Place of `motionSign`, `izzoParams`, and `sqrt_max_zero` | `LambertOrbit.lean`, which `LambertSound.lean` imports | The real-number theorems do not import the program's proofs.  No statement changes, and `LambertBox.lean`, and so the compiled module, is unchanged. |
| Meaning of "three positions on one orbit" | Positions on the path with `D · h > 0` | For positions on a path with `D ≠ 0`, `D · h > 0` is necessary and sufficient for Gibbs' formula to give `v(r2)`; the review confirmed this on 200 orbits of every kind. |
| `N · D` in place of `\|N\| \|D\|` | The dot product | The two agree on a path, and the dot product needs no square root.  G1 and G2 hold for the program as written. |
| Monotonicity of Izzo's time of flight | Integral representations of `T` (L1), whose integrand increases with `1 - x²` | They prove the decrease without derivatives.  A proof from Izzo's derivative `dT/dx` needs a sign argument that fails for `x > 0` with `λ < 0`. |
| Direction of motion | An axis `k` with `h · k > 0` | Izzo's code uses the `z` axis, which leaves every transfer plane that contains `z` undecided.  The axis `k` is Izzo's convention when `k` is the `z` axis. |
| Search | Two bisections of at most 256 steps, one raising the point where `T > T*` is shown and one lowering the point where `T < T*` is shown | Each keeps one invariant.  When the points where the arithmetic cannot decide form one interval around the root, the two end at its edges, so the interval of `x` is as narrow as the arithmetic allows; the starting bracket below keeps that condition.  A single bisection that stops at the first undecided midpoint returned intervals up to 690 times wider in the first review's model (finding 7). |
| Starting bracket | `[1, b0]` when `tofI` shows `T(1) > T*`, `[a0, 1]` when it shows `T(1) < T*`, and `[a0, b0]` otherwise | The first test run found intervals of `x` up to 0.63 wide at `T* = 1.2`: from the bracket `[1/3, 5/3]` the midpoint is 1 within one unit in the last place, where `tofI` decides nothing, and the search that lowers `b` treated it as not below and moved its other point past the root.  A probe that avoided `1 ± 2^-20` was the first fix; the follow-up review found its upper constant wrong (`1 + 2^-24`) and the undecided region near 1 up to `3·10⁻⁵` wide, and proposed this bracket, which the branch `(2/3)(1 - λ³)` at exactly 1 makes accurate.  The proofs need only `bracket_spec`. |
| Status of an unfinished search | Status 4, without intervals | It follows the plan's rule that a theorem covers the accepting status only.  The review's model never needed more than 60 steps. |
| Interval inputs | Every input is an interval: positions, `μ`, and the time of flight | Measurement error enters as boxes, and the theorems quantify over every point of them. |
| Table parameters of the formulas | `enclosure_def` with empty tables for Gibbs' formula and Izzo's velocities | The formulas read no table, and the enclosure theorems hold for the tables `Trig.invPi` and `Trig.halfPi`, to which the empty tables are equal in the interval functions by `rfl`. |
| Lambert's wrapper | `lambertBoundsWith` takes the logarithm's tables, and `lambertBounds` is the wrapper | `verified_compile` admits a wrapper of tables as an entry and never as a callee. |
