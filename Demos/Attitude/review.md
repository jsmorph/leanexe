# Attitude determination requirements: decisions and review

[The requirements](requirements.md) specify TRIAD and the q-method from brief 6.2 of the space-operations candidates and from candidate 3 of the plan that orders them.  This record gives each decision with its reason, and the findings of two independent reviews, of the requirements and of the q-method's statements, with the response to each.

## Decisions

| Question | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| TRIAD's error bound | `F = 2 arcsin √h` from the decomposition of the error rotation into a rotation that carries the first observation onto its reference and a rotation about that reference | The classical bounds of Shuster and Oh are statistical.  The decomposition gives an exact identity for the half-angle, `cos(φ/2) = cos(α/2) cos(ψ/2)`, and a deterministic bound on `ψ` from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in the plane.  A numerical check with adversarial optimization found no error above `F`, and to first order `F` exceeds the worst error by a factor of at most `(1 + √5)/2`. |
| Form of the error hypothesis | One bound `ε_i` on `∠(R(q) b̄_i, r̄_i)` for each observation | It combines the sensor's error and the reference's error by the triangle inequality, and it is the form that `plan_premise` uses for its own premises. |
| Output of TRIAD | Intervals of the nine entries of the matrix, and a bound that covers every matrix in them | A quaternion output needs a branch on the largest diagonal entry, which interval arithmetic evaluates on both sides.  A bound that covers every matrix in the intervals lets a caller use any of them, such as the midpoint, with no further rounding analysis. |
| Arithmetic | The interval layer with `enclosure_def` for the frames, the matrix, and `h` | Each part's enclosure theorem follows from the command, and the real functions it declares are the model's formulas, related to the vector form by `triadOf_eq` and `boundOf_eq`. |
| Box term | `arcsin(η/(1 - η))` for `η ≤ 1/4` | The review notes that `arcsin η` suffices.  The term is below `1e-13` for every well-posed input, so the looser form costs nothing measurable, and its proof exists. |
| Pivots of the q-method's certificate | Intervals that enclose the exact pivots, in place of the plan's exact rationals | The normalized observations make `K` irrational, so exact rational arithmetic would apply to a matrix other than the model's.  Interval pivots enclose the exact pivots of the model's `K`, and their signs certify the inertia when no interval contains zero. |
| Order of the q-method's factorization | A symmetric permutation that puts the diagonal entry of largest magnitude first at each stage | The review constructed a well-posed attitude at which the first pivot of the unpermuted factorization is zero. |
| Cut `λc` | `λ̂2 + (λ̂1 - λ̂2)/16` from the solver's estimates | The review's model showed that the bound to the true attitude falls by about half from a cut at mid-gap to a cut near `λ2`, and a sixteenth of the gap keeps the pivots away from zero. |
| Inertia argument of the q-method | A vector with a positive form and a hyperplane on which the form is negative, carried through the stages of the factorization by induction | The factorization gives the hyperplane and the positive vector directly, and the induction holds for any choice of exchanges, so the proof needs no fact about the program's data-dependent choice of pivots and no result of Mathlib on signatures. |
| Choice of pivot | The remaining diagonal interval whose endpoints have the largest magnitude | The choice affects only whether the certificate succeeds.  The theorem `pivots_enc` holds for the pivots of some choice, whatever the program computes. |
| Terms of the observations | `k K(b, r)` with `k = a/(\|b\| \|r\|)` | It equals `a K(b̄, r̄)` for every input, since division by zero gives zero on both sides in Lean, and it needs one division in place of six and gives narrower intervals. |
| Square root of the residual | The interval of `ρ̂²` clamped at zero, with its own upper endpoint as the upper bound | Without the clamp every result was status 2: the residual of a good eigenvector lies within the width of the interval of `K q̂`, so outward rounding extends the sum of squares below zero, where `sqrt` rejects. |
| Output of the q-method | Eight values, with the cut `c` last | The tests compare `c` with an independent eigendecomposition and the bounds with their formulas, which need `c`. |
| Statement of Q4 | The cut, the existence and uniqueness of the maximizer, and the facts of the input, in place of the enclosure of the exact pivots | The enclosure of the pivots is a step of the proof (`pivots_enc`) and concerns pivots that the program's choices define. |
| Compilation of the q-method | A compilation of its own, `Demos.Attitude.QMethod.compiled` | TRIAD's module, its bytes, and its theorems stay as they are. |
| State of the Jacobi sweeps | A structure of ten floats and four columns, without arrays | The program allocates only its result, so the bound of a call is a constant. |

## Review of the requirements

A fresh agent reviewed the draft against the brief, the plan, and the slew planner's conventions, without the derivation of the bound or the author's scripts.  It built a model in Python and mpmath, recomputed every claim that admits computation, tried to falsify `F` on 295 adversarial cases, and ran wrong programs and wrong bounds against the stated theorems and tests.  It confirmed A1 to A4 on 20,000 inputs, `F` with equality attained at `ε1 = 0`, the composition form of `F`, the first-order worst case and the factor `(1 + √5)/2`, Davenport's identity in the body-to-inertial convention, `K_i² = I`, the inertia argument, the one-vector Davis–Kahan bound, and the `2(α + β)` claim.

| Finding | Verification | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A5's condition `x < 1` holds for parallel references, where `x` is zero and `T` is singular | Confirmed with `r1 = r2` | The condition is `sin(ε1 + ε2) < \|r̄1 × r̄2\|`, as `triad_error` states it in Lean |
| 2. TRIAD's statuses overlap for a non-finite error bound with a short cross product | Confirmed | The table states the conditions of status 1 first, then those of status 2, and moves non-finite error bounds to status 2 only |
| 3. The theorems admit a program that always rejects or returns a large `err` | Confirmed | The tests require status 0 on a stated region and `err` within `1e-12` of its formula, and the requirements state that no theorem excludes such a program |
| 4. The q-method's `λc`, sweeps, and bounds are unspecified | Confirmed | The interface states eight sweeps, `λc`, and both formulas |
| 5. The solver determines `err`, against the text | Confirmed | Sentence corrected |
| 6. The q-method lacks its real-number lemmas and its looseness | Confirmed: the bound exceeds the worst error by `1/sin(θ/2)` for two observations | Q3a to Q3c added, and the looseness stated |

| 9. Status conditions use unstated endpoints, and a zero vector fits no q-method status | Confirmed | Endpoints stated, and a vector of zero length fails the input conditions |

| 12. An unpermuted LDLᵀ fails at well-posed attitudes | Confirmed by the constructed case | Symmetric permutation adopted |
| 13. The tests miss a dropped box term, a lower endpoint as `err`, and errors in `h` at first order | Confirmed | The containment test compares `err` with its formula in both directions in high precision and includes the reviewer's cases |

The review's wrong programs, with what detects each, were TRIAD transposed (A1, A4, A6, and the containment test), one frame left-handed (A3), TRIAD anchored on the second observation (A1 and the containment test), `ε2` in place of `ε1` in `h` (A6 and the formula comparison), a dropped box term or a lower endpoint as `err` (A6 and the formula comparison), and the q-method with the conjugate `K` (Q1 and Q4).  A frame left-handed on both sides gives the same `T` and is no defect.  No case detects a program that always rejects, which the region of required status 0 now does.

## Review of the q-method

A second fresh agent reviewed the q-method's requirements and Lean statements, the program, the generator, and the oracle, without the author's prototype.  Its model in Python and mpmath mirrors the program, interval rounding and Jacobi sweeps included, and reproduces the native results of the 250 inputs of the first test set bit for bit.  Its wrong programs are mutations of that model, whose results it ran through the oracle.  It confirmed Davenport's matrix in this repository's convention against the expansion of `q ↦ r̄ · (q b̄ q*)` and `K_i² = |b|² |r|² I`, both exactly; the residual identity of Q3a and Wahba's identity to `1e-39`; the inertia of 59,870 exact rational matrices whose pivots for arbitrary exchanges were one positive and three negative; the Davis–Kahan bound in 4,000 trials and `true_near` in 600; `rotation_near` in 3,000 trials, with equality when `p` and `q` lie on one great circle on opposite sides of `e`; and Q4 on the 225 random inputs, where an admitted attitude near the largest admitted error lay within `err` and `err` exceeded it by factors from 1.53 to 8.28.

| Finding | Verification | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1. `errOpt` is not tested: on the inputs it lies between `1.1e-14` and `3.9e-13`, at least 29 times the angle to the optimum, so the comparison within `1e-12` accepts a halved or doubled `errOpt` | Confirmed: both wrong programs passed | The class `turned` applies `certify` to the program's quaternion turned by `1e-6`, `1e-4`, and `1e-2`, where `errOpt` is 1.067 times the angle.  The oracle rejects results with `errOpt` halved on all 30 such inputs and with `errOpt` doubled on all 30. |
| 2. A count that is not whole goes undetected: the inputs with counts 2.5 and 17 also had the wrong length, and Q4's truncated `count` admits a program that reads 2.5 as 2 | Confirmed: the two wrong programs returned status 0 on inputs of fitting length | Both inputs added with status 1 required.  Q4 states `InputOk`, which includes `val xs[0]! = count xs` and the input's length as a word count, proved from `valid` through the exact conversion of the count (`toFloat_value`). |
| 3. The looseness factor `1/sin(θ/2)` and the comparison with TRIAD are wrong | Confirmed: with exact data and equal weights and bounds, the factor is `1/sin(m/2)` with `m = min(θ, π - θ)`, times about 16/15 for the program's cut, 1.51 at 90° and 6.14 at 20° and 160°.  `err` lies below TRIAD's `F` only within about 2° of 90°. | Rewritten in the requirements and the README with these values, without the provenance |
| 4. No theorem states them, since `R(q̂)` normalizes `q̂` and ignores its sign, and the requirements now say so. |
| 5. The wrong programs, with what detects each, were: the conjugate convention of `K`, which a wrong sign of `z` and `Bᵀ` in place of `B` give as the same matrix, and an `S + σ I` block, which falsify Q4 at the optimum, with the angle to the optimum above `errOpt` on 226 of 230 results, and on which the oracle stopped before its correction; a missing scale, weight, or count in `K`, detected by the cut, the angle, and the status; the sums of the error bounds over the wrong count, detected by the formula of `err`; sine and cosine exchanged, detected by the status and falsifying Q4 on a `bad2` input; cuts at `λ̂1` or `λ̂2`, the smaller estimate picked, and no clamp, which only reject and are allowed; a dropped factor 2 in `err`, the bound without its first arcsine, `errOpt` in place of `err`, the bounds without their second term, and lower endpoints as the bounds, detected by the formulas and the true attitudes; a count that is not whole and a count of 17, now detected by the status; an unnormalized, negated, or doubled `q̂`, now detected by the unit-length check; a halved or doubled `errOpt`, now detected on the `turned` inputs; and the unpivoted and smallest-first factorizations, sound but now detected by the status of the rotated input.  A negative pivot tested by its lower endpoint and a sign test that accepts any positive pivot leave the results of the sample unchanged.  Q4's proof needs the endpoint tests, so neither program has the theorem.

## Open items

1. An onboard build on A64 or the Cortex-M7.
2. A bound with `ε1 |cos θ|` in place of `ε1` in `x`, which would reach TRIAD's first-order worst case.
3. A tighter bound for the q-method: `err` adds the two arcsines of the triangle inequality and divides by the gap to the cut, not to `λ2`, and it exceeds the largest admitted error by factors from 1.53 to 8.28 on the generated inputs.
4. Inputs on which a pivot's sign tested by the wrong endpoint, or a sign test that accepts any positive pivot, changes the result, since both leave the results of the sample unchanged.
