# Reference semantics N

N is a reference semantics for the Clight forms that the C translator emits, written once in a neutral language in [the specification](N.nsx) and rendered mechanically into Rocq and Lean.  [The check](check.sh) confirms from what each prover elaborated that the two renderings say what the specification says, so the step between the provers rests on a mechanical comparison in place of the review of a hand transcription.  N covers the integer leaf forms, which each prover proves of its own definitions: the operators at the type pairs that the translator emits, the casts, the truth values of conditions, and the integer constants.  N also runs expressions, statements, calls, and whole programs over temporaries and scalar globals, with the volatile store to `lx_out` and its event.  Rocq proves that every derivation of N is a derivation of CompCert 3.18's `Clight2` semantics, and Lean proves that every execution of the Lean copy of Clight is a derivation of N for the programs that the translator's forms make up.  For the bare-metal program of `gcd 1071 462`, the chain runs from the copy's whole-program termination in Lean to the premise of `clight2_bigstep_to_asm` in Rocq, with only the check between the provers.  [The translator design](../docs/native-translator.md#trust-argument-and-validation) describes what N carries and what remains under review and tests.

## The neutral language

A specification is a sequence of S-expressions.  The symbol table declares each sort, data constructor, base symbol, and interpreted symbol with its name in Rocq and its name in Lean, and the renderer writes those names while the check expects them in the elaborated terms.  A name may be an application whose arguments are fixed: the types and instance of the addition of integers, or the type arguments of a polymorphic type or constructor such as `Gvar`, so that the check confirms the arguments that elaboration chose.  Data constructors are the syntax and values that both provers share by name: types, operators, expressions, statements, functions, global definitions, initialization data, external functions, outcomes, events, and values.  The sort `ident` is `positive` in Rocq and `Nat` in Lean, whose 0 no program uses, and the sorts `float` and `float32` are opaque to N's rules, whose floating-point values arise only from constants.  Interpreted symbols name the definitions under test, which the twin files `NBase.v` and `Base.lean` map to CompCert's `sem_binary_operation`, `sem_cast`, `bool_val`, and `eval_expr` and to the Lean copy's at `Archi.aarch64`.

A relation is a list of constructors, each a closed formula whose conclusion applies the relation, and a statement is a closed formula.  Formulas use `forall`, `exists`, implication, conjunction, disjunction, equivalence, negation, equality, `Some`, `None`, the lists `cons` and `nil`, the pairs `pair`, natural numerals, numerals of identifiers `(lit ident N)`, strings of letters, digits, and underscores, and applications of symbols, relations, and bound variables.  Sorts are the declared ones and their options, lists, products, and functions.  The language has no function definitions, pattern matching, or conditional terms, so that every declaration elaborates to a formula that a small reader prints and compares.  A macro is expanded before rendering and before comparison, and an expansion that would capture a variable of its arguments is an error.  A further specification file starts with `(part (rocq MODULE))` and adds constants, `(constant NAME SORT VALUE)`, and statements to N, rendered into the Rocq module `MODULE` and a Lean module of the namespace `ClightN.Spec`.  A statement declared with `(carried NAME (rocq THEOREM) FORMULA)` is proved in Lean and is the first premise of the Rocq theorem `THEOREM`.

The base symbols are integer arithmetic and order, the value of a word or pointer offset (`u32`, `u64`, `uptr`) and the word or offset of an integer (`r32`, `r64`, `rptr`), the signed value of a 64-bit word (`s64`), the quotient and remainder by a positive divisor (`zdiv`, `zmod`), the power of two of a number that is not negative (`pow2`), the bitwise operations on numbers that are not negative (`land`, `lor`, `lxor`), the concatenation of traces (`app`), and the floating-point numbers of bit patterns and their bits (`f64_of_bits`, `f64_bits`, `f32_of_bits`, `f32_bits`).  The laws at the end of the specification characterize every base symbol other than arithmetic and order on the arguments with which N uses it, and both provers prove them.  The meaning that the check trusts is therefore integer arithmetic and order, the identification of words by their values, and the names of the definitions under test.

## The relations of N

N runs a program over two maps from identifiers to values: the temporaries of the running function and the scalar globals, the writable, non-volatile variables of type `unsigned int` or `unsigned long long`.  Its relations take the program's definitions as a parameter, among which a call finds its function and an access its variable.  `neval` evaluates constants, temporaries, scalar globals, and the leaf operators and casts, with `has_type` giving the type of each such expression.  `nexec` follows `exec_stmt` of `ClightBigstep.v` with `function_entry2`: `Sskip`, `Sset`, the store to a scalar global, `Ssequence`, `Sifthenelse`, `Sloop` with its three rules, `Sbreak`, `Scontinue`, `Sreturn`, a call of an internal function by its name, whose rule executes the callee's body from its bound parameters and undefined temporaries, and the volatile store of an `unsigned int` to a variable of type `volatile unsigned int`, whose trace is one `Event_vstore`.  `nprog` requires distinct identifiers, initialization data that CompCert's `init_mem` accepts, and the initial value of each scalar global, and runs a `main` of type `int (void)` to an `int` result.

`nprog_sound` in `NExec.v` carries `nprog` of a program's definitions and `main` to `Clight2.bigstep_program_terminates` of the program.  The proof relates N's temporaries to CompCert's and N's scalar globals to the memory, where each holds its value at offset 0 with a writable permission, and builds the `Clight2` derivation by induction on N's.  `program_to_n` in `Exec.lean` carries the copy's `bigstep_program_terminates` at `Archi.aarch64` to `nprog` for a program that `progOk` accepts: its identifiers are distinct, its variables have the initialization data that N accepts, and the functions that `main` reaches use only the forms above.  The proof follows the copy's derivation through the mutual recursion of `exec_stmt` and `eval_funcall`, and it carries the invariants that no temporary holds a pointer and that each `return` gives a value of a leaf cast to the function's result type.  The copy writes the trace of a loop's three runs as `(t1 ++ t2) ++ t3` and CompCert as `t1 ** (t2 ** t3)`, and N follows CompCert, so the Lean proof applies the associativity of concatenation.

## The GCD program

`check.sh` builds the instance of the bare-metal program of `gcd 1071 462` from the translator's current output.  `gcd/emit.lean` writes the C text, which `native-bare emit` also writes, and the bytes of the result, the tree's `clightgen` prints the program as a Coq file, and `clightgen.py` writes `Gcd.nsx`, which holds the file's definitions, its `main`, the trace of one store to `lx_out` for each byte, and the carried statement `gcd_terminates`: N's program judgment of those definitions with that trace and the result 0.  The script is untrusted, since each prover checks the constants that it writes.  `gcd/Gcd.lean` proves the statement from the copy's theorem `loopCompiled.gcd.program`.  The kernel computes the translation and checks that renaming its whole program gives the file's definitions, the renaming read off the two lists of definitions by position, and the renaming theorem and `program_to_n` carry the copy's termination to the file's program and to N.  In Rocq, `gcd_defs` equates the instance's definitions with those of `clightgen`'s file by computation, `nprog_sound` gives the file's program's termination, `export_terminates` gives the termination of each program from which `clightgen`'s exporter prints that file, and `gcd_asm` composes the result with `clight2_bigstep_to_asm`.

`gcd_asm` takes as premises, besides the statement and the compilation of CompCert, that the exporter of `clightgen` printed the program inside `clightgen` as the file's definitions (`export_defs` of `NExport.v` transcribes `stmt` of `export/ExportClight.ml`), and that the two programs have the same `main`, public identifiers, and composite types.  As for every program of the native path, the chain also trusts that `clightgen` and `ccomp` run the same front end.  The Lean check of the instance takes about 40 seconds and 6 GB, of which the imports take about 4 GB.  Its checks of injectivity test each occurring identifier for membership in the list of the definitions' identifiers and that list's renaming for repetitions, which keeps the kernel's work in proportion to the occurrences.

## Rendering and the check

`uv run clightn/nsx.py render clightn/N.nsx clightn/rocq/NSpec.v LeanExe/Native/ClightN/Spec.lean` writes the two renderings of N with every argument of every constant explicit, and the check trusts none of its output.  For Rocq, `check.sh` generates `NRead.v`, which prints under `Set Printing All` each relation with its list of constructors, the type of the relation and of each constructor, the value and type of each constant, the value of each statement, the type and the assumptions of each proof, and the assumptions of CompCert's `transf_c_program_correct`, each to a file of its own.  For Lean, `ClightN.Read.dump` walks the elaborated expressions of the namespace `ClightN.Spec` and of the proofs in `ClightN.Proof` and writes them as S-expressions with every argument and the full name of every constant, together with the axioms of each proof.

`nsx.py check` maps each printed constant through the symbol table, turns numerals and strings into tokens, drops the type arguments of equality, `Some`, `None`, the existential, `cons`, `nil`, and `pair`, writes a product whose variable is unused as an implication, and replaces bound names by their depths.  It then compares every relation, constructor, constant, and statement with the specification after the expansion of macros.  A constant outside the table never equals a term of the specification, so a coercion, an instance, or a definition that elaboration inserts appears as a difference.  The check also requires each relation to be inductive with exactly the constructors of the specification, each statement `S` to be proved by a theorem `NProof.S` in Rocq and `ClightN.Proof.S` in Lean whose type is the rendered statement itself, each carried statement to be the first premise of its Rocq theorem, and each proof to assume only what CompCert's `transf_c_program_correct` assumes in Rocq and `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` in Lean.  The Rocq theorem of a carried statement may also assume `ClassicalEpsilon.constructive_indefinite_description`, which CompCert's argument about behaviors uses.

`clightn/check.sh --mutations` then plants errors in copies of the renderings and the proofs and requires the check to report exactly those errors.  In the Lean rendering of N they are an operator, a numeral, a numeral of another type, the order of arguments, a coercion through `Nat`, a data constructor, the volatile flag of the output's variable, the order of a sequence's traces, and the calling convention of `main`.  In the Rocq rendering of N they are an operator, a coinductive relation, an extra constructor, the truth value of a branch, the association of a loop's traces, and a membership without its premise.  The GCD instance's renderings receive a changed byte of the trace and a changed argument of the call in Lean, and a changed `main` and a changed argument in Rocq.  In each prover a theorem that proves a weaker statement under a statement's name and a proof that assumes its statement (`sorry` and `Admitted`) complete the set, with an assumed proof of the GCD statement in Lean and of `gcd_asm` in Rocq.  A planted error that the comparison misses, or a difference that no planted error explains, fails the script.

## Running

The check needs `coqc`, a CompCert 3.18 tree configured for `aarch64-linux` with its proofs and `clightgen` built, `uv`, and the Lean toolchain, and it refuses a tree configured for another target.  It works in `build/clightn`, runs Lean through `tools/leanrun` with a memory limit of 10 GB, compiles the rendering of the GCD instance outside the package with `lean -o`, and prints the agreement of the relations, constants, and statements, followed with `--mutations` by a line for each group of planted errors.  From the repository's root, the command is:

```sh
clightn/check.sh --mutations /path/to/CompCert
```

The proofs need `Archi.ptr64 = true`: on AArch64 a cast between two `long` types passes a pointer unchanged, as the constructors of `cast_ok` say, so the Rocq proofs fail against the ARMv7-M tree.  The x86-64 path has no CompCert tree with built proofs, so AArch64 is the only target that the check covers.  [`tools/full-check`](../tools/full-check) runs `clightn/check.sh --mutations` in step 7, after `rocq/check.sh`, when the CompCert tree with built proofs is configured for AArch64.
